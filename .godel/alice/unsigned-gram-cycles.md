# The completed Gram needs signs, even away from its diagonal

For the separate [short-block detector](dense-detector-cost.md), take $N=q^{1.99+o(1)}$, $Q=qT=q^{2.11}$, and one time $|t_i|\le T$ per distinct nonprincipal character at a prime. Let $G_{ij}=\sum_n w(n/N)\chi_i\bar\chi_j(n)n^{i(t_i-t_j)}$, where $w$ is fixed, smooth, nonnegative, one on $[1,2]$, and supported in $(7/8,17/8)$. Put $H=G-dI$, where the common diagonal $d\asymp N$. The sufficient uniform operator bound is $q^{1.995-\delta}$, $0<\delta<.005$ fixed. The principal character costs at most one exception.

**Counterexample to a uniform unsigned estimate.** Split the nonprincipal characters into two classes of comparable size, giving them the two times $0$ and $u=2\pi(3N/2)/q$. Both are allowed. The [single stationary term calculation](dense-detector-cost.md) applies to *every* cross-class pair, since its ratio is nonprincipal: $|H_{ij}|=(1+o(1))\sqrt{3N/2}$, uniformly (Gauss factors of constant modulus, nonstationary error uniform in the character). Hence $\|H\|_F\gg q\sqrt N$. Even the sum of $|H_{ij}H_{jk}H_{kl}H_{li}|$ over **pairwise distinct** indices is $\gg q^4 N^2$, just from alternating classes. This precludes any uniform power saving over $1.995$ by a Frobenius bound, or over $7.98$ by taking absolute values in the distinct-cycle sum. These are not detector peaks. In fact on this list $\|G\|_{\rm op}\ll N$: Stack the two fixed-time evaluation matrices, with weights $\sqrt w$. Each has squared operator norm $O(N+q)$ by fixed-time character orthogonality and Cauchy-Schwarz within each residue class. The squared norm of the stack is at most the sum of those two bounds. Thus the obstruction is in discarding signs, not a lower bound of the target size on the true operator.

**Comparison with dual completion.** Theorem HBT in [Chen, Gupta, and Li, “Energy bound”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08296v2) gives $q^{o(1)}(RL^2+R^2L+R^{5/4}Q^{1/2}L)$ for the sum of squares of length-$L$ dyadic polynomials in character ratios and time differences for the $R$ samples, with common coefficients bounded by one. Distinct primitive characters meet the spacing condition, even if two times are equal. Apply the theorem to the *sample list*, not a list of $R^2$ separately spaced ratios.

For clarity, Mellin invert $w$ off the diagonal, shifting $\Re s$ to $-B$ and applying the functional equation to $L(s-i(t_i-t_j),\chi_i\bar\chi_j)$. Truncating the dual Dirichlet series at $D=Q^{1+\epsilon}/N$ costs $O_{B,w}(Q^{1/2}(Q/(ND))^B)$ by absolute convergence, the gamma ratio bound, and rapid decay of the Mellin transform; take $B$ sufficiently large for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Shift the finite terms back to $\Re s=1/2$, without crossing gamma poles. The functional-equation scalar then has modulus one and is outside the dual sum. Dyadic division, the integrable Mellin weight, and Cauchy-Schwarz give, up to subpower losses (letting $\epsilon$ tend to zero in exponents),

$$\|H\|_F^2\ll N q^{o(1)}\sup_{M\le D,\,v}\ M^{-1}\sum_{ij}|P_{ij}|^2,$$

where $P_{ij}=\sum_{m\asymp M} c_m(v)\bar\chi_i\chi_j(m)m^{-i(t_i-t_j)}$ with common bounded $c_m(v)$ (a negligible truncation error is understood). For fixed integer $k$, $P_{ij}^k$ has length $\asymp_k M^k$ and coefficients bounded by $q^{o(1)}$ by the divisor bound. Split into $O_k(1)$ dyadic blocks. HBT followed by Hölder over the $R^2$ pairs, using $R\le q$, bounds the last display by

$$N q^{2+o(1)}\sup_{M\le D}\big(M q^{-1/k}+1+q^{.305/k}\big).$$

For $k=11$ the extra squared-norm exponent is $\max(.12-1/11,.305/11)=8/275$. Thus even this powered bound gives only $\|G\|_{\rm op}\le d+\|H\|_F\ll q^{2.009546+o(1)}$. The two-class example shows why optimizing such an unsigned estimate *all the way to its natural scale* would still not give the fixed saving.

**If returning to this route**, keep the phases in the distinct-cycle sum. Completion above gives $|H_{ij}|\ll q^{o(1)}\sqrt Q$ by taking absolute values in each length-$M$ dual sum. Repeated-index terms in $\operatorname{tr}(H^4)$ cost $O(q^{7.22+o(1)})$ since $H_{ii}=0$. A signed upper bound $q^{7.98-4\delta}$ on its distinct-index part would suffice. Neither this bound nor a counterexample on actual peak lists is established here.

The [clustered-time bound](clustered-gram.md) does prove the signed operator target for lists covered by at most $q^{.440-\eta}$ unit intervals. Actual peak lists have not been shown to satisfy that restriction.
