# Which through-point components still need an occupancy bound?

Use the [prescribed-product jet proof](prescribed-product-jets.md), including its rule for choosing $(h,f)$ jointly. At fixed $v,h,f$, write the box as $|i|,|j|\le N=q^{.1348+o(1)}$ and the projective jet point as $P=[A:B:q]$. In fact $q\nmid AB$: Multiplying $A,B$ by $f$ and reducing modulo $q$ gives the two nonzero input products. The component degrees are fixed, their primitive coefficient heights are $q^{O(1)}$, and each point costs divisor-many columns. Here are two additions to the priced family. Neither assumes that the family covers the columns.

## Geometric genus zero, including singular cubics

An absolutely irreducible nonlinear component $C$ of geometric genus zero through $P$ costs $q^{o(1)}$ integral points in the box. This assertion is uniform in the coefficients at fixed degree $D$ and fixed polynomial height exponent. The singular points cost $O_D(1)$ by intersecting with a nonzero partial derivative. Unless already done, take three distinct smooth integral points. The smooth projective normalization has a rational point, so is $\mathbb P^1$ over $\mathbb Q$.

**Height of a marked parametrization.** Write the normalization map as a triple $X,Y,Z$ of binary forms of degree $D$ with no common zero (the pullback of a generic line has degree $D$). Mark the preimages of the three points at $0,1,\infty$. The forms may be taken integral with coefficients $q^{O(1)}$. Here is a height argument to avoid assuming that a parametrization found abstractly has controlled denominators. In each of the finitely many charts setting one coefficient to 1, impose the coefficient equations of $C(X,Y,Z)=0$, the three affine image values, and $uZ(0)Z(1)Z(\infty)=1$ with an extra variable $u$. At least one chart contains the parametrization. In any such chart these fixed-size equations of bounded degree and polynomial height have a unique complex solution. Indeed the three distinct images exclude a constant map; after cancellation the degree of a triple giving this map is the covering degree times $D$, hence no cancellation and covering degree one. Any two such maps differ by a source automorphism and a common scalar, both fixed by the markings and chart. The solution is rational by the marked normalization over $\mathbb Q$.

For detail on the height inference, compute a Gröbner basis over $\mathbb Q$ with a degree-compatible order. At fixed initial degrees and variable count the number of rational operations is bounded independently of the input coefficients: Each nonzero remainder added in Buchberger's algorithm has degree at most twice the preceding maximum and leading monomial outside the preceding leading ideal. The possible lists of added leading monomials form a finitely branching tree with no infinite path by the ascending chain condition for monomial ideals. Thus their lengths and degrees are bounded in terms of $D$ alone; ordinary reductions in these bounded degrees take boundedly many steps. A bounded number of rational arithmetic operations on polynomial-height coefficients keeps both numerators and denominators of polynomial height. The zero-dimensional quotient has a standard monomial basis of bounded size (the leading ideal contains a pure power of each variable among its bounded-degree generators). Multiplication by each coordinate on that basis has a rational matrix computed by boundedly many further reductions. Its characteristic polynomial annihilates that coordinate at the solution. Clearing denominators gives a nonzero polynomial of polynomial height; the rational root bound controls the numerator and denominator (remove powers of the variable for a nonzero root). This proves the asserted height after a common scaling. No effective small degree bound is needed.

Smooth integral image points have primitive rational parameters $[a:b]$, with $Z(a,b)\ne0$. Separate the cases by the number of **geometric** roots of the binary form $Z$.

*At least two roots.* Choose a bounded integer $j_0$ such that $X'=X+j_0Y$ is coprime to $Z$; at most $D$ choices are forbidden. Forms of degree $D-1$ multiplying $X'$ and $Z$ span the binary forms of degree $2D-1$: The square coefficient map has zero kernel by coprimality. Its determinant gives a nonzero integer $K$ of polynomial height, and its adjugate gives integral representations of $K a^{2D-1}$ and $K b^{2D-1}$ with multipliers of polynomial height. At the point, $Z(a,b)\mid X'(a,b)$, so $Z(a,b)\mid K$ by primitivity. With $m=\max(|a|,|b|)$ the larger identity gives $|K|m^{2D-1}\ll q^{O(1)}m^{D-1} (1+N)|Z(a,b)|$, whence $m=q^{O(1)}$ as an upper bound.

After a bounded unimodular change on the source, $L=Z(1,0)\ne0$. For the roots $\rho_i$ of $Z(T,1)$ with multiplicities, each $L\rho_i$ is an algebraic integer. The elements $L a-L\rho_i b$ multiply to $L^{D-1}Z(a,b)$, a nonzero divisor of $L^{D-1}K$. Work in the splitting field of degree at most $D!$. The principal ideal of each element has $q^{o(1)}$ possibilities by prime ideal factorization: The number of ideals dividing a rational integer of polynomial size is bounded by a fixed power of its divisor count in bounded field degree. All embeddings of each element have polynomial size by the root bound and parameter height, and are bounded below by an inverse polynomial by the nonzero integral norm. Within any one ideal the elements differ by units. There are only polylogarithmically many such choices. Indeed the vectors of log absolute values of the embeddings lie in a box of side $O(\log q)$ and are uniformly separated in sup norm modulo torsion. In bounded degree there are finitely many algebraic integers all of whose conjugates have modulus at most 2 (bound their monic polynomial coefficients); those with all conjugates of modulus 1 are roots of unity, of uniformly bounded order. Apply this to ratios of generators that are units. Two distinct roots now give two independent linear forms in $a,b$, hence $q^{o(1)}$ parameters in total.

*Just one root.* It is rational, of polynomial height by the rational root bound. A rational change of parameter of polynomial height moving it to infinity gives affine coordinates $u(t),w(t)\in\mathbb Q[t]$ of degree at most $D$, with common denominator and coefficients of polynomial height. Neither coordinate is constant since $C$ is nonlinear. Put $H=Mu\in\mathbb Z[t]$ for a common denominator $M$, and let $c$ be its leading coefficient. At a rational parameter giving an integral point $H(t)$ is integral, so $ct$ is integral by the rational root theorem. Replacing $t$ by $t'/c$ reduces the task to integral $t'$ with two fixed integer polynomials divisible by the common denominator $M'=|Mc^D|$.

For one polynomial of degree at most $D$ and $p^n\parallel M'$, its roots modulo $p^n$ are a union of at most $D$ progressions on each of which substitution makes the polynomial **coefficientwise** divisible by $p^n$. Strip common $p$-content first (stop if the condition is empty or automatic). For a root $r$ of multiplicity $e$ of the nonzero reduction, substitute $r+pT$. The new content valuation $\mu$ is between 1 and $e$ since the $T^e$ coefficient has valuation $e$. After division by $p^\mu$ the reduction has degree at most $\mu$ because the original $T^j$ coefficient was divisible by $p^j$. Stop if $\mu\ge n$; otherwise recurse at $n-\mu$. The sum of root multiplicities at each split is bounded by the degree of the reduction, proving the leaf bound (an automatic initial condition costs one). Intersect the two progression covers at each prime, and apply CRT. There are at most $D^{2\omega(M')}=q^{o(1)}$ progressions, and on each, substitution gives integer polynomials $F(T),G(T)$ for the actual affine coordinates.

If more than $D$ distinct integral parameters on one such progression gave points in the box, interpolation on $\deg F+1$ of them would bound the nonzero leading coefficient by $(D+1)N<q$ (the interpolation denominators are nonzero integers). Likewise for $G$. The polynomial parametrization reaches the affine point $(A/q,B/q)$ at a finite algebraic parameter $T_0$: Its projective extension is surjective and infinity maps to infinity. This holds even if $P$ is singular. The parameter is nonzero, since both constant terms are integral. Writing $n=\deg F$, the reciprocal $qU^n F(1/U)-A U^n$ is Eisenstein at $q$: Its leading coefficient is a unit modulo $q$ and the constant has valuation one. The reciprocal for $G,B$ is Eisenstein too. The common root $1/T_0$ makes them proportional over $\mathbb Q$, forcing the same degree and a linear dependence of $1,F,G$, impossible on $C$. This bounds the points per progression and proves the claim.

## Ordinary multiple points in degrees 7 through 12

The smooth-point determinant of the jet proof still works at an ordinary multiple point, meaning **distinct geometric tangent lines**. Translate affine coordinates to the point $(A/q,B/q)$, and write the translated equation as $\sum_{a=e}^D H_a(x,y)=0$ in homogeneous layers of order $a$, with $e$ the multiplicity. By a bounded unimodular change of directions make $H_e(0,1)\ne0$. In addition to $q$, mark as bad the primes dividing the numerators of $H_e(0,1)$ and of the discriminant of $H_e(1,T)$. Both are nonzero and of polynomial height; translation introduces only $q$ in denominators. Enumerate the full bad-supported part $l_0$ of $l=d/f$ at subpower cost as in the jet proof. If $l_0>2N$ the congruences fix both original coordinates. Otherwise cross $q^{.0194}$ by a good smooth divisor $g$ of $l/l_0$ as before.

At $p^k\parallel g$, both displacements lie in $p^k\mathbb Z_p$. If $v_p(x)>v_p(y)$, the unique lowest-valuation term is the $y^e$ term, impossible. The displacement is nonzero since $(A/q,B/q)$ is not integral. Thus $x\ne0$, $t=y/x\in\mathbb Z_p$, and

$$H_e(1,t)+\sum_{a>e} x^{a-e} H_a(1,t)=0.$$

Separate the points by the root $t\bmod p$ at **each** $p\mid g$. There are at most $e^{\omega(g)}=q^{o(1)}$ classes, not a power cost per prime power. In one class the root is simple modulo $p$, so lifts at $x=0$ to a root in $\mathbb Z_p$. The unit partial derivative in $t$ then gives a single integral power series $t(x)$ on this disk, by exactly the recursion and uniqueness in the smooth case. Both original coordinates are integral power series in $x\in p^k\mathbb Z_p$. Thus every determinant with $a$ evaluation columns as in the jet proof has the same $g^{\binom a2}$ divisor **within a class**. The archimedean estimate still uses the monomials in the original box. Since $.0194 D>.1348$ for $D\ge7$, Bézout gives $O_D(1)$ points in each class for a sufficiently large fixed auxiliary degree. Including $(f,h)$, the price exponent is $.1276+.0194/2=.1373<1121/8125$.

## One paid test on a smooth plane cubic

There is a narrower use of the smooth output that does not require a second independent congruence. At fixed $v,h,f,C$ on a smooth plane cubic, prescribe **one** set $S_1$ of $O(\log q)$ primes before seeing the points. Suppose the fiber polynomial $R(T)=q^3 C(A/q,T,1)$ is nonconstant and squarefree over $\mathbb Q$. Mark as bad $q$ and the primes dividing its leading coefficient or discriminant (use discriminant 1 in degree one). This is a fixed support of $O(\log q)$ primes since $R$ has polynomial height. At an actual point $n=A-qi=lY$ is a positive eligible-prime product of polynomial height, with $n\ge l=q^{.7998+o(1)}$. Let $b$ be its full bad-supported part. The following points cost $q^{.01035+o(1)}$ per filter:

$$ b>2N \qquad\text{or}\qquad (n/b)_{S_1}\ge q^{.0243}. $$

Indeed $b$ costs subpower to enumerate, and $q$ is a unit modulo $b$ because $q\nmid A$. If $b>2N$, $qi\equiv A\pmod b$ fixes $i$, and at most three $j$ lie over it on the irreducible cubic. Otherwise enumerate $U=(n/b)_{S_1}$ at subpower cost. When $U\ge q^{.045}$, cross $q^{.045}$ inside the known $U$ in fixed order. For $q^{.0243}\le U<q^{.045}$ take all of $U$ and cross $q^{.045}/U$ in the remaining good part. There is enough to cross since $n/b\ge q^{.665-o(1)}$. The eligible-prime overshoot is subpower; the possible extra divisors cost at most $q^{(.045-.0243)/2+o(1)}$. The resulting divisor $g\mid n/b$ fixes the **first** coordinate modulo $g$, not both. For each $p^k\parallel g$, the ordinate reduces to one of at most three simple roots of $R\bmod p$. After choosing these roots, lift each at $i=A/q$ by Hensel, and solve on its disk for the ordinate as an integral power series in $i-A/q\in p^k\mathbb Z_p$. This is the same unit-derivative argument as before, but not necessarily the disk of $B/q$. The branch choices cost $3^{\omega(g)}=q^{o(1)}$. Now $.045\cdot3>.1348$, so the determinant and Bézout argument bounds each class by a constant. The summed exponent $.1276+.01035=.13795$ is strictly below $1121/8125$. The same proof permits exchanging the coordinates and using $lZ$ for a squarefree horizontal fiber. Taking the union of the two tests for one prescribed support each costs only a constant factor. No distribution of these supported parts is assumed.

## Revised handoff

Add the three tests above to the definition of priced. At one prescribed support per input the ordered triples with both columns priced still cost $q^{.860+2(1121/8125)+o(1)}$; the all-reference test still costs $q^{1121/8125+o(1)}I'$. This does not pay for free cofactor choices. The unpriced through-point components now have **positive geometric genus** and degree 3 through 12; at degrees 7 through 12 the tangent cone at $P$ must have a repeated line. In particular an absolutely irreducible singular plane cubic has geometric genus zero (arithmetic genus one with a positive singularity correction). For the proposed simultaneous-$Y,Z$ smoothness test on cubics, only a **smooth plane cubic** remains, outside the applicable one-coordinate tests above. Without the supported credit the smooth-point crossing still needs $\tau>.1348/3$, and $.1276+\tau/2>.15$. A next test is whether the two output identities give a saving on the *low*-credit side, not to enumerate a second independent smooth divisor at full cost. Neither this remaining occupancy nor the large outside-support cofactor family is priced here.

**Subsequent handoff:** The [quotient determinant](jet-quotient-determinant.md) adds a weighted coefficient gain, prices more through-point components, and makes squarefreeness unconditional on the remaining low-norm cubic class. Use its small-bad-part, low-credit cubic residual for the next simultaneous-output test.
