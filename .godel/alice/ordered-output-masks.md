# Price old prime factors before choosing the gap

Use the actual ordered edges of the [sorted-prefix graph](shared-complement-count.md) at $t=1.11$. Put $L=\log q$, $B=\log L$, $X\sim(2.1L)^2$, and call primes in $[X/B^4,X]$ eligible. The two reduced products $u,v$ are eligible-smooth, at most $N/g$ and greater than $D=H/g\ge1$, with $u=v+q\delta$, $0<|\delta|\le D$. Here $N=q^{2.5+o(1)}$, $H=q^{.390+o(1)}$, and $g$ is eligible-smooth. For a prime set $P$ let $z_P$ be the full $P$-part of $z$, with multiplicities.

**An ordered choice of two masks.** Fix $1\le\kappa,\lambda\le2$, $a=(2-\kappa)/2$, and $b=(2-\lambda)/2$. At each $g$ prescribe one set $P(g)$ of at most $L^\kappa$ eligible primes. For each $(g,v)$ prescribe one set $S(g,v)$ of at most $L^\lambda$ eligible primes. Then

$$\sum_{\text{actual edges}}\sqrt g\; v_P^a\min(D,u_S)^b\ \le\ q^{1.445+o(1)}.$$

The order matters. The first mask cannot be fitted to the enumerated $v$; the second may depend on it, but not on the output $u$. The proof also gives an outgoing estimate for *every* prescribed second mask at a fixed input; there is no need to sum this estimate over all possible masks.

Indeed, uniformly for a mask $P$ as above and $1\le Y\le q^{O(1)}$, the sum of $z_P^a$ over eligible-smooth $z\le Y$ is at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt Y$. Use Rankin's inequality at $\sigma=1/2+B^{-1/2}$. For the masked Euler factors, $\sigma-a=(\kappa-1)/2+B^{-1/2}$. Even at $\kappa=1$, each $p^{-(\sigma-a)}=o(1)$. Their total is at most $L^\kappa(X/B^4)^{-(\sigma-a)}=o(L)$ because $\log(X/B^4)=2B-O(\log B)$. For the other factors use the same bound with $\kappa=2$ and $O(L^2)$ primes. Thus the Euler product is $\exp(o(L))$, uniformly in the masks, and $Y^{\sigma-1/2}=q^{o(1)}$.

For fixed $g,v$, choose a divisor $f$ of $u$ by multiplying prime factors until first reaching $D$, using factors in $S$ first. Then $D\le f\le XD$ and $f_S\ge\min(D,u_S)$; use $1$ at $D=1$. Each $f$ allows at most three signed integers $\delta$ since $q\delta\equiv-v\pmod f$ and $(q,f)=1$. Count $f$ with the weighted smooth estimate at $XD$ to bound the outgoing sum of $\min(D,u_S)^b$ by $q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$. Count $v$ with the estimate at $N/g$. After the weight $\sqrt g$, sum $\sqrt{NH}/\sqrt g$ over smooth $g$, using $\sum g^{-1/2}\le\prod_{p\le X}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}=q^{o(1)}$ (partial summation with $\pi(x)\ll x/\log x$). Each actual edge is determined by $(g,v,\delta)$; neither arbitrary divisor splits nor numbers of lists multiply the count.

**Sorted prefixes.** Let $c,d$ be the prefixes of $u,v$, and $x,y$ their respective complements, each at least $q^{1.445-o(1)}/g$. If $d$ meets the largest $\lfloor L^\kappa\rfloor$ eligible primes, all of $y$ is supported there. With $\kappa=1.92$ and $b=0$ the weight is at least $q^{1.445(.04)-o(1)}g^{.5-.04}$. Thus $d$ or, by symmetry, $c$ meeting this tail costs at most $q^{1.3872+o(1)}$ edges over **all** gcds. If both meet the larger tail $\kappa=\lambda=1.938$, take both masks to be that tail. Now $u_S\ge x>D$, so the saving exponent is $(1.445+.390)(.031)$, with a nonnegative remaining power $.5-2(.031)$ of $g$. This costs $q^{1.388115+o(1)}$ edges. These are paid deletions, not the impossibility of rich columns in the [smaller prefix tail](forced-output-cuts.md). They do not lower the exponent $2$ of the remaining prime universe.

At $g=1$ fix **one common** $P$ of size at most $L^{1.86}$, and use it for both masks. Orient so the first product contains $x$. The number of rows with $x_P\ge q^{1.30}$ and $y_P\ge H$ is at most $q^{1.445-.07(1.30+.390)+o(1)}$. Thus rich columns on which these are at least half the rows carry at most $q^{1.3267+o(1)}$ edges and number at most $q^{.9927+o(1)}$, since rich means degree $>q^{.334}$. No lower fraction of such rows has been proved. A prescribed subpower family is affordable, not a different mask fitted to each column.

## Strengthen the unresolved packet test on one actual list

Fix a [constructed two-label list](shared-row-labels.md) in the $g=R=j=1$ stratum of the [short-walk test](cofactor-walk-rank.md). Count columns, not weighted incidences over different lists. Here orient with first input $v=cx_1=cF_1h_1$ and output $u=dy_1$. Each column has a unique row for each list position. There are at most $q^{.860+o(1)}$ distinct $v$: The fixed $F_1>W=q^{.780+o(1)}$ divides $v\le N$, so count smooth $v/F_1$. The sorted prefix of $v$ is exactly $c$. Thus within a $v$-fiber **one denominator excludes at most one column**; similarly $u$ determines its prefix $d$ and hence, with $v$, the column. There are at most $q^{.5275+o(1)}$ denominators. At each fixed $v$ and for any one set $S$ of at most $L^{1.70}$ eligible primes, at most $q^{.1365+o(1)}$ columns have $(dy_1)_S\ge H$: Divide the outgoing estimate by $H^{.15}$, or count $S$-smooth divisors crossing $H$ directly at exponent $.35+B^{-1/2}$.

Peel a live $v$ of current degree $\le q^{.137}$ or a live $d$ of degree $\le q^{.467}$, deleting its columns, until neither is possible. Interleave this with the packet removals below. Across **all** rounds peeling costs at most $q^{.997+o(1)}+q^{.9945+o(1)}$ columns: Charge each fiber only when deleted, never restarting its budget. This applies to any starting subfamily on this list.

In any nonempty remainder choose a live $d_0$. It has degree $>q^{.467}$, above the short-walk rank bound $q^{.466052+o(1)}$. Thus at some fixed cofactor endpoints at $d_0$ there is a reference three-step walk and $K+1$ walks whose gap-product quotients by it have independent valuation vectors, $K=\lfloor L^{1.03}\rfloor$. Their union has at most $3(K+2)$ distinct columns. For **each distinct column** in this union, retain its full first input $v$ and attach $M=\lfloor L^{1.70}/(12k)\rfloor$ more live columns at that same $v$, where $k=\lfloor 2.5 L/\log X\rfloor$ bounds the number of prime factors of each of the original products. Require all attached denominators to be distinct across the whole packet and different from $d_0$. Within each $v$ group require that each newly attached $dy_1$ have part $<H$ on the support of **all earlier** $(c,d,x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)$ in that group, starting with its rank-witness column.

These attachments always exist. The initial columns have distinct $v$ because they have common $d_0$. Before each choice the support in its group contains at most $6kM\le L^{1.70}$ primes, **all eligible** because the six coordinates divide the original smooth products. This mask does not include signed gaps; their primes need not be eligible. Conditional on the earlier choices, the outgoing bound excludes $q^{.1365+o(1)}$ candidates. The already used denominators exclude only $O((K+2)(1+M))=q^{o(1)}$ more. Their sum is less than the live degree $>q^{.137}$ for large $q$. In particular each new $d$ has outside part $> d/H=q^{.665+o(1)}$ and introduces a new prime even relative to prior outputs in that group. This is a same-input conclusion, not global disjointness of supports.

Remove the entire packet and peel again. If $T$ enriched packets were removed at exhaustion, the starting column count was at most

$$q^{.997+o(1)}+3(K+2)(1+M)T.$$

The packets are column-disjoint on this list, not necessarily vertex-disjoint. Thus a bound $q^{.998-\epsilon}$ on the number of disjoint **enriched** packets suffices in this stratum. Unlike a bare gap-rank witness at one $d_0$, every witness column must now extend across many other denominators on the *same complete input*. One possible next count is for such distinct denominators with $dy_1\equiv v\pmod q$, $|dy_1-v|\le qH$, and the second labelled row successful on every attachment. Merely counting the available gaps again costs $q^{.195+o(1)}$ per input, above the $.137$ degree threshold. The argument proves existence of the attachments in a dense remainder, not scarcity of the packets. It gives neither the unrestricted list estimate nor joint-key occupancy.
