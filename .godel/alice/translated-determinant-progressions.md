# Count short progressions of the raw row determinant

Fix an actual [constructed two-label list](shared-row-labels.md) at $g=1$ and an occupied denominator $d$. Write $t=(x_1y_2-x_2y_1)/q$, including its sign. The following is a count of **columns**: In the block $D\le R=(|\delta_1|,|\delta_2|)<2D$, $D\le q^{.121}$, a prescribed progression
$$t=t_*+e b,\qquad |b|\le L,\qquad e\ge1,\quad L\ge1,$$
costs at most
$$q^{.1375+o(1)}(\sqrt D+\sqrt L)$$
actual numerators. All parameters have polynomial height. The half-length counts **terms**, not integer diameter. The translation may even depend on the exact $R$ and $j=(h_1,h_2)$; the step may depend on $d,D,j$ but must be fixed across $R$ in the block for this price. Neither may be chosen after seeing the remaining endpoint. In particular a window with $L=q^{.660}$ costs $.4675$ locally, even far from the origin. Another consequence at the same price is agreement with one actual reference modulo **some** cube of size at least $q^{.360}$, including cubes of ineligible bases. The union over cubes is paid below. There is no claim that the residual determinants have either property.

## An affine direction estimate

Use the exact scales and [crossed directions](crossed-gap-filters.md). Thus $cx_i-dy_i=q\delta_i$, $x_i=F_i h_i$, $W=2A_+HX/Z<F_i\le WX$, and $U=A_+/W=q^{.665+o(1)}$. The labels are coprime and units modulo $d$. Set $v_i=\delta_i/R$, $\ell_i=h_i/j$, and $n=(\ell_2v_1,\ell_1v_2)$. These are ambient primitive, nonzero-coordinate vectors with both coordinates units modulo $d$, by row primitivity and the two gcd normalizations. The exact determinant identity is
$$F_2n_1-F_1n_2=dT,\qquad T=t/(Rj)\in\mathbb Z.$$
Indeed $Rj$ is a unit modulo $d$ and elimination gives $Rj(F_2n_1-F_1n_2)=dt$. Put $B=UH/(Dj)$, so $|n_i|\le B$ and $2B<d$ by $d>Z/X$. For $j<q^{.115}$ and $D\le q^{.121}$ one has $B/F_1\ge q^{.039-o(1)}$.

**Lemma.** At fixed $D,j$, impose $T=T_0+E s$, $s\in\mathbb Z$, $|s|\le N$, with $E\ge1$, $N\ge1$, and all data of polynomial height. Write $V=BN/F_1$ and $V_0=B^2/d$. Then the number of possible $n$, including their cofactor and signed-gap splits, is at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{\min(V,V_0)}$. Only cofactors are known to be eligible-smooth; the coordinates $n_i$ need not be smooth.

The centred bound uses $A=\ell_1\ell_2$ to filter the index-$d$ lattice in $(n_1,n_2)$: $V_0/A=U^2H^2/(dD^2 h_1h_2)\le q^{-.275+o(1)}$. A smooth divisor $f$ of $A$ strictly crossing $64V_0$, with split $f_2\mid\ell_2$, $f_1\mid\ell_1$, imposes index $df$ by substitution modulo $d$. There are at most two ambient primitive points per split since their determinant is smaller than this index. There are $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{V_0}$ filters and splits by the half-power count and divisor bound. Both $V_0$ and $V$ exceed one here. In every branch, the nonzero physical coordinates recover $\ell_i,v_i$ at divisor cost.

Suppose $V<V_0$ and put $Q=V_0/V$. In coordinates $(n_1,s)$ the base coset has difference lattice of covolume $F_1$, by the equation defining $T$ and $(F_1,F_2)=1$. For any split filter $f=f_1f_2\mid A$ prime to $E$, the filtered coset if nonempty has covolume $F_1 f$. In fact after setting $n_1=f_2 a$ the equation is
$$F_2 f_2 a-dE s\equiv dT_0\pmod{F_1f_1}.$$
The coefficients and modulus have no common prime: Use $(d,F_1f_1)=1$, $(E,f)=1$, and $(F_1,F_2)=1$. This argument also applies to difference lattices and to subfilters. In particular it does not require assuming $(E,F_1)=1$.

In the homogeneous base lattice there is at most one primitive direction up to sign with $|w_1|\le B/(2\sqrt V)$ and $|w_s|\le N/(2\sqrt V)$, by the determinant bound $F_1/2$. Write $w_2=(F_2w_1-dE w_s)/F_1$ for its physical second coordinate. No direction supporting two actual points can have a zero physical coordinate: The other difference is a multiple of $d$ but has absolute value at most $2B<d$. Mask the eligible primes of $E$ and, if the exceptional direction exists and has both coordinates nonzero, of $w_1 w_2$. This mask is fixed before the points and has $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ primes by polynomial height and the eligible-prime lower bound. The possible masked parts of polynomial-height integers cost $q^{o(1)}$ including powers, by exponent-vector counting.

* If the full masked part of $A$ is at least $Q$, start the centred filter with a masked divisor crossing $Q$ (weak crossing suffices). This seed fits below $64V_0$ since $XQ/V_0=X/V=o(1)$. Extend from unused factors of $A$ until strictly above $64V_0$. The final divisor is at most $64XV_0$ and its outside-mask part is $O(XV_0/Q)=O(XV)$. Enumerate its masked and unmasked parts, then split it. This gives $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$ choices and the centred proof still works; the step $E$ is not used as an index here.
* Otherwise the outside-mask part exceeds $A/Q\gg 64V$. Cross $64V$ strictly with an outside-mask divisor $f=f_1 f_2$, with the same split convention. For each filter/split the points are collinear in the translated box: A determinant of two differences is at most $8BN<F_1f$. Singleton filters together cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$. For a nonsingleton, let $w$ now be the line step primitive in the **base difference lattice**, not a point of the translated coset. The filtered step is $kw$ where
$$k=\frac{f}{(f_2,w_1)(f_1,w_2)},\qquad f/k\mid |w_1w_2|.$$
Here $(f_1,f_2)=1$ since $(\ell_1,\ell_2)=1$. On the exceptional direction $k=f$ by the mask. The run in base steps has length at most $2N$ when $w_s\ne0$; when $w_s=0$ its physical step is $\pm(F_1,F_2)$ and the bound is $2B/F_1$. Both are at most $2V$, since $B/F_1\ge1$ and $N\ge1$. Thus the exceptional direction supports no nonsingleton filter in this branch.

For an ordinary direction let $m+1\ge2$ be the number of counted distinct points of the split filter. The two strip coordinates of $w$ have sizes at most $(2B,2N)/(mk)$, so $mk\le4\sqrt V$ by nonexceptionality. Dyadically fix $M\le m<2M$ and enumerate $k\le4\sqrt V/M$. Cross $8V/(Mk)^2$ strictly with a split divisor $u$ of $f/k$ (take $u=1$ if the threshold is below one); there is enough because $f>64V$. Take its parts from $(f_2,w_1)$ and $(f_1,w_2)$. There are $q^{o(1)}(1+\sqrt V/(Mk))$ choices including splits. In the centred difference box $(2B,2N)/(Mk)$ the determinant of two candidate directions is at most $8BN/(Mk)^2<F_1u$. The split on $w$ has covolume $F_1u$ by the homogeneous index calculation. Hence it fixes the base-primitive direction up to sign. The physical coordinates are nonzero and of polynomial height, so recover $f/k$ from divisors of their product, and all splits at subpower cost. For each $M,k$ multiply the filter count by $O(M)$ points. The result is $O(q^{o(1)}\sqrt V/k)$ since $Mk\le4\sqrt V$. Sum harmonically over $k$, then over dyadic $M$. This proves the lemma without treating translated points as primitive vectors on a line through the origin.

## Pay for a moving residue from the raw determinant

Hold the **exact** $R$ fixed for this step. Put $a=(Rj,e)$. Unless $a\mid t_*$ there are no solutions. Otherwise $Rj T=t_*+e b$ permits one residue of $T$ modulo $E=e/a$. In this residue the tested $T$ occupy an interval of progression parameters of length $2La/(Rj)$; recenter at an integer so $N\ll 1+La/(Dj)$ suffices. Apply the lemma separately for this $R$. Once $n$ and its split are known, $R$ is already paid for: The row fixes $c\bmod d$, allowing only $O(1+Z/d)=O(X)$ integer numerators. Compute both forced outputs at the same numerator; retain only positive primitive rows with all original eligible-factor, prefix, and constructed membership tests. Conversely each original success under the cut has been counted. We use containment for an upper bound, not sufficiency of a residue test.

At fixed $d,D,j,R$ the price from $\sqrt V$ is at most
$$q^{.1375+o(1)}\left(\frac1{\sqrt{Dj}}+\frac{\sqrt{L\cdot(Rj,e)}}{Dj}\right),$$
where $(Rj,e)$ denotes the gcd. For a fixed step in the $R$ sum, use $(Rj,e)\le j(R,e)$ and
$$\sum_{D\le R<2D}\sqrt{(R,e)}\le\sum_{a'\mid e,\ a'<2D}\sqrt{a'}\lfloor 2D/a'\rfloor\le 2D\,\tau(e).$$
Thus this sum costs at most $q^{.1375+o(1)}(\sqrt D+\sqrt L)/\sqrt j$. Dyadic summation of the half-power count gives $\sum_{j\ {\rm eligible\text{-}smooth},\ j<q^{.115}} j^{-1/2}=q^{o(1)}$. This proves the raw progression price. In particular, it would be incorrect to divide out $Rj$ once at the dyadic endpoint, or to use this gcd average if $e$ changes with each $R$. For a progression prescribed directly on $T$ at $D,j$, the alternative [scalar sieve](delayed-output-seeds.md) costs $q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$ per split, giving $q^{.1375+o(1)}\sqrt{N/j}$ without enumerating $R$.

## Large powers of a translated difference

Choose any $|t^0|\le W$ fixed on the denominator, for instance the signed determinant at one actual reference. Count $t=t^0$ or $r^m\mid t-t^0$ for some integer $r\ge1$, $r^m\ge Q_0$, with fixed $m\ge3$ and $1\le Q_0\le2W$. Equality alone uses a one-term window. Otherwise $r\le(2W)^{1/m}$ and one may take $e=r^m$, $t_*=t^0$, $L=\max(1,2W/r^m)$. Summing the raw progression price over **all** bases gives, per denominator and low-$R$ block,
$$q^{o(1)}\left(q^{.1375}\sqrt D\, W^{1/m}+q^{.5275}Q_0^{1/m-1/2}\right).$$
Indeed the second term sums $r^{-m/2}$ from $Q_0^{1/m}$, and the first pays the rounding term even when the reduced progression contains only one point. For $m=3$, $Q_0=q^{.360}$, the two exponents are at most $.1375+.0605+.260=.458$ and $.5275-.060=.4675$. Dyadic summation is free in the exponent; $R\ge q^{.121}$ is covered without a progression by the [empty-mask gap bound](masked-gap-lcm.md). With one reference per occupied $d$, the large-cube test on the raw difference therefore costs $q^{.995+o(1)}$ per list across denominators. With all references it gives only an outgoing allowance $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ per reference; subtract that allowance before squaring a residual fiber size. This is not projective congruence of $T/n_2$, and it need not produce a match of output primes.

**Decision.** A single prescribed raw determinant interval, or the paid cube family, can be removed even when its center varies with the denominator. This differs from the [untranslated determinant mask](row-determinant-mask.md): A large difference divisor need not divide either determinant. Neither restriction is forced by a successful short lift. At $R=j=1$, covering $|t|\le W$ by ordinary intervals of half-width $q^{.660}$ at the proved price costs exponent $.120+.4675=.5875$ per $d$, worse than $.5275$. A dictionary at that width must cost strictly less than $.003$ in the exponent to reach below $.4705$. The next useful test is whether the two actual output congruences concentrate the *signed* $t$ in progressions with small total square-root length after conditioning on $d$ but before paying for the outputs. For low $R$, a sufficient budget per block is $\sum_P(\sqrt D+\sqrt{L_P})\le q^{.333-\eta}$ with steps fixed across $R$, plus a separate bound on uncovered successes; at $D=1$ it is the common-shift tests, not the number of CRT solutions per assignment, that must imply such concentration. There is no such cover or unrestricted $.998$ estimate here.

For the [two coprime output seeds](matched-output-primes.md), a concrete dictionary test at $R=j=1$ would use $e=f_1 f_2=q^{.240+o(1)}$. The actual residues obey $qt\equiv x_1 y_2\pmod{f_1}$ and $qt\equiv -x_2 y_1\pmod{f_2}$. These are not one prescribed class per seed pair: Each right side still involves an opposite output. Even **if** there were one prescribed class per pair, the full range has $L=q^{.540+o(1)}$, costing $.4075$ per class, and summing $.120$ for the pairs returns $.5275$. A sufficient next estimate is a cover of the successes by fewer than $q^{.063-\eta}$ distinct step/residue pairs at that step size, plus a bound for exceptions. Equivalently one could gain more than $.057$ from occupancy inside the candidate classes instead of shrinking the dictionary. A restriction that leaves two independently chosen smooth seeds and does not constrain the opposite outputs has not made this saving.

## A separate homogeneous test for the projective discrepancy

There is also a cube test for the [lifted direction discrepancy](lifted-direction-separation.md), but not by treating $T^0 n_2/n_2^0$ as a fixed translation. Fix one actual reference $n^0$ per $d$ as there, and put $P=\det(n,n^0)/d\in\mathbb Z$. The old test obtained credit from divisors of $d$. Even **away** from the primes of $d$, the set with $P=0$ or $r^3\mid P$ for some $r^3\ge q^{.360}$ costs $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ per $d$. This measures a projective determinant, not the ordinary difference $t-t^0$.

In fact for a prescribed integer $b\ge1$ of polynomial height, $b\mid P$ gives a **centred** lattice of index exactly $db$ in the physical $(n_1,n_2)$ plane: The coefficients of $\det(n,n^0)$ are coprime by ambient primitivity of the reference. At fixed $D,j$ the direction price is $q^{o(1)}(1+\sqrt{V_0/b})$, $V_0=B^2/d$ as above. If $b> 8V_0$ the index alone suffices. Otherwise mask the eligible primes of $b$. If the masked part of $A$ is at least $b$, cross $b$ with a masked seed, then extend past $64V_0$ if necessary using unused factors of $A$. The resulting divisor is at most $64XV_0$ with outside-mask part at most $64XV_0/b$. Use just the original index-$d$ centred congruence with this filter. If the masked part is smaller than $b$, the unmasked part exceeds $A/b\gg64V_0/b$. Cross $64V_0/b$ strictly with an unmasked filter and split it between the coordinates. Now its factors are units modulo **both** $d$ and $b$, and substituting them in the reference determinant gives index $db$ in the substituted coordinates, even if the reference coordinates are not separately units modulo $b$. Thus the ambient index beats $2B^2$. Both branches give the stated price by the primitive-vector and smooth-divisor counts. The case $P=0$ costs at most two directions per $D,j$ before scalar recovery.

For $P\ne0$ we have $|P|\le2B UH/d=q^{1.055+o(1)}/(Dj)$ because the reference need not be in the tested block. For fixed $m\ge3$, sum the direction price over $b=r^m\ge Q_0$, $r\le(2B UH/d)^{1/m}$, multiply by the usual $q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$ scalar price, and sum over smooth $j$. The constant term, including $P=0$, costs at most
$$q^{o(1)}\left(q^{.0575}\sqrt D+q^{1.055/m+.115(1/2-1/m)}D^{1/2-1/m}\right).$$
The second term uses dyadic half-power counting on $j^{-1/m}$. The area term costs $q^{.5275+o(1)} D^{-1/2}Q_0^{1/m-1/2}$ using $\sum 1/j\ll\log q$. For cubes the constant term has exponent at most $.391$ on $D\le q^{.121}$, and $Q_0=q^{.360}$ gives $.4675$ for the area term. Large $R$ is already covered. Summing over occupied denominators gives $.995$ for one reference per fiber; across all references this is again only an outgoing allowance locally. Neither $P$ nor $t-t^0$ is required to have any large cube divisor, and the prices do not multiply on their intersection.
