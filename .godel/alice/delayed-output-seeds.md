# Delay the second output divisor until after scalar recovery

**Decision.** Keep the [two-divisor CRT](matched-output-primes.md) for estimates that exploit its large modulus. Do not pay for both divisors merely to determine the common scalar. On the full-width $R=1$ block, the next quantity to bound is a sum of two output tests at the **same integer shift**, with no output seeds in the outer sum.

Fix the same constructed list and denominator $d$, and a dyadic block $D\le R<2D$ below the large-gap cut $R\ge q^{.121}$. After recovering a normalized direction and its split, $x_i=F_i h_i$ and $v_i=\delta_i/R$ are known. We want $Z/X<c,d\le Z$, $cx_i-dy_i=qRv_i$, and $(R,c)=1$, with all original row and list tests. In particular $(x_i,d y_i)=1$. Let $a$ be the least residue of $qv_1/x_1$ modulo $d$; the direction test gives $a x_2=qv_2\pmod d$ as well.

* At $D=1$, take $R=1$ and scan $c=a+du$ in range. There are at most $1+Z/d\le 1+X$ shifts, with $X$ polylogarithmic. The outputs must be $y_i=(a x_i-qv_i)/d+x_i u$ for **that same** $u$. Test both. No large modulus, factorization witness, or enumeration of coprime pairs is needed to list the candidates.
* At general $D$, on any actual success choose the first sorted prime prefix $f$ of $y_1$ with $df>4DZ$ (one if already strict). Such a prefix exists: $4DZ/d\le 4DX$, whereas $y_1\ge A_-=q^{1.445+o(1)}$. The overshoot is at most $X$. Enumerate eligible-smooth $f\ll DX^2$ at cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$ by the half-power bound. Reject nonunits $x_1$ modulo $m=df$. With $B=qv_1/x_1\bmod m$, a successful pair must satisfy $c=BR-mk$. This uses $df$, not separate reductions mod $d$ and mod $f$, even if $(d,f)>1$.

Two such pairs in the box have determinant divisible by $m$ and of absolute value at most $4DZ<m$. Parallel positive primitive pairs are equal. To **find** the pair, take $B$ least nonnegative. Since $(k,R)=1$ and $2(2D)Z<m$, $k/R$ is a convergent of $B/m$. It is the last of denominator at most $2D$: Any later one in range has no larger absolute remainder, and the nonzero cross-determinant multiplied by $m$ would have size at most $4DZ$. Thus test that convergent, including sign and range. This is the [one-output scalar sieve](lifted-direction-separation.md) in ordinary residue coordinates with a crossing divisor taken from the output; the $\sqrt D$ scalar price itself is not new.

For every recovered $(R,c)$ compute **both** quotients. Check integrality, positivity, primitivity, eligible factors and counts, and the original incidence and prefix requirements; if testing Work's large seeds, one can now recover them at divisor cost from the actual integers. In particular the second crossing must avoid the support of $y_1$, not just the first crossing. Conversely an original incidence supplies the small prefix and cannot be lost. The number of additional divisor witnesses per actual pair is subpower. This gives the same *fully checked* incidences, not the same intermediate sieve: A short representative mod $df_1 f_2$ may be useful analytically even when its near-minimal single-slot counterpart is not.

**Ledger at the same widths.** The [empty-mask direction bound](masked-gap-lcm.md) summed over $j=(h_1,h_2)$, including splits, is $q^{o(1)}(q^{.0575}+UH/(\sqrt d\,D))$. Multiplying by the scalar price gives at most $q^{o(1)}(q^{.0575}\sqrt D+q^{.5275}/\sqrt D)$ candidate tests per $d$ per block. This recovers, but does not improve, the earlier unconditional full-width estimate. Relative to the direct two-large-seed outer enumeration, the scalar exponent falls from $.120$ to $\rho/2$ at $D=q^{\rho+o(1)}$, and to zero at $D=1$. To beat $.4705$ strictly, the dominant term still needs an exponent saving greater than $.057-\rho/2$ wherever this is nonnegative. At $R=j=1$, sum the number of common admissible shifts above over the recovered directions. A bound $q^{.4705-\eta}$, $\eta>0$, on this block requires a $.057+\eta$ saving against the full-width envelope, not $.177+\eta$ against an enumeration that guesses both divisors. Neither many rejected shifts in a small box nor a bound on either marginal supplies this estimate. Other blocks must also be paid for.

**Finite check.** [The exact checker](check-delayed-output-seeds.py) compares brute-force witness sets with last-convergent recovery followed by both divisibility tests, on all the indicated directions in a signed arithmetic box. The sets agree, with 114 witness tuples, 26 with $R>1$, and 81 with $(a,d)>1$ for the first large divisor $a$. For $d=12$, $q=101$, $Z=24$, $x=(11,13)$, $v=(-193,-179)$, $R=1$, the two coprime tests $40\mid y_1$ and $63\mid y_2$ separately pass only at $c=17$ and $c=5$, respectively. There is no common lift for those tests. These are arithmetic controls, not samples or exclusions of rich incidences. [Decision record](delayed-seed-review.md).

**After Work:** Two further restricted counts apply to the successes themselves. [Translated determinant progressions](translated-determinant-progressions.md) price a short prescribed progression of the signed determinant at each $d$, including the moving residue when the exact gap gcd is divided out. Large cube divisors of its difference from one actual reference, or of the projective determinant discrepancy, cost $.995$ across $d$. [Crossed output repeats](crossed-output-repeats.md) give a separate across-$d$ estimate when the two forced outputs and cofactors jointly supply repeat weight. Neither covers generic successful common shifts. At $R=j=1$ the two seed sizes give step $q^{.240}$; even one signed determinant class per pair would cost $.4075+.120=.5275$ pointwise, and the opposite outputs still enter those classes. The next target is the number of occupied classes **with** their seed-pair choices paid, or a saving inside them, not another uniqueness test.
