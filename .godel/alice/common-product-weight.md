# One weight across the complement moduli

Fix a constructed pair of labels at $g=1$ as in [the short-ratio test](short-label-ratios.md). Can one bound their aggregate correlation by using the **same** sparse input for every modulus? Not from mass, the signed box, unit restriction, and nonresonance alone. The distinction is between the raw correlation and the number of columns it can pay for.

Let $\mathcal D$ consist of eligible-smooth $Z/X<d\le Z$ coprime to $F_1F_2$ with $\lambda_\infty(\Lambda_d)>q^{.410}$. Here $\Lambda_d$ tests $F_2z\equiv F_1w\pmod d$. For a weight $f$ on signed integers, let $P_d^*(f)$ sum $f(z)f(w)$ over unit pairs in that lattice with $0<|F_2z-F_1w|\le dW$. Write $B_0=HU=Z/(2X)$. There is **one symmetric set** $A\subset[-B_0,B_0]$ of size at most $q^{.7225}$ for which

$$\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}P_d^*(1_A)\ \ge\ q^{1.083-o(1)}.$$

This is for the ambient modulus sum on the fixed labels, not a claim that any of the moduli actually realizes a list.

**Supply of moduli.** There are $q^{.5275+o(1)}$ eligible squarefree integers in the modulus window coprime to the labels. For the lower bound use primes in $[X/2,X]$, deleting the label primes and reserving $2j+3$ primes, where $j=\lfloor\log(Z/X)/\log X\rfloor$. The remaining pool has size $M\asymp X/\log X$ by the prime number theorem: The excluded supports have size $O(\log q)$ and $X\asymp(\log q)^2$. Start with any $j$ distinct pool primes. Their product is at most $Z/X$; append reserved primes in fixed order until it first exceeds $Z/X$. There are enough since $(X/2)^{2j+3}>Z/X$, and crossing multiplies by at most $X$. Removing the reserved primes recovers the initial subset. Now $\log\binom Mj=j\log(M/j)+O(j)=(.5275+o(1))\log q$. The upper bound is the half-power smooth count. The [short-vector lemma](short-label-ratios.md) removes at most $q^{.410+o(1)}$ moduli, leaving the same exponent.

**Choose the set, then restrict to units for each modulus.** In every surviving $d$ multiply its factors until crossing $q^{.333}$. There are at most $q^{.1665+o(1)}$ possible divisors at this scale, so one $e\in(q^{.333},Xq^{.333}]$ divides at least $q^{.361-o(1)}$ surviving moduli. Call this family $\mathcal G$. Set $B'=B_0/(2X)$. Each $d\in\mathcal G$ has $q^{1.055+o(1)}$ unit edges in the square of radius $B'$: Apply the unit lattice count from the short-ratio test with this radius. Its area term has exponent $1.055$, the fraction lost to prime divisors is $o(1)$, and the error exponent is at most $.645+o(1)$. Since $F_i\le WX$ and $d>2B_0$, the determinant cutoff holds. Delete the exact line, of size $O(1+B'/\max F_i)$ by coprimality. This costs at most $q^{.275+o(1)}$ edges per modulus.

For each unit $r\bmod e$ take $A_r$ to be all signed integers in $[-B',B']$ in classes $\pm r$ or $\pm(F_2/F_1)r\bmod e$. Its size is $O(1+B'/e)\le q^{.722+o(1)}<q^{.7225}$. Every surviving edge on every modulus in $\mathcal G$ contributes for at least the choice $r=z\bmod e$, since $e\mid d$. Average the counts over at most $e$ choices. One $A_r$ contributes exponent at least $.361+1.055-.333=1.083$ on this group alone. This choice depends on the family and labels, not on the individual modulus. There is no issue of distinct signed representatives since $2B'<d$.

**What the cap changes.** The [weighted test](overlapping-lift-count.md) pays $O(1/D)$ per list incidence in degree bin $D=q^\sigma$. With at most $q^{.780+o(1)}$ lists, a uniform column bound of $q^{.608+\sigma+o(1)}$ suffices for cost $q^{1.388+o(1)}$. At top degree this asks for $.998$, below the raw obstruction. But the witness group has **at most** $q^{.361+o(1)}$ moduli since $d/e\le Z/e$ is smooth. Even allowing every smooth $c\le Z$, it supports at most $q^{.8885+o(1)}$ columns on this list. The large raw sum on the group does not obstruct column counting.

Use $f(z)=\#\{(\delta,h):z=\delta h,\ 0<|\delta|\le H,\ h\le U\text{ positive eligible-smooth}\}$. In one unit class modulo this $e$, each unit $h$ allows $O(1+H/e)$ gaps, so the mass of $f$ there is at most $q^{.3325+.057+o(1)}$. This counts factorizations, not just support. For the next test keep the bound per modulus

$$\min\{\Psi(Z,X),\ O(X)P_d^*(f)\}.$$

The first bound counts numerators on the list; for the second, the two product splits fix both gaps and both cofactors, then $cx_1\equiv q\delta_1\pmod d$ fixes $c$ up to $O(X)$ choices and forces both $y_i$. The exact-line deletion and determinant cutoff are valid for actual distinct primitive rows. Sum the minimum over $\mathcal D$, not the two bounds separately. The excluded short moduli cost at most $q^{.9375+o(1)}$ columns per list. A saving for the capped sum (even for relaxed inputs), or direct savings using the forced smooth outputs, remain possible. Charging many different divisor groups would require paying for their union. No full large-$S$, all-gcd, or fixed-window estimate follows.
