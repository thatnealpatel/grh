# Count columns with few independent gap ratios around short walks

Fix an actual [constructed two-label list](shared-row-labels.md), $g=R=j=1$, and a denominator $d$. Make a bipartite graph whose two vertex sets are the values of $h_i=x_i/F_i$ in positions $i=1,2$. Put in **one edge per distinct actual column** realizing this list at $d$. Keep its signed gaps and outputs; parallel edges are allowed. In a traversal from position $i$ to $j$ the label is the rational $\delta_i/\delta_j$. A walk's label is their product. It reduces modulo $d$ to $F_i h_i/(F_k h_k)$ for its initial and final vertices (position $k$ at the end). This follows from $cF_i h_i-dy_i=q\delta_i$, with $q,c,F_i,h_i,\delta_i$ units modulo $d$. It does **not** say that the rational label is determined by the vertices.

**Short-walk test.** In any subgraph, for each occupied pair of endpoints of three-step walks from left to right, fix **one actual three-step walk** between them. Take the other labels divided by that reference label. These are the labels of closed walks of length six, and each is $1\bmod d$. If at every such pair their multiplicative rank (over the rationals on prime valuations, ignoring sign) is at most $K=\lfloor(\log q)^{1.03}\rfloor$, then the entire subgraph has at most
$$q^{\gamma+o(1)}\quad\text{edges},\qquad \gamma=.3325+.3325/3+.780(3/103)=.46605178\ldots .$$
Thus families passing this test, one per $d$, cost $q^{.993552+o(1)}$ per list. The groups, and even the primes used to detect their ranks, may vary with **both** endpoint vertices. There is no enumeration of a global rank dictionary.

**One step with a cofactor fixed.** Let $U=A_+/W=q^{.665+o(1)}$, $H=q^{.390+o(1)}$, $d=q^{1.055+o(1)}$. Fix either vertex $h_i$. For a prescribed set $S$ of $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ primes (not necessarily eligible), fix the full positive $S$-parts $m_i,m_j$ of the two absolute gaps. These parts of polynomial height have $q^{o(1)}$ choices by stars and bars, since their exponent sums are $O(\log q)$. Write $\delta_i=m_i a,\ \delta_j=m_j b$, and localize $|a|,|b|$ dyadically with upper bounds $A,B$. At these widths the edge price, for the fixed vertex, is
$$q^{o(1)}\left(1+\sqrt{UAB/d}\right).$$
In fact choose a prefix $s\mid h_j$ strictly crossing $4UAB/d$, or $s=1$ if the threshold is below one. The threshold is at most $q^{.390+o(1)}< h_j=q^{.665+o(1)}$ and overshoot is at most the eligible prime bound $X$. Enumerate these **before** the unknown $h_j$, paying the displayed half-power by the smooth count. The original sorted label $F_j$ is unchanged. The pair $((h_j/s)a,b)$ obeys
$$F_j s m_i ((h_j/s)a)\equiv F_i h_i m_j b\pmod d.$$
It is primitive by $(\delta_i,\delta_j)=1$ and $(h_j,\delta_j)=1$. Its bounding box has half-widths $UA/s,B$, so any two solutions have determinant divisible by $d$ and of size $<d$. There are at most two primitive solutions including signs. Split the nonzero first coordinate by divisors; a reconstructed $h_j$ and gap pair determine $c\bmod d$ with $O(1+Z/d)=q^{o(1)}$ shifts. The outputs for each shift are forced. This upper bound retains all actual successes and is valid on every subgraph; it does not declare all these splits and shifts successful.

**Count paths without paying for arbitrary cancellations.** Call a walk clean if at each extension the part of $|\delta_i\delta_j|$ supported on primes of all *earlier* gaps in this walk is $<q^{.390}$. For fixed bounded length this old support is a permissible $S$. From a fixed current vertex at most $q^{o(1)}$ actual edges fail the extension test: On failure $AB\ll H^2/q^{.390}$, so $UAB/d\le q^{o(1)}$ in the one-step bound. This is a uniform outgoing bound **at that vertex**, not a simultaneous union over masks.

Conversely, for a fixed final vertex and a fixed **exact signed rational** product $r$ of a clean walk of fixed length, there are at most $q^{o(1)}$ such walks. To peel the last edge, put $S=\operatorname{supp}(|r|)$ using its reduced numerator and denominator. Any prime in the last edge and no earlier gaps survives in $r$, since the two last gaps are coprime. Hence the full outside-$S$ part of the last gap product is $<q^{.390}$ by cleanliness. Enumerate the $S$-parts up to the gap height, not just divisors of the reduced product. Here $AB\ll q^{.390}$, so the same pointwise bound, now with the known vertex at the **end**, is $q^{o(1)}$. Divide $r$ by the recovered traversal ratio (previous gap over final gap) and repeat on the clean prefix. Signs and any possible parallel-edge multiplicities are included. The forward and reverse prices meet because $d/U$ and $H$ have the same exponent.

**Walk count and rank.** Write $N=q^{.3325+o(1)}$ for an upper bound on the size of each side (smooth $h_i$). For $E$ edges with $E/N$ polynomially large, delete vertices of current degree less than $E/(4N)$ until none remain. At most $E/2$ edges are lost. Starting with an oriented surviving edge from the left and applying the extension estimate twice gives $\gg E(E/N)^2$ clean three-step walks. There are at most $N^2$ ordered endpoint pairs. Thus at some fixed pair there are at least $E^3/(q^{o(1)}N^4)$ **different rational products**, by peeling. For example, $E>q^{.4705-\eta}$ forces at least $q^{.0815-3\eta-o(1)}$ there. An arbitrary one-reference choice at these endpoints suffices; we have not replaced an object count by an unpriced sum over reference paths.

If the indicated rank is at most $K$, some at most $K$ prime coordinates give an injective projection of the rational span of the cycle valuations (pick independent rows of a basis matrix). Thus the valuations on these primes determine each *path* product within the fixed coset up to sign. The numerator times denominator of the reduced path product is at most $H^6=q^{2.340+o(1)}$. Apply this bound to the path itself, **not** to its quotient by a reference. For any $K$ primes $P$, the number of signed valuation vectors with $\prod_{p\in P} p^{|v_p|}\le H^6$ is at most
$$ (H^6)^\sigma\prod_{p\in P}\left(1+2/(p^\sigma-1)\right)=q^{2.340(3/103)+o(1)},$$
where $\sigma=3/103+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$. The logarithm of the Euler product is $O(\sum_{2\le n\le K+1}n^{-\sigma})=o(\log q)$, using the smallest integers to dominate the distinct primes. Combine with the lower bound for distinct products to get the claimed $\gamma$; the small $E/N$ case already satisfies it. Numerically the rank budget allows exponent $.068156$ for the number of products at fixed endpoints, less than $.0815$ at target density. This counts a whole low-rank family, not just equations with a given cycle product. More generally, choose one translate of a rank-$K$ valuation space per ordered pair of endpoint vertices (for example through one actual reference product at that pair). Without requiring all products to belong, **at most** $N^2 q^{2.340(3/103)+o(1)}$ clean walks land in the chosen translates. This follows by peeling each product, then summing over endpoint pairs, not over reference paths. In a graph with $E>q^{.4705-\eta}$ this is a fraction at most $q^{-.0815+2.340(3/103)+3\eta+o(1)}$ of the clean walks, by the same pruning lower bound. The exponent at $\eta=0$ is $-.01334466\ldots$. A union over several reference translates must pay their number unless they are the same translate; no lower fraction is established.

There is a length versus rank tradeoff. For any **fixed odd** $L\ge3$, use $L$-step paths from the left and closed walks of length $2L$. With rank bound $(\log q)^a$, $a>1$ fixed, the identical count gives
$$E\le q^{.3325+.3325/L+.780(1-1/a)+o(1)}.$$
Indeed the walk lower bound is $\gg E(E/(8N))^{L-1}$ before dividing by $N^2$, while the path height product is $\le H^{2L}$. Take $\sigma=1-1/a+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$ in the same Euler bound. For instance $L=51$, $a=1.20$ costs $.46902$ locally (rounded up), still below $.4705$. There is no uniformity in growing $L$ claimed. Thus a residual that defeats both tests needs many distinct rational path labels even in long fixed windows, not just many edges with large individual gap primes.

**What remains on unrestricted successes.** Greedily remove packets from the graph as long as some endpoint pair supports more than $K$ independent reference quotients: A packet needs at most $K+2$ actual three-step paths (one reference and $K+1$ witnesses), hence at most $3(K+2)$ distinct columns. The rest costs $q^{\gamma+o(1)}$ at this $d$. In particular, on a list with $M_d$ edge-disjoint packets from such a maximal removal, the total in this stratum costs at most $q^{.993552+o(1)}+3(K+2)\sum_d M_d$. The same applies after removing columns already paid for by an economical joint cover. This alternative does not estimate the passing fraction of [joint keys](partial-scalar-classes.md). Nor does it bound $\sum_d M_d$: The kernel of reduction modulo $d$ in the rational units need not have small rank. Replacing the rational products by their common modular residue would incorrectly make the rank test automatic.

A concrete next count is the number of disjoint packets admitting **both** forced outputs on every edge. Besides the gap-cycle congruence modulo $d$, on a path the product of the output ratios $y_{\rm from}/y_{\rm to}$ is the endpoint ratio modulo $q$, and is that ratio over the reals with relative error $O(qH/(\min c\, A_-))=q^{-1.110+o(1)}$. These follow edge by edge from the signed equations. A comparison must use one actual reference path per endpoint pair or pay for all reference paths. Neither a congruence of gap products nor this output approximation alone bounds the packet count. Other $R,j$ blocks and the all-gcd objective remain separate.
