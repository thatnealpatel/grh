# Price the height needed for a fixed Weil window

For the positive Weil floor at fixed $A=2.1$, try the following count before insisting on cancellation through $q^{1.2}$. At height $q^{1.11}$ the worst squarefree row needs a saving of **more than $0.055$ in the exponent**, rather than more than $0.1$. This prices two different sufficient estimates. The smaller height gives a smaller positive floor, and, at the same product length, requires counting pairs at *larger* gaps. Neither estimate is proved here.

## Conversion from a pair bound to a floor

Write $L=\log q$, $B=\log L$, $T=q^t$. In the [two-band argument](prime-conductor-window.md#recombining-the-two-frequency-ranges-without-losing-the-support-bound) use $Y=T/2$, so a bound $O(L/B)$ for the prime sum on $[-T,T]$ covers the low band. For every complex $C_c^\infty(-b,b)$ test, $b=\log(AL)$, and every finite Pick block on distinct real nodes $m\ge B^3$, $\sum_m1/m\le b$, take $D_0=2\log(AL)+B^{-5/4}$, $d=Y^{-1/2}$, and $X=\exp(D_0+2d)$. The [common cutoff and block tail](critical-conductor-window.md#one-finite-operator-for-the-tests-and-the-blocks) cost $o(L)$ per original unit input. The omitted prime powers in the multiplier bound cost $O(\log X)$. Thus the low-band floor is $(1-o(1))L$. On the high band the unsigned compression costs $(A+o(1))L$ and Gamma supplies $\log Y-O(1)$. The uniform floor relative to squared norm or the block's Cauchy Gram form is at least $[\min(1,1+t-A)-o(1)]L$. This conversion does not use the low-band moment theorem with its hypothesis $t<1$: The proposed pair estimate supplies that band's cancellation instead.

Let $k=\lfloor\nu L/\log X\rfloor$, $N=X^k=q^{\nu+o(1)}$, for a fixed $\nu>2t$. Count ordered distinct product **values** from $k$ primes in $[X/B^4,X]$, with repetitions allowed, $q\mid n-m$, and $|n-m|\le 2N/T$. The [squared-sinc moment](critical-conductor-window.md#next-arithmetic-test-for-a-fixed-gain) bounds the bad **fraction** by $q^{t-\nu+o(1)}$ times the diagonal plus this count. Indeed $k\asymp L/B$, $n\ge NB^{-4k}$, and the coefficients of the powered sum divided by $(L/(4B))^k$ are at most $k!(4B\log X/L)^k/\sqrt n\le N^{-1/2}q^{o(1)}$. The discarded primes cost $O(L/B^2)$, and the $O(L\log X)$ derivative gives persistence for length $q^{-o(1)}$. The time weight is bounded below there and has transform $O(T)$ supported on log-gaps at most $1/T$. Character orthogonality is at one prime. The diagonal costs fraction $q^{t-\nu/2+o(1)}$, so a distinct count $O(q^{\nu-t-\xi})$ for fixed $\xi>0$ suffices.

Set $H=2N/(qT)$ and use $g,u,v,h,e$ as in the [reduced-gap count](sparse-pair-gap.md). In the range used below, $1<t<1.2$ and $2t<\nu\le 5/2$. The proof there, with $\sqrt N$ in place of $q^{5/4+o(1)}$, bounds the count on $h\le S e(u)e(v)$ by $q^{\nu/2+o(1)}\sqrt S$ for $S\ge1$. In particular, the divisor construction still has $v\ge NB^{-4k}/H=q^{1+t-o(1)}$ whereas its row cutoff is at most $2H$. The smooth-number Euler product and $2^k$ losses are still $q^{o(1)}$. Taking the whole range with the ordinary one-divisor row gives $\sqrt{NH}\,q^{o(1)}$. Its exponent exceeds the sufficient boundary by

$$\frac\nu2+\frac{\nu-1-t}{2}-(\nu-t)=\frac{t-1}{2}.$$

Changing the degree alone does not remove this deficit. For fixed $A=2.1$, letting $t$ approach $1.1$ from above makes the deficit approach $1/20$ from above; at equality the high-band estimate no longer certifies a positive leading margin.

**Concrete experiment.** Keep $\nu=5/2$ and take $t=111/100$. The diagonal costs $q^{-0.14+o(1)}$ in fraction. Remove $h\le q^{0.27}e(u)e(v)$ for at most $q^{1.385+o(1)}$ pairs. Bound the remaining pairs, summed over $g$ at each prime, by $O(q^{1.39-\xi})$ for some fixed $\xi>0$. They satisfy $g e(u)e(v)<2q^{0.12}$. Even $g=e(u)=e(v)=1$ leaves $q^{0.27}<h\le H=q^{0.39+o(1)}$, with row exponent $1.445$ at the top. Success would give a common exceptional fraction $q^{-\min(0.005,\xi)+o(1)}$ and floor $(0.01-o(1))L$ for all the tests and blocks. It would not prove the $q^{1.2}$ cancellation statement or its $0.1L$ leading margin. No comparison of the true pair counts follows from the two deficits.

A literature check already relevant here is [Harper, *Minor arcs, mean values, and restriction theory for exponential sums over smooth numbers*, Smooth Numbers Result 3](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X15007782). At a squarefree starting value $u$ with $g=1$, count smooth endpoints above it in $[u,u+qJ]$ in its class modulo $q$. At $J$ near $H$ the theorem applies: $qX\le qJ\le u$ and $\log u\le X\le u$. Its upper bound is $\ll\Psi(u,X)(J/u)^{\alpha(u,X)}\log u$. Here $\Psi(u,X)\le\sqrt u\,q^{o(1)}$ and $\alpha=1/2+o(1)$: At $\sigma=1/2\pm\epsilon$ the saddle sum $\sum_{p\le X}\log p/(p^\sigma-1)\asymp_\epsilon X^{1-\sigma}$ brackets $\log u\asymp L$ for fixed small $\epsilon>0$. This substitution gives $q^{o(1)}\sqrt J$ per row, not the missing power. A useful new bound would exploit summation over the smooth starts, not just reapply this progression estimate to each start.
