# Price relations between the two output ratios modulo one space

Fix one actual constructed list, $g=R=j=1$, with the signed row equations, reciprocal full-support masks, and successive-row freshness of [the global double ratio](global-double-ratio.md). Write $z=Y/Y_0$, $S=kZ/(hZ_0)$, and $L=S/z$. Both $z$ and $S$ are $e/d\bmod q$ and relatively $q^{-1.110+o(1)}$-close to $e/d$ over the reals. Write $h(R)$ for the **product** of the reduced positive numerator and denominator of a positive rational $R$, and $[R]$ for its vector of signed prime valuations. In particular $h(z)\le q^{2.890+o(1)}$ and $h(S)\le q^{4.220+o(1)}$.

**Ordinary passing class.** In the rational vector space on the eligible primes, prescribe any one linear subspace $V$ of dimension at most $K=\lfloor(\log q)^{1.44}\rfloor$. Prescribe two families of extensions of $V$, each extension adding at most $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ dimensions, with uniform constant. Across *all* ordered prefix pairs on the list, at most $q^{1.13526+o(1)}$ ordinary triples satisfy **either** test:

1. $[L]$ lies in one of the first $q^{.0085+o(1)}$ spaces.
2. $a[z]+b[S]$ lies in one of the second $q^{.125+o(1)}$ spaces for some integers $a,b$ with $a+b\ne0$.

There is no prior bound on the coefficients. The base and the two families may be chosen from the actual data, once for the whole count. In particular one may absorb *every* error on a prime set whose squarefree product is at most $q^{.017}$ in test 1 or $q^{.250}$ in test 2: Include all the corresponding coordinate extensions, paying the half-power smooth count for their indices. This puts no bound on the error's valuations at those primes. The count charges actual triples directly, not independent memberships of $z$ and $S$.

## Two recovery facts

**An exact $S$ costs subpower even with both prefixes moving.** Use $D=(d,e)$ and $(d',e')=(d,e)/D$. From $S$ recover $e'/d'$ at subpower cost: In a dyadic bin $J\le d'<2J$ the common-residue spacing is $q/(4J^2)$, the absolute interval length is $q^{-1.110+o(1)}$ since $e/d=q^{o(1)}$, and $J\le q^{1.055+o(1)}$. For each primitive pair, use the [twisted split](twisted-second-output-ratio.md): $h=Ar$, $k=As$, $Z=Ku'$, $Z_0=Kw'$ (here $K$ denotes the output gcd only in this paragraph). The reduced $S=x/y$ has $x=su'$, $y=rw'$, giving divisor-many splits. Then $B=(d'x-e'y)/q$ is a nonzero integer: Equality of the reduced fractions $dZ/h=eZ_0/k$ would give the same output with two different deterministic prefixes. Put $T=DK$. After swapping columns if needed, $r\ge s$ and

$$ TB=r\beta_0-s\beta,\qquad d'u'T+q\beta=cAF_2r,\qquad (T,\beta)=1.$$

Indeed $T\mid dZ$, which is coprime to its input. Thus $T\le2rH/|B|$. In $J\le T<2J$ the primitive slopes $\beta/T$ are separated by $F_2r/(4J^2)$ since $q$ is a unit modulo $F_2r$. Their interval length is $2H/J$, so there are $O(1+HJ/(F_2r))=q^{o(1)}$ choices, using $H^2/F_2\le q^{o(1)}$. Split $D\mid T$ and $c\mid cA$ at divisor cost. The actual label $F_1$ identifies at most one row on $(c,d)$, giving $v$; the columns and their rows are then specified. No extra modulus from the other second row was charged. The [global recovery of exact $z$](global-double-ratio.md) likewise costs $q^{o(1)}$.

**For specified $a,b$ with $a+b\ne0$, an exact $W=z^a S^b$ also costs subpower, uniformly.** A zero coefficient reduces to a unique positive rational root and one of the preceding recoveries. Otherwise on an occupied fiber $Q=\operatorname{supp}W$ has $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ primes, irrespective of the height of $W$, since it is supported on $z,S$. Enumerate the full signed $Q$-parts $z_Q,S_Q$ at subpower cost by stars and bars: Each has only $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ factors with multiplicity. Outside $Q$, let $(a',b')=(a,b)/\gcd(|a|,|b|)$. The exact kernel of the primitive integer row gives

$$ z=z_Q\theta^{b'},\qquad S=S_Q\theta^{-a'},\qquad L=C\theta^{-m},\quad C=S_Q/z_Q,\quad m=a'+b'\ne0.$$

Here $\theta$ is positive rational. If $\theta\ne1$, then $h(\theta)\ge2$ and $h(\theta)^{|b'|}\le h(z)$, $h(\theta)^{|a'|}\le h(S)$, so both primitive exponents are $O(\log q)$. If either has magnitude at least two, these inequalities give $h(\theta)\le q^{2.110+o(1)}$. Otherwise $a'=b'=\pm1$. Up to inversion the numerator of $\theta$ then divides $(Y,h Z_0)$ and its denominator divides $(Y_0,k Z)$. For the first bound use $(Y,h)<q^{.115}$ by freshness and $(Y,Z_0)<q^{.118}$ by the reciprocal mask on the other column's full support; swap for the second. Thus $h(\theta)\le q^{.466+o(1)}$. This case uses actual row conditions.

The congruence $\theta^m=C\bmod q$ leaves at most $|m|$ residues, and $\log\theta$ lies in an interval of length $q^{-1.110+o(1)}/|m|$. For distinct reduced fractions $A_i/B_i$ in the same residue of product height at most $M$, one has

$$ q\le |A_1B_2-A_2B_1|=2\sqrt{A_1B_1 A_2B_2}\sinh\bigl(|\log((A_1/B_1)/(A_2/B_2))|/2\bigr). $$

With $M=q^{2.110+o(1)}$ this gives at most subpower many in the interval per residue (log spacing $\ge 2\operatorname{arsinh}(q/(2M))$). Including $\theta=1$ gives only subpower many exact $z$, hence actual triples. No polynomial-height hypothesis on $W$, or equal-size hypothesis on the numerator and denominator of $\theta$, was used.

## Count the relations, including their directions

Choose a set $P$ of at most $K$ pivot primes on which restriction is injective on $V$, taking exactly $\dim V$ independent rows. The number of signed $P$-parts of product height at most $q^u$ is $q^{.22u+o(1)}$ for bounded $u\ge0$: Use the signed Rankin product at $.22+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$ and the eligible prime floor $(2.1\log q)^2/(\log\log q)^4$. The log Euler product is $o(\log q)$ even on $K$ primes. On all eligible primes the corresponding half-power bound is $q^{u/2+o(1)}$, by counting the absolute product and its divisor-many signed splits, as in the [outside-product estimate](ordinary-outside-product.md).

For test 1 choose the extension at cost $q^{.0085+o(1)}$. Its dimension is at most $K+O(\log q/\log\log q)$, so the same pivot Euler bound applies. Apply the [signed pivot sieve for $L$](global-double-ratio.md) in that space. In a bin $J_0\asymp q^t$ the divided determinant is at most $q^{5-2t+o(1)}$ and the ordinary multiplicity is $q^{\mu+o(1)}$, $\mu=\min(t/2,.223)$. The maximum of $.0085+.22(5-2t)+\mu$ for $0\le t\le.466+o(1)$ is $1.13526+o(1)$, attained at $t=.446$. There are subpower many bins.

For test 2 bin the product heights of $z_P,S_P$ dyadically at $q^u,q^w$, using the **base pivots**, before choosing an extension. Counting *one ratio* including its entire outside part, then applying its global recovery, gives two separate bounds on the whole bin, with exponents

$$ 1.445-.28u,\qquad 2.110-.28w, $$

up to $o(1)$. A third price enumerates both $P$-parts and chooses the extension $V'$ first, with exponent $.22(u+w)+.125$. The rows $P$ remain independent on $V'$ because they were independent on $V$. Complete them to pivot rows $P'$ deterministically for $V'$; the additional $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ rows cost only subpower many possibilities for both signed parts there, by the factor count with multiplicities. Only the third price pays for the family. Charging the family before binning both individual ratios would lose this distinction.

Let $T$ be the inverse from $P'$-coordinates to $V'$, and $R(X)=X-T(X|_{P'})$. Write $A=[z]$, $B=[S]$. After the enumeration the correction vectors $T(A|_{P'}),T(B|_{P'})$ are known. The system for the two coefficients is

$$ aR(A)+bR(B)=0. $$

Choose at most two prime coordinates giving independent rows spanning the row space of this two-column matrix. Specify their prime indices and the **actual valuations** of $z,S$ there, at subpower cost (polylogarithmically many indices and values). The selected rows determine the whole kernel, including when the row basis is empty. From the small system choose one deterministic integer solution with $a+b\ne0$, for instance of least $|a|+|b|$ with a fixed tie rule. Even if a different pair originally certified the pass, the chosen solution works for each actual triple with this code and row basis. It fixes

$$ [W]=T\bigl(aA|_{P'}+bB|_{P'}\bigr). $$

On occupied codes this is the valuation vector of $z^a S^b$. Thus uniform exact-monomial recovery proves the third price, with no sum over the coefficient sizes and no bound on minors of a basis of $V'$.

The minimum of the three exponents is at most $8171/7200=1.1348611\ldots$: If the first two exceed this value, $u<319/288$ and $w<1003/288$, forcing the third below it. This is an aggregate price for the transverse relations, with no exact-$L$ multiplicity factor. Combining the tests proves the assertion.

## What would suffice now

Use the squarefree coordinate extensions above (including the empty prime set). In an over-budget ordinary family, start with $V=0$, select a triple failing both tests, and adjoin $[z],[S]$ to $V$. Both dimensions are new: A rational dependence modulo the old $V$ clears denominators; if its coefficient sum is zero then $[L]\in V$, and otherwise test 2 passes already in the base. Repeat as long as $\dim V\le K$. If selection stops earlier, the **last** space alone prices the family. Hence an over-budget family gives at least $\lfloor K/2\rfloor+1$ successive independent pairs. At each stage even an integral nonzero-sum combination in the old space after ignoring a prime set of product at most $q^{.250}$ is forbidden, as is $[L]$ after ignoring a prime set of product at most $q^{.017}$. This argument pays for no union over selection histories. It is compatible with the existing outside-product test during its shorter sequence by taking the union of the two passing classes at each of those steps.

**A test directly on fresh primes of ordinary labels.** In one $J_0$-bin, suppose one builds a pool $P$ from the six raw supports of earlier triples, now allowing $|P|\le (\log q)^{1.10}$. For $R=z$ or $S$ let $r$ be the log-base-$q$ of the product of *distinct* primes in $R$ outside $P$, and put $b=2.890$ or $4.220$, respectively. The $P$-part of $R$ has product height at most $q^{b-r+o(1)}$. Bin the outside radical dyadically, enumerate it at half power, and enumerate *all* signed valuations there at subpower cost once its primes are known. Enumerate the inside at exponent $.05$ by Rankin. Global recovery of $R$ prices this bin at

$$q^{.05(b-r)+r/2+o(1)}\quad\text{actual triples across all prefix pairs.}$$

Thus $r\le2.20$ for $z$ or $r\le2.05$ for $S$ together cost at most $q^{1.1345+o(1)}$ (the two exponents are $1.1345$ and $1.1335$). Use the coordinate space on $P$ as the base in the two global tests and discard these two additional passing classes at each selection. An over-budget bin must admit successive triples, on distinct ordinary labels, with **both** $Y,Y_0$ each having fresh squarefree product $>q^{.755-o(1)}$ and $ZZ_0$ having fresh squarefree product $>q^{.720-o(1)}$. Indeed each first output costs at most $q^{1.445+o(1)}$ in the radical of $z$; for $S$ strip off at most $hk\le q^{1.330+o(1)}$. Add the six supports after each success. Each step adds $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ primes, so the length forced is at least a constant times $(\log q)^{.10}\log\log q$. Only the current pool is priced. Unlike the rank sequence of length $\asymp K$, this shorter sequence controls *prime supports themselves*, not merely combinations of their valuations. It gives a concrete ordinary-occupancy obstruction without claiming that this many primes exhaust the eligible universe.

The missing estimate is still on actual ordinary triples outside these classes. For example, an economical cover by $q^{.0007}$ spaces passing either test would cost $q^{1.13596+o(1)}$ triples, enough together with the exceptional estimate. There is no such cover established here. The eligible universe has order $(\log q)^2/\log\log q$, larger than $K$. The modular character of each ratio or the length of the independent sequence alone does not exclude it.

## What changing to one actual reference per input buys

There is a smaller-space test that **can** change spaces with the input. At each occupied $v$, choose one actual reference denominator $e$ there. In this paragraph no reciprocal pair deletions or ordinary-label condition are required. Set $U_d=Y_d$, $V_d=Z_d/h_d$, and $p_d=V_d/U_d$. Then $z=U_d/U_e$ and $S=V_d/V_e$. Prescribe a space $V_v$ of dimension at most $\lfloor(\log q)^{1.10}\rfloor$. Also prescribe at most $q^{.0185}$ extensions of this space, each of additional dimension at most $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ with uniform constant. The choices may depend on $v,e$. Count $d$ for which **some nonzero** rational linear combination of $[z],[S]$ lies in one of those extensions, including combinations proportional to $[L]$.

*Local exact recovery.* At fixed $v$, for specified integers $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, an exact $U_d^a V_d^b$ costs $q^{o(1)}$ actual columns uniformly. An exact $U_d$ determines $d$: The unique possible gap in $[-H,H]$ follows from $v-q\alpha\equiv0\pmod{U_d}$ and $U_d>2H$. This covers $b=0$. For $a+b=0$ one has the exact $p_d$, which [recovers the column](same-input-second-bracket.md) using successive-row freshness. In the remaining cases enumerate the $U_d,V_d$ parts on the support of the exact monomial as before. Off that support the primitive kernel gives $U_{d,\rm out}=\theta^{b'}$, $V_{d,\rm out}=\theta^{-a'}$, also valid if $a=0$. Here $h(\theta)\le q^{1.445+o(1)}$ since $b'\ne0$. Now $p_d=C\theta^{-m}$ with $C$ known and $m=a'+b'\ne0$. At this fixed input $p_d$ has the known nonzero residue $F_2/(v/c)\bmod q$ and is relatively $q^{-1.110+o(1)}$-close to that same rational in the reals. If $\theta\ne1$, $|m|=O(\log q)$ by the two product heights (including the case $a'=0$). The log-spacing proof above gives subpower many $\theta$, hence $U_d$ and $d$. Fixing $v$ here is essential to the residue and interval for the *unreferenced* $p_d$.

*Price the gap before the whole vector.* Choose the extension first. Its pivot set $P$ has size at most $(\log q)^{1.10}+O(\log q/\log\log q)$, so the pivot price uses $\sigma=.05+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$. If the $P$-part of $U_d$ exceeds $2H$, select a divisor strictly crossing $2H$ before enumerating any other coordinate. It costs $q^{.05(.390)+o(1)}$ choices and fixes the first gap, hence $d$. Otherwise that whole pivot part costs at most this same price.

Bin the $P$-part of $h_d$ at $q^j$, $0\le j\le.665+o(1)$. One price enumerates the full pivot parts of $U_d,V_d$; the latter come from $Z_d,h_d$, bounded in pivot height exponent by $1.445+j$. Since $e$ is fixed, these data specify the pivot parts of $z,S$. The row-basis code for test 2 now chooses any deterministic nonzero integer kernel vector, with no sum restriction. It fixes $z^a S^b$ and therefore $U_d^a V_d^b$. Local exact recovery proves the price $.05(1.835+j)$, up to $o(1)$ in all exponents.

If the $P$-part of $Z_d$ is at most $2H$, this price is already at most $.05(.390+.390+.665)$. Otherwise there is a second price: Choose a pivot divisor of $Z_d$ strictly crossing $2H$, enumerate the pivot part of $h_d$, and then its outside part by the half-power estimate. For this fixed $v$, $c$ is known, so $cF_2h_d$ is now known. The selected divisor determines $\beta_d$ uniquely in $[-H,H]$, hence the entire second output and its sorted prefix $d$. This costs $.05(.390+j)+(.665-j)/2$. This step would not fix the second input in a count with moving $c$. The minimum of the two prices peaks at $j=.665-2(.05)(1.445)=.5205$, at exponent $.117775$. The smaller branches and the polylogarithmic bins fit this bound.

After charging the extensions, the exponent per reference is $.0185+.117775=.136275$. With **one** reference per occupied input this counts passing *incidences* at $q^{.996275+o(1)}$ across the $q^{.860+o(1)}$ inputs. With **all** references this counts passing ordered pairs at $q^{.136275+o(1)}I$, not $q^{.996275+o(1)}$; this term is still absorbable in the Cauchy incidence inequality (linear contribution $q^{.996275+o(1)}$ to $I$). The same price applies if the spaces are simply specified per reference rather than combined across inputs. At these dimensions the exact repeated-reference argument controls both directions per choice, whereas the earlier local $p_d$ test reaches dimension $(\log q)^{1.18}$ for the single difference direction only.

**Next decision.** At an input of degree exceeding $q^{.137}$, fix an actual reference. Grow a span from pairs $([z],[S])$ against it. Before each selection one may include *every* coordinate extension on a squarefree eligible product $\le q^{.037}$ in the local test: There are $q^{.0185+o(1)}$ such products and each adds $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ dimensions. Hence, while the span has dimension at most $(\log q)^{1.10}$, one more denominator exists for which the two directions are independent even after ignoring any such prime set. The exponent gap $.137-.136275>0$ pays for the search at each step, not all histories. Test whether the actual lifts $dU_d=v-q\alpha_d$ and $dZ_d=cF_2h_d-q\beta_d$ admit these sequences with *one fixed $v$*, rather than comparing rows at unrelated inputs. Ruling them out uniformly would bound each degree by $q^{.137+o(1)}$ and hence $I$ by $q^{.997+o(1)}$. A uniform bound just on the dimension of the ambient eligible-prime universe is too large; no such exclusion, global cover, or unrestricted incidence estimate is proved here.
