## Complete an evaluation matrix without another split

Rank at most two for the span of the represented $w=(K,C,B_0)$ is already payable by [two-minor recovery](normal-factor-minors.md). Otherwise fix three independent represented $w_1,w_2,w_3$. At fixed degree $m$ use the supplementary rows obtained by **evaluating at** $w(t)=\sum_i t_i w_i$, $t\in\{0,\ldots,m\}^3$. These evaluation rows span the full dual of the space of degree-$m$ ternary forms: A form annihilated on the grid pulls back to a polynomial of degree at most $m$ in each variable, zero by successive univariate interpolation. The substitution is invertible over $\mathbb Q$. We evaluate at point combinations; we do not assume that degree-$m$ evaluation is linear in the point, or that a supplementary point is represented.

Keep the two coordinate rows $E_A,E_B$ as in the sextic determinant. Put $N=\binom{m+2}{2}$, $S=\binom{m+2}{3}$. If $k$ actual evaluation rows are independent modulo the two coordinate rows, they and the coordinate rows can be completed to an invertible $N$-row matrix from the grid. Each grid point satisfies the same bounds on $K,C,B_0$ and the thin linear form $K'$, up to constants depending only on $m$; also $K'\in q\mathbb Z_{(q)}$ there. Thus the archimedean bound after shear and the divisor $q^{S-(2m-1)}$ are unchanged. **Only the $k$ actual rows** contribute the omitted-factor products: Expand the unsheared determinant along $E_A,E_B$ to obtain them, independently of the $q$-divisibility. No extra divisor or primitivity is asserted for a grid point.

With $u=.619$, $v=.9745-\sigma/2$, and $h_A h_B\ge q^{.599-o(1)}$ as in the sextic argument, the absolute determinant divided by the coprime divisors has exponent at most

$$E_m(k;\sigma)=(.458-\sigma)S+2.110(2m-1)-(1.949-\sigma)m-.599k+o(1).$$

If this is negative by a fixed margin, the invertible completion is impossible. Hence the full evaluation matrix of both coordinate points and **all** represented points has rank at most $k+1$, not merely less than $N$. The null forms can be taken integral of polynomial height, by solving on maximal independent rows using minors. Neither the reference basis nor the grid is enumerated over filters in the triple count.

## What the rank pays for

* At $m=6$ take $k=25$. The bound is worst at $\sigma=.446-o(1)$ and gives $E_6(25;.446)=-.111+o(1)$. Thus the rank is at most 26 out of 28, giving **two independent sextics**. For comparison the original 26 actual rows give $-.710$, while 24 give $+.488$ at this endpoint. The latter is *not* a vanishing test there.
* Fix two such sextics and let $G$ be their gcd over $\mathbb Q$. Its degree $e$ is at most five. Off $G=0$ the residual forms are coprime even over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ (a common geometric factor would have all its conjugates in common). Bézout leaves at most $(6-e)^2$ projective points. Each point determines at most one primitive oriented normal in the original coordinates and gives divisor-many indices by the nonzero normal evaluation. The fixed-degree factors of $G$ have polynomial height. Apply the original line, conic, missing-coordinate-point, and reduction tests to each rational factor. Since $\deg G\le5$, two factors of degree at least three cannot both remain.
* In the upper bins take $m=12$, $k=53$. Now $N=91$, $S=364$, and at $\sigma=.455$ the exponent is $.003(364)+2.110(23)-1.494(12)-.599(53)=-.053$. It decreases with $\sigma$. The kernel on the whole filter has dimension at least $91-54=37$. Multiples of any one rational quintic in degree twelve have dimension only $\binom{9}{2}=36$. For a remaining irreducible quintic there is therefore a rational null form not divisible by it; they have at most 60 common projective points. Unlike a second copy of the sextic, this additional equation actually pays for the quintic. The same degree-twelve test at $.446$ gives $+3.115$, so this last pruning is not uniform down to the worst bin.

All finite exceptions and payable factors together cost $q^{1.1355+o(1)}$ triples by the original $\sqrt D\sqrt T$ charge. The possible **single** residual factor must pass through both coordinate points and contain the specified line after reduction modulo $q$. There is no bound here on the population of that factor, and it need not be the same curve in different filters. The unrestricted exceptional triple exponent remains $1.1385$. For the next occupancy test keep the proposed cubic restriction to $k_0^\perp$: Control the content and the third root rather than importing the conic discriminant bound.
