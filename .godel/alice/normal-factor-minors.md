# Recover the visible split from two normal minors

On one actual two-label list, consider the exceptional ordered visible splits of the [double-ratio count](global-double-ratio.md): The box of the first-row lattice spans rank two, the primitive normal has $a\ell\ne0$, and $R_0>q^{.446}$. The total number of residual ordered triples on these splits is at most **$q^{1.1385+o(1)}$**. More precisely, the subfamily with $a/(F,a)\le q^\kappa$ costs $q^{1.055+\kappa/4+o(1)}$ for fixed $\kappa\ge0$. Thus $\kappa=.323$ passes the $1.136$ budget with a fixed margin. The unrestricted estimate misses that budget by $.0025$ in the exponent.

**Inputs and bins.** Write $F=F_1$ and $Q=Q_0$. The ordered coprime smooth moduli $P,Q$ are prime to $Fq$ and at least $q^{1.212-o(1)}$ each. For the lattice $Fm\equiv q\alpha\pmod P$, $Fm\equiv q\alpha_0\pmod Q$ in the box $(V,H,H)$, use $V=q^{1.720+o(1)}$, $H=q^{.390+o(1)}$, $F=q^{.780+o(1)}$. Orient its primitive normal $(a,b,c)$ by $a>0$. Let $g$ be the image index of the normal, $\lambda=PQ/g$, and $R_0=VH^2/(\lambda\|(aV,bH,cH)\|_2)$. We use the [normal-fraction note](normal-fraction-occupancy.md) for $(a,g)=1$, $qa+F(b+c)=g\ell$, $0<|\ell|\le q^{.020+o(1)}$, and the per-split price $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{R_0}$ for **actual triples**. That price uses a divisor of $m/j_0$, with $j_0$ the generator of the plane lattice's $m$-projection. Smooth integers up to $T\ge1$ cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$; eligible primes and divisor counts of nonzero polynomial-height integers are subpower.

Fix dyadic bins $R\le R_0<2R$, $J\le\lambda<2J$ and a bin with upper endpoint $X$ for the positive integer $a'=a/s$, where $s=(F,a)$ is fixed at divisor cost. Put $F'=F/s$. Since $s$ is prime to $g$, it divides $\ell$ and

$$qa'+F'(b+c)=g\ell/s\ne0.$$

The transformed normal $(a',b,c)$ is still primitive, and $(a',g)=1$. The normal box has radii $(X,Y,Y)$, with

$$X\ll H^2/(RJ),\qquad Y=VH/(RJ).$$

In an occupied bin $X\ll H^2/(sRJ)$ as well. Here $r=\log_q R\ge.446-o(1)$, $j=\log_q J\ge0$, $r+j\le.780+o(1)$ and, on the residual $a/(a,|\ell|)>q^{.160}$ of the earlier count, $j\le.174+o(1)$. The present argument works without that residual restriction: $j\le.334+o(1)$ suffices and follows from $a\ge1$.

**Separate index and full-rank price.** Let $A=(P,g)$, $B=(Q,g)$, so $AB=g$ and $A,B\ge q^{1.212-o(1)}/(2J)$. Evaluating the original normal on $(0,0,Q)$ and $(0,P,0)$ gives $A\mid c$ and $B\mid b$, respectively, using $(P,Q)=1$. For smooth $f_A\mid A$, $f_B\mid B$, put $d=f_Af_B$. The transformed normal lies in

$$L_d=\{(a',b,c)\in\mathbb Z^3:f_B\mid b,\ f_A\mid c,\ d\mid qa'+F'(b+c)\}.$$

The index is exactly $d^2$: The two coordinate divisibilities cost $d$, and then the last congruence costs $d$ because $q$ is a unit modulo $d$. If the box has rank three in $L_d$, its entire point count is $O(XY^2/d^2)$. Indeed three independent box vectors generate a sublattice. Within each coset, translate their fundamental parallelepiped to the points being counted; the translates have disjoint interiors and fit into the box dilated by $4$. Sum over the sublattice index. This full-rank price does not require the vectors used for the parallelepiped to be actual normals.

**Rank-two recovery, with the vanishing cases excluded.** Suppose $dF'>2XY$ and the box points span rank two. Use the integral forms on $L_d$

$$K=(qa'+F'(b+c))/d,\qquad C_0=c/f_A,\qquad B_0=b/f_B.$$

On an actual normal, $h_A=A/f_A$ divides both $K$ and $C_0$, and $h_B=B/f_B$ divides both $K$ and $B_0$. Fix any two independent box vectors $v_1,v_2$ in $L_d$. The actual normal is primitive in $\mathbb Z^3$, hence has coprime coefficients in any integral basis of the lattice $L_d$ intersected with this plane. Complete it to such a basis by Bézout. In that basis the first row of the $(K,C_0)$ matrix is divisible by $h_A$. The determinant on $v_1,v_2$ is an integer multiple of the basis determinant; consequently

$$h_A\mid\det(K,C_0)(v_1,v_2),\qquad h_B\mid\det(K,B_0)(v_1,v_2).$$

This argument uses a basis of the entire lattice on the plane, not the possibly unsaturated lattice generated by the two small vectors. The minors are evaluated on the small vectors, so no bound on the basis height is needed.

To see that the first minor is nonzero in an occupied filter, suppose it vanishes, and write all determinants on $v_1,v_2$. Then

$$q\det(a',c)+F'\det(b,c)=0.$$

As $(q,F')=1$, $F'\mid\det(a',c)$. Also $f_B\mid\det(b,c)$ and $(q,f_B)=1$ give $f_B\mid\det(a',c)$; $f_A\mid\det(a',c)$ follows from $f_A\mid c$. These three divisors are pairwise coprime. Since $|\det(a',c)|\le2XY<dF'$, it vanishes, and so does $\det(b,c)$. The $c$-coordinate is not identically zero on the plane: On an actual normal with $c=0$, the conditions $B\mid b$ and $B\mid qa'+F'(b+c)$ would imply $B\mid a'$ because $(q,B)=1$, contrary to $(a',g)=1$ and $B>1$. Both vanishings would therefore make the three coordinate columns on $v_1,v_2$ proportional to the nonzero $c$-column, contradicting their independence. Interchanging $b,c$ and $A,B$ proves nonvanishing of the second minor.

The two nonzero minors have polynomial height and give subpower many choices for $h_A,h_B$, hence for $g=d h_A h_B$. No count of the actual normals on the plane is needed. Rank one also gives subpower many $g$: There is at most one primitive normal with $a'>0$ on the line, and $g$ divides its fixed nonzero $qa'+F'(b+c)$. Rank zero is unoccupied.

**Charge all splits.** Take $D=4\sqrt{XY^2}$ and, for each occupied split, choose $f_A,f_B$ by the first crossing of $\sqrt D$ in $A,B$, using an ordered multiplication of prime factors. There is enough room on each side: The exponent of $\sqrt D$ is at most $1.25-.75(r+j)+o(1)$, less than $1.212-j-o(1)$ even for $r=.446$, $j=.334$. The overshoot is subpower, so $D<d\le Dq^{o(1)}$. The number of ordered filters is at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$ by counting the smooth product and splitting it at divisor cost. The determinant of three box vectors has absolute value at most $6XY^2<d^2$, so the box spans rank at most two. Moreover $dF'>2XY$, since $F'\ge q^{.760-o(1)}$ and $\sqrt X\le q^{.167+o(1)}$.

With $g$ recovered, enumerate the smooth $\lambda<2J$ at $q^{o(1)}\sqrt J$ cost and the ordered $P,Q$ from $PQ=g\lambda$ at divisor cost. This majorizes every occupied split in the bins, chosen from the same list, even when several splits share a normal or a filter. Multiplying by the triple price gives

$$q^{o(1)}\sqrt{RJ}\,(XY^2)^{1/4}=q^{1.055+o(1)}X^{1/4}.$$

Sum over dyadic bins and $s\mid F$ at subpower cost. The worst bin has $X=q^{.334+o(1)}$, $R=q^{.446+o(1)}$, $J=q^{o(1)}$, giving $1.1385$. The bound also charges the previously treated $a/(a,|\ell|)\le q^{.160}$ at $q^{1.100+o(1)}$, since $a\le q^{.180+o(1)}$ there. The new cut $a/(F,a)\le q^{.323}$ costs $q^{1.13575+o(1)}$. Above that cut, the borderline for this method is the reduced first-coordinate exponent $.324$; in particular $r+j>.456$ by a fixed margin is already affordable.

**Specific remaining test below the determinant cutoff.** In a remaining bin try the same ordered selection with $D=Yq^{.161}$ instead of $4Y\sqrt X$. The factors are still available and $dF'>2XY$. There are at most $S=q^{(2.271-r-j)/2+o(1)}$ filters. Per filter let $k_f$ count the distinct represented $g$ (at least one occupied split in the bin assigned to the filter). The rank-two argument gives $k_f\le q^{o(1)}$ if the represented normals span at most two: Apply it to two independent representatives, irrespective of other, non-represented lattice vectors. In a full-rank box the volume argument and divisor recovery of $g$ from each normal only give

$$k_f\ll q^{o(1)}XY^2/d^2\le q^{\log_q X-.322+o(1)}.$$

At the worst endpoint this overhead is $.012$. The baseline triple exponent, with $q^{o(1)}$ choices of $g$ per filter, is $1.1355$. It suffices to show uniformly that the sum of $k_f$ over full-rank represented fibers is at most $q^{(2.271-r-j)/2+.0005-\eta+o(1)}$ for any fixed $\eta>0$. A supremum or averaged estimate must include all the actual filters chosen at this height; a rank-two estimate alone does not do so.

There is one exact restriction on that full-rank population. For three independent represented normals, their $(K,C_0,B_0)$ rows have determinant $qt$, up to sign, for a nonzero integer $t$ of size $\le 6XY^2/d^2$ (the coordinate change has determinant $\pm q/d^2$, and the normal lattice has index $d^2$). Row $i$ is coordinatewise divisible by $(h_{A,i}h_{B,i},h_{A,i},h_{B,i})$. The gcd of the six products of these row divisors over determinant permutations divides $t$, because it is prime to $q$. In particular, if two independent representatives have same-side overlap $\gcd(h_{A,1},h_{A,2})>6XY^2/d^2$, they cannot be extended to an independent triple of representatives, and likewise for $B$. But with pairwise disjoint residual prime supports the six-product gcd is $1$: For each prime the determinant permutation can put its single affected row in the column it does not divide. So the divisibility has not yet bounded the fibers with dispersed support. This is a limitation of the estimate, not an example of a list exceeding the target.
