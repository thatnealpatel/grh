# Separate the lifted crossed-gap directions at an actual denominator

The [smooth-support obstruction](diffuse-support-test.md) drops the signed gaps. Here is an additional deletion for **actual** endpoints. It tests a real direction and a residue one power deeper than the automatic determinant divisibility. It can be combined with the partial-divisor deletions, not substituted for a proof that their complement is empty.

Use the [crossed directions](crossed-gap-filters.md) at $g=1$: $R=(|\delta_1|,|\delta_2|)$, $j=(h_1,h_2)<q^{.115}$, $v_i=\delta_i/R$, $\ell_i=h_i/j$, $n=(\ell_2v_1,\ell_1v_2)$. The rows satisfy $cx_i-dy_i=q\delta_i$, $x_i=F_i h_i$, $t=(x_1y_2-x_2y_1)/q\ne0$. Set
$$G=(y_1,y_2),\quad M=(|n_1n_2|)_{\rm eligible},\quad C_* = MG/\operatorname{rad}(MG).$$
Fix any actual reference at the same $d$ with vector $n^0$. The integer $D'=\det(n,n^0)/d$ is defined since both vectors satisfy $F_2 n_1\equiv F_1 n_2\pmod d$. Set $e=(d,D')$, including $e=d$ if $D'=0$. Take dyadic lower endpoints $L_i\le|\delta_i|<2L_i$ for the **tested** gaps. Let $s$ be the absolute sine between $(n_1/L_1,n_2/L_2)$ and $(n^0_1/L_1,n^0_2/L_2)$, using the same scaling on both. Then at most $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ distinct numerators satisfy
$$\boxed{\quad \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(|\delta_1|,|\delta_2|)}{\min(R,G) C_*}\;s\;\frac{(e,G)}{e}\le q^{.660}.\quad}$$

In particular in the full-gap, $R=G=C_*=1$ box, an arbitrary actual reference tests relative direction at sine width $q^{-.120+o(1)}$, or lifted agreement with that reference at any $e\mid d$ of size $\ge q^{.120+o(1)}$ (use the displayed inequality at boundaries). This is not the congruence modulo $d$ shared by the entire list. Writing $T=t/(Rj)$, we have $F_2 n_1-F_1 n_2=dT$, so $F_2D'=T n^0_2-T^0 n_2$. Both second coordinates and $F_2$ are units modulo $d$. Thus the extra credit tests equality of $T/n_2$ and $T^0/n^0_2$ modulo $e$. No independence between the two tests is presumed.

**Direction sieve.** Row primitivity gives $(G,Rj n_1n_2 F_1F_2)=1$: A prime of $G$ divides each output and therefore divides neither input product nor either gap. In addition $(Rj,d)=1$. Since $G\mid t$ and $Rj(F_2n_1-F_1n_2)=dt$, we have $dG\mid F_2n_1-F_1n_2\ne0$, even when $(G,d)>1$.

Enumerate $e\mid d$ and $(e,G)\mid e$ at divisor cost and put $b=e/(e,G)$. Dyadically group $R,G,C_*$ by lower bounds $D,g_0,K$, respectively. For $s$ use upper dyadic bounds $S\le2s$ and one innermost band $s\le S=q^{-10}$ (irrelevant rounding at the junction). For fixed block and $j$, two tested vectors have determinant bounded by $16U^2 L_1 L_2 S/(D^2 j^2)$: In the scaled plane each length is at most $2\sqrt{2} U/(Dj)$ and the sine between the two directions is at most $2S$, including antipodal directions. Here $U=q^{.665+o(1)}$ bounds $h_i$. Put
$$V=32 U^2 L_1 L_2 S/(d D^2 j^2 b),\qquad A=\ell_1\ell_2.$$
We will beat the determinant using an enumerated smooth filter $f\ge V$ together with $d b$. If $V\le1$ take $f=1$. Otherwise choose $w\mid C_*$ crossing $\min(K,\sqrt V/X^2)$ with overshoot $X$ (take one if the target is at most one). The seed $w\operatorname{rad}(w)\mid MG$ fits within $V$. Extend it to $f\mid MG$ in $[V,XV]$ by using remaining powers in $A$: The least common multiple of the seed and $A$ divides $MG$ and is large enough since $V/A\le q^{-.275+o(1)}$. The final filter has $\operatorname{rad}(f)\le XV/w$. Counting smooth radicals by the half-power bound, and assigning eligible prime exponents and their splits at subpower cost, gives
$$q^{o(1)}(1+\sqrt{V/K}+V^{1/4})$$
choices. The last term pays for saturation at $\sqrt V/X^2$; it is not permissible to assume that a seed of size $K^2$ always fits a thin sector.

Split $f=\gamma m_1m_2$ with $\gamma\mid G$ and $m_i\mid n_i$. The three factors are pairwise coprime by the preceding coprimalities and ambient primitivity of $n$. Require $m_i\mid n_i$, $d\gamma\mid F_2n_1-F_1n_2$, and $de\mid\det(n,n^0)$. In the multiples-of-$m_i$ lattice the first congruence has index $d\gamma$ and the second has index $de$: The substituted coefficients are units for their respective moduli, using $e\mid d$. Their intersection has index at least their lcm, **not necessarily their product**. In ambient coordinates this gives index at least $df e/(e,\gamma)\ge df b$. Since determinants in the block are strictly smaller, only parallel vectors remain for each filter/split, hence at most two ambient primitive vectors. Divisors of the nonzero coordinates recover $h_i,v_i$ for the fixed $j$. Recover $G$ at divisor cost from $(F_2n_1-F_1n_2)/d\ne0$.

**Scalar sieve with one output congruence.** For a recovered direction take $a x_1\equiv qv_1\pmod d$. Both $B_i=(a x_i-qv_i)/d$ are integral by the direction congruence. Then $c=aR+du$ and $y_i=B_iR+x_i u$ with $(R,u)=1$ since $(R,c)=1$. Require $G\mid B_1R+x_1u$, an index-$G$ condition as $(x_1,G)=1$. Cross $\max(1,8DZ/(dG))$ with a smooth $f_c\mid c$, possible since $D\le H$, $Z/d<X$, and $c>Z/X$. Its condition on $aR+du$ has index $f_c$ since $(d,c)=1$. The two indices multiply because $(G,c)=1$. The area of $|R|\le 2D,\ |aR+du|\le Z$ divided by $G f_c$ is $O(1)$. Pick's bound in rank two, or primitivity on a line through zero, gives $O(1)$ scalar lifts per filter. This costs $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{\max(1,D/g_0)}$, not two savings from requiring both outputs to vanish modulo $G$.

Multiplying the two sieves and summing over smooth $j$ costs $q^{o(1)}$ times the sum of three terms. The constant term costs at most $q^{.0575}\sqrt H$ up to subpowers. The saturation term costs at most $(U^2 H^2/d)^{1/4}=q^{.26375+o(1)}$ since $\sum_{j\ {\rm smooth}}1/\sqrt j\le q^{o(1)}$ and the factor $D^{-1/2}$ pays for the lift. The main term is
$$\frac{U}{\sqrt d}\sqrt{\frac{L_1 L_2 S}{D\min(D,g_0) K b}}.$$
On a block meeting the displayed cut the radicand is $O(q^{.660})$ by dyadic comparability; the innermost band also satisfies this by $L_i\le H$. The resulting exponent is $.665-.5275+.330=.4675$. All block counts are subpower. This proves the cut. For one reference per occupied $d$ the cost is $.995$ in the list exponent. If using all references subtract the local outgoing allowance before squaring; the dyadic angular convention need not give a symmetric graph. A forward bound in every induced subgraph bounds its undirected average degree by twice that bound.

**Two exact Euclidean tests for occupancy.** A one-reference lift of this kind still leaves the full-width, $e=R=G=C_*=1$, separated sector open. Paying $q^{.120}$ real sectors at $.4675$ costs $.5875$, worse than the unrestricted local $.5275$. For a test that keeps both forced outputs, define $Q(a,m,B)$ to be the denominator of the **last** continued-fraction convergent of the canonical expansion of $a/m$ with denominator at most $B$. Use the least nonnegative representative of $a$ modulo $m$.

For each tested smooth $c$ and either label $F$, an actual row must satisfy the simultaneous identities
$$h=Q(cF q^{-1},d,A_+/F),\qquad y=Q(-d q^{-1},cF,A_+).$$
Indeed if $az-mk=\delta$, $z\le B$, $(k,z)=1$, and $0<|\delta|\le H$ with $2HB<m$, then $|a/m-k/z|<1/(2z^2)$ and $k/z$ is a convergent. Its denominator is the last one up to $B$: A later convergent $k'/z'$ in that range would have $|az'-mk'|\le|\delta|$ by the Euclidean remainder recurrence, but $0<m\,|kz'-k'z|\le 2HB<m$. For the first identity use $r\equiv cFq^{-1}\pmod d$, so $rh-dk=\delta$; for the second use $s\equiv-dq^{-1}\pmod{cF}$, so $sy-cFk'=\delta$. Primitivity gives $(\delta,h)=(\delta,y)=1$, enough for both reduced approximations. The strict inequalities follow from the **exact** $F>W=2A_+HX/Z$ and $c,d>Z/X$, not merely equal size exponents. In particular the larger modulus in the second test is $cF$, not $c$. At fixed $d$, the two input residues are rotations by $F_2/F_1$ of one another, while the two output residues coincide modulo $c$ although their full moduli differ.

Equivalently, one Euclidean pass per slot suffices. With the last input convergent $k/h$, take $\delta=rh-dk$ and $y=((cF-qr)/d)h+qk$. Reject if the gap, positivity, eligible factors, exact lengths, primitivity, or prefix conditions fail; even passing both slots need not give a constructed rich-column incidence. This is $O(\log d)$ Euclidean divisions per slot, not an enumeration over cofactors. As a small check of *both* identities, take $q=17$, $6\le c<50$, $8\le d<50$, $F\in\{2,3,5\}$, $H\in\{1,2,3\}$, $(cF,d)=(cd,q)=1$. Use bounds $B_h=\lfloor(d-1)/(2H)\rfloor$, $B_y=\lfloor(cF-1)/(2H)\rfloor$ and enumerate positive $h,y$ in these bounds with primitive input/output products and $0<|cFh-dy|/q\le H$ an integer. All 4,393 tuples, 2,128 with negative gap, had both last convergent denominators as predicted. This check tests recovery, not smooth-family occupancy.

**Next decision.** Count joint success of the two Euclidean passes on eligible $c$ at fixed $d$, testing smoothness in the two recovered quotients rather than enumerating $y_1,y_2$. Even the criterion that both *last* denominators are eligible-smooth, with no choice of convergent left, is not a density estimate. Dropping quotient eligibility or treating the rotated inputs as independent requires a new argument. No unrestricted $.998$ bound or fixed-window conclusion follows here.
