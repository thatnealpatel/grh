# Remove the known part of the within-column determinant

The [lifted direction test](lifted-direction-separation.md) measures agreement with one actual reference. There is also a strip supplied by the labels themselves. Its determinant can be tested **before** fixing a list, when the cost is charged per starting row rather than per reference per denominator. Use the actual primitive rows at $g=1$ and the sizes of [shared row labels](shared-row-labels.md).

## Thin the list construction

Fix a column $(c,d)$, a starting row $(a,b)$, and any dictionary of $q^{o(1)}$ positive integers fixed across its neighbours. For a distinct row $(x,y)$ put
$$t=(ay-bx)/q,\quad A=(a,x),\quad G=(b,y),\quad R=(|(ca-db)/q|,|(cx-dy)/q|).$$
Then $t\ne0$ is integral, and $RAG\mid t$. Indeed the two gaps are units at any prime of their own row products or of $d$, so $R$ is coprime to $AGd$. Eliminating $c$ gives $R\mid dt$. Also $AG\mid qt$ by both terms and $(AG,q)=1$. For any $J\ge1$, the number of neighbours with
$$ E\mid |t|/(RAG),\qquad |t|/(RAGE)\le J$$
for **some dictionary entry** $E$ is at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt J$.

Enumerate $A\mid a$, $G\mid b$, $R$ dividing the starting gap, and $E$ at subpower cost. If $(x/A)(y/G)\le J$, enumerate this smooth integer and split it, at cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt J$ by the half-power smooth count. Otherwise select a smooth divisor $f=f_x f_y$ in $(J/X,J]$ from this product, split between $x/A$ and $y/G$ (take $f=1$ for $J<X$). The choices cost the same. Write $x=A f_x z$, $y=G f_y v$. In these coordinates impose just
$$ (a/A)f_y v-(b/G)f_x z\equiv0\pmod {qRE}.$$
The coefficients are coprime as integers: Use $(a,b)=(x,y)=1$, $(a/A,x/A)=1$, and $(b/G,y/G)=1$. Thus the index is $qRE$ **even when $E$ shares primes with $RAG$**. It comes from the quotient of $qt$ itself, not by multiplying two possibly dependent congruence indices. The centred strip $|x|\le A_+$, $|ay-bx|\le qRAGEJ$ in $(z,v)$ has area divided by this index at most $4(A_+/a)J/f=q^{o(1)}$ in the nontrivial range $J\le q^{O(1)}$ (larger $J$ is unnecessary by $|t|\le W$). Use Pick's bound on the lattice hull if it spans the plane, or at most two primitive points on a line through zero otherwise. Here $(z,v)=1$ since $(x,y)=1$. Dividing out the **full** common input and output factors matters for the surjectivity argument.

The same count preserves the earlier [mask on row coordinates](rich-column-rows.md). Fix a set $S$ of $O(k)$ primes, let $m_x,m_y$ be the full $S$-parts of $x/A,y/G$, and replace the size test by $|t|\le RAGE m_xm_y J$, keeping $E\mid |t|/(RAG)$. Enumerate $m_x,m_y$ at subpower cost, and choose the filter or enumerate the small remaining product after dividing by these parts. With coordinate factors $m_x f_x,m_y f_y$ the coefficients are still coprime by the same four gcd tests, and the extra area cancels the two coordinate factors in the Jacobian. No divisibility of $t/(RAGE)$ by $m_xm_y$ is needed. For $S$ containing the primes of $ab$, $AGm_xm_y$ is precisely the old full coordinate mask. Thus this incorporates its size credit, rather than replacing it with just the gcds.

In a degree bin $D_s\le s<2D_s$, $D_s=q^\sigma$, $\sigma\ge .334-o(1)$, take $J=D_s^2q^{-\eta}$ for fixed small $\eta>0$. Excluding $q^{o(1)}D_sq^{-\eta/2}=o(s)$ neighbours per start leaves $(1-o(1))s^2$ constructed ordered two-row lists on the column, as before. In particular take $E$ to be the full part of $|t|/(RAG)$ on any one set $P$ of $O(k)$ primes named at the start, for example all primes of $cdab$ and the eligible primes of the starting gap. All such $E\le W$ form a subpower dictionary: On $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ primes even exponent sum $O(\log q)$ admits only $\exp(o(\log q))$ vectors. On the retained lists,
$$ (|t|/(RAG))_{P^c}>D_s^2q^{-\eta},\qquad RAGE<W q^\eta/D_s^2. $$
Unlike [thinning by $RG$](overlapping-lift-count.md), this rules out rich lists supported by large powers on named primes **inside the residual determinant**, without requiring them to divide a row or a gap. At $\sigma=.390+o(1)$ that quotient outside $P$ must itself reach $q^{.780-\eta+o(1)}$. This is an optional reduction for the edge objective, not a count of all the discarded lists on a fixed fiber.

## The two strips at a fixed list and denominator

Use $j,n,G,C_*,L_i,s,e,T=t/(Rj)$ exactly as in [lifted direction separation](lifted-direction-separation.md), now writing $s$ for its tested sine, not the column degree. Put $E=(|T|/G)_{\operatorname{supp}(d)}$, $b=e/(e,G)$, and $I=\operatorname{lcm}(E,b)=Ee/(EG,e)$. For one actual reference on the same list and $d$, at most $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ distinct $c$ satisfy either inequality
$$\frac{|\delta_1\delta_2|s}{R\min(R,G) C_* I}\le q^{.660},\qquad
\frac{|t|\min_i|\delta_i|}{R\min(R,G) C_* I}\le q^{1.050}.$$
With no reference use $s=e=1$. In particular the second test with $E=e=1$ restores the coefficient strip without buying a reference for it.

Here are the changes to the linked proof. Enumerate $E$ on the fixed support of $d$ as above, $e\mid d$, and $(e,G)\mid e$. Fix dyadic lower endpoints $D,g_0,K$ for $R,G,C_*$, upper bounds $S,\tau$ for $s,|t|$ as in the angle test (use $\tau<2|t|$ up to endpoint convention), and fix $j$. Let
$$ B=\min(U^2L_1L_2S/d,\ U\tau\min_i L_i/\min_i F_i),\qquad V=64 B/(D^2 j^2 I). $$
Any two tested vectors have determinant strictly smaller than $d I V$. For the first bound use their common scaled sector. For the second use $F_2 n_1-F_1 n_2=dT$: The area scaling in $(n_1,T)$ is $F_1/d$, and in $(n_2,T)$ is $F_2/d$. Use $|n_i|\le 2UL_i/(Dj)$ and $|T|\le\tau/(Dj)$. The two widths can be intersected without translating a primitive vector.

As before choose $f=\gamma m_1m_2\ge V$ from $GM$ with $\gamma\mid G$, $m_i\mid n_i$ (one if $V\le1$). The capped repeat filter applies unchanged since $V/(\ell_1\ell_2)\le q^{-.275+o(1)}$. On the coordinate-multiple lattice the form $F_2n_1-F_1n_2$ is zero modulo $dE\gamma$ and has that index: The coefficients after substitution are units at primes of $dE$ and of $\gamma$. The reference determinant is zero modulo $de$ with index $de$ there. Without a reference omit this second condition. The ambient intersection index is at least
$$ d m_1m_2\operatorname{lcm}(E\gamma,e)\ \ge\ df I,$$
since $(E\gamma,e)\mid(EG,e)$. This leaves at most two primitive parallel vectors per filter. Do not multiply the two indices. Recover $G\mid |T|$ afterwards. Use the original **single** output congruence for the scalar, at cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{\max(1,D/g_0)}$.

The direction price still includes $1+\sqrt{V/K}+V^{1/4}$. Summing over smooth $j<q^{.115}$, the constant and saturation costs are no larger than before (exponents $.2525$ and $.26375$). The middle term costs $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{B/(D\min(D,g_0)KI)}$. In a block meeting the respective cut its exponent is at most $.665-.5275+.660/2$ or $(.665-.780+1.050)/2$, both $.4675$. The innermost $S=q^{-10}$ band is already cheap. Summing the two cuts does not change the exponent. One reference per occupied $d$ still costs $.995$ in the list exponent, not an ordered-pair bound.

**Overlap and remaining test.** Agreement is not a product of free credits. For $E=1$ the second bound can also be obtained by picking an actual reference *inside each nonempty block*: For two members the determinant strip gives scaled sine $\ll q^{-.390+o(1)}\tau/\max L_i$ by the cofactor lower bounds $h_i=q^{.665+o(1)}$ on both. Their mutual determinant remains zero modulo $de$ because both vectors are proportional to the given reference modulo $de$ (its second coordinate is a unit). The direct coefficient proof avoids needing those witnesses. Nor should one budget the entire no-reference full-width box as a gain on rich lists. For $R=G=C_*=1$, $|\delta_i|=q^{.390+o(1)}$, either no-reference cut forces $|t|/E\le q^{.660+o(1)}$ (use $|t|\le W$ for the first). With $P=\operatorname{supp}(d)$, the new thinning already excludes this for $\eta<.008$, since $A$ and $j$ are units on $d$. This does not exclude the entire clean fiber; endpoints with large determinant outside the mask can miss both cuts. Test joint eligible outputs there, not how many $E$ can be enumerated on the known support. No density or $.998$ unrestricted bound follows.
