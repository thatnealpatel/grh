# Count nonempty lifts in a translated gap square

Use the two ordered row positions of the $g=1$ [shared-label test](shared-row-labels.md). After fixing the first realization and the two second-column cofactors $h_i=x_i/F_i$, prescribe **any translated square** of radius $V=q^\nu$ for the two raw signed gaps $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ of the second column. Its center may depend on all data fixed so far. Uniformly for $1\le V\le 2H$, the number of actual second reconstructions in this square, **with the $h_i$ fixed**, is at most

$$q^{o(1)} V.$$

This counts nonempty reconstructions, not a subpower saving on an individual lift. A translated square need not enjoy the centred primitive-point bound: Many primitive points can lie on an affine line. Here the saving from $V^2$ to $V$ comes from averaging the possible line spacings over smooth auxiliary moduli. Neither gap primitivity nor a smoothness assumption on independent outputs is needed.

**Affine square lemma.** Put $A=x_2/(x_1,x_2)$, $B=x_1/(x_1,x_2)$ on the second column. We have $A\ge F_2>W$ since the second label is taken outside the support of the first row, hence $(F_2,x_1)=1$. We may cancel the gcd modulo $d$ since $(x_1,d)=1$. The actual gap point $(a,b)$ satisfies $d\mid Aa-Bb\ne0$ by $x_2\delta_1-x_1\delta_2=dt$ and $t\ne0$. Choose a smooth $e\mid d$ with $8V^2<e\le8XV^2$; $Z/X>32XH^2$ for large $q$. The total number of pairs $(e,(a,b))$ in the prescribed square with $e\mid Aa-Bb$ is $q^{o(1)}V$, as we now show. The center does not enter this sum bound.

There are $q^{o(1)}V$ smooth $e$ at this scale. For each, the lattice has index $e$ by coprimality. Any two differences of points in the square have determinant of absolute value at most $8V^2$, so all the points are collinear. For at least $m+1$ points, $m\ge1$, two consecutive ones have nonzero difference $w$ of sup norm at most $2V/m$ (order along the coordinate of greatest variation). For $m\le2V$ the number of such $e$ is $q^{o(1)}V/m$. Here are the details needed for a **nonprimitive** difference: Put $w=jv$ with $j>0$, $v$ primitive, and $k=(e,j)\le 2V/m$. Then $e/k\mid Av_1-Bv_2\ne0$: The primitive exact direction $(B,A)$ is longer than $2V$. For $m\ge2$, choose a smooth divisor $u$ of $e/k$ strictly greater than $2(2V/(mk))^2$ and at most $X$ times that threshold. It exists by $e>8V^2$, and the threshold is at least two. For fixed $k,u$, the primitive $v$ lies in the **centred** square of radius $2V/(mk)$ and is confined by the determinant bound to at most one direction up to sign. Thus there are $q^{o(1)}V/(mk)$ choices of $u$, then subpowerly many $e/k$ from the nonzero linear form. Sum over $k$ using the harmonic bound $\sum_{k\le 2V}1/k=O(\log q)$. For $m=1$ just use the total number of $e$; $m>2V$ is impossible. Summing this tail bound costs only a logarithm and proves the claimed pair count.

For each actual gap point recover $d$ among divisors of its nonzero linear form. Recover $c$ from $cx_1\equiv qa\pmod d$ at cost $O(1+Z/d)=O(X)$, and then both $y_i$ are forced. The $q^{o(1)}V$ bound follows, including at gap gcd one. In particular this is a count *before* selecting a direction or enumerating a positive lift multiplicity.

**Pair prices.** Per first incidence the two smooth $h_i$ cost $q^{.665+o(1)}$. Even a dictionary of $q^{\rho+o(1)}$ such square centers, specified after the $h_i$, costs in total at most $q^{.665+\rho+\nu+o(1)}$ second realizations per first. The unweighted subcount is $q^{2.500+\rho+\nu+o(1)}$: One arbitrary translated square of radius $q^{.165}$ thus costs $2.665$, with **no restriction on $S$ or $T_1T_2$**. For example center it at the first raw gap pair, or at $(\delta_{11} h_{21}/h_{22},\delta_{21} h_{11}/h_{12})$ where subscripts on $\delta,h$ are row, column. The latter tests proximity of the two signed product pairs $(\delta_1h_2,\delta_2h_1)$: Since each $h_i=q^{.665+o(1)}$, a product square of radius $q^{.830}$ about the first pair fits in a raw gap square of radius $q^{.165+o(1)}$. Restore either column order at constant cost.

In the degree/popularity bins of [the weighted reduction](overlapping-lift-count.md), there are at most $q^{1.055+2\sigma+o(1)}$ first incidences, including their lists. Dividing the resulting pair bound by $DM$ gives the exponent $1.720+\sigma+\rho+\nu-\mu$. Thus $\rho+\nu\le\mu-\sigma-.3315$ prices this part by $1.3885$. At $(\sigma,\mu)=(.390,1.055)$ one can use a square of radius $q^{.3335}$ at this price, or equivalently product radius $q^{.9985+o(1)}$ with the subpower distortion just described. Alternatively $\rho+\nu<\mu-.665$ by a fixed margin makes the dictionary loss $o(MI)$, since its per-first cost is $o(M)$. Neither use pays for every edge on a column having a nearby partner. Tiling the entire raw gap square at scale $V$ requires $\asymp(1+H/V)^2$ centers, not $H/V$: The affine estimate does not save on the full square over its $H$ price. The substantive remaining question is which squares actually contain jointly smooth reconstructions.

**Rectangular version.** For axis-parallel half-widths $1\le V_1,V_2\le2H$ the cost is $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{V_1V_2}$, even if the aspect ratio grows as a power of $q$. Use only $e$ coprime to $AB$: Actual $e\mid d$ have this property since both $x_i$ are coprime to $d$. Take $8V_1V_2<e\le8XV_1V_2$. Collinearity still follows by the difference-determinant bound. A line containing two points cannot be horizontal or vertical since a nonzero axis difference must have length at least $e>2\max(V_1,V_2)$. Along the line of $m+1$ points at least one consecutive difference has $|w_i|\le2V_i/m$ **in both coordinates**, by taking the shortest gap along that line. Both coordinates are nonzero; in particular $k\le j\le 2\min(V_1,V_2)/m$. Now choose $u\mid e/k$ crossing $8V_1V_2/(mk)^2\ge2$, strictly less than $e/k$. The centred difference rectangle for primitive $v$ has determinant bound at most this threshold, so again costs at most two vectors per $u$. The number of $u$ is at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{V_1V_2}/(mk)$; the exact kernel direction remains too long since $A>4H$. The same two harmonic sums, singleton count, and reconstruction prove the claim. For example, at the top weighted scales it suffices to restrict just **one** raw gap to a prescribed interval of radius $q^{.277}$: Leaving the other unrestricted gives mean half-width $(q^{.277}H)^{1/2}=q^{.3335+o(1)}$. This covers a pair subcount, not an assertion that most second columns have a gap in that interval.

## Excluding short label ratios in the complement modulus

There is also a cut on **nonempty realizations** that does not involve $S,T,r$, or $G$. For a fixed list $(F_1,F_2)$ put

$$\Lambda_d=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:F_2a\equiv F_1b\pmod d\}.$$

Count a column if this lattice contains any nonzero vector of sup norm at most $L=q^\kappa$. For fixed $0<\kappa<.5275$, the number of such columns on the list is at most $q^{.5275+\kappa+o(1)}$. The vector need not be primitive, or come from either selected row. The bound counts moduli **admitting** a short ratio, not just the number of lifts per ratio. No independent smoothness heuristic enters.

**Modulus lemma.** More generally fix coprime integers $A,B$ of size $q^{O(1)}$ with $\max(|A|,|B|)>L$. Count smooth $Z/X<d\le Z$ admitting $Aw_1\equiv Bw_2\pmod d$ with $0<\|w\|_\infty\le L$. Write $w=jv$ with $j>0$ the content and $v$ primitive. Put $k=(d,j)$, $d'=d/k$. Then $d'\mid N=Av_1-Bv_2$, and $N\ne0$: The primitive exact kernel direction is $(B,A)$ up to sign. Also $\|v\|_\infty\le L/k$. Fix the smooth $k\le L$. Since $d'>Z/(Xk)>4(L/k)^2$, multiplying eligible prime factors of $d'$ up to the first crossing gives a divisor

$$4(L/k)^2< e\le4X(L/k)^2.$$

The lattice $Av_1\equiv Bv_2\pmod e$ has index $e$ by coprimality. Two independent vectors in the square of radius $L/k$ would have nonzero determinant divisible by $e$ and at most $2(L/k)^2$ in absolute value. Thus at most two primitive vectors are possible per $e$. The half-power smooth count gives at most $q^{o(1)}L/k$ choices of $e$, and then $d'$ is a divisor of the nonzero polynomial-height integer $N$. The divisor bound and $\sum_{k\text{ smooth}}1/k\le\prod_{p\le X}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}=q^{o(1)}$ prove that there are at most $q^{o(1)}L$ moduli. Omitting the $k$ reduction would not bound a lattice minimum: Dividing a vector by its content need not preserve its congruence.

Apply the lemma with $A=F_2,B=F_1$, both larger than $W=q^{.780+o(1)}$. Each counted $d$ allows at most $q^{.5275+o(1)}$ smooth $c\le Z$. Each actual column realizes the fixed list at most once by row identification. In particular this argument tests all values of $r$, including $r=1$ where little can be saved on a lift that already exists.

**Paying for the cut.** In a degree bin $D=q^\sigma$ there are at most $q^{.780+o(1)}$ lists and the weight per incidence is at most $1/D$. Short-ratio incidences at $\kappa=.410$ cost at most $q^{1.3835+o(1)}$ uniformly in $\sigma\ge.334-o(1)$. At $\sigma=.390+o(1)$ the incidence exponent is $.9175+\kappa$, below $1.390$ for fixed $\kappa<.4725$. Charge these incidences, not all edges on a column meeting any such list.

One can instead discard **pairs** in the [degree/popularity test](overlapping-lift-count.md). With $M=q^\mu$ and $I=\sum m$ there, the number of pairs short on either side is $O(Iq^{.5275+\kappa+o(1)})$. At $\kappa=.410$ this is $o(MI)$, since $\mu\ge.608+\sigma-o(1)\ge.942-o(1)$. The reciprocal freshness and earlier thinnings are unaffected: The lower bound $(1-o(1))MI$ survives removing these pairs. In the unweighted rectangle count the same argument with at most $q^{1.835+o(1)}$ first incidences gives $q^{2.3625+\kappa+o(1)}$ on this subset, hence $2.665$ for $\kappa=.3025$. Neither comparison gives the cost of the rest.

The pair bound also allows a dictionary chosen **after the first realization but before the second**. Use at most $q^{\rho+o(1)}$ reduced coefficient pairs $(A,B)$ satisfying the modulus lemma, and discard a second $d$ if any such lattice has a short vector. The loss is $o(MI)$ when $\rho+\kappa<\mu-.5275$ by a fixed margin, even after symmetrizing. For example one entry can be the reduced ratio $A:B=x_{21}:x_{11}$ of the first column's two row numerators (indices are row, column): $F_2\mid x_{21}$ and $(F_2,x_{11})=1$ by construction, so $A\ge F_2$. Use the **reduced** form in the congruence; canceling a nonunit modulo the second $d$ is not licensed. At the top popularity exponent any fixed $\rho+\kappa<.5275$ is allowed. This latter threshold is for a negligible fraction of pairs, not a direct edge-weight bound of $q^{1.390}$ on the discarded pairs.

**What constraint must the remaining count use?** Write $U=A_+/W$, so $B_0=HU=Z/(2X)<d/2$. The actual pair $(a,b)=(\delta_1 h_2,\delta_2 h_1)$ lies in $\Lambda_d$ by the row equations, with each coordinate of size at most $B_0$. Both are units modulo $d$ since $c,x_i,q$ are. For fixed labels and $d$, let $f(z)$ count signed products $\delta h\equiv z\pmod d$, $0<|\delta|\le H$, $1\le h\le U$ eligible-smooth, restricted to units. Each $z$ has at most one signed representative here and subpowerly many splits. The mass and squared norm are at most $q^{.7225+o(1)}$ by the smooth count for $h$. Put $\lambda=F_2/F_1\pmod d$. The correlation $\sum_z f(z) f(\lambda z)$ bounds the number of actual realizations at this $d$ up to $O(X)$: Splitting the products supplies both gaps and both $h_i$; then $cx_1\equiv q\delta_1\pmod d$ allows $O(X)$ values of $c$ and the $y_i$ are forced. This is an upper test, with several smoothness conditions dropped. The earlier short lattice vector is not asserted to be this product pair.

Here is a barrier to using only nonresonance, size, support interval, and bounded weights. Fix any admissible $d$ with $\lambda_1(\Lambda_d)>q^{.410}$ in sup norm. There is a common zero-one function on units in the same signed interval, with mass $n+O(1)$ for $n=\lfloor q^{.7225}\rfloor$, whose correlation with its $\lambda$-dilate is at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

Indeed a planar lattice of determinant $D_0$ and shortest Euclidean length $s$ has $4B_0^2/D_0+O(1+B_0/s)$ points in the square of radius $B_0$. For a proof, parallel lines in the shortest-vector direction have spacing $h_0=D_0/s\ge\sqrt{3}s/2$ (reduce the second basis vector along the first). Count the points along each intersecting line by its slice length divided by $s$, with $O(1)$ error per line. The slice-length function extended by zero has variation $O(B_0)$ by convexity, so its sum on the $h_0$ grid differs from its integral divided by $h_0$ by $O(B_0)$. This proves the formula, including boundary lines. For each prime $p\mid d$, requiring $p\mid a$ cuts out a sublattice of index $p$ in $\Lambda_d$, since projection to $a$ is surjective; it then forces $p\mid b$. The same length lower bound holds on each sublattice. Subtract their counts by a union bound. Here $\sum_{p\mid d}1/p=o(1)$ since the eligible primes are at least $X/(\log\log q)^4$ and $d$ has $O(k)$ factors. Thus the square contains $\gg B_0^2/d=q^{1.055+o(1)}$ unit pairs: The total error $O((1+\omega(d))(1+B_0/q^{.410}))$ is smaller. These are edges $a\mapsto\lambda a$ of a partial permutation, because $2B_0<d$. There are no unit loops since $\lambda=1$ would give the lattice vector $(1,1)$. Use a matching of $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ vertex-disjoint edges: Each greedy choice deletes at most four directed edges since in- and out-degrees are at most one. Their endpoints give the claimed function.

The witness depends on $d$ and is **not** a smooth-product weight. It rules out a per-modulus gain from the relaxed constraints, not an averaged gain or the arithmetic conjecture. The remaining test is to bound occupancy of these nonresonant ratios by the actual $\delta h$ products, or to impose the forced $c,y_1,y_2$ smoothness before taking that correlation. The unrestricted large-$S$, all-gcd, and fixed-window conclusions remain open.
