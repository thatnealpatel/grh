# Separate simultaneous outputs inside a gap-rank packet

Fix one actual constructed two-label list at $g=R=j=1$, with the graph, heights, and signed equations of [the gap-walk count](cofactor-walk-rank.md). Work in any subgraph at fixed $d$, in particular after deleting already paid columns. In addition to the gap product of a three-step walk, use its positive output product $p=\prod(y_{\rm from}/y_{\rm to})=n/w$ in lowest terms. Fix *one actual reference walk* at each occupied pair of endpoints, with output $p_0=n_0/w_0$. Write $S_0$ for the primes of $n_0w_0$, and $z_S$ for the full $S$-part of $z$.

**Packet test.** If there are more than $q^{.467}$ columns at this $d$, some endpoint pair admits a packet with all the following properties. Put $K_g=\lfloor(\log q)^{1.03}\rfloor$, $K_y=\lfloor(\log q)^{1.021}\rfloor$.

* Besides the reference there are $K_g+1$ actual three-step walks with independent gap valuation vectors after subtracting the reference vector.
* In each consecutive block of size $K_y+1$ (partition the witnesses in order, with a possibly shorter last block), the **output** valuation vectors after subtracting the same reference vector are linearly independent as well.
* Every new output satisfies, against the reference and also against **each earlier witness** used in its place,

$$\frac{|nw_0-wn_0|}{q}\frac{n}{n_0}>q^{.138}(nw)_{S_0}.$$

The last cut does not require a rank hypothesis. In particular the common modular residue and the real approximation are used to price a set of simultaneous successes, not treated as proving low rank. No assertion is made that most columns are in those priced sets.

## Two different recoveries on the same walks

Use gap-clean walks as in the linked note, and in addition require at each step that the far output $y_j$ have part $<q^{.930}$ on the primes of all earlier outputs in the walk. The latter requirement costs at most $q^{.0625+o(1)}$ bad extensions, uniformly at a current vertex. Indeed even without the current vertex, fix the old support and the far position $j$. It has only $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ old primes at bounded length. Enumerate the offending full part $m$ of $y_j$ at subpower cost (the multiplicity sum is $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ for eligible primes). In $y_j/m$ choose a factor $f$ by crossing $2ZUH/(dm)$ strictly, using $1$ below one. This is possible since $d y_j=q^{2.500+o(1)}>2ZUH=q^{2.110+o(1)}$. Enumerate $f$ **before** $ch_j$. The overshoot is at most $X$ for thresholds at least one, so the smooth half-power bound costs $q^{.0625+o(1)}$ since $2ZUH/(dm)\le q^{.125+o(1)}$. The equation $F_j(ch_j)=q\delta_j\bmod dmf$ implies that $dmf$ divides the cross determinant of any two pairs $(ch_j,\delta_j)$ ($q$ is a unit). Its size is at most $2ZUH<dmf$. These pairs are primitive with positive first coordinate, since $(cF_jh_j,dy_j)=1$. Thus one pair suffices per choice. Split $ch_j$ at divisor cost; $c,d$ already specify the column and the list specifies its rows. This is an upper bound, not a replacement for the sorted-prefix construction.

Conversely, fix an exact $p$ and the final vertex $h_j$. Any far-output prime of the last step which is not old and disappears in the reduced $p$ must have its entire far exponent cancelled by the near output. Its contribution divides $(y_i,y_j)<q^{.115}$, from the freshness of the second row on the first row's support in the original list, in either traversal direction. Hence the full part $m'$ of $y_j$ on $\operatorname{supp}(nw)$ is at least $q^{1.445-.930-.115-o(1)}=q^{.400-o(1)}$. Enumerate this part at subpower cost on the known support; it need not divide $nw$. The primitive pairs $(c,\delta_j)$ satisfy $F_jh_j c=q\delta_j\bmod dm'$, and $dm'>2ZH$ since $.400>.390$. The same determinant argument recovers at most one pair per choice. Knowing the column recovers its rows, the previous vertex, and its exact traversal ratio. Peel the clean prefix in the same way. Thus each output product costs at most $q^{o(1)}$ such paths at the endpoints, **including** parallel edges. Independently the gap cleanliness costs $q^{o(1)}$ forbidden extensions at a fixed current vertex and gives the same multiplicity bound for an exact signed gap product. Output cleanliness alone was not used for gap peeling.

Write $E$ for the column count and $N=q^{.3325+o(1)}$ for the vertex bound per side. Prune degrees below $E/(4N)$ as in the gap-walk count. For $E>q^{.467}$, the surviving minimum degree dominates the sum of the two outgoing deletion prices. Starting on either surviving left-to-right edge (that is, using any such first edge) and extending twice gives $\gg E^3/N^2$ doubly clean walks. Some endpoint pair therefore has $\gg E^3/N^4\ge q^{.071-o(1)}$ of them. All subsequent upper bounds are pointwise at these endpoints, so no sum over reference paths is needed.

## The one-reference bracket price, without a rank condition

At fixed endpoints all $p$ are congruent to the ratio of endpoint $F_i h_i$ modulo $q$ and differ relatively from it by $O(qH/(\min c\, A_-))=q^{-1.110+o(1)}$. This follows by substituting $dy_i=cF_i h_i-q\delta_i$ at every traversal; the input ratios telescope. In particular $q\mid nw_0-wn_0$. For any $1\le T\le q^{O(1)}$, the number of doubly clean paths at the endpoints satisfying

$$ |nw_0-wn_0|\, n/(q n_0)\le T(nw)_{S_0} $$

is at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$, uniformly in the actual reference. To see this fix the full $S_0$-parts of $n,w$ (subpower choices) with product $m$, and localize $n$ dyadically. If $nw/m\le 5T$, enumerate the eligible-smooth remainder, including its split, at the claimed price. Otherwise select from that remainder a divisor strictly crossing $5T$, at most $5TX$, and enumerate the divisor $f$ and its split between $n,w$ **before** either coordinate. For two candidates $(n_i,w_i)$ in this bin the bracket inequalities, subtracted after multiplying by the other numerator, give

$$ |n_1w_2-n_2w_1|/q\le Tm(n_1/n_2+n_2/n_1)\le4Tm.$$

But $mf$ divides this quotient, since the signed split into numerator and denominator is common and all its primes are units modulo $q$. Hence the two reduced fractions agree. Count the choices by the smooth half-power bound, and apply output peeling. This price permits the reference to have very different reduced height from that of the candidate. It uses one reference fixed before counting, not a bound on the sum over all possible reference choices. By the relative approximation, the left side of the bracket test is at most $q^{-2.110+o(1)}nw$. Thus on the unpriced side $nw/(nw)_{S_0}\ge q^{2.248-o(1)}$. Large fresh product is **necessary**, not sufficient, to escape the cut.

## Counting either low-rank translate

For outputs at fixed endpoints, the absolute determinant of two reduced fractions divided by $q$ is bounded by $Q_y=q^{6.560+o(1)}$: Use the relative discrepancy $q^{-1.110+o(1)}$, the four coordinate bounds $A_+^3$, and divide by $q$. For gaps, each path is a signed reduced fraction with numerator and denominator of size at most $H^3$ and a common residue at the endpoints modulo $d$. Use $Q_g=2H^6/d=q^{1.285+o(1)}$ for the divided determinant. Take actual upper bounds in both cutoffs, not rounded powers. All label primes are coprime to their respective modulus (for the gaps this follows from row primitivity).

Consider products whose valuations lie in a fixed affine translate of dimension $\le K$ at this pair, separately for the two kinds of labels. Choose at most $K$ pivot primes giving injective projection on the direction space and hence on the translate. Project **path** valuations, not reference quotients. If their absolute pivot product $\prod p^{|e_p|}$ is at most $Q$, the number is bounded, apart from sign, by $Q^\sigma\prod_p(1+2/(p^\sigma-1))$. If it is larger, take a signed pivot-factor prefix strictly crossing $Q$, in fixed order with multiplicities. Its pre-crossing part has the same count and the last signed prime costs at most $2K$. A common signed prefix means fixed factors in numerator and denominator whose product exceeds $Q$. It divides the determinant even after division by the modulus, forcing exact equality. This high case uses actual path labels and does not require an overshoot bound on the last gap prime.

For outputs take $K=K_y$, $\sigma=.021/2+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$. All pivot primes that are needed can be taken eligible (take the direction span of actual reference quotients here). They are $\ge X/(\log\log q)^4$, so the logarithm of the Euler factor is $o(\log q)$. This costs $q^{.06888+o(1)}$ labels. For gaps take $K=K_g$, $\sigma=3/103+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$ and dominate the sum of inverse prime powers at exponent $\sigma$ by $\sum_{2\le j\le K+1}j^{-\sigma}=o(\log q)$. This costs $q^{1.285(3/103)+o(1)}<q^{.038}$ labels for large $q$, including both signs. The respective peeling bounds convert both counts to counts of doubly clean walks.

These prices also improve the original conditional statements. If every occupied pair has gap rank at most $K_g$, the pruning lower bound $E^3/(q^{o(1)}N^4)$ is at most $q^{1.285(3/103)+o(1)}$, so summing over $d$ costs $q^{.9833091+o(1)}$ per list (exponent rounded up). With output rank at most $K_y$ instead, the corresponding price is $q^{.993794+o(1)}$. In both arguments one can discard $E\le q^{.400}$ first, so $E/N$ dominates the forward output-cleaning price. Neither rank restriction follows from the congruences.

## Greedy choice and limit

At the rich endpoint pair keep its one actual reference fixed. At each choice exclude paths in the gap translate through the reference spanned by the preceding gap quotients, and in the output translate through it spanned by the preceding output quotients **within the current block**. Restart only the output span when its block is filled. The dimensions before each choice are at most $K_g,K_y$, respectively. Also apply the bracket deletion with $T=q^{.138}$ separately at the reference and every preceding witness. These at most $K_g+1$ prices are paid, not identified with a single price. The total excluded is $\le q^{.069+o(1)}<q^{.071-o(1)}$. The selected path increases both required ranks and meets all the directed bracket cuts. The argument makes no claim about the bracket with the two roles reversed.

Removing the union costs at most $3(K_g+2)$ columns. Repeating across denominators leaves at most $q^{.9945+o(1)}$ columns per list below the $q^{.467}$ threshold; thus the unrestricted $.998$ target only requires pricing disjoint packets of this stronger type, not all high gap ranks. The same witnesses can be used in the [same-input enrichment](ordered-output-masks.md) with its original peeling budget. This strengthens the necessary simultaneous-success configuration but does **not** bound the number of packets. A second, pointwise [attachment test](same-input-second-bracket.md) uses the fixed first input to price a normalized second-row bracket. The remaining count should use the large *divided brackets* jointly with the shared denominator or with the second successful row at an attached fixed complete input. Counting output pivot sets over the whole eligible universe, or forgetting the real widths after projecting modulo $q$, does not price that residual.
