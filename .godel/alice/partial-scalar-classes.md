# What a partial scalar residue buys inside a determinant class

Fix a [constructed two-label list](shared-row-labels.md) at $g=1$ and an occupied $d$. At $R=j=1$ put $A=h_1h_2$, $x_i=F_i h_i$, $n=(h_2\delta_1,h_1\delta_2)$, and $F_2n_1-F_1n_2=dt$. Thus $|t|\le W=q^{.780+o(1)}$, $|n_i|\le B=UH=q^{1.055+o(1)}$, and $2B<d$. Choose coprime ordered [output seeds](matched-output-primes.md) $f_i\mid y_i$ crossing $q^{.120}$, the second from outside the full support of $y_1$. Write $e=f_1f_2=q^{.240+o(1)}$, $k=cA\bmod e$. The seed menu costs $q^{.120+o(1)}$; it is not a menu for $t\bmod e$.

**Partial scalar bound.** Prescribe the seeds, $t\equiv\tau\pmod e$, a divisor $b\mid e$, and **only** $k\equiv\kappa\pmod b$. The number of actual numerators is at most
$$q^{.4075+o(1)}/\sqrt b.$$
In particular prescribing $k\bmod f_1$ costs $.3475$ in the exponent, and prescribing all of $k$ costs $.2875$. Even allowing every lift of a prescribed residue modulo $f_1$ gives the first price: It is not the sum of $f_2$ full-class prices. This interpolation uses a single affine lattice, not independent bounds on two marginals.

Indeed $q n_i\equiv F_i k\pmod{f_i}$ by the two row equations, and $(F_i,f_i)=1$. Modulo $(b,f_1)$ the first coordinate is known; the equation for $dt$ determines the second using the unit $F_1$. Modulo $(b,f_2)$ the roles reverse. Hence $n\equiv\rho\pmod b$ for a single vector $\rho$. Write $n=\rho+bz$, $t=\tau+es$, with $0\le\tau<e$ and $|s|\le N=1+W/e$. Then
$$F_2 z_1-F_1 z_2=C_0+d(e/b)s,\qquad C_0=(d\tau-F_2\rho_1+F_1\rho_2)/b.$$
The division is exact. In coordinates $(z_1,s)$ the difference lattice has index $F_1$, even if $(e,d)>1$. In the original $n$-plane the coset has index $deb$: Without the scalar condition the $t$ class has index $de$ since $(F_1,F_2)=1$, and at fixed $t$ one step $(F_1,F_2)$ increments the relaxed scalar $k$ by $q$ modulo $b$, a unit. These index statements concern relaxed congruences, not successful lifts.

## Sieve in the translated box

Here is the proof of the count and a second use where the modulus can meet $d$. In both cases $V_0=B^2/d$ denotes the relative area of the *original* centered box. In the second use take the [usual normalized directions](translated-determinant-progressions.md): In a block $D\le R<2D$, fix $j$, take $n=(\ell_2 v_1,\ell_1 v_2)$, $\ell_i=h_i/j$, $v_i=\delta_i/R$, $A=\ell_1\ell_2$, $B=UH/(Dj)$. The physical $n$ is ambient primitive with nonzero coordinates prime to $d$, $2B<d$, and $V_0/A\le q^{-.275+o(1)}$. The first use is its $R=j=1$ specialization.

1. **Partial scalar.** Use the chart $(z_1,s)$ above, of half-widths $(B/b,N)$, and put $V=(B/b)N/F_1$. This is $q^{.815+o(1)}/b$. In difference coordinates let $w_2=(F_2w_1-d(e/b)w_s)/F_1$. The physical differences are $b(w_1,w_2)$.
2. **Ordinary vector residue.** Prescribe $n\equiv\rho\pmod b$ with no determinant restriction beyond the base congruence. Use the chart $(z_1,z_2)$ for $n=\rho+bz$, of half-widths $(B/b,B/b)$. The base difference lattice is $F_2w_1\equiv F_1w_2\pmod{d'}$, where $d'=d/(d,b)$. Thus $V=(B/b)^2/d'=V_0(d,b)/b^2$, **not** always $V_0/b^2$. We use this chart only for $Dj\ll q^{.120}$ and $1\le b\ll X q^{.120}/(Dj)$, with $X=q^{o(1)}$ the largest eligible prime bound.

In both charts the count of $n$ including splits is $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$. Here are the checks for transferring the [affine direction sieve](translated-determinant-progressions.md) to these coordinates. In the first chart mask the eligible primes of $e$, and in the second those of $b$. For each chart with half-widths $(L_1,L_2)$ there is at most one base-primitive difference direction up to sign in the box of radii $L_i/(2\sqrt V)$, since the determinant of two such vectors is at most half the base index. Also mask its $w_1w_2$ when nonzero, with $w_2$ the second *spatial* coordinate, not the parameter $w_s$. The mask has subpower-many polynomial-height supported products by exponent-vector counting. In the first chart $V\ge q^{.575-o(1)}$ and in the second $V\ge q^{.815-o(1)}$; $1\ll V\le V_0$ for large $q$.

If the masked part of $A$ reaches $V_0/V$, cross this level first with masked factors, then extend past $64V_0$ using the remaining factors of $A$. The first seed fits since $X\ll V$; the final outside-mask part is $O(XV)$. Enumerate the final divisor and its split for $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$ by the half-power smooth count. In original centered $n$ coordinates the split gives lattice index $df$ for final divisor $f$, because its factors are units mod $d$. Two vectors have determinant at most $2B^2<df$, leaving at most two ambient primitive vectors. This branch does not claim an additional congruence index from masked primes.

Otherwise the unmasked part exceeds $AV/V_0\gg64V$. Cross $64V$ strictly with an unmasked $f=u_1u_2$, $u_1\mid\ell_2$, $u_2\mid\ell_1$ coprime. For this split impose $u_i\mid n_i$. The difference index in the chosen chart is the base index times $f$, also for any subfilter: $b$ is a unit at $f$, so differences require $u_i\mid w_i$. In chart 1 write $w_1=u_1 a$; the residual congruence is $F_2u_1 a\equiv d(e/b) w_s\pmod{F_1u_2}$. Its coefficients and modulus have gcd one, by $(F_1,F_2)=1$, $(d,F_1f)=1$, and $(e,f)=1$. Chart 2 follows by substitution modulo $d'$ since the $u_i$ and $F_i$ are units there. Translations change neither calculation for nonempty filters. The filtered points are collinear since a determinant of two differences is at most eight times the product of the half-widths. Singleton filters cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$ in total.

On a nonsingleton line let $w$ be primitive in the base difference lattice. Neither spatial coordinate vanishes: In chart 1 a zero coordinate forces the other to be a multiple of $d$; in chart 2 it forces a multiple of $d'$, and $2B/b<d'$ since $(b,d)\le b$. The filtered step is $a w$ where $a=f/((u_1,w_1)(u_2,w_2))$. On the masked exceptional direction $a=f$. There cannot be $f$ base steps along a run in this direction:

* In chart 1 the run has at most $2N$ steps if $w_s\ne0$, and at most $2B/(b F_1)$ otherwise. Both are $\le2V$, since $B/(b F_1)\ge q^{.035-o(1)}$ and $N\ge1$.
* In chart 2 any nonzero direction has spatial sup norm at least $\min(F_1,d'/(F_1+F_2))$ by distinguishing zero and nonzero values of $F_2 w_1-F_1 w_2$. The run divided by $2V$ is therefore at most $\max(d'b/(BF_1),b(F_1+F_2)/B)=o(1)$ at the stated widths. A short exceptional line is not being counted by area alone.

For completeness, on an ordinary line with $m+1\ge2$ points, $ma\le4\sqrt V$ by nonexceptionality. Dyadically fix $M\le m<2M$. Cross $8V/(Ma)^2$ strictly with a split divisor of $f/a$ from the two coordinate gcds (use one below unit threshold). This fits since $f>64V$. There are $q^{o(1)}(1+\sqrt V/(Ma))$ choices. The split fixes $w$ up to sign: Its index beats the determinant bound in the centered difference box of half-widths $2L_i/(Ma)$. Recover $f/a$ from divisors of $|w_1w_2|\ne0$ and the full split at divisor cost. For each filter the single line costs $O(M)$ points in this block. The resulting $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V/a$ is summable with subpower loss over $a$ and dyadic $M$. Finally each physical $n$ has subpower-many cofactor and signed-gap splits by division. At $R=j=1$ the first row fixes $c\bmod d$, so the scan of $O(X)$ shifts, retaining only actual two-row completions, proves the partial scalar bound.

## A paid one-reference deletion with squarefree steps allowed

Fix one actual normalized vector $n^0$ at each occupied $d$, not necessarily in the tested block. For $n\ne n^0$ let $P$ be the full eligible part of $\gcd(|n_1-n_1^0|,|n_2-n_2^0|)$. The columns with $n=n^0$ or
$$ RjP\ge q^{.120} $$
cost at most $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ per $d$, hence $q^{.995+o(1)}$ per list. This tests *ordinary coordinate differences*; neither equality of determinant classes nor a cube divisor is required. The primes can be squarefree and need not come from either output seed.

Indeed put $j$ in a dyadic block $J\le j<2J$. Large $D\ge q^{.121}$ is already paid by the empty-mask bound. Otherwise if $DJ\ge q^{.120}/4$ the centered bound $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{V_0}$ per $j$, times the [scalar price](delayed-output-seeds.md) $q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$ and $q^{o(1)}\sqrt J$ choices, suffices. For smaller $DJ$, cross $Y=q^{.120}/(4DJ)$ with $b\mid P$, so $Y\le b\le XY$. Chart 2 with $\rho=n^0$ costs $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{V_0}\sqrt{(b,d)}/b$ per $b,j$. To sum, fix $g'=(b,d)$ at divisor cost and count smooth $b/g'\le XY/g'$ in at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{XY/g'}$ ways. Thus even with denominator overlap the full menu costs $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{V_0/Y}$ per $j$. Including $\sqrt J$ and $\sqrt D$ gives $q^{.5275+o(1)}/\sqrt{DJY}=q^{.4675+o(1)}$. Exact equality needs only the splits of one vector per $j$ and scalar recovery; their cost is smaller. Dyadic blocks and the $q^{.5275+o(1)}$ possible $d$ give the assertion. All references instead give an outgoing allowance per reference, not this same total price. No argument forces a large common eligible divisor.

## Decision for the common shift

Assign one admissible seed pair per actual numerator at $R=j=1$. For each pair and occupied $\tau$ cover the attained $k$ by classes $(b,\kappa)$ with $b\mid e$, also allowing $b=1$. A sufficient cover budget, over **all seed pairs and determinant residues** at one $d$, is
$$\sum_{f_1,f_2,\tau}\ \sum_{(b,\kappa)\text{ in cover}} b^{-1/2}\ \le q^{.063-\eta}.$$
Exceptions must cost at most $q^{.4705-\eta+o(1)}$. With just $b=e,1$ this asks for $\sum\min(q^{.120},K)\le q^{.183-\eta}$, where $K$ counts occupied full scalars per determinant class (all exponent ledgers up to subpowers). Partial residues can be better: **One joint value of $(t\bmod e,k\bmod f_1)$ per seed pair**, if proved, costs $.120+.3475=.4675$ even if all $f_2$ full scalar lifts are occupied. A uniform $q^{.003-\eta}$ such joint values per pair still suffices. In contrast one $t$ per pair alone gives $.120+.4075=.5275$.

The common shift has not proved that joint compression. For a split let $a=[q\delta_1/x_1]_d$, $B_i=(a x_i-q\delta_i)/d$. Both quotient tests require the same $u$ with $c=a+du$, $y_i=B_i+x_i u$; the residues $u\equiv-B_i/x_i\pmod{f_i}$ are tested **after division by $d$**. The short interval for $u$ has $O(X)$ integers, but $k=(a+du)h_1h_2$ varies at fixed $u$. Likewise $q t\equiv x_1y_2\pmod{f_1}$ and $q t\equiv-x_2y_1\pmod{f_2}$ depend on opposite outputs. Keep the seed-free scan as the unconditional count. Test the joint cover or bound actual successes outside it; counting admissible residues or repeating the short-lift uniqueness proof is not such a test. The [finite arithmetic control](check-partial-scalar.py) checks partial reconstruction, the full relaxed scalar fibers, and the gcd loss upon forgetting the determinant. It is not evidence of occupancy or an unrestricted $.998$ bound.

**A test on actual pairs for the one-seed scalar price.** Allow any crossing divisor in $[Q,XQ]$, $Q=q^{.120}$, not just a canonical prefix, and put $Y_2$ equal to the full part of $y_2$ outside the support of $y_1$. For an actual pair $C,C'$ at this $d$, let $z=t-t'$ and $v=n_1-n_1'$. They share at least one key $(f_1,f_2,t\bmod e,k\bmod f_1)$ exactly when
$$\gcd(y_1,y_1',z,v)\ge Q,\qquad \gcd(Y_2,Y_2',z)\ge Q.$$
For necessity use $q n_1=F_1 k\pmod{f_1}$ on each endpoint. Conversely the two positive gcds are eligible-smooth; cross $Q$ in each to get coprime seeds. The shared first-coordinate residue gives a shared partial scalar by the same unit identity, and $e\mid z$. This also covers zero differences with the usual gcd convention. At a reference there are only divisor-many keys. Thus the number of its passing neighbors (including itself) is at most $q^{.3475+o(1)}$, uniformly in the reference. For example, to rule out an actual fiber $U$ larger than $q^{.4705-\eta}$ it suffices to force a passing ordered-pair fraction at least $q^{-.123+2\eta}$ there: Some actual reference would then have more than $q^{.3475+\eta}$ neighbors. A mask or a short-shift count that bounds this fraction **above** does not establish the needed lower bound. This test is sufficient, not necessary for an economical cover.

Running `python3 .godel/alice/check-partial-scalar.py` checked 12,785 full scalar classes (5,328 with $(d,e)>1$), 83,540 partial residue reconstructions, and 4,335 spatial difference congruences on the stated finite grids. The sieve and the reference allowance above are mathematical bounds, not extrapolations from these grids.
The same checker compared the two-gcd test with explicit key intersection for 62,001 ordered pairs of toy row-equation solutions (1,858 passing). These are not constructed list incidences.
