# Count joint two-row output ratios over one complete input

Fix an actual $g=R=j=1$ constructed list $(F_1,F_2)$ and $v=cF_1h_1$. The sorted prefix fixes $c$, hence $x_1=F_1h_1=v/c$. Count actual $(c,d)$ at this input with their uniquely specified rows. Use one positive rational number per column,

$$p=\frac{y_2}{h_2 y_1}=\frac{n}{w}\quad\text{in lowest terms}.$$

Unlike a budget for each gap separately, this compares the two successful outputs at the **same** denominator. Indeed $dy_1=v-q\delta_1$, $dy_2=cF_2 h_2-q\delta_2$, so every $p$ is $F_2/x_1$ modulo $q$ and differs relatively from $F_2/x_1$ by at most $q^{-1.110+o(1)}$. Consequently for two columns at the *same* $v$ the determinant $nw'-n'w$ is a multiple of $q$, and its quotient has absolute value $\le Q=q^{1.445+o(1)}$. Here $n\le A_+=q^{1.445+o(1)}$, $w\le (A_+/W)A_+=q^{2.110+o(1)}$, and $1.445+2.110-1.110-1=1.445$. Use a uniform actual upper bound $Q$ including the subpower factors. This determinant height is much smaller than the $q^{6.560+o(1)}$ for two three-step output products; the fibers being counted are different.

**Exact ratio costs subpower columns.** Since $(h_2,y_2)=1$, the cancellation factor is precisely $G=(y_1,y_2)<q^{.115}$ by [successive row freshness](shared-row-labels.md). Thus $h_2\mid w$. Enumerate it among the divisors of $w$, before guessing $G$ or $d$. The known $w/h_2=y_1/G\ge q^{1.330-o(1)}$ divides $v-q\delta_1$. Since it is a unit modulo $q$ and exceeds $2H$, the interval $[-H,H]$ allows at most one $\delta_1$. Positivity and the actual sorted prefix of $v-q\delta_1$ recover $d$. No factorization of an unknown output, or guess of the $q^{.115}$ cancellation, is paid for. Neither the first gap congruence alone nor ignoring the second row would give this ratio and recovery.

**One-reference count.** Fix any actual ratio $p_0=n_0/w_0$ at $v$ before counting the others, and put $S_0=\operatorname{supp}(n_0w_0)$. For $1\le T\le q^{O(1)}$, at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$ columns satisfy

$$ |nw_0-n_0w|\,n/(q n_0)\le T(nw)_{S_0}. $$

Here the subscript denotes the full supported part, not the gcd. This is the divided-bracket sieve of [the endpoint proof](output-path-packets.md): Fix the two full parts at subpower cost, bin $n$ dyadically, and choose a signed divisor split crossing $5T$ in the remaining product before enumerating the vector. If the remaining product is too small, count it directly instead. A common split forces the inter-candidate determinant divided by $q$ to vanish. Apply the exact ratio recovery above. At $T=q^{.272}$ this costs $q^{.136+o(1)}$ per $v$, or $q^{.996+o(1)}$ over the $q^{.860+o(1)}$ inputs if just one reference is used on each. This is a bound on the passing set, not its complement. A sum over *all* references at $v$ would cost $q^{.136+o(1)}\deg(v)$ there and count ordered pairs, not columns.

**Same-input rank test.** At each occupied input prescribe a translate of a valuation space of dimension at most $K=\lfloor(\log q)^{1.18}\rfloor$ on eligible primes, for example through the ratio of its actual reference. The columns whose $p$ lie in the chosen translate number at most $q^{.13005+o(1)}$ at that input, hence $q^{.99005+o(1)}$ per list. The translate may depend on the input. In fact the signed pivot sieve of the endpoint proof applies with $Q$ as above and Rankin exponent $.18/2+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$. Up to the strict crossing of $Q$, the projected valuations cost $Q^{.09+o(1)}$ since the Euler factor on at most $K$ eligible pivots is $q^{o(1)}$; the last signed pivot costs at most $2K$. Above the crossing the shared factors force exact rational equality by the divided determinant bound. Below it, injection of the pivot projection on the translate suffices. The exact-ratio multiplicity is subpower in either case. Counting the pivot choices themselves or counting prime vectors of the larger reference *quotient* height would not prove this price.

**Consequence for the attachments.** In the [interleaved peeling](ordered-output-masks.md) one may insist that *every* witness column at the main denominator $d_0$ has $K+1$ attachments at its complete first input with independent valuations of $p/p_0$, $p_0$ from that column. Require distinct denominators packet-wide. Also insist that every attachment fails the displayed small-bracket test, against the group's reference and each preceding attachment in the group. One may keep the earlier full-support freshness test for consecutive blocks of $M=\lfloor (\log q)^{1.70}/(12k)\rfloor$ attachments, each block starting its old support at the root column. Do not claim freshness on the union of all $K+1$ preceding supports: That mask could be too large.

Indeed at each choice the live degree exceeds $q^{.137}$. The previous ratios of the group span a translate through its reference of dimension at most $K$; excluding it costs $q^{.13005+o(1)}$. The union of all the bracket tests costs $(K+1)q^{.136+o(1)}$ by the pointwise bound. The *one* active block mask costs $q^{.1365+o(1)}$ by the ordered-mask estimate. Each forbidden denominator costs at most one column at this input, with only subpower many used across the packet. The next choice exists. This counts failure of independence and proximity at the actual fixed input, not by multiplying hypothetical first- and second-gap densities. The number of removed columns per enriched packet remains subpower, and the total deletion to low-degree fibers remains $q^{.997+o(1)}$. There is no upper bound here on the number of remaining packets.

**Decision for the next count.** Work on the large-bracket side or abandon this lift; small determinant and low rank have now been priced in both the fixed-$d$ walk and the fixed-input attachment. One alternative way to exploit the distinct attachment denominators is to count *common inputs of two denominators*. If $I$ is the incidence count on the remaining simple $(d,v)$ graph and $t(d,d')$ the number of common complete inputs, then $I^2\le \#\{v\}\,(I+\sum_{d\ne d'} t(d,d'))$. Thus an average $q^{.081-\epsilon}$ over all $q^{1.055+o(1)}$ potential ordered distinct denominator pairs suffices, since $(.860+1.055+.081)/2=.998$. A bound of that size for common **denominators of input pairs** would not give the same saving: Its exponent would be $(.5275+2(.860)+.081)/2=1.16425$. In fact the average is needed only on **reciprocally separated** pairs. For an ordered pair at $v$ use its first column as reference. Discard the second if its normalized bracket passes the small test at $T=q^{.272}$, or its $dy_1$ has full part at least $H$ on the eligible primes of the first column's six smooth coordinates. These pointwise prices are $q^{.136+o(1)}$ and $q^{.1365+o(1)}$, respectively. Sum them over the actual first columns and repeat with orientations reversed. This time all references are counted: Including the diagonal, the discarded pairs cost at most $q^{.1365+o(1)} I$, not the one-reference object price. If $C_{\rm sep}$ counts just the remaining ordered pairs with their shared inputs, Cauchy now gives

$$I\le q^{.9965+o(1)}+q^{.430+o(1)}\sqrt{C_{\rm sep}}.$$

Hence $C_{\rm sep}\le q^{1.136-\epsilon}$ suffices for a power margin below $.998$. This counts two actual two-row columns with the same input, not a choice of arbitrary residue representatives for that input.

### Two more deletions on the same pair, before summing over inputs

There is a separate normalized test on each row. Fix an actual first column as reference (subscript $0$) at the specified $v$. In position one put $N_1=dy_1$ and $S=\operatorname{supp}(d_0y_{10})$. The candidates with

$$ |\delta_1-\delta_{10}|\le T(N_1)_S $$

cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$ at this reference, $1\le T\le q^{O(1)}$. Fix the full part $m=(N_1)_S$ at subpower price. Choose $f\mid N_1/m$ strictly crossing $2T$ before enumerating $\delta_1$. If there is no crossing, enumerate the entire remainder $\le2T$ and recover the actual $d$ from $N_1$. Otherwise $f\le 2TX$, and $v-q\delta_1\equiv0\pmod{mf}$ fixes at most one gap in the interval of length $2Tm$. Either branch costs the claimed smooth half-power. This one-reference test is stronger than just comparing the gcd with $H$. On escape at $T=q^{.272}$, $(dy_1)_S<2H/T=q^{.118+o(1)}$, even though no small overlap between different columns was assumed in their construction.

In position two use $h=h_2$, $b=\delta_2$, $N=dy_2=cF_2h-qb$. Write $A=(h,h_0)$, $B=(|b|,|b_0|)$, $r=(N,N_0)$, and

$$ D_2=\frac{h_0b-b_0h}{ABr}. $$

The three gcds are pairwise coprime by primitivity of the reference triple $(h_0,b_0,N_0)$ in pairs. Each divides the numerator (for $r$, use $h_0N-hN_0=-q(h_0b-b_0h)$ and $q\nmid r$). At the same actual fixed $v$ and reference, the number of columns with $|D_2|\le T$ is again $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$. Enumerate $A,B,r$ from the reference by divisors, then write $u=h/A$, $u_0=h_0/A$, $w=N/r$, $w_0=N_0/r$. Now $u_0w-w_0u=-qBD_2$ and $(u,w)=1$. For two candidates with this same divisor data, $qB\mid w_1u_2-w_2u_1$ since $(u_0,qB)=1$. Moreover

$$ |w_1u_2-w_2u_1|/(qB)\le T(u_1+u_2)/u_0\le 2TU/h_0. $$

Set $M=2TU/h_0=q^{o(1)}T$, using the **lower** cofactor bound $h_0\ge A_-/(WX)$. If $w\le M$, enumerate $w$ itself, which gives $N$ and hence $d$ by the sorted prefix. If $w>M$, enumerate an eligible divisor $f\mid w$ strictly crossing $M$, before either unknown coordinate. It is coprime to $qB$ as $(N,b)=1$. The common $f$ divides the displayed determinant quotient, forcing equality of the fractions, hence of both positive primitive coordinates. Either branch costs $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$. Exact recovery in the first branch does not require $w$ to contain a divisor crossing any threshold. This uses the same coefficient $cF_2$ on both columns, not a bound averaged at fixed cofactor and then used at varying cofactor.

Thus $C_{\rm sep}$ in the preceding sufficient test may be replaced by its subcount escaping **both** row tests at $T=q^{.272}$ in both orders: The extra discarded ordered pairs cost at most $q^{.136+o(1)} I$. In particular put $g_*=(d,d_0)$ (not the original $g=1$). Only $g_*<q^{.118+o(1)}$ can survive. The integer shifts $(\delta_1-\delta_{10})/g_*$ and $(h_0b-b_0h)/g_*$ both have magnitude $>q^{.272}$ there, since $g_*\mid r$ and $g_*\mid (N_1)_S$. They enter the two forms on the **same reduced denominator pair** $d/g_*,d_0/g_*$, with coefficients $(y_1,y_{10})$ and $(h_0y_2,h y_{20})$, respectively. Counting the large shifts independently is not a bound of the required size.

### A bound in the opposite order of fixing parameters

Now fix **two distinct denominators** $d,d_0$ first, and vary the common input $v$. The first-output ratio $z=y_1/y_{10}=n/w$ in lowest terms recovers $v$ at subpower cost, without knowing its sorted prefix $c$. Indeed write $y_1=Gn$, $y_{10}=Gw$. Then $t=(dn-d_0w)/q$ is integral, $|Gt|=|\delta_1-\delta_{10}|\le2H$, and $t\ne0$: Equal outputs $dy_1=d_0y_{10}$ have equal sorted prefixes. Thus $G\le2H$. The unknown positive $G$ and signed $\delta_1$ are coprime, and $dnG+q\delta_1$ is divisible by the prescribed label $F_1$. For two such pairs the determinant is divisible by $F_1$. In each bin $J\le G<2J$ their distinct slopes $\delta_1/G$ are spaced by at least $F_1/(4J^2)$ in an interval of length $2H/J$. This costs $O(1+HJ/F_1)=q^{o(1)}$ as $J\le 2H$ and $F_1>W=q^{.780+o(1)}$. Primitivity gives one pair per slope, then $v=dnG+q\delta_1$. This argument does not assume the two inputs in the slope comparison have the same $c$.

For the varying common inputs at this prescribed denominator pair, all $z$ are $d_0/d\bmod q$ and relatively $q^{-1.110+o(1)}$-close to $d_0/d$, since $|dy_1-d_0y_{10}|\le2qH$. Each reduced coordinate is at most $A_+$. Thus the inter-ratio determinant divided by $q$ costs at most $Q=q^{2(1.445)-2.110+o(1)}=q^{.780+o(1)}$. The signed pivot sieve now prices a chosen translate of dimension $\le\lfloor(\log q)^{1.20}\rfloor$ by $q^{.078+o(1)}$ **common inputs** ($\sigma=.10+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$); exact ratio recovery above is essential here. Independently, with **one actual reference input per occupied denominator pair**, the divided-bracket test on $z$ against its reference at $T=q^{.158}$ costs $q^{.079+o(1)}$ inputs by the same split-before-vector argument. Either choice may be made on the residual alone. Removing the union of these two classes of pair-input triples adds at most $q^{1.134+o(1)}$ to the Cauchy pair budget, not that price times an unknown number of references. In particular this is below $q^{1.136}$ even over all potential denominator pairs. Low rank everywhere in these fibers would suffice; there is no proof of that hypothesis.

The remaining decision is whether the simultaneous smooth lifts at common $v$ outside these classes cost less than $q^{1.136}$ by a power. The first ratio already recovers the input subpolynomially, so a successful upper bound need not also enumerate all anchor inputs at that pair. Use the second equation on the same denominator pair rather than count all possible large first-output brackets. No such average is proved here.

**After Work:** The [rectangle count](gap-area-rectangles.md) tests when reversing to fixed input pairs can help. A uniform codegree of two on that side is insufficient in the abstract graph. In the actual graph, rectangles with zero output determinant or small normalized first-row gap area in the smaller-gcd orientation can be charged. An aggregate $q^{1.217-\epsilon}$ bound on the remaining ordered rectangles would suffice. This is an alternative target for the same second-row restriction, not a bound on the residual.
