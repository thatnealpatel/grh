# Count joint two-row output ratios over one complete input

Fix an actual $g=R=j=1$ constructed list $(F_1,F_2)$ and $v=cF_1h_1$. The sorted prefix fixes $c$, hence $x_1=F_1h_1=v/c$. Count actual $(c,d)$ at this input with their uniquely specified rows. Use one positive rational number per column,

$$p=\frac{y_2}{h_2 y_1}=\frac{n}{w}\quad\text{in lowest terms}.$$

Unlike a budget for each gap separately, this compares the two successful outputs at the **same** denominator. Indeed $dy_1=v-q\delta_1$, $dy_2=cF_2 h_2-q\delta_2$, so every $p$ is $F_2/x_1$ modulo $q$ and differs relatively from $F_2/x_1$ by at most $q^{-1.110+o(1)}$. Consequently for two columns at the *same* $v$ the determinant $nw'-n'w$ is a multiple of $q$, and its quotient has absolute value $\le Q=q^{1.445+o(1)}$. Here $n\le A_+=q^{1.445+o(1)}$, $w\le (A_+/W)A_+=q^{2.110+o(1)}$, and $1.445+2.110-1.110-1=1.445$. Use a uniform actual upper bound $Q$ including the subpower factors. This determinant height is much smaller than the $q^{6.560+o(1)}$ for two three-step output products; the fibers being counted are different.

**Exact ratio costs subpower columns.** Since $(h_2,y_2)=1$, the cancellation factor is precisely $G=(y_1,y_2)<q^{.115}$ by [successive row freshness](shared-row-labels.md). Thus $h_2\mid w$. Enumerate it among the divisors of $w$, before guessing $G$ or $d$. The known $w/h_2=y_1/G\ge q^{1.330-o(1)}$ divides $v-q\delta_1$. Since it is a unit modulo $q$ and exceeds $2H$, the interval $[-H,H]$ allows at most one $\delta_1$. Positivity and the actual sorted prefix of $v-q\delta_1$ recover $d$. No factorization of an unknown output, or guess of the $q^{.115}$ cancellation, is paid for. Neither the first gap congruence alone nor ignoring the second row would give this ratio and recovery.

**One-reference count.** Fix any actual ratio $p_0=n_0/w_0$ at $v$ before counting the others, and put $S_0=\operatorname{supp}(n_0w_0)$. For $1\le T\le q^{O(1)}$, at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$ columns satisfy

$$ |nw_0-n_0w|\,n/(q n_0)\le T(nw)_{S_0}. $$

Here the subscript denotes the full supported part, not the gcd. This is the divided-bracket sieve of [the endpoint proof](output-path-packets.md): Fix the two full parts at subpower cost, bin $n$ dyadically, and choose a signed divisor split crossing $5T$ in the remaining product before enumerating the vector. If the remaining product is too small, count it directly instead. A common split forces the inter-candidate determinant divided by $q$ to vanish. Apply the exact ratio recovery above. At $T=q^{.272}$ this costs $q^{.136+o(1)}$ per $v$, or $q^{.996+o(1)}$ over the $q^{.860+o(1)}$ inputs if just one reference is used on each. This is a bound on the passing set, not its complement. A sum over *all* references at $v$ would cost $q^{.136+o(1)}\deg(v)$ there and count ordered pairs, not columns.

**Same-input rank test.** At each occupied input prescribe a translate of a valuation space of dimension at most $K=\lfloor(\log q)^{1.18}\rfloor$ on eligible primes, for example through the ratio of its actual reference. The columns whose $p$ lie in the chosen translate number at most $q^{.13005+o(1)}$ at that input, hence $q^{.99005+o(1)}$ per list. The translate may depend on the input. In fact the signed pivot sieve of the endpoint proof applies with $Q$ as above and Rankin exponent $.18/2+1/\sqrt{\log\log q}$. Up to the strict crossing of $Q$, the projected valuations cost $Q^{.09+o(1)}$ since the Euler factor on at most $K$ eligible pivots is $q^{o(1)}$; the last signed pivot costs at most $2K$. Above the crossing the shared factors force exact rational equality by the divided determinant bound. Below it, injection of the pivot projection on the translate suffices. The exact-ratio multiplicity is subpower in either case. Counting the pivot choices themselves or counting prime vectors of the larger reference *quotient* height would not prove this price.

**Consequence for the attachments.** In the [interleaved peeling](ordered-output-masks.md) one may insist that *every* witness column at the main denominator $d_0$ has $K+1$ attachments at its complete first input with independent valuations of $p/p_0$, $p_0$ from that column. Require distinct denominators packet-wide. Also insist that every attachment fails the displayed small-bracket test, against the group's reference and each preceding attachment in the group. One may keep the earlier full-support freshness test for consecutive blocks of $M=\lfloor (\log q)^{1.70}/(12k)\rfloor$ attachments, each block starting its old support at the root column. Do not claim freshness on the union of all $K+1$ preceding supports: That mask could be too large.

Indeed at each choice the live degree exceeds $q^{.137}$. The previous ratios of the group span a translate through its reference of dimension at most $K$; excluding it costs $q^{.13005+o(1)}$. The union of all the bracket tests costs $(K+1)q^{.136+o(1)}$ by the pointwise bound. The *one* active block mask costs $q^{.1365+o(1)}$ by the ordered-mask estimate. Each forbidden denominator costs at most one column at this input, with only subpower many used across the packet. The next choice exists. This counts failure of independence and proximity at the actual fixed input, not by multiplying hypothetical first- and second-gap densities. The number of removed columns per enriched packet remains subpower, and the total deletion to low-degree fibers remains $q^{.997+o(1)}$. There is no upper bound here on the number of remaining packets.

**Decision for the next count.** Work on the large-bracket side or abandon this lift; small determinant and low rank have now been priced in both the fixed-$d$ walk and the fixed-input attachment. One alternative way to exploit the distinct attachment denominators is to count *common inputs of two denominators*. If $I$ is the incidence count on the remaining simple $(d,v)$ graph and $t(d,d')$ the number of common complete inputs, then $I^2\le \#\{v\}\,(I+\sum_{d\ne d'} t(d,d'))$. Thus an average $q^{.081-\epsilon}$ over all $q^{1.055+o(1)}$ potential ordered distinct denominator pairs suffices, since $(.860+1.055+.081)/2=.998$. A bound of that size for common **denominators of input pairs** would not give the same saving: Its exponent would be $(.5275+2(.860)+.081)/2=1.16425$. Neither average bound has been shown. Retain the two-row equation, the small actual divisor $G$, and the non-small divided brackets if using this test.
