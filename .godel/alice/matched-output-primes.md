# Count sparse prime matchings in the two forced outputs

Fix one [constructed ordered list](shared-row-labels.md) at $g=1$. In each actual realization write $x_i=F_i h_i$, $cx_i-dy_i=q\delta_i$, $0<|\delta_i|\le H$, and $G=(y_1,y_2)$. Keep the positive primitive prefix graph and all its factor counts. A matching below is between **occurrences** of primes, one in each integer; no occurrence may be used twice. Its weight is the product of both ends of all its edges.

For every cofactor pair and $G$ prescribe a bipartite graph on two copies of the eligible primes, of maximum degree $L^{.2}$ on **both** sides, $L=\log q$. For fixed $\beta\ge0$, the number of realizing columns on which $y_1,y_2$ admit a matching of weight at least $q^\beta$ is at most
$$q^{o(1)}\big(q^{.899}+q^{1.055-.20\beta}\big).$$
At $\beta=.300$ the exponent is $.995$. The graph may use a prescribed first list incidence, but it must be fixed across all second outputs at the indicated data. A union over a subpower menu there is free in the exponent. For example, one may test all integer offsets $|b|\le X$ for $|p'-p-b|\le L^{.2}/4$. This detects joint concentration without requiring either output to use any small, globally named prime set. The count is across denominators, not pointwise on a fiber.

**A cheap divisor made of pairs and single primes.** There are $L^{2.2+o(1)}$ possible edge products. We can also allow singles from any fixed set of $L^{1.2+o(1)}$ eligible primes. Products up to $V=q^{O(1)}$, $V\ge1$, made of these atoms with repetitions cost $q^{o(1)}V^{.30}$. Indeed the log of their multiplicative Rankin product at $.30+o(1)$ is $o(L)$: Each edge product is $L^{4+o(1)}$ and each single is $L^{2+o(1)}$, uniformly by $X/(\log L)^4\le p\le X$. One can choose the positive $o(1)$ slack to dominate all the subpower errors in these **$L$-exponents**, so the edge sum is $o(L)$, and the single sum is $L^{.6+o(1)}$. This counts possible integer divisors, not their decompositions; the latter need not be enumerated. Unrestricted eligible divisors up to $V$ cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$.

Put $Y_i=y_i/G$. Mark as many occurrences for deletion on each side as needed to divide out $G$. An edge survives intact, loses both endpoints, or leaves a single prime adjacent to a prime of $G$. The product of the deleted matched occurrences divides $G^2$. Thus $Y_1Y_2$ contains an atom product of size at least $q^\beta/G^2$; the extra singles live in the two neighbourhoods of $\operatorname{supp}G$, in total $O(k L^{.2})$ primes. This is why the statement concerns the **raw** forced outputs, even for matches involving repeated common primes.

**Price before counting the vectors.** Set $E=W/G^2$. On constructed lists $G<q^{.115}$: The support masked when choosing the second row contains that of $y_1$, and the full masked part of $x_2y_2$ is smaller than $q^{.115}$. In particular $E>1$. The primitive $(Y_1,Y_2)$ satisfies
$$|Y_1|\le A_+/G,\qquad |x_1Y_2-x_2Y_1|\le q W/G,\qquad x_1Y_2-x_2Y_1\equiv0\pmod q.$$
The area of this centered strip with the first-coordinate bound, divided by $q$, is at most $4(A_+/x_1)E=q^{o(1)}E$. The congruence comes from the two actual row equations on the **same** column. It is not imposed by the smooth-support packing model.

Use $B=\min(E,\max(1,q^\beta/G^2))$. Take an atom product first crossing $B$ from the surviving occurrences (one if $B=1$). It overshoots by at most $X^2$. If necessary extend it by unrestricted eligible primes from the remaining quotient of $Y_1Y_2$ to cross $E$, within a factor $X$. This is possible since $Y_1Y_2/E=y_1y_2/W\gg1$. Enumerating the two factors costs $q^{o(1)} B^{.30}\sqrt{E/B}$. Split their product as $f_1 f_2$ dividing the respective coordinates, at divisor cost; no edge ordering is charged. The combined lattice index is $q f_1 f_2$, since the $f_i$ are prime to $q$ and the substituted linear form still surjects modulo $q$. The area divided by index is subpower. Pick's theorem bounds the lattice points of a spanning hull by twice this ratio plus two; otherwise the line contains zero by central symmetry and has at most two ambient primitive points. The recovered first row determines at most one actual column by [row uniqueness](shared-row-labels.md).

At fixed $h_1,h_2,G$ the price is therefore at most $q^{o(1)}(E^{.30}+\sqrt E\,(q^\beta/G^2)^{-.20})$, including saturation at either end. Both terms sum over eligible-smooth $G$ with weight $G^{-.60}$, whose sum is subpower by Rankin. At full widths this gives $q^{o(1)}(q^{.234}+q^{.390-.20\beta})$ pointwise per cofactor pair, not per denominator. The graph can vary even in the sum on $G$, but not in the count of atom products at a fixed triple. Restoring the two smooth cofactors costs $q^{.665+o(1)}$, giving the assertion.

**Trading adaptivity for four available factors.** Put $a=h_1y_2$, $b=h_2y_1$, and $J=(a,b)$. Here $(h_1,y_1)=(h_2,y_2)=1$, so $J=(h_1,h_2)(y_1,y_2)$ by prime valuations. Moreover $F_1 a-F_2 b=qt\ne0$ implies $J\mid t$ as $q\nmid J$. Before either second cofactor is given, choose the graph using only the list, first incidence, and $J$. A match between $a$ and $b$ of weight $q^\beta$ or more costs
$$q^{o(1)}(q^{.633}+q^{1.055-.20\beta})$$
actual second columns. Indeed $(a/J,b/J)$ is primitive in the analogous strip with fixed coefficients $F_1,F_2$ and first half-width $UA_+/J$. Here $E=UA_+ W/(F_2 J^2)=q^{2.110+o(1)}/J^2$, still large since $J\le W$. Also $(ab/J^2)/E=q^{2.110+o(1)}$, so the extension divisor exists. The same orphan, crossing, and $J^{-.60}$ summation argument now has no cofactor multiplier: Splitting the recovered $a,b$ recovers the rows at divisor cost. This tests, for instance, matching between $h_1$ and $h_2$ or between $y_1$ and $y_2$ with the same prices; it does not allow the adaptive cofactor-dependent graph of the first result.

In this crossed strip one may instead require weight $q^\beta$ from **single** primes of $ab$ in a prescribed set of size $L^{1.9}$. Rankin costs $V^{.45}q^{o(1)}$ for this first crossing, by the same calculation with size $L^{2+o(1)}$ per atom. Deleting $J^2$ costs at most that factor in weight, with no orphans. The two terms now have common summable weight $J^{-.90}$ and give $q^{o(1)}(q^{.9495}+q^{1.055-.05\beta})$. Thus the fixed-mask threshold $\beta>1.14$ is sufficient, whereas sparse paired atoms need only $\beta>.285$. Neither implication says that weight in the first model implies matched weight in the second.

**A pointwise version with a different matching.** At a fixed $d$ and one slot $i$, use the same graph size, now allowed to depend on $d$ and a selected actual reference there. Test for a matching between $ch_i$ and the forced $y_i$. These are coprime, so there is no $G$ or orphan cost. The primitive vector $(s,v)=(ch_i,y_i)$ satisfies $|s|\le ZU$, $|F_i s-dv|\le qH$, and $F_i s\equiv dv\pmod q$. The relative area is $4E_d$, $E_d=ZUH/d=q^{1.055+o(1)}$, and $s v\gg E_d$ by the lower coordinate bounds. The same two crossings, split, and primitive count cost $q^{o(1)}(E_d^{.30}+\sqrt{E_d}\,q^{-.20\beta})$. Splitting $s$ recovers $c$ at divisor cost. At $.300$ this is $.4675$ locally, also for the union of the two slots. One reference per occupied $d$ costs $.995$ across the list; summing over all references would give a local **outgoing** allowance per reference, not the same total. This version does not give the pointwise price for a match *between* the two outputs.

**A pointwise test on both Euclidean remainders.** With the same fixed $d$ let $R=(|\delta_1|,|\delta_2|)$, $j=(h_1,h_2)$, $\ell_i=h_i/j$, $v_i=\delta_i/R$. Use the two integers $|n_1|=|\ell_2v_1|$, $|n_2|=|\ell_1v_2|$ as matching pools, ignoring all ineligible factors. Prescribe the graph using $d$, its reference, $j$, and the **dyadic range** $D\le R<2D$, not the two recovered convergents. The number of distinct smooth $c$ at $d$ for which these pools admit a matching of weight at least $q^\beta$ is at most
$$q^{o(1)}\left(q^{.3165}+q^{.5275-.20\beta}\right).$$
This does not require any matched prime in $y_i$. In particular the test can be applied to the signed remainders before enumerating the forced outputs. It is a necessary-data count for actual successes, not a condition that every pair of remainders must satisfy.

Indeed use the [crossed-gap normalization and scalar recovery](masked-gap-lcm.md) with empty mask. The signed vector $n$ is primitive and both entries are units modulo $d$. Its centered box has relative area $O(V)$ for the index-$d$ congruence $F_2 n_1\equiv F_1 n_2\pmod d$, where $V=4 U^2 H^2/(dD^2 j^2)$. The primitive sign and divisor count for each recovered vector gives both $h_i,v_i$ at subpower cost, after which the cited scalar estimate is $q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$ for $c$; we do **not** enumerate $R$ first. If $V\le1$, the vector count is bounded. Otherwise start with matched atoms crossing $B=\min(q^\beta,V)$, with no orphans since the test is on the normalized pools. For seed $s$ so obtained use an unrestricted extension from $A/(s,A)$ with $A=\ell_1\ell_2$: Both $s$ and $A$ divide $|n_1n_2|$, so their lcm divides it, and $V/A\le q^{-.275+o(1)}$ by the cofactor lower bounds. Thus the extension can cross $V$ when needed without spending a prime power twice. Cost per $j,D$ for the vectors is $q^{o(1)}(1+V^{.30}+\sqrt V\,q^{-.20\beta})$ by the split-divisor argument, now using that the divisors are units modulo $d$. For the constant term there are at most $q^{.0575+o(1)}$ smooth $j<q^{.115}$. The other terms have summable $j^{-.60}$ and $j^{-1}$, respectively. After scalar recovery the dyadic box costs at most
$$q^{o(1)}\left(q^{.0575}\sqrt D+q^{.3165}D^{-.10}+q^{.5275-.20\beta}D^{-1/2}\right).$$
Since $D\le H$, summing the boxes proves the pointwise bound. The graph cannot be changed at each value of $R$ in the scalar recovery. At $\beta=.300$ the aggregate for one reference per $d$ is again $.995$. With all references the local count bounds the deleted **degree** by $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ uniformly; it does not bound the total deleted ordered pairs by $q^{.995+o(1)}$.

**Why the residual needs more than dispersed support.** Even testing many graphs cannot force this cross-output match by a support argument. In the [diffuse experiment](diffuse-support-test.md), $G=1$ and, conditional on the cofactors, $y_1$, and the chosen numerator, $y_2$ is a uniform subset of prescribed size $O(k)$ from a separate pool of $L^{2+o(1)}$ primes (use the draw before any conflict deletion). For a prescribed graph the neighbours of $y_1$ number at most $L^{1.2+o(1)}$. A $.300$ match needs at least $m=\lceil .300 L/(2\log X)\rceil$ neighbours in the draw. A union over the $m$ draw positions gives probability at most $2^{O(k)}(L^{-.8+o(1)})^m=q^{-.060+o(1)}$, also for a subpower menu fixed at the stated data. This excludes only an $o(1)$ fraction of the draws. Conditioning it away inflates the original independent two-draw conflict probability by at most $(1-o(1))^{-2}$, so the separated family of size $q^{.060-o(1)}$ can avoid these matches too. This is still **not** an occupied family.

**A common-quotient test even on the unmatched residual.** Work pointwise at $d$, not at a fixed cofactor pair. If $R\ge q^{.121}$ the empty-mask [raw-gap bound](masked-gap-lcm.md) already costs $.4675$ locally since $H^2/R<q^{.660}$. Below this cut choose smooth $f_i\mid y_i$ by crossing $q^{.120}$, using only primes outside $y_1$ for $f_2$. This is always possible on a constructed list: The full $y_1$-support part of $y_2$ is smaller than $q^{.115}$. Thus $(f_1,f_2)=1$, both $f_i$ are between $q^{.120}$ and $Xq^{.120}$, and the number of possible ordered seeds is at most $q^{.120+o(1)}$ **without** a prime match. No disjointness from $d$ is needed. This time select the two divisors before counting any crossed-gap vector.

For a recovered direction and split $(h_i,v_i)$ as above, discard nonunits $x_i=F_i h_i$ modulo $d f_i$. Put $b_i=qv_i/x_i\bmod df_i$. The index-$d$ direction congruence already gives $b_1=b_2\bmod d$. There is exactly one $B\bmod M$, $M=d f_1f_2$, with $B=b_i\bmod df_i$, by the generalized CRT. At this direction the two conditions of integrality and divisibility $f_i\mid (cx_i-qR v_i)/d$ (i.e., membership of the quotients in $f_i\mathbb Z$) are **equivalent** to
$$ c\equiv B R\pmod M. $$
Without coprime seeds the overlap would be $d(f_1,f_2)$, to be tested first, and $M=d\operatorname{lcm}(f_1,f_2)$. One cannot also charge this CRT index on the direction lattice. In the toy primitive rows $q=3411649$, $(c,d)=(128,243)$, $(x_1,y_1)=(24367,26875)$, $(x_2,y_2)=(36517,33275)$, both signed gaps are $-1$. Direct integer enumeration gave compatibility and $c=BR\bmod M$ for all 120 output-divisor pairs, including 64 with nontrivial gcd. This checks the identity, not a frequency.

In a dyadic range $D\le R<2D$ the lattice of $(R,c)$ in this last congruence has index $M$; the determinant of any two in $|R|<2D$, $|c|\le Z$ is at most $4DZ<M$ since $Z/d\le X$, $D<q^{.121}$, and $f_1f_2\ge q^{.240}$. Hence at most one positive primitive pair survives (actual $(R,c)=1$). Equivalently test the last convergent of $B/M$ with denominator at most $2D$, using $BR-Mk=c$, $2(2D)Z<M$, and $(k,R)=1$. Here $B$ means the least representative of the CRT solution, not a quotient reduced modulo $d$. Test range, sign, smooth numerator, the two forced outputs, and the original list conditions; a candidate is not necessarily an incidence.

**What must be priced.** The seed test is stronger than compatibility: Every such modulus has size $q^{1.295+o(1)}$, whereas $c$ is at most $q^{1.055+o(1)}$. At $R=1$ it is a short *ordinary representative* test on $B$, not a new equation modulo $d$. For each $d$, sum the number of successful direction/split assignments over all ordered pairs of these coprime smooth seeds and the dyadic ranges. A uniform sum below $q^{.4705-\eta}$ per $d$ for fixed $\eta>0$ suffices; allowing $q^{.120+o(1)}$ seed pairs leaves a budget of $q^{.3505-\eta}$ per pair on that normalization. It would suffice even to test just the short primitive lift and smooth $c$, before testing the rest of the output. At full gaps $R=j=1$ the direction bound with no short-lift saving is $q^{.5275+o(1)}$ per seed. Even for actual completions the one-slot estimate using $f_i\mid y_i$ alone is only $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ per seed: In the one-slot strip of relative area $E_d$ above, impose this divisor first and cross $E_d/f_i$ with unrestricted factors of $ch_i y_i/f_i$. The $.120$ seed cost cannot then be ignored. Prove cancellation or scarcity in the **sum of short CRT lifts**, or bound the successful subset after the smooth-output tests; neither uniqueness nor uniform distribution of $B$ has supplied the needed saving. An averaged sum over occupied $d$ with the same aggregate budget would also suffice. The cross-output matching estimate, which holds at fixed cofactors before counting denominators, cannot be substituted at fixed $d$. No unrestricted $.998$ estimate follows.

**After Work:** For the successful subset after both output tests, [delay the divisor witnesses](delayed-output-seeds.md). At $R=1$ neither seed is needed: Scan the same short shift in both forced quotients. A saving greater than $.057$ on the full-width direction envelope remains open. Retain the large-modulus CRT test when seeking an estimate *before* testing the full outputs.
