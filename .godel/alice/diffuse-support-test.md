# A diffuse family on the smooth supports, not on the row equations

The [partial-divisor packing question](partial-row-ratios.md) has a negative test for a support-only proof. For all sufficiently large primes $q$, one smooth denominator and two fixed labels admit $q^{.060-o(1)}$ **distinct numerators in the relaxation below**. All admissible pairs of partial divisors are separated in both positions, even after imposing the moving modular discrepancy tests in both directions. This exceeds the proposed $q^{.003-\epsilon}$ packing scale. The relaxation removes the equations that force the two outputs. It is not a counterexample on an occupied fiber.

**Support.** Use the parameters $X,k,N,Z,W$ of [shared row labels](shared-row-labels.md). Take disjoint prime pools in finitely many fixed positive-width subintervals of $[X/2,X]$, each containing $\asymp X/\log X$ primes by the prime number theorem. Use separate pools for $c,d$, their respective padding primes, $F_1,F_2,h_1,h_2,y_1,y_2$. Put column and padding pools below the row pools, and label pools below cofactor pools. Choose $P<X$ above the padding primes and below the row primes. For each column coordinate take a uniform subset of $m=\lfloor\log(Z/X)/\log X\rfloor$ primes from its pool. Starting with their product, multiply by distinct padding primes in a prescribed order until first exceeding $Z/P$. Before padding the product is in $[(X/2)^m,Z/X]$, so the padding multiplier is $q^{o(1)}$ and only $o(k)$ padding primes are needed. The result is in $(Z/P,Z]$ and the next row prime crosses $Z$. Stripping the padding is injective back to the sampled subset (the padding is deterministic). The number of distinct coordinates is $\binom{M}{m}=q^{.5275+o(1)}$, since $M\asymp X/\log X$, $\log(M/m)=(1/2+o(1))\log X$, and $X^m=q^{1.055+o(1)}$.

Fix labels by first crossing $W$ with distinct sorted primes in the two label pools. Among the denominator supply choose $d$ minimizing the fixed-label center-uniform envelope at radius $q^{.900}$ in [simultaneous output cuts](simultaneous-output-cuts.md). Its value is at most $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ by the sum bound $.995$. Restrict $c$ to a most populous value of $\Omega(c)$, losing at most $O(k)$ in mass. Independently sample uniform subsets of sizes $k-\Omega(c)-\Omega(F_i)$ and $k-\Omega(d)$ for $h_i$ and $y_i$. These are positive sizes with $X$ to those powers equal to $q^{.665+o(1)}$ and $q^{1.445+o(1)}$. Each product $cF_i h_i,dy_i$ has exactly $k$ eligible factors, is in the original product range, and has the specified longest column prefix. The products on opposite sides are coprime. No anchor, rich-column incidence, or signed gap is asserted.

**All partials.** For a specified $s$-prime subset of an $M$-prime pool sampled at size $r=O(k)$ its inclusion probability is at most $(r/M)^s$. Here $r/M=O(X^{-1/2})$ and the cost of the constant to the power $O(k)$ is $q^{o(1)}$. For either slot this gives, uniformly,
$$\Pr(a\mid ch_i,\ b\mid y_i)\le q^{o(1)}(ab)^{-1/2}.$$
Indeed all possible padding factors together in a draw multiply to $q^{o(1)}$; even the longest padding prefix is this small. Remove these primes from the specified divisors, charge the $O(k)$ conditioning loss, and use the independent pools. Divisors not supported or not squarefree have probability zero.

Fix any reference draw. For fixed reference divisors, $e\mid d$, and dyadic coordinate boxes with $ab\asymp T$, the [small-pair lemma](partial-row-ratios.md) bounds the number of aligned primitive smooth $(a,b)$ by $q^{o(1)}(1+\sqrt{T q^{-.120}})$. Its first step uses only the support and ratio test, not the row equations. Multiply by the inclusion probability: Since $ab\ge q^{.120}$, the chance of any such hit is $q^{-.060+o(1)}$, even summed over both slots, all reference divisors, moduli, and boxes. Taking $e=(d,ab_0-a_0b)$ includes equality. This bound is not obtained by prescribing one favorable splitting rule.

**Modular cuts.** The cofactor-pair atoms have mass $q^{-.665+o(1)}$ by the same binomial count. Discard cofactor pairs failing the all-nonzero-shifts $q^{.880}$ nonresonance inequality: The cofactor count in [simultaneous output cuts](simultaneous-output-cuts.md) is at most $q^{.605+o(1)}$ before any row completions. Conditioning away this $o(1)$ mass preserves the inclusion bound. For any retained reference put $z=(c-c_0)/d\bmod q$ and $s_i=\|F_i h_i(c_0)z\|_q$. The [pointwise moving test](moving-row-product.md) counts at most $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ smooth $c$ with $s_1s_2\le q^{1.800}$ or $\min_i s_i\le q^{.880}$. The normalized test $\max_i\|F_i z\|_q\le q^{.900}$ has the same allowance at this $d$. A numerator atom costs at most $q^{-.5275+o(1)}$, so each failure probability is at most $q^{-.060+o(1)}$. This argument uses the fixed labels for the averaged envelope; it does not average moving multipliers. Exchange two independent draws to include reverse tests. Equal numerators fail the moving test.

If $p=q^{-.060+o(1)}$ is the resulting upper bound on conflict probability, in $\lfloor1/p\rfloor$ independent draws the expected number of conflicting unordered pairs is at most half the sample size. Some sample therefore retains at least half its vertices after deleting one per conflict. This proves the support claim.

**Decision.** Do not seek the missing exponent by a packing argument using only simultaneous divisor choices, shared $c,d$, the exact prefix rule, and these modular discrepancies. The sampled $y_i$ need not even satisfy $dy_i\equiv cF_i h_i\pmod q$. An estimate on independent draws cannot be conditioned on the signed row equations at subpower cost without a new argument. For an actual-row cut using the omitted data see [lifted direction separation](lifted-direction-separation.md).
