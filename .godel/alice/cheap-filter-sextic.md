# Confine the cheap-filter normals to a sextic

Keep one actual two-label list, the bins and transformed normals of [the two-minor count](normal-factor-minors.md), and its cheap filter $d=f_A f_B$ with $D=Yq^{.161}$. There is a single rational projective curve of degree six per filter and bin containing **all** represented normals in the coordinates below. The argument uses the omitted factors in each row, even if their prime supports are disjoint across rows. Lines, conics, and components missing either of two specified points together contribute only $q^{o(1)}$ distinct image indices per filter. The remaining components have degree three through six, pass through both points, and contain a specified line **upon reduction modulo $q$**. This does not bound the number of represented indices on such a component.

**Stronger handoff:** [Complete the evaluation matrix with non-represented rows](cheap-filter-kernel.md). At most one degree-three through degree-five component per filter still needs a population bound; for $\sigma\ge .455-o(1)$ the quintic case is payable too.

**Coordinates and uniform prices.** Use $T=RJ=q^\sigma$, $s=(F,a)$ fixed, $a'=a/s$, $F'=F/s$. Write the integral coordinates as

$$w=(K,C,B_0)=((qa'+F'(b+c))/d,\ c/f_A,\ b/f_B).$$

Their change of variables from $(a',b,c)$ has determinant of absolute value $q/d^2$. Put $h_A=A/f_A$, $h_B=B/f_B$, where $AB=g$ is the image index. Thus $h_A\mid K,C$ and $h_B\mid K,B_0$, and $(h_A,h_B)=1$. Both are units modulo $q$. For $p\mid h_A$ the coordinate $B_0$ is a unit modulo $p$: Reduce the normal identity modulo $p$ and use $(a'q,g)=(F',g)=1$, $p\mid c$, and $(f_B,A)=1$. Likewise $(C,h_B)=1$.

Here and below exponent estimates allow $o(1)$, including smooth first-crossing overshoot. We only need the remaining bins $.446-o(1)\le\sigma\le .457+o(1)$, with $r=\log_q R\ge .446-o(1)$. Both filter factors have exponent $(2.271-\sigma)/2$. Set $u=.619$, $v=.9745-\sigma/2$. Then

$$|C|,|B_0|\le q^{v+o(1)},\quad |K|\le q^{u+o(1)},\quad K'=K-(F'/d)(f_A C+f_B B_0)=qa'/d=O(q^{u-1.110+o(1)}).$$

Indeed $|b|,|c|\le VH/T$, $a'\le H^2/T$, and $F'\le F$. Each shear coefficient is at most $q^{u-v+o(1)}$. From $g=PQ/\lambda$ we have $h_Ah_B=g/d\ge q^{.153+r-o(1)}\ge q^{.599-o(1)}$. Individually $h_A,h_B\ge q^{1.212-\log_q J-(2.271-\sigma)/2-o(1)}>q^{.28}$ for large $q$ in these bins.

**Two extra rows in a Veronese determinant.** Append the two coordinate points $E_A=[0:0:1]$, $E_B=[0:1:0]$ to any $n=\binom{m+2}{2}-2$ actual rows. Form the square evaluation determinant $\Delta$ on all homogeneous monomials of degree $m$. Expanding on the two coordinate rows deletes the two pure powers $B_0^m,C^m$. Every surviving entry in row $i$ is divisible by $h_{A,i}h_{B,i}$. Hence their product over the rows divides $\Delta$, without requiring any cross-row coprimality.

Replacing $K$ by $K'$ on **every** row preserves the determinant by a unitriangular rational column operation. In the full set of monomials the sum of the $K$-degrees is $S=\binom{m+2}{3}$, also the sum of the degrees of each other variable by symmetry. At most $b_m=2m-1$ of the $K$-degrees can land on the two appended rows in a determinant term: Only one column has $K$-degree $m$. Relative to the full column weight $q^{(u+2v)S}$, each appended row saves $q^{mv}$ since its sheared coordinates are bounded by $(q^{u-v+o(1)},1,1)$. The actual rows save $q^{1.110(S-b_m)}$ by thinness. All dimensions here are fixed. Separately in $\mathbb Z_{(q)}$ the same shear has integral coefficients and $q\mid K'$ on actual rows, giving $q^{S-b_m}\mid\Delta$. This latter divisor is coprime to the product of row divisors. No division by $d$ in an omitted prime's residue ring is needed.

The exponent bound for the absolute determinant **after** dividing by these integer divisors is

$$(u+2v-2.110)S+2.110 b_m-2mv-.599n+o(1).$$

For $m=6$ it is largest at the smallest $\sigma$ since $S>m$. At $.446$ we have $v=.7515$, $(S,b_m,n)=(56,11,26)$, giving $.012(56)+2.110(11)-12(.7515)-26(.599)=-.710$. Thus every such determinant vanishes. For comparison, the same test at degrees two and five gives $+.976$ and $+.514$, not vanishing criteria. It is the gain on *each* of 26 rows, not a common residual divisor of three rows, that changes the sign.

Consequently the 26 columns after deletion have rank at most 25 on the whole fiber, also if it contains fewer than 26 points. A nonzero integral kernel vector gives the sextic, which has no pure $B_0^6,C^6$ terms and passes through both coordinate points. One can take coefficients of polynomial height in $q$ by maximal-rank minors. Its primitive integral factors over $\mathbb Q$ still have polynomial height: For fixed degree one may substitute distinct radix powers of a single indeterminate for the variables (radix exceeding all degrees), and use the elementary univariate factor bound from the leading coefficient and a root bound. Projective points suffice because the coordinate change is invertible over $\mathbb Q$ and the transformed integral normal is primitive and oriented. Each fixed normal gives divisor-many $g$ from its nonzero evaluation.

**Components payable at this filter.** A line of represented points gives a normal span of dimension at most two, already recovered by [the nonvanishing minors](normal-factor-minors.md). If a primitive factor $G$ misses $E_A$, its coefficient $G(0,0,1)$ is a fixed nonzero integer. For any actual point on $G$, reduction modulo $h_A$ gives $h_A\mid G(0,0,1)$ because $B_0$ is a unit. Enumerate $h_A$ at divisor cost, then use the normal lattice with first filter enlarged to $f_Ah_A$. Its index is $(dh_A)^2$, not a presumed extra congruence on a subspace. This exceeds the full determinant bound $6XY^2$ since $6XY^2/d^2\le q^{.012+o(1)}$. Rank is now at most two and the same minors recover the remaining factors. The case missing $E_B$ is symmetric.

Write $L=dK-F'(f_A C+f_B B_0)$. If $G\bmod q$ does not contain the line $L=0$, its restriction to that line is a nonzero binary form. The nonzero reductions of actual rows on $G$ thus lie in at most $\deg G$ projective classes. In each class (allowing zero rows modulo $q$ as well), the rows have rank at most one modulo $q$, so $q^2$ divides every three-row determinant. But for three actual $w$ the determinant is at most $q^{1.012+o(1)}$: Use the shear and the widths above. Each class therefore spans at most a plane over $\mathbb Q$. This is a rank argument, with no nonsingularity assumption.

**Exclude conics even at bad reduction.** Suppose an irreducible rational conic through both coordinate points contains at least five distinct actual projective points. If its geometric components were two conjugate lines, its rational points would lie in their intersection, so it is geometrically irreducible. Select three independent actual rows on it as the rows of a matrix $U$ (otherwise use the plane count directly). Write a general projective row as $z^t U$ for a coordinate column $z$. At actual points one can choose integral $z$ with $\|z\|_\infty\le q^{.012+o(1)}$: Use the three row-replacement determinants divided by $q$ in Cramer's rule. Each is divisible by $q$ by the common linear congruence, and bounded as above. This includes $\det U=qt$, $0<|t|\le q^{.012+o(1)}$.

In these small coordinates the conic has an integral symmetric matrix $M$ of height $q^{.120+o(1)}$. Indeed evaluation on five points determines its equation up to scale (two independent quadrics would contradict Bézout). The $5$-row quadratic minors have the claimed height, and a factor 2 clears the half-integral entries. Let $k,c_1,b_1$ be the three columns of $U$. The lifts of $E_A,E_B$ are respectively $k\times c_1$ and $k\times b_1$, as projective points. Modulo $q$, $U$ has rank two since $q^2\nmid\det U$, and $k$ is a linear combination of $c_1,b_1$ with both coefficients units. Thus these cross products are nonzero and proportional modulo $q$, though distinct in characteristic zero. Their common line has primitive normal $k_0=k/\gcd(k_i)$ of height at most $q^{.619+o(1)}$.

Take an integral basis of $k_0^\perp\cap\mathbb Z^3$ as the columns of $D_2$. Their cross product is $\pm k_0$ by primitivity and saturation (adjoin a vector pairing to 1 with $k_0$). Thus they are independent modulo $q$. Restricting the conic to this line gives a binary quadratic with matrix $D_2^t M D_2$. It has two distinct rational roots which reduce to the same projective point modulo $q$. Its signed determinant

$$-\det(D_2^t M D_2)=-k_0^t\operatorname{adj}(M) k_0$$

is a nonzero integer square: The discriminant of the split quadratic is four times this number. It is divisible by $q$ because the two primitive root directions coalesce (factor the integral binary quadratic as a scalar times primitive linear factors by Gauss's lemma). Hence the square is at least $q^2$. The adjugate identity instead bounds it by $q^{2(.120+.619)+o(1)}=q^{1.478+o(1)}$. This contradiction leaves at most four actual projective points on such a conic. The small-coordinate change is essential for this height comparison.

**Decision.** On each potential cheap filter and bin, the union of the payable cases costs $q^{o(1)}$ distinct represented indices across all layers, so its aggregate triple price is $q^{1.1355+o(1)}$ by the existing $\sqrt D\sqrt T$ accounting. No enumeration of sextics across filters, second payment for reference choices, or replacement of the two successful rows and sorted prefixes is involved. All other indices can be assigned to at most two rationally irreducible components per filter of degrees 3 through 6, passing through both coordinate points and containing $L$ in reduction. For these the whole-fiber cap from [short witnesses](primal-witness-price.md) is still $q^{.006+o(1)}$ in the worst bin; confinement alone is not a point count. A next test is to restrict a cubic in the small $z$-coordinates to $k_0^\perp$. Two roots still coalesce, but its discriminant has degree four in the cubic coefficients and degree six in $k_0$, so the conic's $q^2$ comparison does not carry over. Price the rational quadratic factor of that restriction instead, including its content and the height of the third root. The needed average on the residual components is still $q^{.0005-\eta+o(1)}$ indices per potential filter, without holding a layer fixed.
