# A pointwise product test for the two moving row discrepancies

Fix an occupied denominator $d$ on the $g=1$ two-position list, **after** the [full cofactor-fiber cut](simultaneous-output-cuts.md). Fix any actual reference $c_0$ there. Write $A_i=F_i h_i(c_0)\bmod q$, $z=(c-c_0)/d\bmod q$, and $s_i=\|A_i z\|_q$ (absolute least residue). Then

$$\#\{Z/X<c\le Z:c\text{ eligible-smooth},\ s_1s_2\le q^{1.800}\}\le q^{.4675+o(1)}.$$

This does not charge all partners on a cofactor pair. It charges numerators **inside** the product test, including equal modular slopes. The multipliers can be chosen anew at every reference. In fact the count works for any units $A_1,A_2$ with $\|A_1 w\|_q\|A_2 w\|_q>q^{.880}$ for all nonzero $w\bmod q$, and arbitrary translations in the two coordinates. Here $q$ is prime, $Z=q^{1.055+o(1)}$, the eligible primes are bounded by $X\asymp(\log q)^2$, and we use the smooth count $\ll q^{o(1)}\sqrt L$ up to polynomial height $L\ge1$. The general translated test uses $s_i=\|A_i c/d-t_i\|_q$ for arbitrary $t_i\in\mathbb F_q$. The retained reference, not the tested numerator, supplies the nonresonance hypothesis in the application.

**Reduce to rectangles.** If either coordinate is at most $q^{.880}$, the [pointwise single-band bound](modular-complement-bands.md#full-numerator-width-rotate-each-denominator-separately) suffices. Otherwise the smaller coordinate lies between $q^{.880}$ and $q^{.900}$. Dyadically partition this interval from $q^{.880}$ upward. On a block with lower endpoint $K$, the small coordinate is at most $2K$ and the large one at most $q^{1.800}/K$. Splitting the wider interval of radius $2K$ into a bounded number of translates, it suffices to count two translated cyclic bands of radii $K_1,K_2$, with

$$K_1 K_2=q^{1.800},\qquad q^{.880}\le K_i\le q^{.920}.$$

Constant factors from integral rounding have no effect. Keeping the aspect ratio matters: The result is not a claim that the square of radius $q^{.920}$ has the same price.

**Translated occupancy.** Put $G=q^2/(K_1K_2)=q^{.200}$, $V=Z/G$, $C=XV$. Cross $V$ with an eligible-smooth $f\mid c$, $V\le f\le C$, and write $c=fg$, $g\le G$. The translated box for $(g,r_1,r_2)$ has half-widths $(G,K_1,K_2)$ and congruences $d r_i\equiv f A_i g\pmod q$; the centers occur in the box, not in the lattice. The scaled lattice has determinant one. With $\tau$ the shortest sup norm in its dual, the [translated occupancy proof](simultaneous-output-cuts.md#larger-bands-for-the-normalized-outputs) gives $q^{o(1)}(1+1/\tau)$ numerators for this $f$. Indeed it uses the product of the scaling widths, successive minima and transference for the scaled cube, and $2K_i<q$ in each coordinate: A nonzero difference on a rank-one slice cannot be vertical. On that slice the smooth $g$ progression count is unchanged. No denominator or multiplier is averaged here.

**Count exceptional filters.** For $\tau\le\eta\le1$, a nonzero dual witness satisfies

$$db+f A\cdot a\equiv0\pmod q,\qquad |a_i|\le H_i=\eta q/K_i,\qquad |b|\le B=\eta q/G.$$

If $a=0$, then $b=0$ since $B<q$. If $A\cdot a=0\bmod q$, the nonzero $a$ is congruent to $w(A_2,-A_1)$ with $w\ne0$. The product of its absolute coordinates is at most $H_1H_2\le q^{.200}$, impossible at this reference. Thus $A\cdot a\ne0$ and $b\ne0$. Also $\max H_i\ge1$, so $\eta\ge q^{-.120}$.

Set $E=CB/q=q^{.655+o(1)}\eta$. Write $(f,b)=j(u,v)$, $j>0$, $u>0$, $v\ne0$, $(u,v)=1$. Since $j\le C<q$, we can cancel $j$ in the congruence. Fix $a$. For each $j\le2\sqrt E$ choose a smooth divisor $l\mid u$ crossing $4E/j^2\ge1$. There is enough product since $(4E/j^2)/u\le4XB/(qj)=o(1)$. Its choice costs $q^{o(1)}(1+\sqrt E/j)$. The lattice $d v+u A\cdot a\equiv0$, $l\mid u$, has index $ql$ (the first coordinate is free before filtering). Two pairs in the centered box of half-widths $C/j,B/j$ have determinant of absolute value at most $2CB/j^2<ql$, hence zero. Primitivity and $u>0$ leave at most one pair per filter. Summing the displayed cost over these $j$ costs $q^{o(1)}\sqrt E$.

For $j,j'>2\sqrt E$ at this $a$ the two primitive pairs have determinant divisible by $q$ and of size at most $2CB/(jj')<q$. With $u>0$ there is only one such pair. For $2\sqrt E<j\le E$ its smooth multipliers cost at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt E$. The total price through $j=E$ is therefore bounded by

$$q^{o(1)}(H_1H_2+\max H_i)\sqrt E.$$

The second term counts axis witnesses as well when one $H_i<1$.

**Long content, across all $a$.** For any two witnesses with $j,j'>E$, the vector $D=uv'a-u'va'$ is in the kernel of $A\bmod q$. It has sup norm at most $2CB\max H_i/E^2\le q^{.465+o(1)}$. Thus any two such differences have determinant zero: It is divisible by $q$ and strictly smaller in absolute value. If not all differences vanish, take a primitive integer generator $w$ of their common line. It too is in the modular kernel, by cancelling a nonzero content smaller than $q$. No $a$ lies on this line, since an integral multiple of $w$ would be in the kernel. Take an integer normal $L$ to $w$ of polynomial height. Then $L(a)\ne0$ on every witness and $L(D)=0$. If all differences vanish and the set is nonempty, take a coordinate functional $L$ nonzero on a chosen witness's $a$; the comparison with it shows this coordinate is nonzero for all, since $u v'\ne0$.

In either case $v/(uL(a))$ is one rational for the entire long set. Its reduced denominator has polynomial height. Since $(u,v)=1$, $u$ divides this denominator. The possible $u$ therefore cost $q^{o(1)}$, and the smooth $j\le B$ cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt B$. This bounds **distinct $f$**, not witness multiplicity; it does not pay for a line or a normal separately in each fiber of $a$.

**Sum the occupancy.** The ordinary filter count is $q^{.4275+o(1)}$. In a dyadic range $\eta/2<\tau\le\eta$, divide the exceptional count by $\eta$. Up to subpower factors the two-coordinate term, capped by the ordinary filter count before weighting, costs

$$\min(q^{.4275}/\eta,\ q^{.5275}\eta^{3/2})\le q^{.4675}$$

by splitting at $\eta=q^{-.040}$. The axis term costs at most $q^{.4475}\sqrt\eta$; the long term at most $q^{.400}/\sqrt\eta\le q^{.460}$. These include all real aspect ratios in the displayed range; constants, dyadic sums over $\eta$ and the small coordinate, and the $\tau>1$ term fit the claimed bound.

**Use on the diffuse family.** The old normalized discrepancy uses the fixed $F_i$, not these moving multipliers. For one reference per $d$ the aggregate allowance for the product test is $q^{.995+o(1)}$, since there are at most $q^{.5275+o(1)}$ smooth denominators. For ordered references subtract the allowance in each outgoing fiber before squaring. One can strengthen the [partial-divisor packing test](partial-row-ratios.md) itself. On the retained vertices at one $d$, add directed edges from each reference to the other endpoints failing $s_1s_2>q^{1.800}$ or $\min_i s_i>q^{.880}$. The latter cut still matters at extreme aspect ratios. Each outdegree is $O(q^{.4675+o(1)})$, including the symmetric divisor-alignment edges. Indegrees need not satisfy this bound. In every induced subgraph the undirected average degree is at most twice the outdegree bound, so greedily removing a minimum-degree vertex and its neighbors gives an independent set of size at least $n/q^{.4675+o(1)}$ from $n$ retained vertices. In that set the partials are separated and the moving discrepancy tests hold in **both orientations** of every distinct pair. This is compatible with retaining the earlier fixed-reference cuts, including the test on the fixed $F_i$, before taking the graph.

**Does the product test remove a new projected region?** Even with the actual reference fixed, the previous modular tests do not imply it. This comparison concerns field shifts $z$, **not actual smooth endpoints**. Put $U_*=q^{.910}$, $V_*=q^{.885}$, and $M=q^{.900}$. Count points of the index-$q$ lattice $w_i\equiv A_i z\pmod q$ (some common $z$) in $[U_*,2U_*]\times[V_*,2V_*]$. Divide the respective coordinates by $U_*,V_*$. In this scaling the shortest nonzero sup norm is at least $q^{.440}/\sqrt{U_*V_*}$: For $z\ne0$ use nonresonance, and for $z=0$ a nonzero coordinate before scaling has magnitude at least $q$. The determinant is $q/(U_*V_*)$. Thus the second minimum is $O(q^{-.3375})$ by the product of minima. Two independent vectors at the successive minima span a sublattice with fundamental parallelogram of diameter $o(1)$ and area $O(q/(U_*V_*))$. Its tiles covering the middle half of the scaled rectangle have their base points in the rectangle, giving $\gg U_*V_*/q=q^{.795}$ points. Each is a distinct field shift, as both widths are below $q$.

Among these points the old normalized test can fail only if $\|F_2z\|_q\le M$. For such a point write $h m=w_2+q n$, where $h=h_2(c_0)=q^{.665+o(1)}$ and $m$ is the signed least residue of $F_2z$. There are $O(1+hM/q)$ choices of $n$ and then $O(1+V_*/h)$ choices of $m$; $m$ determines $z$. The price is $q^{.785+o(1)}$, strictly smaller than the rectangle count. Thus some shifts pass both old modular tests (both moving coordinates exceed $q^{.880}$ and at least one normalized coordinate exceeds $M$), and are outside the moving $.900$ square, yet their product is at most $4q^{1.795}<q^{1.800}$. No bracket, partial-divisor, or list-membership condition was imposed on these shifts.

**Remaining question.** It suffices to exclude a family of size $q^{.003-\epsilon}$ with the simultaneous properties of the diffuse family above. No lower bound on newly removed actual endpoints, upper bound on the size of the independent family, or unrestricted $.998$ estimate has been proved.
