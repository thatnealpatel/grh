# Price one signed product in a progression

On a constructed $g=1$ list, put $z=\delta_1h_2$ and $w=\delta_2h_1$. The common-weight correlation counts pairs $(z,w)$. To remove a structured set $E$ from the **actual** realizations, it is not enough to bound the correlation with both inputs restricted to $E$: One must also pay for a product in $E$ paired with an unrestricted product. Here is a one-coordinate bound, uniform over the complement moduli, that does so.

Fix a first realization, then prescribe an integer $1\le e\le H$ **before** the second cofactors. For one residue $a\bmod e$, the number of actual second columns with $z\equiv a\pmod e$ is at most

$$q^{.665+o(1)}H/\sqrt e.$$

The other product is unrestricted. The residue need not be a unit, and $e$ need not divide, or be coprime to, the second modulus $d$. If in addition $\delta_1$ is in a prescribed interval of half-width $1\le L\le H$, the bound is

$$q^{.665+o(1)}\sqrt{H\max(1,L/e)}. \tag{1}$$

The residue and the interval may be chosen after the cofactors; their number must be priced before taking a union. The same bounds hold with row positions exchanged. As usual $H=q^{.390+o(1)}$, the smoothness cutoff $X\asymp(\log q)^2$, and $W=q^{.780+o(1)}$. Only the objects of the [constructed lists](shared-row-labels.md) are counted.

**A spaced gap rectangle.** Fix both $h_i$ and prescribe $\delta_1=a_0\bmod s$. We claim a cost of $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{H\max(1,L/s)}$. For $s>L$ there are at most three possible gaps in the interval; apply the [translated rectangle bound](short-label-ratios.md) with half-widths $1,H$ for each. For $s\le L$ put $U=L/s$, $V=H$. In coordinates $(j,b)$ where $\delta_1=a_0+sj$, $\delta_2=b$, the box has half-widths $U,V$. Put $A=x_2/(x_1,x_2)$, $B=x_1/(x_1,x_2)$. We have $(A,B)=1$, $(AB,d)=1$, and $A\ge F_2>W$ because $F_2$ is coprime to $x_1$. Each actual point satisfies

$$N=A(a_0+sj)-Bb\ne0,\qquad d\mid N.$$

Nonvanishing follows from the nonzero row determinant. Choose a smooth $f\mid d$ crossing $8UV<f\le 8XUV$; this is possible since $d>Z/X>8XH^2$. Count only $f$ coprime to $AB$. For each $f$ the congruence is affine of index $f$ in $(j,b)$, since $B$ is a unit. Differences have determinant at most $8UV$, so all points are collinear. A vertical difference would require $f\mid\Delta b$ with $0<|\Delta b|\le2V$. A horizontal one would require $f\mid s\Delta j$ with $0<|s\Delta j|\le2L\le2V$. Both are impossible, **even if $s$ shares primes with $f$**.

If $m+1$ points occur, the shortest consecutive spacing on the line gives a difference $n v$ with $n>0$, $v$ primitive, and $|nv_i|\le(2U/m,2V/m)_i$. Both coordinates are nonzero. Put $k=(f,n)\le2U/m$. Then $f/k\mid Asv_1-Bv_2$, a nonzero polynomial-height integer: The exact primitive kernel direction has second coordinate of magnitude $As/(As,B)\ge A>2H$. For $m\ge2$ choose the first divisor $t$ obtained by multiplying smooth prime factors of $f/k$ until strictly exceeding $8UV/(mk)^2$, with overshoot at most $X$. The threshold is at least two by the bound on $k$ and is strictly below $f/k$. For fixed $k,t$, two vectors from the centred rectangle of half-widths $2U/(mk),2V/(mk)$ have determinant of absolute value at most $8UV/(mk)^2$. The direction lattice has index $t$ by $(B,t)=1$; hence there are at most two primitive $v$. The half-power smooth count gives $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{UV}/(mk)$ choices of $t$, and the nonzero linear form gives $q^{o(1)}$ choices of $f/k$. Summing over $k$ shows that at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{UV}/m$ moduli have this occupancy. The number of moduli with even one point is at most $q^{o(1)}\sqrt{UV}$ by their size; this also covers the $m=1$ tail. Summing in $m$ now counts the pairs of a modulus and a point. At each actual point recover $d$ by the nonzero physical linear form, then $c$ modulo $d$ from the first row at cost $O(X)$, forcing both $y_i$. This proves the claim. For the swapped restriction use the same argument with coordinates interchanged: Both physical coordinates of $n v$ after restoring the spacing are at most $2H$, while the physical exact kernel has primitive direction $(B,A)$ of norm at least $A>2H$.

**From gaps to products.** For $l=(h_2,e)$ only $l\mid a$ contributes. The gap is now in one class modulo $s=e/l$. The number of smooth $h_2$ divisible by $l$ is at most $q^{.3325+o(1)}/\sqrt l$, by counting $h_2/l\le q^{.665+o(1)}/l$. The other cofactor costs $q^{.3325+o(1)}$. Thus $l^{-1/2}\sqrt{\max(1,Ll/e)}\le\sqrt{\max(1,L/e)}$; sum over the subpowerly many divisors of the prescribed $e$. For the swapped restriction group $h_1$ instead. This proves (1) without assuming independence of either output.

**At the cap experiment's scale.** Set $e=q^{.1375+o(1)}$. On the full signed interval, one residue costs $q^{.98625+o(1)}$ per first incidence. The [pair-to-edge comparison](overlapping-lift-count.md) has at most $q^{.780+o(1)}M$ first incidences in an $M$-bucket (up to a constant); division by $DM$ at top degree prices this subcount at $q^{1.37625+o(1)}$. For a fixed list even without a first incidence, the same proof with a prescribed $e$ shows that any one residue accounts for at most $q^{.98625+o(1)}$ columns. A list of popularity $q^{.998+o(1)}$ therefore occupies at least $q^{.01175-o(1)}$ residues of this product. This is a spacing condition, not a bound on the popularity.

For the particular four classes of [the common-input experiment](common-product-weight.md) at this new conductor scale, the witness radius is only $q^{.860+o(1)}$. Since $h_i\ge A_-/(WX)=q^{.665+o(1)}$, membership in this box implies $|\delta_i|\le q^{.195+o(1)}$ for the corresponding gap. Equation (1) costs $q^{.665+(.390+.195-.1375)/2+o(1)}=q^{.88875+o(1)}$ even when **only one product** belongs to the witness. The finite union over classes and the two positions costs no exponent. No cap on $c$ and no restriction $e\mid d$ is needed. The interval alone already costs $.665+(.390+.195)/2=.9575$ by the old rectangular bound. The extra gain buys a bound below $.942$, the bottom rich-degree target, and the full-interval result does not require the witness radius.

On the benchmark subfamily $e\mid d$ one can do better: Since both products and the labels are units there, one residue for $z$ specifies one for $w$ by the label congruence, and hence fixes both gap residues after the cofactors. Write the gaps as $(a_1+ej,a_2+eb)$. The congruence is now affine with coefficients $A,-B$ modulo $d/e$, since $e\mid Aa_1-Ba_2$ for a soluble class. Apply the preceding occupancy count with index half-widths $U=q^{.0575+o(1)},V=q^{.2525+o(1)}$ in the benchmark box: $d/e>8XUV$, the coefficients are units, and a nonzero exact difference would give a physical kernel vector of norm at most $2H$. The nonzero form $N/e$ recovers $d/e$ by divisors. This gives exponent $.665+(.0575+.2525)/2=.820$ on that family across all its moduli. It is not necessary to control the remaining products *on those same moduli* to pass the $.998$ test there: Their aggregate smooth-numerator cap alone has exponent $.45875+.5275=.98625$.

By contrast, summing the full-interval price over all $e$ residues costs exponent $1.12375$, worse than the old $1.055$ bound. Do not turn the minimum number of occupied classes into cancellation. The dispersed remainder still needs arithmetic, for example the simultaneous smooth completion in the [forced-output handoff](forced-output-cuts.md). Neither an arbitrary-weight capped estimate nor an unrestricted edge bound follows.
