# Price the nonzero layers by their short witnesses

On the remaining exceptional splits of [the common-vector count](common-plane-vector.md), there is a bound on the whole fiber at its cheap filter height, including the nonzero layers. In the worst bin the multiplicity allowance is $q^{.006+o(1)}$, not $q^{.012+o(1)}$. This halves the exponent of the overhead, **not** the number of occupied splits. It still misses the budget. Here are two ways of trying to eliminate the varying first coordinate of the normal and the difference in their prices.

Keep the same one actual list, ordered filters and **untransformed** normals $n=(a,b,c)$. Put $T=RJ=q^\sigma$, $C=VH^2/T$, and bin the shortest scaled length of each saturated plane in $[h,2h)$. Write $S=\operatorname{diag}(1/V,1/H,1/H)$. The scaled dual length of $n$ is at most $C$, and the covolume of its scaled saturated plane is $\Delta=1/(\lambda R_0)\asymp 1/T$. Extend a shortest vector to a plane basis and reduce the second vector parallel to the first. Its parallel component is at most half the first length and its perpendicular component is $\Delta$ divided by that length. By shortestness, the perpendicular component is at least $\sqrt{3}/2$ times the first length. Thus $h^2\ll 1/T$ and the second length is $O(1/(Th))$.

**One primal lattice per ordered filter.** Let $d=f_Af_B$ and let $L$ be the scaled integral lattice cut out by $Fm\equiv qx\pmod{f_A}$ and $Fm\equiv qy\pmod{f_B}$ in the original coordinates $(m,x,y)$. Its determinant is $\delta=d/(VH^2)$ since the two last coordinates solve the two independent congruences at each $m$. Every assigned saturated plane lattice is contained in $L$, by the [annihilator identity](common-plane-vector.md). For any shortest vector $u=(m,x,y)$ of such a plane (length measured after applying $S$), the full factors $A,B$ divide $Fm-qx,Fm-qy$ respectively. Both integers are nonzero: $|x|,|y|\le 2Hh<F$ since $H=q^{.390+o(1)}$, $F=q^{.780+o(1)}$, and $h^2\ll 1/T$. Here $q$ is prime and $0<F<q$. If $Fm=qx$, this gives $x=m=0$, and then $cy=0$ in the plane. We have $c\ne0$, since otherwise $B\mid b$ and $B\mid qa+F(b+c)$ would imply $B\mid qa$, impossible as $B>1$ and $(B,qa)=1$. The other case uses $b\ne0$ symmetrically. The heights are polynomial. Each fixed witness therefore recovers divisor-many $g=AB$ across all splits for which it is a witness; one does not pay for the number of planes containing it.

Let $k_f$ count distinct represented $g$ in the bin assigned to this filter. Then

$$k_f\ll q^{o(1)}\left(1+\frac{Ch}{d}\right).$$

If there is only one plane, one witness suffices. Otherwise let $W$ be the span of the points of $L$ in the ball of radius $2h$. In rank one there are at most two possible shortest witnesses: They are primitive in the original $\mathbb Z^3$, since the planes are saturated. In rank three the number of points is $O(h^3/\delta)$. In rank two put $M=L\cap W$. At least one candidate plane differs from $W$, so one of its two basis vectors is outside $W$ and has length $O(1/(Th))$. Call this scaled vector $v$. Since $M+\mathbb Zv$ has full rank in $L$, $\det M\,\operatorname{dist}(v,W)\ge\delta$. The point count in $W$ is thus $O(h^2/\det M)=O(h/(T\delta))$. In both volume counts the short points **span** the space being counted: Choose independent ones, pack translates of their parallelepiped in a ball of radius $O(h)$ in each coset of their span lattice, and sum over its index. There is no uncharged long final minimum. Rank three fits the same bound because $h^2\ll1/T$; an empty filter needs no witnesses. These cases prove the estimate.

**Why two layers do not give divisor cost per filter.** Fix just one reference plane in the bin and its reduced basis $u_1,u_2$ in integral coordinates. Put $h_i=\|Su_i\|$, so $h_1h_2\ll 1/T$. For each target, $k_i=n\cdot u_i/d$ are integers of absolute value at most $Ch_i/d$. If both vanish use $u_1$; otherwise reduce the pair to its primitive projective direction $(p_1:p_2)$ and use $w=p_2u_1-p_1u_2$. Then $n\cdot w=0$ over the integers. No cancelling a nonunit modulo $g$ is needed. The nonzero vector $w$ is fixed *per direction*. Its length after scaling is $O(C/(dT))$. At $d>D=Cq^{-\tau}$ for the cheap cutoff $\tau=.229$ or the balanced cutoff $\tau=\sigma/2$, we have $H C/(dT)\ll H q^{\tau}/T<F$. Hence the two evaluations on $w$ are nonzero by the same argument, using the reference plane, and recover the target's full $A,B$. The axes cost two directions irrespective of the nonzero layer magnitude. The count of candidate non-axis directions is at most $4\lfloor Ch_1/d\rfloor\lfloor Ch_2/d\rfloor$, not subpower merely because the varying $a$ has disappeared.

**Cost on the same inputs.** First crossings of $\sqrt D$ on both sides are available at both cutoffs in the remaining bins: $\sigma\ge .446-o(1)$, $a/(F,a)>q^{.323}$ implies $\sigma\le .457+o(1)$ by $Ta\le H^2$, and $A,B\gg q^{1.212-o(1)}/J$ with $J\le q^{.011+o(1)}$. Pay $q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$ for the filters, including overshoot, and $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$ per recovered $g$ for smooth $\lambda$, divisor splits, and actual triples. Bins cost subpower. Write $h=q^{-\mu}$, so $\mu\ge\sigma/2-o(1)$. The two upper bounds on the triple exponent are respectively

$$1.250-\tau/2+\max(0,\tau-\mu),\qquad
1.250-\tau/2+\max(0,2\tau-\sigma),$$

up to $o(1)$. At $\tau=.229$, $\sigma=.446$, the first bound is at most $1.1415$, versus $1.1475$ by direction enumeration. Neither beats the prior $1.1385$ overall. The worst allowed $\mu=\sigma/2$ balances both envelopes at $\tau=\sigma/2$, giving $1.250-\sigma/4$, or $1.1385$ at $.446$. These are optimized **upper-bound formulas**, not lower bounds for represented directions or actual triples.

**Decision for the next count.** At the cheap height keep the one-vector central-layer recovery. For the remainder, use the whole-fiber witness cap above rather than charging all candidate directions. For example, with $N=q^{o(1)}\sqrt D$ the potential-filter budget, it suffices that at most $Nq^{-.0055-\eta}$ filters per bin have two distinct represented planes **and** two independent shortest witnesses among those planes, for some $0<\eta<.0005$. Choose just one shortest witness per plane, with fixed tie breaking. All other filters cost divisor-many $g$; on this exceptional set the cap is $q^{.006+o(1)}$. The sum is then $\ll N q^{.0005-\eta+o(1)}$, within the strict triple budget after multiplying by $q^{o(1)}\sqrt T$. This is a sufficient frequency test, not a prediction of rarity. A weaker weighted tail on *distinct recovered indices* would do. The ordinary labels are outside this argument, and the unrestricted $q^{.998}$ incidence target is open.
