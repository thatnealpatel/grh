## Next worker: One paired gate, not two censuses

For the shared pure second-output test let $H(r)=.9284+.06r$.
Use the same actual off-screen count $C_{\rm off}$ at both
radii. For target exponent $1.1354$, a uniform lower
fraction $q^{-a-o(1)}$ suffices when $a\le1.1354-H(r)$:

| Radius | Hit upper exponent | Sufficient fraction exponent $a$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $1.300$ | $1.0064$ | $.1290$ |
| $1.475$ | $1.0169$ | $.1185$ |

Thus the relaxation must improve the *certified* hit
fraction by a factor of $q^{.0105}$ just to break even in
this ledger, up to subpower errors. The same target is
below the earlier exceptional price $1.1355$ and the
budget $1.136$. For just a strict saving below $1.136$,
the two loss exponents need fixed slack below $.1296$
and $.1191$, respectively. Improving the complement below
$1.1355$ does not improve the combined exponent. Equality
at the break-even gain ties the two implied complement
bounds; it is not a reason to prefer
either radius. This compares guarantees made with these
passing prices, not the actual optimal capture at either
radius.

If an admissible [pre-screen export][coverage] becomes
available, make the widened screen, its tie rule, and the
ordered residual once. For one frozen $B$, score each
allowed $E$ on **all** actual off-screen edges at $v$ via
membership of $[S]$ in $B+\mathbb Q^E$, then maximize the
count separately at the two radius cutoffs. Sum the maxima
over $v$. This uses one erasure per input at each radius,
not a union of separately erasable edges. If $B$ is seed
generated, tag seed edges and short-certificate hits
separately. A finite gain or miss is evidence only for the
specified list and space; a miss requires an exact optimizer
or an upper certificate, and a gain cannot establish a
uniform coverage theorem.

Without that export, the paired comparison is an analytic
premise, not a runnable census. One exact discriminator is
available for the same frozen $B$ and its pivots: The
large-radius saddle $x=y=.635$ costs only $.9294$ in the
small-radius moving-reference code. The small-radius
saddle $x=y=.810$ still costs at most $1.0064$ in the
large-radius fixed-reference code. Up to $o(1)$ in
shrinking neighborhoods, neither saddle can contribute
the other radius's full passing exponent. The two radii
thus cannot reach their respective caps on the same
saddle bin. These arithmetic tests do not prove that
either bin occurs. No nonseed coverage, bound
on the unrestricted complement, or RH/GRH result follows.
No Alpöge input is used.
