**Exclude the zero space at radius $.530$.** Use the scope
and notation of [the three-ratio price][three]. If $B=0$:

1. For $j\ne0$, the two isolated first-output prime sets
   in the proof leave weight $1.904-.530-o(1)=1.374-o(1)$
   after erasure. On these primes only $z$ is nonzero, so
   the relation cannot vanish.
2. For $j=0$, $k'\ne0$, any prime of $S$ not erased must
   occur in $D$. For a prime of $d$ meeting the raw support
   of $S$, its meeting $h$ is impossible by primitivity,
   and its meeting $Z$ costs at most the one shared sorted
   boundary prime. Any meeting with $kZ_0$ is paid by the
   opposite-support mask on $dY$. Swap endpoints for $e$.
   Thus the intersection of the supports of $D$ and $S$
   has weight at most $.236+o(1)$, and $\operatorname{rad}S$
   (the product on the signed support) is at most
   $q^{.766+o(1)}$. Half-power enumeration, valuations
   on the known support, and [global exact $S$ recovery][s]
   price this branch at $q^{.383+o(1)}$.
3. For the pure $D$ branch, enumerate its radical of size
   at most $q^{.530}$ and the signed valuations on that
   support. This costs $q^{.265+o(1)}$. The smooth gcd
   $(d,e)\le q^{.118+o(1)}$ costs $q^{.059+o(1)}$; the
   fixed-pair fiber costs $q^{.390+o(1)}$ by the pure
   branch in [the proof][three]. Hence this branch costs
   $q^{.714+o(1)}$ directly.
