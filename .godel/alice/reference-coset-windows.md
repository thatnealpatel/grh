# Count the whole reference coset, not just the shared keys

Use the [partial scalar bound](partial-scalar-classes.md) on an actual constructed list, at one $d$ and $g=R=j=1$. The new test has the same $q^{.3475+o(1)}$ outgoing allowance as sharing an output key. It does **not** test divisibility of either output of the second column.

Fix an actual reference $(n^0,t^0)$ in this fiber. Name a set $S$ of $O(\log q/\log\log q)$ eligible primes at the reference, fixed across its neighbors. Write $a_S$ for the full $S$-part of a positive integer $a$, $Q=q^{.120}$, and $L=2W/Q^3=q^{.420+o(1)}$. For a candidate put $z=t-t^0$ and $v=n-n^0$. Count $z=0$, or, for $z\ne0$ and $m=\gcd(|z|,|v_1|,|v_2|)$, count
$$\frac{|z|}{|z|_S\,m_S}\le L.$$
The number of actual numerators passing is at most $q^{.3475+o(1)}$ for the entire test. One reference per $d$ costs $q^{.875+o(1)}$ across denominators in this stratum. Testing every reference instead bounds its outgoing degree, not the total number of pairs by the same price.

**Why this includes the old test.** Choose $S=\operatorname{supp}(y_1^0 y_2^0)$. If two columns share an old key, then for the reference seeds $f_i$ we have $f_1 f_2\mid z$ and $f_1\mid v_1$ by the first row equation. Since $F_2v_1-F_1v_2=dz$ and $(F_1,f_1)=1$, also $f_1\mid v_2$. This puts the candidate in a *reference coset*, whether or not its outputs are tested. For $z\ne0$ the product $|z|_S m_S$ is at least $f_1^2 f_2\ge Q^3$, and $|z|\le2W$. The new test also allows the primes to come from either reference output, with different multiplicities, or a shorter determinant difference to make up the deficit. This inclusion has only one direction. Sharing a key is still the correct test for a dictionary of output divisors.

**Menu and price.** The hit condition is a union of the windows
$$ e\mid z,\qquad b\mid v_1,v_2,\qquad |z|\le L e b,$$
where $b\mid e$, both are $S$-supported, and $e b\le X^2 Q^3$. Indeed for $z\ne0$, take $e$ from $|z|_S$ and $b$ from $m_S$. For each prime, use a paired copy in $e,b$ for each occurrence in $m_S$ and a single in $e$ for each remaining occurrence in $|z|_S$. Stop when $eb\ge |z|/L$, starting at $(1,1)$. All atoms have size at most $X^2$, and the target is at most $Q^3$. Conversely $e\mid |z|_S$ and $b\mid m_S$ on such a window. For $z=0$ use $(1,1)$. The entire menu is subpower by exponent-vector counting on the **fixed** $S$; in particular it is not a union over the supports of differences. We always have $b\le X Q^{3/2}=q^{.180+o(1)}$.

Fix one window. Write $n=n^0+b u$, $t=t^0+e s$. In the chart $(u_1,s)$,
$$F_2u_1-F_1u_2=d(e/b)s,\qquad |s|\le L b.$$
The box has half-widths $B/b,L b$, with $B=UH=q^{1.055+o(1)}$; its first interval is translated. Its relative area parameter is $V=BL/F_1=q^{.695+o(1)}$, independent of the two moduli. Apply the [translated-box sieve](partial-scalar-classes.md#sieve-in-the-translated-box) in its first chart. Here are the conditions for using it with these new widths and steps:

* The base index is $F_1$ by coprimality of the labels. Mask the eligible primes of $e$ and of the nonzero spatial coordinates' product of the exceptional direction when both are nonzero, as in that sieve. For an unmasked cofactor split $a_1a_2$, imposing $a_i\mid n_i$ on a nonempty filter multiplies the difference index by $a_1 a_2$. Indeed $b$ is a unit there; after $w_1=a_1 h$ the difference congruence is $F_2a_1 h\equiv d(e/b) w_s\pmod{F_1a_2}$. Its coefficients and modulus have gcd one since $(F_1,F_2)=1$, $(d,F_1a_1a_2)=1$, and $(e,a_1a_2)=1$. This also holds for every subfilter. There is no $(d,e)=1$ hypothesis.
* In the centered fallback the original $V_0=B^2/d=q^{1.055+o(1)}$ and $V_0/(h_1h_2)\le q^{-.275+o(1)}$ are unchanged; $X\ll V < V_0$. Thus a masked crossing of $V_0/V$ and an extension past $64V_0$ cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$. Otherwise the unmasked part suffices to cross $64V$ in the translated chart.
* On a nonsingleton line neither spatial difference coordinate is zero: The other would be a multiple of $d$, too long since $2B/b<d$. On the exceptional direction an unmasked filter advances by the whole filter size in base steps. Its run has at most $2Lb$ steps if $w_s\ne0$, and at most $2B/(bF_1)$ otherwise. Both are $\le2V$ since $B/(bF_1)\ge q^{.095-o(1)}$ and $Lb\ge1$. The ordinary-line determinant and divisor count uses just the two half-widths, the subfilter indices, and the nonzero spatial coordinates, as in the cited chart. No primitivity of $u$ or of $v$ is needed.

The resulting $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$ includes the splits of the actual $n$; recovery at $R=j=1$ scans only $O(X)$ possible numerators per split. This proves the price after summing the menu. It does not treat every point of a relaxed window as a successful column.

**Next decision.** On a putative actual fiber $U$ of size $>q^{.4705-\eta}$, it now suffices to force a fraction $\ge q^{-.123+2\eta}$ of *ordered* pairs to pass the displayed window test. Averaging would contradict the outgoing bound for large $q$. The reference mask can include, for instance, the eligible primes of its gaps as well as its outputs; their union still has the stated size. Seek this lower bound for reduced determinant differences, not necessarily a common divisor of the outputs at both endpoints. Just putting $t$ into intervals of length $L$ supplies a Cauchy lower bound of order $L/W=q^{-.360+o(1)}$, short of the requested scale. No argument here supplies the extra concentration, treats all $R,j$, or establishes an unrestricted list bound.
