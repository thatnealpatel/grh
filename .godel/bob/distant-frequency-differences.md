# A signed difference count between distant prime supports

At the genuine center $t=2q^{3/5}$, one can square a detected excess without losing the balanced selections, the rounded prime-pair gate, or the **linear log frequency**. The resulting signed count compares pairs of supports both lying in a mildly enlarged frequency window. At the squared detection scale all pairs for which too many starts of either support approach the other support can be discarded absolutely. This last deletion is in a two-copy measure, not a deletion of their sources from the original one-copy measure.

Use [the separated test](separated-signed-tuples.md) at one admissible $d$, $A>a_0>0$, $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $L=\log t/D$, $n=(4+2\gamma)L+O(1)$, $0<\gamma<1/2$. Set $\kappa=\gamma/2$, $R_*=X^\xi$, $\xi=\gamma/[16(2+\gamma)]$. Let $W$ be smooth and even, valued in $[0,1]$, equal to 1 on $[-1/2,1/2]$, and supported on $[-1,1]$. Give each original ordered, separated, balanced tuple its product weight times $W(t^{1-\kappa}\nu)$, imposing the original completed-tuple gate. Call the measure $\mu$. Thus one designated positive start must equal the rounded candidate and be a prime pair in the band, and there must be more than $(1+\gamma)L$ private slots at $R_*$. On the support of the taper the true omitted start is necessarily recovered: $|x-p|=O(X^2/(d t^{1-\kappa}))$ by [the recovery identity](last-slot-residues.md). This does **not** mean the low-private constraint can be ignored throughout the enlarged window. Separation inside each tuple is at the chosen test radius ($R_*$ or $D^2$).

Put $s=\lceil(3+\gamma)L\rceil$. Call two tuples distant if **in each direction** fewer than $s$ starts are within distance $R_*$ of any start of the other tuple. Hence each has at least $(1+\gamma)L-O(1)$ starts remote from the entire opposing support, even for the $D^2$ test. Put

$$E_{\rm far}=\iint_{\rm distant}G(t(\nu-\nu'))\,d\mu\,d\mu',\qquad G(z)=(1-|z|/(2\pi))_+\cos z.$$

The integration uses two independent copies with all unnormalized weights. In particular there is no division by central mass, and the omitted starts in the two gates need not be the same. For any positive $B=t^{-1+o(1)}$, at a shift with $J_R\ge B$ one necessarily has

$$E_{\rm far}\ge (1-o(1))B^2. \tag{1}$$

For example one may use $B=\delta(X/D)(1/16)^n/t$ for fixed $\delta>0$ at the $D^2$ radius. This is a signed difference test, not an upper bound on the number of nearly equal frequencies. The implication is one-way: A positive two-copy excess does not itself certify a positive one-copy excess.

**Squaring the actual kernel.** With $\operatorname{sinc}(y)=\sin(y)/y$, let $K_t$ be the average of the two densities $t^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(\pi(u/t\pm1))$. It is a nonnegative probability density with angular transform $G(t\nu)$. On the kernel support the taper is identically one eventually; the low-private exclusion costs $O(t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)})$ there by the last-slot proof, and costs nothing for the power-radius test. Thus $S=\int G(t\nu)\,d\mu$ differs from $J_R$ by at most that error. Write $\widehat\mu(u)=\int e^{iu\nu}\,d\mu$. Probability Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$ |S|^2=\left|\int K_t(u)\widehat\mu(u)\,du\right|^2
 \le\int K_t(u)|\widehat\mu(u)|^2\,du
 =\iint G(t(\nu-\nu'))\,d\mu\,d\mu'.$$

There is no sum of absolute Fourier coefficients in the last expression. No approximation of a nonlinear difference of two rounded coordinates is required.

**Price the complement at the squared scale.** First price the original probability of $|\nu|\le t^{-1+\kappa}$ by hiding $j_1=\lfloor\log(t^{1-\kappa}/8)/(2D)\rfloor$ positive slots in the first tuple. Different sums of their log labels have spacing $\ge X^{-2j_1}$ by comparing integer cross products. The permitted interval thus holds at most one such sum, fixing the multiset by the prime valuation at the least differing start. At most $j_1!$ orderings remain. Since $b_0=8D^2/(a_0 X)$ bounds a start probability and $\log n=D/2+O(\log D)$, the probability is at most $j_1! b_0^{j_1}\le t^{-(1-\kappa)/4+o(1)}$. Ignore the gates only for this upper bound.

Given the entire first tuple, mark $s$ labels of the second that must approach the first support. Each costs at most $n(2R_*+1)b_0\le X^{-1/2+\xi+o(1)}$. Leave a further $j_2=\lfloor\log(t/32)/(2D)\rfloor$ unmarked labels hidden, possible since $n-s>(1+\gamma/2)L$. Given the disclosed labels of the second tuple, the condition $|t(\nu'-\nu)|\le2\pi$ restricts the hidden signed sum to an interval shorter than $X^{-2j_2}$. The one possible sum fixes the signed multiplicities, again by prime valuations. The hidden labels may now have mixed signs, but their starts are distinct in a separated completion; hence at most $j_2!$ assignments remain. This costs an additional $t^{-1/4+o(1)}$ uniformly in the first tuple and every disclosure, without treating the product law conditioned on success as independent. Reversing the two tuples covers the other bad direction. With the $\binom ns$ choices, $|G|\le1$, and $A^{2n}=t^{o(1)}$, the total absolute weight charged is at most $t^{-b+o(1)}$, where

$$ b=\frac{1-\kappa}4+(3+\gamma)(1/2-\xi)+\frac14
 =2+\frac{3\gamma}{8}-(3+\gamma)\xi>2+\frac\gamma4.$$

This is $o(B^2)$ even at the squared detector scale. Combining the two steps proves (1).

**The distant positive core is power-sized on the squared scale.** This is true at every shift under consideration, not just under (1). Put $T=4t^{1-\kappa}$. Test the original power-separated tuples by $(1-T|\nu|)_+$. Its inverse density $(2\pi T)^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(u/(2T))$ is nonnegative and $\gg 1/T$ on $|u|\le cX/(nD)$, where $|Z_d(u)|^n\gg A^n$. The arbitrary-density separated comparison gives unnormalized mass at least $\gg e^{-\eta}A^n X/(TnD)-O(t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)})\ge t^{-1+\kappa-o(1)}$ in $|\nu|\le1/T$. These power-separated tuples pass both possible separation tests and the private-slot gate; their designated start is recovered, and their taper is 1. This argument supplies an actual lower bound without extending a one-copy absolute discard to this broader window.

Partition $[-1/T,1/T]$ into $O(t^\kappa)$ intervals of length at most $1/(2t)$. The mass of pairs in the same cell is the sum of squared cell masses, hence at least $t^{-2+\kappa-o(1)}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. There $G(t(\nu-\nu'))\ge(1-1/(4\pi))\cos(1/2)>0$. Subtracting the bad-pair cost above still leaves $\gg t^{-2+\kappa-o(1)}$ in the **weighted positive part** $P_2=\iint_{\rm distant}G_+(t(\nu-\nu'))\,d\mu\,d\mu'$. In particular an unsigned estimate for distant pairs is already too large by a *power* of $t$. To prove $E_{\rm far}\le O(B^2)$, the actual weighted negative annulus would have to supply at least $(1-t^{-\kappa+o(1)})P_2$, since it is the only negative part. This accuracy requirement is for the two-copy sufficient test, not a necessary consequence of a small $J_R$.

**How many distinct rational differences could carry the excess?** On the distant family, even allowing arbitrary collisions of the two signed tuples, the total pair weight at any *one* value of $e^{\nu-\nu'}$ is at most $t^{-2-\tau+o(1)}$, $\tau=3/4+11\gamma/8$. This is not a random-spacing assumption. For a realizable rational value its complete signed incidence difference is unique by the least-start valuation argument. Denote its support by $P$; $|P|\le2n$. A start occurring in either tuple but not in $P$ must occur in both with the same sign since the tuples are internally separated. Suppose there are $g$ of these invisible starts. Then $g<s$ by distance. Mark their labels in both tuples, with only $2^{O(n)}$ mask choices. All $2(n-g)$ other labels must lie in the *fixed* pool $P$, costing $(2nb_0)^{2(n-g)}$. Reveal those labels first. On the first tuple, hide $j=\min(g,j_1)$ of its remaining labels until last in that tuple; the taper interval and spacing cost $j!b_0^j$ under internal separation, also for mixed signs. Once this first tuple is known, each of the $g$ remaining second labels must hit its support, costing $(nb_0)^g$ in total. The saving exponent is at least $(2n-g+\min(g,j_1))/(2L)-o(1)$, minimized at maximal $g$. Substituting $s$ for $g$ there gives $C-(3+\gamma)/2+(1-\kappa)/4=2+\tau$, where $C=4+2\gamma$. This includes both product weights since $A^{2n}=t^{o(1)}$.

Thus deleting even $t^{\tau-\delta}$ prescribed rational differences, for fixed $0<\delta<\tau$, changes (1)'s signed observable by $o(B^2)$. Equivalently an excess of size $B$ forces at least $t^{\tau-o(1)}$ distinct rational differences with positive kernel among the distant pairs; the negative part can only increase the necessary positive weight. The bound is uniform in the rational, so ranking the difference atoms by their exact total mass does not evade it. This prices individual **values**, not intervals holding many distinct values.

**What this tests.** Form $N_2(z)$ by counting these distant pairs at $|t(\nu-\nu')|\le z$ with the two measures above. Then

$$ E_{\rm far}=-\int_0^{2\pi}G'(z)\left[N_2(z)-\frac z{2\pi}N_2(2\pi)\right]dz .$$

Indeed $G(y)=-\int_y^{2\pi}G'(z)dz$ for $0\le y\le2\pi$, and $\int_0^{2\pi}G=0$. Here $e^{\nu-\nu'}=UW'/(WU')$ for the actual balanced numerator-denominator products of each tuple. In the negative annulus $\pi/2<|t(\nu-\nu')|<3\pi/2$ the weight is genuinely negative. The taper need not itself introduce a squared-scale energy from a smooth background: For a bounded density $g$ of uniformly bounded first derivative in the broad window, the density $f(\nu)=g(\nu)W(t^{1-\kappa}\nu)$ satisfies $\|f'\|_2^2\ll t^{1-\kappa}$. Its unrestricted self-energy is $\ll t^{-3}\|f'\|_2^2\ll t^{-2-\kappa}$ by Plancherel and $K_t(u)\ll u^2/t^3$ (Taylor expansion near $u=0$, boundedness elsewhere). This is a diagnostic, not a density or derivative bound for actual prime tuples. A useful next comparison is between the small-defect and negative-annulus counts for the reduced rational $UW'/(WU')$, with at least $(1+\gamma)L-O(1)$ remote starts per side. An unsigned bound on the full pair moment or a count omitting the two taper and gate factors is not this signed shape estimate. A [compact-window harmonic witness](windowed-harmonic-witness.md) avoids the broad-window positive-core obstruction by using a circular harmonic and two frequencies inside the original window. It does not restrict their difference to the original window. The [one-copy frequency band](rounded-slot-frequency-band.md) gives an alternative without squaring, together with the loss in applying the ordinary additive large sieve to its main block.
