# An arc test on a single pool of distinct prime endpoints

In the [fixed harmonic test](windowed-harmonic-witness.md), distant supports can still share starts, even starts invisible in the rational difference. One can forbid **every** cross collision. This is a signed comparison, not an absolute estimate for the pairs removed. It preserves the detector with amplitude slack, and preserves its exact amplitude on the common-dip branch at radius $(\log X)^2$.

Freeze at $t=2q^{3/5}$. Use $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $L=\log t/D$, $h=\lceil(2+\gamma)L\rceil$, $n=2h$, $0<\gamma<1/2$, and $R_*=X^{\gamma/(16(2+\gamma))}$. At $1\le d\le5D$ use the actual weighted starts $p,p+d$ prime in $[e^{-8}X,X]$, $w_p=X^{-1}\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$, $A=\sum w_p>a_0>0$, and $\lambda_p=\log(1+d/p)$. Fix $r=D^2$ or $R_*$. Write $I$ for the integrated arc difference in the fixed harmonic test at this radius. Define $H_r$ by exactly the same weights, arcs, balance, cuts, and completed gates, but require **all $2n$ starts** to be pairwise at distance $>r$. Keep also the old distance condition (in each direction fewer than $\lceil(3+\gamma)L\rceil$ starts approach the opposite support within $R_*$). Only at $r=D^2$ can the latter be an additional restriction.

**Comparison.** For some uniform $\eta=o(n)$, $\eta\ge0$, and $E_2=t^{-2-\gamma/4+o(1)}$,
$$
 e^{-\eta}\max(0,I)-E_2\ \le\ H_r\ \le\ e^{\eta}\max(0,I)+E_2. \tag{1}
$$
Thus $J_r\ge B=t^{-1+o(1)}$ implies $H_r\ge e^{-o(n)}B^2$. This does **not** let one replace $I$ by $H_r$ at the identical fixed margin. There is a separate conclusion at the endpoint amplitude $a=1/16$: Under the [common-dip failure hypothesis](detection-radius.md) on $[t-bX/D,t+bX/D]$, at $r=D^2$ one has on $\gg_\varepsilon D$ shifts
$$
 H_r\gg_{b,c,\varepsilon,\gamma} \left(\frac{X}{D}\frac{a^n}{t}\right)^2. \tag{2}
$$
In particular an $o(D)$ exceptional set at every fixed positive multiple of this scale would suffice for the common dip. Equation (2) does not assume that the sets of large shifts are constant through the window.

## Project two copies at once

Write $Z(u)=\sum w_p e^{iu\lambda_p}$. Use independent heights of common probability density $K$ and independent uniform angles $\theta_1,\theta_2$. First take the even part of $t^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(\pi(u/t-1))$, with $\operatorname{sinc}(v)=\sin(v)/v$; its transform is $G(t\nu)=T(t\nu)\cos(t\nu)$, $T(z)=(1-|z|/(2\pi))_+$. Set $P_0=\int K|Z|^n$. For fixed heights and angles form a graph on two copies of the starts, joining any two vertices at start distance $\le r$, including the two versions of the same start. Its independence polynomial $P(z_1,z_2)$ uses activities $z_i x_{i,p}$, $x_{i,p}=w_p\cos(\theta_i+u_i\lambda_p)$. Put $F_{kl}=k!l![z_1^k z_2^l]P$, $S_i=\sum_p x_{i,p}$. Flip the signs of an entire color to make $S_i=s_i=|S_i|$; $F_{nn}$ is unchanged since $n$ is even.

Let $w_*=\max w_p$, $B_0=C(1+r)w_*$ for a sufficiently large absolute $C$. For $i\ge1,j\ge0$ put $c_{ij}=[z_1^{i-1}z_2^j]\partial_1\log P$. Then $c_{10}=s_1$ and $|c_{ij}|\le4A B_0^{i+j-1}$. Indeed the vertex-deletion argument of the [one-color comparison](separated-signed-tuples.md) applies on the bidisk $|z_i|\le1/B_0$: The maximum degree is $O(1+r)$, and every vertex-to-deletion ratio is $1+\alpha_v$, $|\alpha_v|\le2|z_{i(v)}|w_p$, on every induced subgraph. This follows inductively by removing the neighbors one at a time, at total inverse-ratio cost at most $(1-2w_*/B_0)^{-O(1+r)}\le2$. In $z_1\partial_1\log P$ sum $\alpha_v/(1+\alpha_v)$ only over color 1, so $|\partial_1\log P|\le4A$. Bivariate Cauchy gives the claim, and likewise in color 2.

The coefficient recurrence for $k>0$ is
$$
 F_{kl}=\sum_{1\le i\le k,\ 0\le j\le l} c_{ij}(k-1)_{\underline{i-1}}l_{\underline j} F_{k-i,l-j},
$$
with the interchanged recurrence for $l>0$. Set $s_0=t^{-(1+\gamma/8)/n}$, $e_0=s_0^n$. For $k,l\le n$ and $s_1,s_2\ge s_0$, induct in total degree to bound **both** incoming ratios, in direction $v$ by $s_v(1+O(\epsilon_0))$, $\epsilon_0=nAB_0/s_0^2=o(1)$. In fact, assuming preceding ratios are $\ge s_v/2$, divide the first recurrence by $s_1 F_{k-1,l}$. To bound each lower coefficient walk down $i-1$ steps in the first direction and $j$ in the second, all using the induction hypothesis. The terms of total index $m+1\ge2$ in the relative error cost at most $(4A/s_0)(m+1)(2nB_0/s_0)^m$. Summing gives $O(\epsilon_0)$, closing both inductions from $F_{00}=1$. The small parameters hold as in the one-color proof, since $w_*\le8D^2/X$, $A=O(D^2)$, and $r\le R_*$ eventually. Multiplication along a path to $(n,n)$ compares $F_{nn}$ to $s_1^ns_2^n$ within $e^{\pm o(n)}$ on this region.

Everywhere, $|F_{kl}|\le U_1^k U_2^l$, $U_i=(1+C'\epsilon_0)\max(s_i,s_0)$. For the induction divide by the proposed majorant: The first term costs at most $(1+C'\epsilon_0)^{-1}$ and the rest $O(\epsilon_0)$ by the same series, so choose $C'$ large. Axes use just the nonempty direction. With $C_n=2^n/\binom nh$, normalized angular averaging $C_n^2\mathbb E_{\theta_1,\theta_2}$ selects the ordered balanced lists in each color: Each allowed pair of unordered sets and specified sign subsets has coefficient $(h!)^4$. Also $C_n\mathbb E_{\theta_i}s_i^n=|Z(u_i)|^n$. On the union where one $s_i<s_0$, independence of heights and angles now bounds the normalized integral of **either** $|F_{nn}|$ or $s_1^n s_2^n$ by $E(P_0+E)$, writing $E=t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)}$ with subpower losses included. Indeed the small factor contributes at most $C_ne_0$, and the other at most $P_0+C_ne_0$, before the $e^{o(n)}$ envelope and the two choices. A uniform one-copy error times a trivial supremum for the other color would not work.

Consequently the signed sum $H^{\log}$ with the joint separation, multiplier $G(t\nu)G(t\nu')$, and no privacy or old distance cuts satisfies
$$
 e^{-o(n)}P_0^2-E(P_0+E)\le H^{\log}\le e^{o(n)}P_0^2+E(P_0+E). \tag{3}
$$
The argument to this point works for any common even probability density $K$ with its own real transform in place of $G$.

## Return to the completed prime-pair gates

Inside the support of $G$ the designated positive slot is recovered automatically by nearest-integer rounding, in either signing. At $R_*$ privacy is automatic too. At $D^2$ the [private-slot estimate](last-slot-residues.md) prices a compact-window tuple with at most $(1+\gamma)L$ private slots at $R_*$ by absolute product mass $E$. This is **not** a squared-scale absolute deletion. Fix one such first tuple. The unrestricted second signed sum is a one-color projection on the induced graph excluding the $r$-neighborhoods of the disclosed starts. Its linear projection differs from the original by at most $n(2r+1)w_*=o(s_0)$. Apply the absolute coefficient envelope above on this graph (degree and total weight only decrease), using the original bounds $A,B_0$: Its integrated signed sum has modulus at most $e^{o(n)}(P_0+C_n e_0)$, uniformly in the disclosure. Indeed $\max(|S_{\rm induced}|,s_0)\le(1+o(1))\max(|S|,s_0)$. The first bad tuple therefore costs $E(P_0+E)$ after summing its weight. Repeat with the colors reversed; the intersection of the bad events costs at most $E^2$ absolutely by dropping all cross constraints. Inclusion-exclusion thus preserves (3) for the two gates. Impose the old distance condition now, at absolute cost $t^{-2-\gamma/4+o(1)}$ or better by the compact two-window nondistant charge; a subset cannot increase this absolute cost.

Reversal of either block signing preserves all these conditions on the kernel support. Hence $G(t\nu)G(t\nu')$ can be replaced **in the sum** by $T(t\nu)T(t\nu')\cos(t\nu-t\nu')$. Use the [quadratic comparison on the same subfamily](windowed-harmonic-witness.md) to replace each coordinate $z=t\nu$ by $a=y-y^2/(2t)$, $y=t(e^\nu-1)$, $|y|\le8$, at absolute cost $t^{-5/2+o(1)}$. This gives $H_r$ by the arc identity. No reversal symmetry is required of the quadratic coordinate. Absorb the mixed error in (3) by Young's inequality, spending at most half the lower main term and $t^{-2-\gamma/4+o(1)}$ (since $e^{o(n)}=t^{o(1)}$). This gives a two-sided comparison of $H_r$ to $P_0^2$ with $e^{\pm o(n)}$ factors and additive $E_2$. The one-color comparison under the same $K$ bounds $J_r$ between $e^{\pm o(n)}P_0$ up to $E$; the fixed harmonic theorem bounds $|J_r-\sqrt{\max(0,I)}|$ by $E$. Squaring the resulting nonnegative bounds, with the same absorption, proves (1).

## Keep the amplitude on common-dip shifts

Use the retained shifts from the proof of [the endpoint detector](detection-radius.md): Each has $n^2 C_d(r)\le K_0$ for a fixed $K_0$, where $C_d(r)=\sum_{|p-p'|\le r}w_pw_{p'}$, and $|Z(u)|\ge a-o(1/n)$ on a fixed fraction of the frozen interval of length $2bX/D$. On $s_1,s_2\ge a/2$ improve both degree-two bounds in each recurrence. The coefficient of $z_j$ in $\partial_i\log P$ is exactly
$$
 -\sum_{|p-p'|\le r}x_{i,p}x_{j,p'}.
$$
For $i=j$ this counts the diagonal and both orders of each edge; for $i\ne j$ the conflicting same-start clones are also an edge. Either way the modulus is $\le C_d(r)$. The relative error summed along a path of at most $2n$ ratios is therefore $O_a(n^2 C_d(r)+n^3 A B_0^2)=O_{a,K_0}(1)$, using the previous ratio lower bounds and summing the geometric tail starting at $m=2$. Here $B_0=O(D^4/X)$, so the second term tends to zero. On these projections $F_{nn}\gg_{a,K_0}s_1^n s_2^n$.

Take both heights in the incidence set for this same shift and each angle within $1/\sqrt n$ of alignment. The two normalized angle widths cost only a constant, and the even density $K$ is $\gg1/t$ on the frozen interval. This region alone contributes a constant multiple of the right-hand side of (2). The rest of $s_1,s_2\ge s_0$ is nonnegative. The total possible negative projection and gate error is $E(P_0+E)+O(E_2)$. If $P_0\le t^{-1+\gamma/32}$ this is power-saving on the scale (2). Otherwise (3) itself, after the gate and coordinate transfers, gives a larger lower bound than (2) by a power of $t$. This proves (2) in both cases; one cannot simply subtract $E$ times a trivial upper bound for the other tuple.

## The arithmetic gain and the remaining sign

* Each difference $Q=e^{\nu-\nu'}$ in the new test is a reduced squarefree ratio with **exactly $2n$ primes on either side**. All $4n$ endpoints are distinct since $r>d$. Its signed list of starts (invert the signs of the second copy) is unique: At the least start of disagreement of two incidence lists the incoming edges agree, so its prime valuation would disagree. There are no invisible common starts. At fixed $Q$ all labels must therefore hit a fixed pool of $2n$ starts. The total unnormalized weight is at most $(2nw_*)^{2n}=t^{-(4+2\gamma)+o(1)}$, even before pricing the two compact-window constraints. Thus **any** $t^{2+2\gamma-\delta}$ exact ratios, even the heaviest at this shift, cost $o(B^2e^{-o(n)})$ for fixed $\delta>0$. In contrast the distant-family atom estimate had to pay for starts invisible in $Q$. This estimate does not price an interval containing many such ratios.
* Even on this reduced family the compact positive part is too large for an unsigned bound at the common-dip amplitude. At shifts with $A>1/4$, use (3) at the power radius with $K(u)=(4\pi t)^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(u/(4t))$. Its transform is $(1-2t|\nu|)_+$. On $|u|\le cX/(nD)$ the derivative bound $|Z'|\ll AD/X$ gives $|Z|^n\gg A^n$, so $P_0\gg X A^n/(tnD)$. Equation (3) lower bounds the jointly separated pair weight in $|t\nu|,|t\nu'|\le1/2$ by $e^{-o(n)}(X A^n/(tnD))^2$: The two nonnegative tent factors are bounded by one and the mixed error is negligible. All these pairs pass both gates and the old distance cut; they are admissible at either radius. There $T(a)T(a')\cos(a-a')$ is bounded below by a positive constant, since $|a-a'|\le1+o(1)$. For the range constants in the [matching mass bound](matched-prime-overlap.md), there are $\gg D$ eligible shifts with $A>1/4$. On each the positive part exceeds $((X/D)a^n/t)^2$ by a factor at least $(4^{2n}/n^2)e^{-o(n)}$. Thus a bound at that squared scale must match the positive part with the negative part to relative deficit at most $4^{-2n}e^{o(n)}$, even though no two of the $4n$ endpoints can coincide. This concerns the positive and negative parts of the **integrated kernel**, not arc counts at individual widths. It is not a claim of a power-sized obstruction in $t$ on this compact window.

**Price the splits, not just the pool.** For each realized $Q$ let $P_+,P_-$ be its recovered starts with positive and negative pooled sign, respectively, and write $e_p$ for that sign. Both pools have size $n$. In exact log coordinates put $z_Q=t\log Q$. A split chooses $U$ consisting of $h$ starts from each pool for copy 1, with $z_U=t\sum_{p\in U} e_p\lambda_p$. Copy 2 has the reversed pooled signs on the complement, hence its coordinate is $z_U-z_Q$. On the compact product window the rounded slots are recovered in every ordering. Call the split admissible if both privacy counts and the original distance cut pass. The coefficient of $\cos z_Q$ in the exact sum is therefore the nonnegative number
$$
 (h!)^4\left(\prod_{p\in P_+\cup P_-}w_p\right)
 \sum_{U\ {\rm admissible}}T(z_U)T(z_U-z_Q).
$$
In particular, recoloring at fixed $Q$ cannot supply opposite sign. One also cannot simply insert the unrestricted split count $\binom nh^2$ as if both triangles had been met. In fact let $k=\lfloor\log(t/16)/(4D)\rfloor$. Within the set of splits with nonzero coefficient, two different $U,V$ must satisfy $|U\mathbin{\triangle} V|>4k$: Otherwise $(z_U-z_V)/t$ is the log of a nonunit rational with both sides at most $X^{4k}$ (no endpoints repeat), so $|z_U-z_V|\ge tX^{-4k}\ge16$, incompatible with $|z_U|,|z_V|<2\pi$. Around each surviving split, exchange exactly $k$ selected positive starts with $k$ unselected positive ones. The resulting $\binom hk^2$ splits are disjoint across centers by the Hamming triangle inequality, and are contained in the space of all balanced splits. Thus at most $\binom nh^2/\binom hk^2$ splits can contribute. The divisor $\binom hk^2$ grows as $\exp(\Omega_\gamma(n))$ since $k/h\to1/(4(2+\gamma))$. This is a bound on the multiplicity for each realized ratio, not an equidistribution statement about the ratios themselves.

The [pool isolation estimate](joint-pool-isolation.md) goes further: Even changing the signs, the two lists, and up to $\lfloor\gamma L/100\rfloor$ starts cannot reach a negative term from most of the central mass. It prices the existence of a partner, not a randomly redrawn partner.

To bound the signed observable one still needs a comparison **between different ratios**, weighted by these split coefficients, in the positive and negative cosine annuli. Neither (1) nor (2) gives that signed upper bound at a genuine center. In particular the independent-window averaging identity still applies to this family, but the common-window variance argument for an unrestricted product does not apply just by invoking positivity: The pair-compatibility matrix has zero diagonal and can have nonzero off-diagonal entries.
