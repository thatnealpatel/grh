# What same-angle overlap determines in the signed pair test

Use the frozen notation of [(4)](prime-pair-dispersion.md): $I=[e^{-8}X,X]$, $D=\log X$, $cD\le d\le4D$, $T=bX/D$. The start bin is one of $J$ equal intervals in $I$; both endpoints of every edge $(p,p+d)$ are prime and in $I$. Write $w=w_{d,p}=X^{-1}\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$, $\lambda_{d,p}=\log(1+d/p)$, and $A_d=\sum_p w_{d,p}$. With probability measure on the height interval $u=t+s$, $|s|\le T$, and on the full angle circle, the test is $F_{jk}=\int\Re(P_j\overline{P_k})$, where $P_j=\sum_d\sum_{p\in I_j}w_{d,p}e^{id\theta+iu\lambda_{d,p}}$ (the bin restriction is on $p$ only).

A genuine violation on two nonneighboring bins reduces to a pair of vertex-disjoint matchings. On the full prime support there are twisted controls with *opposite* violations but asymptotically identical unsigned product densities in $L^1$ after division by $D$, at the **same** heights and angles. Their positive integral is not concentrated on sets of vanishing measure. Thus neither pricing the largest peaks of each polynomial nor even knowing the full unsigned overlap to this precision determines the sign.

## Reduction of a genuine violation

List the primes in increasing order. An edge from index $i$ to index $i+r$ has rank $r$. Deleting edges of rank $r>K$ from both bins costs $O(D/K)$ in $F_{jk}$, uniformly in the phases. Indeed if $B_{d,\ell}$ is deleted mass in bin $\ell$, the error is at most $\sum_d A_d(B_{d,j}+B_{d,k})$. Charge each deleted edge to its $r-1$ interior primes, at cost $1/K$ per interior prime. With $G(n)=n/\varphi(n)$, [Ford, Theorem 2.5](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf) gives $A_d\ll G(d)^2$ and, for $0<a<d$, a count $\ll X D^{-3}G(ad(d-a))^3$ for prime triples $p,p+a,p+d$ in the band. These are distinct monic linear forms with determinant product of absolute value $ad(d-a)$; for inadmissible triples an obstructing prime is at most three, below the band. The theorem allows starts up to $X$, so no sieve on short start intervals is required. The weight cap $8D^2/X$ now bounds the error by

$$\frac{O(1)}{KD}\sum_{1\le a<d\le4D}G(d)^5G(a)^3G(d-a)^3\ll D/K.$$

For the last inequality use Hölder with the prefix moments of orders $15,9,9$ from the dispersion note; each argument occurs $O(D)$ times. This prices the other bin as well, not just the number of long edges.

For each $1\le r\le K$ color the starts by $\lfloor i/r\rfloor\bmod 2$. Each color is a matching: The only possible other start meeting its edge is $i\pm r$, of opposite color. Vertices from two nonneighboring bins cannot meet for fixed $J$ and large $X$, since bin width exceeds $4D$. If $F_{jk}\ge\eta D$, choose fixed $K$ so the tail costs less than $\eta D/2$. One of the $4K^2$ ordered pairs of matching polynomials then has signed integral $>\eta D/(8K^2)$ using the **untwisted** coefficients.

For this restricted product only, multiplying coefficients of shift $d$ in both bins by one shared unit $\xi_d$ leaves the angle integral unchanged at *every* height. This can be done by a single prime twist because the edges form a matching: Multiply each end prime by $\xi_d$, leaving starts alone. Independent uniform $\xi_d$ give each restricted polynomial expected fourth power at most $2(\sum_d A_d^2)^2\ll D^2$ at any $(\theta,u)$, by the two pairings of shift indices. Some choice therefore bounds the integrated fourth powers in both bins by $O(D^2)$. On a set of measure $h$ the normalized absolute product costs $O(\sqrt h)$ in this representation. In particular a signed violation can be studied on a fixed range of multiples of $\sqrt D$ in both factors: Small factors cost $O(\delta D)$ below $\delta\sqrt D$ by Cauchy, and the fourth moments and Markov make factors above $L\sqrt D$ cost $O(D/L^2)$. This changes the representation, not the value of the restricted integral; there is no assertion about fourth moments of the original untwisted full polynomials.

## A matching large enough for a full-support control

Let $H_0=4D$, $v_d=2(1-d/H_0)/H_0$ for $1\le d<H_0$, $z(x)=\log(X/x)$, and $m=1-9e^{-8}$. For consecutive primes $p_i,p_{i+1}$ in $I$, put $g_i=p_{i+1}-p_i$, $h_i=\min(g_i,H_0)$. PNT and partial summation give $\sum_{i<N}z(p_i)=(m+o(1))X/D$. The disjoint intervals $[p_i,p_i+h_i]$ (apart from endpoints) lie in $I$. Since $|z'|\le e^8/X$ and $\sum h_i\le X$, their left sums give $\sum z(p_i)h_i\le mX+O(H_0)$, where $mX=\int_I z$. Both logs at a pair are at least $D-8$. Using $(1-g_i/H_0)_+=1-h_i/H_0$ gives

$$\sum_{g_i<H_0}v_{g_i}w_{g_i,p_i}\ge \frac{2(D-8)^2}{H_0 X}\sum_{i<N}z(p_i)(1-h_i/H_0)\ge 3m/8-o(1).$$

Keep the better of the even- and odd-indexed edges, **once** for all $d$. Its mass $B_d$ has aggregate $\sum v_d B_d\ge3m/16-o(1)$. No lower sieve for any particular prime gap was used. For constants compatible with [the peak extraction](short-prime-pair-bias.md), write $\sum_{d<H_0}A_d^2\le K_0 H_0$, $K_0\ge1$, by the pair sieve. Choose $M\ge1024K_0$, $0<c\le 2^{-18}/K_0$, $0<b\le1/(256Me^8)$. Removing $A_d>M/2$ costs at most $4K_0/M$ in the aggregate, and restricting to $cD+1<d<(4-c)D-1$ costs at most $2\sqrt{K_0c}+o(1)$ by Cauchy. These are also valid choices in the peak extraction. The even $d$ have $\sum v_d=1/2+o(1)$ and odd gaps are absent. After removing $B_d\le1/4$, more than $3m/16-1/128-1/8-o(1)>1/32$ remains. Each term costs at most $2M/H_0$, so $B_d>1/4$ on at least $D/(16M)$ eligible shifts.

## Change the sign without changing the absolute overlap

Take sparse named centers with disjoint prime bands. At each center prescribe $z_p=\omega(p)p^{it}$; thus one completely multiplicative twist per control realizes all prescriptions, using $\omega(p)=1$ elsewhere on primes. The frozen polynomial now has edge phase $z_{p+d}\overline{z_p}e^{is\lambda_{d,p}}$. Group the band primes into the selected matching edges and singleton vertices. Give each group an independent uniform circle variable as its common phase, and at the end of each matching edge multiply by $\xi_d$, independent uniform circle variables indexed by the **actual shift**, shared across bins. Write $P_\ell=Q_\ell+E_\ell$ for matching and other edges, respectively. $Q_\ell$ depends only on the $\xi_d$.

On the matching, the integrated signed product at any bin pair has all the phases $\xi_d$ canceled and the positive kernel $\operatorname{sinc}(T(\lambda_{d,p}-\lambda_{d,r}))\ge1/2$. Hence the sum over all ordered bins is at least $\tfrac12\sum B_d^2\gg D$ in the tested range. The equal and adjacent bins cost $O(D/J+D^3/X)$ by the unsigned collision estimate [(2)](prime-pair-dispersion.md), also valid on this subset. For each sufficiently large fixed $J$, select separated $j,k$ with matching integral $\ge\eta_J D$, $\eta_J\gg 1/J^2$; pass to a subsequence to fix $j,k$. This choice does not depend on random phases. Define a second control by further multiplying by $-1$ the end vertex of **each matching edge starting in bin $k$**. The matching polynomials are then identical except that $Q_k$ changes sign. This prescription does change some nonmatching edges.

Here is why those edges cannot restore the missing sign. Conditional on the $\xi_d$, each nonmatching oriented edge carries the character of an ordered pair of *distinct* group variables (end divided by start), times a fixed coefficient of modulus $w$. At most four edges have any given ordered pair of groups. Orthogonality and Cauchy give, for either control, pointwise in $(\theta,s)$,

$$\mathbb E_{\rm groups}|E_\ell|^2\le4\sum_{d,p}w_{d,p}^2\ll D^3/X.$$

The same argument at the center, shift by shift and then summed, bounds the expected sum of squared nonmatching coefficient errors by $O(D^3/X)$. Deterministically $|E_\ell|\le\sum A_d\ll D$. Thus the errors can have integrated squared norm $o(1)$ simultaneously in both controls and every bin and contribute $o(D^2)$ to the integrated fourth powers. For $Q_\ell$ use the independent-shift fourth-moment bound above. The expected sum of the normalized fourth moments for the fixed $J$ bins is bounded; choose a fixed large bound so it holds with probability at least $3/4$. The other required small errors hold with probability tending to one by Markov. In fact the two controls can keep the **same** persistent shifts. Write $B_{d,k}$ for matching mass starting in the flipped bin. Its square sum is $O(D/J+D^3/X)$ by the same-bin collision estimate, uniformly in the bin. For sufficiently large fixed $J$, deleting $B_{d,k}>1/64$ leaves at least half the eligible shifts. With probability tending to one, a positive proportion of these have $\Re\xi_d\ge3/4$, by independence. On these shifts both controls have full real coefficient at the center $\ge(3/4)(1/4-2/64)-o(1)>1/8$, including the error over all bins. The derivative bound $4Me^8D/X$ and moving-weight error $o(1)$ from the peak extraction give $>1/16$ on the whole moving interval on the same shifts. Edges newly entering the moving band have negligible mass regardless of their twist. This is persistence, not a ramp peak.

Both controls can simultaneously satisfy the [local character tests (1) and pooled tests (2)](separated-bin-phase-laws.md) for any prescribed allowed cell and block widths. For clarity, at fixed $d$ and $k'+l'\ne0$, each character of the group variables arising from $z_p^{k'}z_{p+d}^{l'}$ is nonconstant. Its multiplicity at fixed $d$ is bounded absolutely: One group with a nonzero exponent must be an endpoint group, and its at most two vertices determine at most four edges at fixed $d$. Thus the total expected sum of squared cell errors is $O(D^3/X)$ even conditional on the shift phases. Summing absolute values costs $O(D^2/\sqrt H)=o(D)$ at start width $H\gg D^2$, $H/D^2\to\infty$. For nonzero ratio powers on the pooled blocks the nonmatching characters again have bounded multiplicity, even across shifts. For matching edges, orthogonality of the independent shift powers leaves at most the sum of the squared unsigned masses of the individual shift-cells, $O(D(D^2+H)/X)$ by (2) of the dispersion note. The bin sign changes do not increase either bound. Cauchy on $O((D/L)(X/H))$ boxes gives $o(D)$ at block length $L\to\infty$, $L\le D$. Markov and a slowly growing prefix of the fixed characters allow these conditions in the same choice as the fourth-moment bound.

In particular, for the selected pair the two **full-support** signed integrals are opposites up to $o(D)$ and the first is at least $\eta_JD-o(D)$. Indeed Cauchy bounds every error product with one $E$ by $o(\sqrt D)$ against a matching factor. Denoting the controls by $+$ and $-$, we have

$$\frac1D\int\big|\,|P_j^+P_k^+|-|P_j^-P_k^-|\,\big|=o(1).$$

This compares the unsigned product-weighted contributions **uniformly over all measurable subsets** of the common height-angle space. Each normalized squared modulus is bounded in $L^2$; sets of measure $o(1)$ carry $o(D)$ absolute product mass. The earlier common additive slope demonstrates the opposite possibility of concentration. The new controls distinguish sign even when that concentration is excluded. More explicitly, there are constants $0<\delta<L$ and $\rho>0$ independent of the center on this subsequence, and a common set of measure at least $\rho$ at each such center, on which **all four** moduli lie in $[\delta\sqrt D,L\sqrt D]$ and the two relative-phase cosines are respectively $>\rho$ and $<-\rho$. To see this, let $f^\pm=D^{-1}\Re(P_j^\pm\overline{P_k^\pm})$ and $g^\pm=D^{-1}|P_j^\pm P_k^\pm|$. The matching products are pointwise opposites, so the same error estimates give $\|f^++f^-\|_1=o(1)$ as well as $\|g^+-g^-\|_1=o(1)$. On the complement of the common modulus range the integral of $g^+$ is $O_J(\delta+L^{-2})+o(1)$: For failures in the minus control use $g^-$ first, and then the $L^1$ comparison. Choose this cost below $\eta_J/4$. The integral of $f^+$ on the common range is then at least $\eta_J/2$ eventually. There $|f^+|\le L^2$, so $f^+>\eta_J/4$ on a set of measure at least $\eta_J/(4L^2)$. Removing the $o(1)$ measure where $|f^++f^-|>\eta_J/8$ proves the assertion, since $g^\pm\le L^2$. Thus even restricting to the common moderate-modulus range of the two controls does not determine the sign.

Nothing here rephases the genuine logarithms while keeping a genuine named center. A proof using the reduced test still needs the relative complex arguments at matching shift labels, not just simultaneous large moduli.
