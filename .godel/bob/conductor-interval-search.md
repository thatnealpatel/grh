# List conductors with the same real shifts on one interval

The [positive bucket search](moment-bucket-search.md) can serve a whole interval of conductors from one frozen prime band. The new point is local: The available moment order is set by the **length of the height interval**, not its distance from zero. This gives a sublinear search and a density saving even on conductor intervals shorter than the previous [dyadic exceptional count](dyadic-prime-pair-moments.md). Keeping the labels when converting the height list to conductors gives a converse with amplitude slack, not just a superset obtained by projecting a modulus test.

**Output contract.** Fix $a=1/16$ and $0<\delta<3/5$. Input integers $Q,J$ with $Q^{2/5+\delta}\le J\le Q$. Write $c(q)=2q^{3/5}$, $U=c(Q)$, $X(v)=(3\log(v/2))^2$, $X=X(U)$, and $D=\log X$. Use any fixed list $\mathcal D\subset[1,5D]$ of integer shifts, an integer $1\le k\le |\mathcal D|$, a nonnegative integer radius $W$, and integer offsets $A\le B$; the last three window parameters are bounded in absolute value by a fixed power of $\log Q$. For each height $v$, use the **untwisted** sum $Z_d(v;X(v))$ from [the prime-pair test](short-prime-pair-bias.md): Sum $w_p e^{iv\log((p+d)/p)}$ over prime pairs in $[e^{-8}X(v),X(v)]$, with $w_p=\log(X(v)/p)\log p\log(p+d)/X(v)$.

Let $E(s)$ consist of the integers $q\in[Q,Q+J]$ for which some $z\in[c(q)+A,c(q)+B]$ has **at least $k$ common labels** $d\in\mathcal D$ satisfying $|\Re Z_d(v;X(v))|\ge s$ throughout $[z-W,z+W]$. For example, $A=B=0$ tests a centered interval. Taking $A=W$, $B=b_0-W$ tests for an interval of length $2W$ anywhere in the forward window of integer length $b_0\ge2W$.

For all sufficiently large $Q$, put $N\asymp JQ^{-2/5}$ as below. There is a Las Vegas algorithm returning a union $C$ of closed integer intervals with

$$E(a)\subseteq C\subseteq E(a-\eta),\qquad \eta\asymp X^{-1/3}.$$

Here $C$ is identified with its integer points. Its compressed construction takes expected $N^{3/4+o(1)}$ bit operations. Its **cardinality**, independent of any runtime bound, is at most $Q^{2/5} N^{3/4+o(1)}$. Both $o(1)$ exponents are in $N$ at fixed $\delta$ and fixed window growth bound. For $J=Q$ these are the costs $Q^{9/20+o(1)}$ for the interval list and $Q^{17/20+o(1)}$ to expand it. For $J\asymp Q^{1/2}$ the exceptional fraction is at most $Q^{-1/40+o(1)}$ **in every such block**. The old dyadic cardinality bound alone allows an entire block that short to be bad.

## Freeze the coefficients once, not the phases

Take $V$ to be the floor of a certified rational lower bound for $X$ of error $<1/2$. Sieve through $V$. Keep exactly edges with prime $p,p+d$, $p+d\le V$, and $p$ at least a rational upper bound for $e^{-8}V$ of error $<1/2$; use weights at $V$. Denote this sum by $F_d(v)$. Only the weights and support are frozen; the phase still uses the true height and the unrounded ratio frequency. The bit algorithms for the elementary functions in the bucket note give the required absolute approximations in $\operatorname{polylog} Q$ time. In particular the sieve to $V=O(\log^2 Q)$ needs no discrete support oracle or decision at an exact transcendental boundary.

For all target heights and their unit neighbors, $v\asymp U$ and $X(v)=V+O(\sqrt V)$. At most $O(\sqrt V)$ integer starts per shift change membership: Each of the two endpoints can cross only a band edge. On either support $|w_p|\ll D^2/V$. On common pairs the derivative of $x^{-1}\log(x/p)$ in $x$ is $O(V^{-2})$ throughout the cutoff change. Thus, uniformly in the phases,

$$ |Z_d(v;X(v))-F_d(v)|\ll D^2/\sqrt V,\qquad |F'_d(v)|\ll D^3/V.$$

For the derivative use total absolute weight $O(D^2)$ and maximum frequency $O(D/V)$. In particular, for **any** left node $j$ and target $v\in[j,j+1]$ the sum of the two errors is $o(\eta)$ for the computable dyadic $\eta=2^{-\lfloor (\log_2 V)/3\rfloor}$. This argument does not differentiate a prime cutoff. It would not justify freezing the values $e^{iv\lambda_p}$ over the conductor block.

## Search only the short block

Enclose $c(Q)$ and $c(Q+J)$ by integer endpoints using fifth-root comparisons ($c(q)^5=32q^3$). Extend the range on both sides by $2+|A|+|B|+W$. Start the lattice at this lower integer $u_0$ and pad the number of lattice points up to a power of two $N$. The mean value theorem and the lower bound on $J$ give $N\asymp J Q^{-2/5}$ and $\log N\asymp_\delta\log Q$. Only the portion before this extra power-of-two padding needs to be labeled.

Choose the largest integer $h$ with $4V^{2h}\le N$ by exact power comparisons; it is positive eventually. For one frozen shift let $y_i=|F_d(u_0+i)|^{2h}$, $0\le i<N$. In the proof of the bucket search use the majorant $4\operatorname{sinc}^2((v-u_0-(N-1)/2)/N)$ with $\operatorname{sinc}x=\sin(x)/x$. The scale here is **$N$**, not $u_0$. Its transform vanishes outside $[-2/N,2/N]$. After multiplying by the moment the bandwidth is still $o(1)$ since $hD/V=o(1)$; summing this product on the entire integer lattice therefore gives the integral. The rational frequency separation is at least $V^{-2h}\ge4/N$ off the diagonal. As in the dyadic argument, at zero the least differing start would have an unmatched prime valuation, so only identical multisets occur. Using the trivial bound $\sum w_p^2\ll D^4/V$ gives

$$ S_d=\sum_{i<N} y_i\ll N h! (O(D^4)/V)^h\le N^{3/4+o(1)}.$$

Indeed $h\log V=\tfrac12\log N+O(\log V)$ and $\log h=\tfrac12\log V+O_\delta(\log D)$. For the same reason there are at most $\binom{V+h}{h}^2\le N^{1/2+o(1)}$ ordered multiset pairs. The coefficient mass is at most $(O(D^2))^{2h}=N^{o(1)}$. These bounds do **not** require the pressure cut. With threshold $(a/2)^{2h}$ the bucket search therefore covers every node with $|F_d|\ge a/2$ in expected $N^{3/4+o(1)}$ bit operations. The list size bound of the same order is deterministic, from the buffered leaf mass. The $u_0$ phase in each coefficient still needs $O(\log Q)$ guard bits, affordable since $\log Q=O_\delta(\log N)$. This is the same checked bucket algorithm, not a claim that an arbitrary sparse Fourier query costs sublinear time.

## Keep the labels through the interval test

For each returned node $j$ (absolute ordinate) estimate $|\Re F_d(j)|$ with certified absolute error at most $\eta/16$. Label $[j,j+1]$ by $d$ if the rational estimate is at least $a-\eta/2$. One can sum the original prime terms directly at each such node in $\operatorname{polylog} Q$ bit operations. A point in a true $a$-window for label $d$ is in a labeled cell: Its left node has $|F_d|>a/2$ and passes the real test for large $Q$. In the other direction **every target point** of a labeled closed cell has moving absolute real part $\ge a-\eta$ for that label. Both implications follow from the uniform errors above, regardless of which gray-zone leaves the hash search returns.

Merge touching cells **per label**, obtaining maximal closed runs $[l,r]$. Replace each by $[l+W,r-W]$, keeping equality and dropping empty intervals. These are exactly the midpoints where the entire window fits in that label's cells. Now sweep for multiplicity $\ge k$ among distinct labels, counting starts before removing ends at a tied endpoint. This can produce isolated points. Seeking $k$ labels separately at each height *before* shrinking would allow them to switch inside the window. Only **after** the multiplicity test, expand each surviving midpoint interval $[l,r]$ to $[l-B,r-A]$ for possible conductor centers. The witness midpoint is common, not one choice per label. This proves both inclusions, also for $W=0$ or $A=B$.

For each resulting height interval intersect with the conductor range by comparing its integer endpoints' fifth powers with $32q^3$ via binary search. Monotonicity includes equality; no inverse evaluated by floating point is needed. Sorting, binary searches, and direct real tests cost only polylogarithmic factors per entry. There are at most $N^{3/4+o(1)}$ cells over all $O(D)$ lists. Even after offset expansion the union has length and number of components each at most $N^{3/4+o(1)}$: Charge initial unit length and at most $B-A$ additional length per component, with $B-A=\operatorname{polylog} Q$. Center spacing on $[Q,Q+J]$ is $\gg Q^{-2/5}$, which gives the asserted cardinality, including isolated points. Uniformly enumerating $J$ separate frozen lists is not required.

**Scope.** The sandwich is for the stated common shift list. A fixed superset can cover every moving admissible trim, but its positive matches need not obey moving trim or retention cuts. The statement is neither a classifier of equality at the threshold nor an exclusion at a specified conductor. With $W=A=B=0$, $k=1$, the complement clears even a single real hit from the list at the center; using all possible shifts in an enlarged list gives a real common dip there. In the other direction, exclusion of fixed-shift persistence as in [the failure implication](retained-shell-overshoot.md) must use its actual density, margin, trim, and window; a positive match of the cover is not a failure of the common dip. Weighted small excursions in the single-shift shell budget are a separate question. For one locally flagged interval, evaluate the real prime sums there directly; do not run the block algorithm just to test one $q$.

## Finite checks and the flagged cell

Run [check-conductor-intervals.py](check-conductor-intervals.py) with `/opt/sage-10.9/bin/python`, without `-O`. It tests three separate claims:

* Use the frozen support of the bucket check at $q_0=100000007$, all shifts $8$ through $28$, and 256 left nodes. Define closed cell marks by the actual threshold at the left node, **not** by a claim about interpolation. At radius 4, 64,754 of the 327,767 eligible integer conductors have four persistent labels in this cell model (16 output runs); 303,974 pass the rotating pointwise count. The checker compares interval-list membership with independent mask intersections at every conductor, including rejected ones. A separate exact fifth-power control requires the output to contain a singleton. This checks the sweep and inverse, not the complexity or continuous transfer of the hash search.
* The distinction also occurs for actual moving prime sums. At $q=100277386$ the full trim is $8\le d\le28$ throughout $c(q)\pm4$. On each of 512 closed subintervals, 160-bit interval arithmetic certifies at least five real hits at $1/16$. For every label except possibly 12 and 18 some subinterval lies entirely below threshold. Thus pointwise multiplicity four holds and fixed-label multiplicity four fails on the **same continuous window**. This does not rule out persistence on a shorter interval, including the subintervals used for the lower bound.
* For the actual [quadratic-overlap fixture](quadratic-overlap.md) take $q=2^{127}-1$, $\ell=L/\sqrt{24}$, $u_0=t+44\ell$. In the adjacent cell $[u_0,u_0+\ell]$ the interval of radius $1/2048$ about $u_0+247\ell/512$ satisfies $\max_{11\le d\le40}|\Re Z_d(v;X(v))|<0.061<1/16$, **without** any pressure deletion. A numerical scan suggested the offset; the checker needs only that offset and verifies the moving band, moving trim, weights, and cosines over the full subinterval. Thus not even one label can clear $1/16$ throughout this particular flagged cell. It does not clear all subintervals of the cell or replace $u_0$ by the conductor center $t$.
