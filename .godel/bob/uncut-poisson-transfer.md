# What a Poisson average says about the central screen

Freeze **all** parameters of [the uncut screen](uncut-screen-mean.md) at $q$, including the depth switch. Translate only its height argument. Put $t=2q^{3/5}$, $R=(9/5)\log q$, $D=\log(R^2)$, $a=1/D$, $A=1/2+a$, $B_0=2-2/e$, and $T_h f=P_h*f$ with $P_h=k_h/\pi$, $k_h(x)=h/(h^2+x^2)$. At the center let $d=R^{-1}\sum_{b>a}\delta_b(\gamma-t)$ in the [one-defect notation](uncut-defect-price.md). For fixed $C>0$, $h=C/D$, and $r=e^{-C}$, we get the transfer in **both directions**:

$$ (T_h Z)(t)=r Z(t)-(1-r)(2/e)d+O_C(\epsilon)+o_{C,\epsilon}(1). \tag{1} $$

Use sufficiently small fixed $\epsilon$ and then large $D$. In particular an estimate of the average alone at the old threshold is not the pointwise test. The quantity to bound above is $(T_h Z)(t)+(1-r)(2/e)d$, at threshold $r\cdot .0658$ with room for the errors. This uses only one additional scalar at the **original** height. It does not require a bound on how many pairs cross the switch under a change of cutoff, since no cutoff was changed.

**Proof.** Separate the full signed Weil charge $H$ and $B_0 W_A/R$ before doing any averaging. By the all-ordinate summand estimate in [the census proof](uncut-census-window.md), what remains, before summation with weights $\nu/R$, is $L_b(x)+G_b(x)+o_\epsilon(1)\ell(x)$, where $x=\gamma-t$, $c=bD$, $\ell=(1/4+x^2)^{-1}$, $g=g_\epsilon$, and $\phi=Dx-2\arctan(2x)$. Here

$$ L_b=\mathbf1_{c\le1}(2g-B_0)\ell,\qquad G_b=j_c g\ell\cos\phi,\qquad j_c=\begin{cases}2\cosh c,&c\le1,\\-2\sinh(1)e^{1-c},&c>1.\end{cases} $$

Count a right member once and a line zero with weight $1/2$. In particular a supporting pair is *shallow*. The error majorant here is $o_\epsilon(1)\ell$, not a small multiple of a possibly narrow pole. Translating and averaging the exact identity is legitimate at each fixed $q$: An inverse-square majorant with constant depending on $q,\epsilon$ suffices for the absolute summands in the original screen, convolution with $P_h$ preserves such a majorant, and unit-interval zero counts grow at most logarithmically. For the normalized asymptotic errors use $\ell\ll p_b^1$ from the census proof, so that after convolution their budget at $t$ is $O(W_{1+h}/R)=O(1)$.

Freeze the slow envelopes in the Poisson integral as in [the signed remainder](uncut-defect-price.md#the-signed-remainder). For $f,f'=O_\epsilon(\ell)$ the error in $T_h(e^{i\omega x}f)-e^{-h|\omega|}e^{i\omega x}f$ is $O_\epsilon(h\log(2/h)\ell)$. On $|y|\le1$ use the derivative bound, and on $|y|>1$ use $P_h(y)\ll h/(1+y^2)$ and inverse-square convolution. Use $\omega=0,D$ respectively, putting $e^{-2i\arctan(2x)}$ in the slow envelope. The $j_c$ are bounded independently of depth. Thus $L_b$ is unchanged and $G_b$ acquires exactly the leading factor $r$, up to summable $o_{C,\epsilon}(1)\ell$ errors. Also $T_hW_A=W_{A+h}$ by the Poisson semigroup. Both exterior traces at the center divided by $R$ are $1/6+o(1)$ by Stirling and absolute Euler convergence (the logarithmic derivative costs $O(D)$).

For $T_h H$ apply the full translated Weil identity to $T_h F$: Convolution on the real axis preserves strip holomorphy and the fixed-parameter inverse-square bounds there. The real-axis envelope is uniformly $O(\ell)$, the two pole terms are $O(R/(1+t^2))$ before normalization by convolution of the corresponding strip bound, and the mass $m_D=\int F/(2\pi)$ is unchanged. The gamma term gives $-m_D/6+o(1)$ as at $h=0$: The change in $\log(2+|t+x|)$ from the central value is bounded by $\log(2+|x|)$, integrable against the envelope. The prime coefficients gain $n^{-h}$. The [absolute coefficient errors](short-buffered-packet.md#price-the-smoothing-on-the-original-prime-side) in reducing to the positive prime ramp still cost $O(\epsilon)+o_\epsilon(1)$ after multiplication by $0<n^{-h}\le1$. With $V=R^2$, write $s=\Re S_V(t)$ and $w_p=V^{-1}\log p\log(V/p)$ for $p<V$. Then $0\le p^{-h}-r\le h\log(V/p)$ by the mean value theorem on $[0,D]$ for $e^{-hu}$. In particular $\sum w_p|p^{-h}-r|\ll h$: Partial summation with Chebyshev's bound gives $V^{-1}\sum_{p<V}(\log p)\log^2(V/p)=O(1)$. Hence the arithmetic part is $rs+O(\epsilon)+o_{C,\epsilon}(1)$, with no typical-height assumption. The unaveraged charge has arithmetic part $s$ with the same accuracy.

The part common to both expansions, apart from these errors, is

$$ B=-m_D/6+B_0/6+R^{-1}\sum\nu L_b(x). $$

Here $m_D=2+O(\epsilon)+o_\epsilon(1)$. Uniformly in $x$, $(1-g)\ell\ll\epsilon k_{1/\epsilon}$: Use $1-g\ll\min(1,\epsilon^2 x^2)$. Summing costs $O(\epsilon)$ asymptotically by comparison with $W_{1/\epsilon}/R=1/6+o_\epsilon(1)$ (the two widths $1/\epsilon\pm b$ are comparable to $1/\epsilon$). On $b\le a$, $p_b^A=(1+O(a))\ell$, and on $b>a$, $p_b^A=(1+O(a))\delta_b$ uniformly. Thus $R^{-1}\sum_{b\le a}\nu\ell=1/6-d+o(1)$. Substituting $2-B_0=2/e$ gives $B=-(2/e)d+O(\epsilon)+o_\epsilon(1)$. The remainder $s+R^{-1}\sum\nu G_b$ has acquired the factor $r$ in its entirety. This proves (1).

**Two tests.** If two signed averages at different widths are cheaper than measuring $d$, set $h=(\log 2)/D$. Eliminating the *same* $d$ between (1) at $h,2h$ gives

$$ Z(t)=3(T_hZ)(t)-2(T_{2h}Z)(t)+O(\epsilon)+o_\epsilon(1). $$

Here $3/2-2/4=1$ and $3(1-1/2)-2(1-1/4)=0$. Two upper bounds alone do not price this signed combination. Neither formula proves the measurements are cheaper or available at any exceptional conductor.

For comparison take $C=4$ in the [all-deep finite Euler calibration](uncut-depth-discount.md), with $c=40$. It has $d=1/6+o(1)$, and the shallow low part is absent. The same attenuation calculation applies to its **finite** Euler coefficients: Their absolute smoothing error is bounded by the full coefficient error, their constant-intensity term is unchanged, and their ramp is the same. Its exterior budget follows from the finite translated Poisson identity. The residual carrier costs at most $2\sinh(1)e^{-39}/6$ before attenuation. Thus the benchmark moves from $s-1/(3e)$ to $e^{-4}s-1/(3e)$ up to this residual and the iterated smoothing errors. At $s=47/81$ this moves from above $.4576$ to below $-.1119$. In fact it stays negative throughout $47/81\le s\le1+o(1)$. This calibrates the **same prime data** on the two sides, not the existence of a prime peak or a failing conductor. Even a negative averaged screen need not suffice: At $d=1/6$ the corrected upper target for the average is about $-.11918$, not zero. No arithmetic bound meeting the corrected target is known here.
