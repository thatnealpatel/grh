# The cost of a shared catalogue of endpoint moduli

A progression fine enough to resolve the signed window in the [last-slot residue](last-slot-residues.md) is easy to get *after seeing all endpoints*. The same is not true of a small preassigned catalogue. Here the cost is absolute, not relative to a central slice.

Use the separated gate and notation of the [frequency-band reduction](rounded-slot-frequency-band.md), and set $C=4+2\gamma$. The two completed products $U=M(p+d)$ and $W=Np$ are coprime and squarefree, each with $n$ prime factors: Separation at either radius makes all $2n$ endpoints distinct. Both products are $t^{C+o(1)}$ by the band bounds. Thus the half-width in the variable $\ell=M-kp$ is $2\pi V=t^{C-1+o(1)}$, $V=W/(td)$. The exact identity is $d\ell=U-W$. For divisors $Q_U\mid U$, $Q_W\mid W$, this gives $d\ell\equiv-W\pmod{Q_U}$ and $d\ell\equiv U\pmod{Q_W}$. The moduli are coprime to each other and to $d$. Once the opposing residues are given, these conditions specify one progression of step $Q_UQ_W$ in $\ell$. For step comparable to $V$ only $O(1)$ terms remain in the kernel; a continuum balance on that progression need not follow. This observation is not a lower bound on actual imbalance.

Fix $0<\delta<\gamma/2$. Before the draw, allow at most $t^{\gamma/2-\delta}$ candidate positive integers $Q$, each $\ge t^{C-1-o(1)}$ with uniform $o(1)$. Even choosing the modulus and its split *after* seeing the tuple, the absolute original product weight of separated tuples with $|t\nu|\le8$ for which some such $Q$ divides $UW$ is at most $t^{-1-\delta+o(1)}$. In fact the same estimate holds for a catalogue of that many pools of at most $X^{1/2+o(1)}$ primes each, asking that **some subproduct** of endpoints drawn from a single pool be at least $t^{C-1-o(1)}$.

For one pool $P$, the original start probability of touching it is at most $2|P|b_0=X^{-1/2+o(1)}$ as an upper bound, $b_0=8D^2/(a_0X)$. A slot contributes at most $X^2$ in distinct prime factors. Mark $r=(C-1-o(1))L/2+O(1)$ labels touching the pool, rounding down the necessary lower bound. Sum over $\binom nr=t^{o(1)}$ choices; for each choice its probability is at most $(2|P|b_0)^r$. Leave $j=\lfloor\log(t/32)/(2D)\rfloor$ further labels hidden (possible since $r+j<n$). Given all disclosed labels, including the marked ones, the $j$ hidden log ratios with their prescribed signs must sum into one interval of width $16/t$. Their distinct signed sums are spaced by $X^{-2j}>16/t$. The one possible sum fixes the signed multiplicities by prime valuations at the least differing start. Separation forbids unseen opposite-sign collisions, leaving at most $j!$ assignments. Under the original law the additional cost is at most $j!b_0^j\le t^{-1/4+o(1)}$. This uses separation as a restriction on assignments, not as independence under conditioning. Including $A^n=t^{o(1)}$, the total per pool is at most $t^{-(C-1)/4-1/4+o(1)}=t^{-1-\gamma/2+o(1)}$. For an integer candidate one can discard it if it cannot divide any $UW$; otherwise its prime divisors form a pool of size at most $2n=X^{1/2+o(1)}$. The union bound finishes both claims.

This prices a proposed way of sharing fine progressions across supports. It does not discard support-dependent moduli outside a preassigned catalogue or justify uniform residues in the surviving progressions.
