# Counting quadratic twists with a peak below a quasipolynomial height

Write $X=(A\log q)^2$, $D=\log X$, $R=X^\theta$, and $T=\exp(\kappa D^2)$, with $A\ge1$, $\theta>0$, and $\kappa>0$ fixed. Use fundamental discriminants $|d|\le R$ coprime to the primitive base conductor $q\to\infty$, with $c=q|d|$. Two consequences of the same hybrid large sieve are:

* For $1<A<2$, at most $X^{\theta/2+\kappa\log A+o(1)}$ twists have a nonpositive [endpoint quotient](endpoint-arithmetic-test.md) at some modulation $|y|\le T$, at the twist's **own** cutoff. In particular $\kappa=\theta/(4\log A)$ gives $R^{3/4+o(1)}$ exceptions, uniformly even if the base Legendre character is bad. No reality hypothesis on the primitive base is needed for the count.
* For each fixed $C>4A$ there are $\eta,\nu>0$ such that, outside $X^{\theta/2+\kappa\log C+o(1)}$ twists, **all** unit compact tests of diameter at most $2\log(A\log c)$ and all unit [Pick block inputs](short-cell-weil-list.md) there specified have Weil quotient at least $\nu\log c$ if their squared Fourier mass beyond $[-T,T]$ is at most $\eta$. In particular this holds if the Gamma energy excluding conductor is $\le c_0 D^2$ for sufficiently small fixed $c_0>0$. The count saves a power of $R$ when $\kappa<\theta/(2\log C)$.

The price **in this argument** of exceeding every fixed power of $X$ is a growing exceptional list. For endpoints the [sieved Gram argument](sieved-height-gram.md) instead keeps the list bounded through $\log T=k D^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log D}$ when $\theta<1/2$. The same argument gives a bounded (larger) list for the Fourier-mass-restricted inputs. It does not reach the $\kappa D^2$ scale here. Neither conclusion covers $q$-power heights or proves a floor on unrestricted inputs.

## Two reductions to one counting problem

For the endpoint use probability weights $w_p$ proportional to $X^{-1}\log p\log(X/p)$ on $p<X$; their unnormalized sum is $1+o(1)$ by PNT. The [uniform endpoint formula](endpoint-arithmetic-test.md) and the [common-cutoff transfer](local-quadratic-twists.md#a-sharper-alternative-for-the-unmodulated-endpoint-alone) imply that a failure at $y$ needs $|\sum_p w_p\xi(p)\chi_d(p)p^{iy}|\ge 1/A-o(1)$. Indeed $X_d/X=1+O_\theta(D/\log q)$; the absolute cutoff and prime-power errors do not depend on $y$, and the Gamma term is bounded below. Put $Y=X$ in this case.

For arbitrary shapes put $Y=(A\log(qR))^2\exp((\log\log q)^{-1/2})\sim X$, and take $w_p=(\log p)/(S\sqrt p)$ on $p\le Y$, where $S\sim2\sqrt X$ by PNT. If the normalized polynomial $\sum_{p\le Y}w_p\xi(p)\chi_d(p)p^{it}$ stays below $1/C$ in modulus throughout $[-T,T]$, the truncated prime multiplier $2\Re\sum_{p\le Y} S w_p \xi(p)\chi_d(p)p^{it}$ integrated against the test's Fourier probability measure costs at most $2S(1/C+\eta)$. The omitted prime powers up to $Y$ cost $O(D)$; there are no higher shifts for compact tests, and those above $Y$ on the stated blocks cost $o(1)$ by the [block tail estimate](short-cell-weil-list.md#turn-the-prime-measure-into-one-crossing-operator). Since $4A/C<1$ and $G\ge-O(1)$, small enough $\eta,\nu$ give the claimed margin. The Gamma symbol plus a fixed constant dominates $\log(2+|t|)$, so the energy implication follows by Markov's inequality. This reduction bounds a signed form via a sufficient multiplier criterion; it does not turn a large multiplier into an admissible bad shape.

## Amplification in the number of witnesses, then in the polynomial length

Let $B$ count distinct twists with a peak at least $a$, where $a=1/A-o(1)$ or $a=1/C$ in the respective cases. Both probability weights satisfy $\sum_p w_p^2\ll D^2/X$: Use the maximum weight $O(D^2/X)$ in the endpoint case, and $\sum_{p\le Y}\log^2 p/p\ll D^2$ in the multiplier case. Both have $\max w_p\ll D^2/\sqrt X$. Define $D'=\log Y=D+o(1)$.

Take even $m\sim(\log D)^2$ and let $F(p)=\sum_d z_d\chi_d(p)p^{it_d}$ over one peak per bad twist, with $|z_d|=1$ rotating its mean to positive real. Then $Ba\le|\mathbb E_w\xi(p)F(p)|\le(\mathbb E_w|F|^m)^{1/m}$. Expand $F^{m/2}\overline F^{m/2}$ and use the triangle inequality. At least $(Ba)^m/2$ ordered tuples yield product-character means of modulus $\ge a^m/2$ at signed sums of heights $|t|\le mT$: There are $B^m$ terms, each bounded by one, and all subthreshold terms together cost at most $(Ba)^m/2$. This step discards the base and permits repeated indices.

Write $v_i$ for the signed squarefree radicands of the discriminants and $s$ for the signed squarefree part of $\prod v_i$. The primitive quadratic character for $s$ has conductor $\le Q=4R^m$ (including conductor one) and agrees with the product away from primes dividing $2\prod v_i$. At most $O(mD)$ distinct primes are erased, at cost $O(mD^3/\sqrt X)=o(a^m)$ in these weights. Every counted tuple therefore gives a primitive polynomial of modulus $\ge W=a^m/4$. The [ordered preimage bound](local-quadratic-twists.md#a-weighted-product-count-in-place-of-distinct-conductors) in this range reads

$$2^m\frac{R^{m/2}}{\sqrt{|s|}}\max_{n\le R^m}\tau_m(n)\le \frac{R^{m/2+o(m)}}{\sqrt{|s|}}.$$

Indeed $n=|s|h^2$; choose $h$, the ordered factors of $n$, and their signs. For primes up to $\sqrt D$, use $\tau_m(p^j)\le(j+1)^m$, giving total log cost $O(m\sqrt D\log(mD))$. Above $\sqrt D$ use $\tau_m(p^j)\le m^j$, costing $O_\theta(mD\log m/\log D)$. Both are $o(mD)$.

Count the eligible $s$ by [Gallagher's hybrid large sieve, Young, Theorem 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07754): The integral on $[-H,H]$ of the sum over primitive characters of conductors at most $Q$ of the squared modulus of a polynomial of length $N$ is $\ll (Q^2H+N)\sum_{n\le N}|a_n|^2$ for $H\ge1$. The negative interval follows by shifting coefficients. This uses all primitive characters, not a hypothetical hybrid quadratic sieve. Set $H=mT+1$, $k=\lceil\log(Q^2 H)/D'\rceil\sim\kappa D$, and apply it to the $k$th power of the normalized prime polynomial. Its coefficients, independent of the character, are multinomial products supported on $n\le Y^k$; their squared sum is $\le k!(\sum w_p^2)^k$ by unique factorization. A peak of size $W$ persists above $W/2$ on length $\gg W/D'$ inside $[-H,H]$, since the original polynomial has derivative bounded by $D'$. Distinct signed squareclasses give distinct primitive characters. Thus their eligible number $L$ satisfies

$$L\ll (D'/W)(Q^2H+Y^k)\, k!(O(D^2)/X)^k(2/W)^{2k},\qquad \log(1+L)\le 2km\log(1/a)+o(km).$$

The ceiling ensures $Q^2H\le Y^k$, while $\log(Y/X)=o(1)$ and $\log(k!)+O(k\log D)=o(km)$. Summing $1/\sqrt{|s|}$ over any $L$ signed classes costs $O(\sqrt L)$. Combine this with the preimage bound and $(Ba)^m/2$ and take $m$th roots: $\log(1+B)\le (\theta/2)D+k\log(1/a)+o(D)$, as asserted.

**Boundary of this estimate.** At $T=q^\beta$ for fixed $\beta>0$, $k$ would be of order $\log q/D$, not $D$, and even the factorial in the length-balanced estimate could no longer be absorbed by this $m$. The quasipolynomial result cannot be extrapolated to that band. Its proof loses an $a^m$ threshold by expanding tuples. For a bounded list at the smaller quasipolynomial scale, a more discriminating test is to keep the times of a pair and study the product with their sum and difference phases, as in the [short-cell endpoint obstruction](short-cell-weil-list.md#two-modulated-endpoints-at-small-power-radius).
