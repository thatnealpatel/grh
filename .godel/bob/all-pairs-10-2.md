# Every two second translates admit a common shallow completion

In the fixed-spline $(10,2)$ comparison at $c=39/20$, **for every pair of real shifts** $s_1,s_2$ there is a witness matching all first translates at both heights, the zero answers at orders two through ten at $t$, and the two zero second answers at $3t$, with angular mean strictly greater than $8/9$. The witness is allowed to depend on the pair. Thus no two such measurements, even retained separately, distinguish every abstract failure law from the shallow comparison. This does not assert arithmetic realizability.

Order the pair as $(s,s+d)$ with $d\ge 0$. The [gap bridges](gap-bridges.md) and their dependencies leave only $-9/50<s<1/5$, $1<d<141/100$ to check. For **same-sign** directions take a constant mixture of $3/10$ of the [right-sector prime law](right-sector-10-2.md) and $7/10$ of the [hard-pair prime law](two-term-10-2.md). Both first representers are linear in this mixture, so their uniform caps are inherited by the triangle inequality. The mixture's mean exceeds $8/9$ by more than $2.66\cdot10^{-7}$. In particular, the moment completion at $t$ is unchanged in scope. At $3t$ one must reprice the new first moment, not average the component slacks.

After mixing the normalized jumps at their original knots, the [checker](check-all-pairs.py) uses [chords in squared imbalance](imbalance-chords.md) to prove normalized capacity minus price greater than $10^{-5}$ for every same-sign coefficient direction and its reverse orientation on the closed residual rectangle. The check actually runs over $-9/50\le s\le8$ and $1\le d\le141/100$, with 908 compact boxes. It encloses the capacity series at imbalance nodes $j/16$ with uniform tail loss $10^{-8}$, certifies all intermediate imbalances by concavity in their square, and interpolates smooth signed branches across each box. Run `sage .godel/bob/check-all-pairs.py` at the repository root with assertions enabled. It replays both component laws first.

For **opposite-sign** directions use the [single-shift law](separated-second-shifts.md). To pass from scalar to simultaneous matching, fix the pair. For each law used there, its attainable second-answer vectors form a compact convex set: With conditional first moment $m(x)$ the possible second moments are $m^2+(1-|m|^2)u(x)$, $|u|\le1$. This disk follows from the chord support calculation for every complex direction, and the integrated image is weak-star compact (the two test functions are integrable). Every real functional on the two answers has a zero on at least one law's image. Strict separation would contradict this if the convex hull of the finite union missed the origin. Take a convex combination realizing the origin and mix the **complete** witnesses, including their prime laws. The linear matches and strict failure mean survive. Neither one completion working at every shift nor a bound on an actual signed measurement follows.

Together with the [finite six-shift separator](sparse-10-2.md), this bounds the minimum number of distinct second translates required at $3t$ between three and six inclusive in this comparison. The upper bound uses additional finite higher-order measurements at $t$, as permitted; six does not count those shifts. A next test is to delete one of the six terms, refit the other five with the first masks fixed, and reprice the actual support function including the finite cost at $t$. A miss by one such support would not prove five impossible.
