# Cancellation cannot stay on nearly the same pool of prime starts

Use the notation and both radii of the [jointly separated arc test](jointly-separated-arc-test.md). A pool is the **unsigned set** of all $2n$ starts in a pair; it records neither their signs nor their partition between the copies. For a pair in $H_r$ call it central if its log coordinates satisfy $|t\nu_1|,|t\nu_2|\le 1/2$. Let $M_2$ be its central mass with original product weights, gates, and distance conditions (no tapers). Fix $C=4+2\gamma$, $L=\log t/D$, and put $b=\lfloor\gamma L/100\rfloor$.

**Pool isolation.** The central mass that can have *any* negative partner at the same shift missing at most $b$ of its $2n$ distinct starts is at most $t^{-\gamma/25+o(1)}M_2$. If the partner must use the same pool the bound improves to $t^{-\gamma/[2(3+2\gamma)]+o(1)}M_2$. The partner may choose its two lists and all its signs after seeing the complete source. Negativity refers to the integrated kernel $T(a'_1)T(a'_2)\cos(a'_1-a'_2)$ under the actual rational cuts, not to one arc width. The result holds also in log coordinates. In fact the negative partner need not pass either privacy, separation, or distance for this upper bound. It must still use $2n$ slots of genuine prime pairs at the same $d$. This is not one-copy isolation applied separately: A partner may mix both copies in any proportions.

**Three independent constraints.** Divide all product weights by $A^{2n}$ until the end. Write $\lambda_p=\log((p+d)/p)$, and $\epsilon_p$ for each central source slot's own sign in its own copy. Every negative partner obeys $|t(\nu'_1-\nu'_2)|\le18$, since on the cut $a'_i=t\nu'_i+O(t^{-2})$ and each positive triangle requires $|a'_i|<2\pi$. If its pooled log is $g_1\nu_1+g_2\nu_2$ for any $g_i\in\{-1,1\}$, its scaled absolute value is at most one and its quadratic cosine is positive. Thus the pooled log cannot equal any of these four values.

Suppose $\ell\le b$ source starts are absent in the target. For every retained start designate one of its target occurrences and use its pooled sign (negate the signs of the second target copy). For each source copy choose $g_i$ so that on retained starts at most half the designated signs differ from $g_i\epsilon_p$. Let $m_i$ count those minorities, $m=m_1+m_2$, and let $\ell_i$ count the removed slots in copy $i$. Write $W$ for the sum on minorities with signs $g_i\epsilon_p$, $R$ for the sum on removed slots with those oriented source signs, and $S$ for the sum of the $\ell$ remaining target occurrences with pooled target signs. Then
$$
 g_1\nu_1+g_2\nu_2-(\nu'_1-\nu'_2)=2W+R-S.
$$
The left side is nonzero and has absolute value at most $19/t$. Each side of the rational product on the right after exponentiation is at most $X^{2m+2\ell}$ before reduction. Integer spacing $|\log(N/D')|\ge1/\max(N,D')$ for $N\ne D'$ gives $m\ge L/2-\ell-O(1)$. If $\ell=0$, apply spacing to $W$ itself, not $2W$: It is nonzero, $|W|\le 10/t$, and both sides of its ratio are at most $X^m$. Thus $m\ge\log(t/10)/D$ in this case.

Fix the removed and minority slot masks and orientations, expose the removed values, and enumerate the extra target list in at most $(2X)^\ell$ ways; its values are not sampled. No target ordering beyond its signed sum is needed. Then $W$ must hit an interval of radius $10/t$ at a known center. Conditional on the minority values, the two source windows yield one interval per majority group at centers now known, for $M_i'=n-\ell_i-m_i$ draws in copy $i$. Drop separation and all remaining cuts *after* deriving these constraints. Use independent draws of law $\rho_p=w_p/A$. Set $f=Z/A$, $K_{B,t}(u)=B(2\pi t)^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(Bu/(2t))$, and $U_s=\int K_{80,t}|f|^s$. Each translated interval for a group of size $s$ and arbitrary fixed signs costs at most $2U_s$ by translated tent majorization. Price both majority hits conditionally, then the minority hit. Their product bound is $8U_{M_1'}U_{M_2'}U_m$. The masks have only $\exp(O(n))$ choices, since they concern labeled source slots, not all bijections to the partner.

**Moment budget.** Put $k=2\lfloor\log(t/160)/(2D)\rfloor=L+O(1)$. Both majority sizes lie in $[k,n]$ eventually and $m\le n$. The [diagonal and cell estimates](isolated-tuple-hits.md) give $U_k\le t^{-1/4+o(1)}$, $U_n\ge t^{-1+o(1)}$ as a lower bound, and $U_n\ll V_n=\int K_{1/2,t}|f|^n$. Indeed the full balanced $n$-tuple log sums arise as differences of two one-sided sums. Cells of width $1/(4t)$ need only $O(1)$ offsets for the large tent, each with correlation at most the same-cell mass by Cauchy; that mass gets weight at least $1/2$ for the small tent. Applying (3) of the joint comparison to the small tent at the power radius gives
$$
 M_2/A^{2n}\ \ge\ e^{-o(n)} V_n^2.
$$
Here $A>a_0$; after normalization the additive error is at most $t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)}(V_n+t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)})$, negligible by the origin lower bound. These power-separated pairs pass all actual cuts at either radius. No upper bound $M_2\ll t^{-2+o(1)}$ has been used.

For $m\ge k$ interpolate each of the three orders between $k,n$ using the same height probability. Since their sum is $2n-\ell$, after division by $U_n^2$ the price is at most
$$
 U_k^{(n+\ell)/(n-k)}U_n^{-(k+\ell)/(n-k)}.
$$
Set $\delta=(C/4-1)/(C-1)=\gamma/[2(3+2\gamma)]$, $x=\ell/L$. Including the list enumeration, the saving in the exponent of $t$ is $\delta-x(1+3/(4(C-1)))-o(1)$. For $\ell=0$ we have already shown $m\ge k$, so this proves the sharper assertion.

For $m<k$, use $|f|\le1$ and the exact permutation diagonal at even order $j=2\lfloor m/2\rfloor$: Nonzero frequencies have size $\ge X^{-j}>80/t$. Thus $U_m\le (j/2)!(\max\rho_p)^{j/2}\le t^{-m/(4L)+o(1)}$ uniformly here, since $\max\rho_p\ll D^2/X$ and $\log n=D/2+O(\log D)$. Interpolate only the two majority orders. Their product divided by $U_n^2$ is at most $(U_k/U_n)^{(m+\ell)/(n-k)}$. The saving after enumeration is at least
$$
 \delta\,m/L-x\big(1+3/(4(C-1))\big)-o(1).
$$
In both regimes this is at least $\gamma/25-o(1)$: In the smaller-order regime use $m/L\ge1/2-x-o(1)$, $x\le\gamma/100$, $\delta\ge\gamma/8$, and $1+3/(4(C-1))\le5/4$. The masks and the comparison of $U_n^2$ to the normalized $M_2$ cost $t^{o(1)}$. This finishes the source bound, without conditioning the original product law on the windows.

**Consequence for summing before taking absolute values.** Group the terms of $H_r$ in any partition such that all pairs in one part miss at most $b$ of one another's starts. This permits, in particular, summing over *all* repartitions and resignings of a fixed unsigned pool before taking its absolute value. A central pair in a part with a negative term is marked in the theorem. The other parts containing central pairs have no negative terms whatsoever. On those central pairs the actual kernel is bounded below by an absolute constant. Hence the sum of the absolute values of the part sums is $\gg(1-t^{-\gamma/25+o(1)}) M_2$. On the $\gg D$ eligible shifts with $A>1/4$ in the [matching lemma](matched-prime-overlap.md), this is larger than the squared common-dip scale $((X/D)(1/16)^n/t)^2$ by $4^{2n}e^{-o(n)}/n^2$ or more. Thus grouping within small support changes cannot supply the needed sign cancellation even after freeing **both** tuple partitions. This is a lower bound for a sum of absolute values, **not** a lower bound for $H_r$ or $I$. The marked-source estimate is relative to $M_2$, not an absolute error one may subtract at the detector threshold.
