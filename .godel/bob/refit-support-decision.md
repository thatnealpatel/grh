# Price a support chosen by the refit

**Decision.** At the conversion of local fixed-moment savings to a
necessary condition, price the best support of a given size rather
than only a named mask. The model can choose which slabs change.
Keep the named masks when direction or location matters. This costs
a sort once the individual savings exist, not another fitter or node.

**What Work knew.** Run `yMilxzL/01a20dd6-b7bf-7fcf-aeb7-406c9f644cb2`
launched Bob at `3b6ca9d444e9` (Work session
`01a20dd6-ba04-7523-8c0d-99e986cad33e`). It correctly rejected a
positive-surplus, linear-cut-passing refit by the original quadratic
and both first caps. The price child `OyqPfKe` produced a positive
full upper, not a feasible law; `tuJWtqO` tested a different left
cutoff with moments fixed, not free original-slab support. By
`7e13fe7ccd8c` the full price and scalar-damping obstruction were
available. Events 291–309 computed per-slab fixed-$B$ nominations and
selected short regional masks. The resulting signed scores really did
exclude a negative shape with positive compensation at `6662ffc02eaa`.
That is a reason to keep those scores. Work's handoff left independent
negative refits open, without bounding how many slab values must move.
Events 66–68 also show that a reduced-rank quadratic was tried; there
is no evidence the full dense quadratic optimization was solved.

**Independent comparison.** Alice's [denominator-pair test](../alice/denominator-pair-gap-regions.md)
at `e6b43a82d74c` prices a whole passing region for one choice of
forms per pair, without multiplying its price by the number of choices.
Its maximizing occupancy is open. The comparable decision is *which
quantifier to price*, not which program proves theorems faster: Bob's
complement can be priced for every best-placed set of $k$ changes, with
no occupancy heuristic. This yields a verified sparse-refit exclusion,
not a full-release exclusion. The control's named locations answer a
different question and may still be more useful for a first-cap packet.
