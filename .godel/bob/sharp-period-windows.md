# Sharp full-period windows for the distant-pair test

The triangles in the [fixed harmonic test, (A) and (B)](windowed-harmonic-witness.md) can be removed without a search over harmonics. Vary the *location* of a sharp window, not its frequency. The windows must still be applied to the actual completed tuples.

Use that note's arithmetic coordinate $a=y-y^2/(2t)$ with $|y|\le8$, at the same $t,d$, and either separation radius. Write $m$ for the unnormalized tuple measure with the original product weights, balance, separation, and completed gate, including the $|y|$ cut. Put $P=2\pi$. For $0\le u\le P$ let $W_u=[u-P,u)$. Define $D(u,w)$ to be the integral of $\cos(a-a')$ over **distant** pairs with $a\in W_u$, $a'\in W_w$, under $m\otimes m$. There is no triangle in this count. Let $Q(u,w;v)$ be the difference of the same two counts with the cosine replaced by the indicators of circular distance at most $v$ from $0$ and from $\pi$, respectively, applied to $a-a'$.

**Two independent locations give an equivalence.** Use normalized Lebesgue measure for the window locations. For every real $a$,
$$\mathbb E_u 1_{W_u}(a)=(1-|a|/P)_+.$$
Indeed the allowed locations are $(a,a+P]\cap[0,P]$. This holds also for atoms at $0,\pm P$. Thus for $u,w$ independent, (B)'s arithmetic integral is exactly
$$ I=\mathbb E_{u,w}D(u,w),\qquad D(u,w)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin v\,Q(u,w;v)\,dv. $$
The second formula is the arc-indicator identity for each phase difference. Consequently (A)'s two-sided additive reconstruction $J_R=\sqrt{\max(0,I)}+O(t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)})$ applies to this sharp-box average. Do **not** change a sharp cutoff from $a$ to $t\nu$ by a pointwise Taylor estimate. The identity mixes boxes *after* the arithmetic comparison of the Lipschitz triangles. There is no extra cut on $|t\nu|$ in the arithmetic boxes. Unlike shrinking a symmetric interval about zero, translating a full period creates no arc excess for an unrestricted product reference flat in $a$ on $[-P,P]$: Each cut produces uniform angular density modulo $P$. Flat marginals need not have this property after imposing a pair restriction.

**One common location gives a necessary witness.** Set $H(u)=\int_{a\in W_u}e^{ia}\,dm$. Without the distance cut the cosine count is $\Re(H(u)\overline{H(w)})$. The cost of imposing distance is $O(t^{-b'+o(1)})$ *uniformly in both locations*, where $b'=1/2+(3+\gamma)(1/2-\xi)>2+\gamma/4$ as in the compact-window proof. In fact $|a|\le P$, $|y|\le8$ imply $|t\nu|\le9$ eventually; hiding $\lfloor\log(t/32)/(2\log X)\rfloor$ slots still gives spacing $\ge32/t>18/t$ for the entire enlarged interval. The first window, the $s$ marked second labels, the second window on the unmarked labels, and the exchange of copies therefore have the same absolute price as before. This is one charge on an enclosing product of windows, not a union bound over translations. Subtract the independent-location average from the common-location average to get
$$\mathbb E_u D(u,u)-I=\mathbb E_u|H(u)-\mathbb E_w H(w)|^2+O(t^{-b'+o(1)}).$$
Hence $J_R\ge B=t^{-1+o(1)}>0$ forces $Q(u,u;v)\ge(1-o(1))B^2$ for some single $u$ and $0\le v\le\pi/2$ at that shift: The common-location average is at least $(1-o(1))B^2$, and $\sin v\,dv$ has mass one. There is neither a growing frequency range nor a logarithmic loss. Compared with the sharp-window harmonic witness, this asks for a bound uniform in translations *inside* the detector window, not just for the original fixed window. Only the triangles were removed, not the prime weights or the gates.

**Use and limitation.** If an arithmetic estimate handles interval indicators, an upper bound $(1-\epsilon)B^2$ on all the common-window arc differences at the required shifts would suffice. It is stronger than controlling the independent-window signed average. Even with no distance cut, the common-window average can overstate $I$: Equal unit atoms at $a=\pm\pi/2$ have zero triangle-weighted first moment, but a positive-measure set of $u$ selects just one atom, giving $|H(u)|^2=1$. This is a diagnostic for the identity, not a model of the prime tuples. Neither an estimate for the sharp arc counts nor a common translation across different shifts is proved here.
