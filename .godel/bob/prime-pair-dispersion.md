# How close to a named height can dispersion force a common dip?

Put $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, and $L_1=X/D$. At **every** sufficiently large center $t$, even a continuous one, there is a common ordinate in $[t-KL_1,t+KL_1]$ with only $o(D)$ large moving pair sums, for **any** $K=K(X)\to\infty$. Here and below large means $|C_{d,X(u)}(u)|>1/16$, and we count all integer $cD\le d\le4D$ for fixed $c>0$. The [peak implication](short-prime-pair-bias.md) needs the smaller radius $bL_1$. The estimate below allows a bounded number of isolated clusters of the required size, **including one right at the center**. A test using separated prime bands identifies which terms would have to support such a cluster.

## A translated second moment with its diagonal floor

For now freeze $X$ independently of the real center $v$. On the prime pairs in $I=[e^{-8}X,X]$, write $w_p=X^{-1}\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$, $\lambda_p=\log(1+d/p)$, $Z_d(u)=\sum_p w_p e^{iu\lambda_p}$, and $A_d=\sum_p w_p$; weights depend on $d$ and vanish off the support. Then $C_{d,X}=\Re Z_d$, $0\le w_p\le8D^2/X$, and $\sum_d A_d^2\ll D$ by the pair sieve. We claim, for $T>0$,

$$\sum_d\int_{v-T}^{v+T}|Z_d(u)|^2\,du\ll_c X+T D^3/X. \tag{1}$$

Use a constant multiple of $(\sin(s/(2T))/(s/(2T)))^2$ centered at $v$ as a nonnegative majorant. Its angular-frequency transform is $O(T)$ and vanishes for $|\xi|>1/T$. Thus after expanding the square and taking absolute values, the bound is $O(T)$ times the mass of ordered starts $p,r$ with $|\lambda_p-\lambda_r|\le1/T$, summed over $d$. Since $-\lambda'(x)=d/[x(x+d)]\ge d/X^2$ between starts, these have $|p-r|\le Y=\min(X,X^2/(cDT))$.

In fact for **any** $0\le Y\le X$, with an absolute implied constant for the specified upper shift bound,

$$\sum_d\sum_{|r-p|\le Y}w_p w_r\ll D^3/X+DY/X. \tag{2}$$

The diagonal and both orientations with $|r-p|=d$ cost at most $3\sum_{d,p}w_p^2\ll (D^2/X)\sum_d A_d\ll D^3/X$, using $2ab\le a^2+b^2$. For $h=|p-r|>0$, $h\ne d$, the four distinct offsets are $0,d,h,d+h$. Write $G(n)=n/\varphi(n)$ for $n>0$ and $U(d,h)=[G(d)G(h)G(|d-h|)G(d+h)]^4$. By [Kevin Ford, *Basic sieve methods and applications*, Theorem 2.5](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf), the count of contributing lower starts for fixed $d,h$ is $\ll XU(d,h)/D^4$. The four forms are primitive with slope one; the prime divisors of the determinant product divide $dh|d-h|(d+h)$. Nonadmissible tuples have no survivors in $I$ for large $X$, since an obstructing prime is at most four. The theorem has constants depending only on the number of forms. The two weights together cost $\ll D^4/X^2$ and there are two orientations.

For each fixed $s>0$, $\sum_{n\le N}G(n)^s\ll_s N$: Expand over squarefree divisors with nonnegative multiplicative coefficients $g_s$, where $g_s(\ell)=(1-1/\ell)^{-s}-1=O_s(1/\ell)$, and use $\sum_a g_s(a)/a<\infty$. For $Y\ge1$, apply Hölder to the four factors of $U(d,h)$ on $cD\le d\le4D$, $1\le h\le Y$, $h\ne d$. For each $|h-d|$ or $h+d$ moment, fix the variable with shorter range and sum over the longer: Each nonzero argument is $\le 4D+Y$ with multiplicity at most two. The initial-interval moment bound suffices. Thus $\sum_{d,h} U(d,h)^r\ll_r DY$ for any fixed $r>0$. This proves (2), including $Y<1$ by the diagonal bound. Substitution gives (1); no lower estimate for four prime tuples is used.

On any radius $T\le X^B$ with fixed $B$, $\sup_{|u-t|\le T}|X(u)-X|=o(1)$ by differentiation. Per $d$, only $O(1)$ integers cross the two band endpoints and each affects at most two pairs. The common weights have total change $O(D^2|X(u)-X|/X)$ by differentiating $X^{-1}\log(X/p)$. Hence the complex sums differ by $O(D^2/X)$ uniformly; the extra integrated squared error is $O(TD^5/X^2)$, absorbed in (1). A moving shift range may be enclosed in a slightly larger fixed one. Taking $T=\min(K,X^{1/2})L_1$ in (1), the average count above threshold is $O_c(D/\min(K,X^{1/2})+D^3/X)=o(D)$, proving the common-ordinate assertion.

## What fits inside the error bound?

Write $L_2=X^2/D^3$. In **each** interval of radius $b_2 L_2$ the fraction of ordinates with even one large pair sum is $O_{c,b_2}(D^3/X)$. In particular, at a putative peak all the pair sums can be small throughout most of the very interval where [positive local four-prime squares](prime-rectangle-persistence.md) persist. There is no contradiction: Local squares do not bound a global sum from below.

More precisely, consider centers within $L_2$ of a specified large $t$ that meet the first two conditions in (1) of the [pair theorem](short-prime-pair-bias.md) for at least $\kappa\log X(v)$ shifts at their own cutoffs. Their $bX(v)/\log X(v)$ neighborhoods each contribute $\gg_{b,\kappa} X$ to the moving integral of the sum of squares, by that theorem's derivative proof (it uses only the center conditions). All these neighborhoods lie in a common interval of radius $2L_2$, with shifts in a common range $[cD/2,5D]$; the proof of (1) applies there unchanged. Its total cost is $O_c(X)$. Thus only $O_{c,b,\kappa}(1)$ of the neighborhoods can be disjoint. Equivalently, the candidate centers there can be covered by boundedly many intervals of length $O(bL_1)$. This does not bound the number of conductors in one cluster. Even charging the $\kappa D$ simultaneous biases in a single forced interval gives the **same order** as (1), not a contradiction with it. For the [free twist at its own moving cutoff](prime-band-phase-controls.md) such a cluster actually occurs: The argument for (1) also works with arbitrary unit twists on the pair coefficients, since they disappear in (2).

## A separated-band test for an isolated cluster

Short-range collisions can be priced even at the center, without averaging in height. Partition $I$ into $J$ equal intervals (disjoint at endpoints), and restrict $Z_d$ to interval $j$ of **starting primes** to obtain $Z_{d,j}$. Let $E$ be the shifts selected by a peak, $|E|\ge\kappa D$. Define $N_d$ as the total unsigned product mass in equal or adjacent bins, including both orientations. These starts differ by at most $2X/J$, so (2) gives $\sum_d N_d\le C(D^3/X+2D/J)$ for an absolute $C$. Choose a fixed integer $J>1024(C+1)/\kappa$. Then for all sufficiently large $X$ fewer than $\kappa D/2$ shifts have $N_d\ge1/128$. This choice spends the error against the **actual threshold**; discarding only the exact diagonal would not give separated bands.

For each remaining $d\in E$, $|Z_d(t)|^2>1/64$. After removing those equal and adjacent bin pairs, the signed sum of the remaining ordered cross products exceeds $1/128$. Hence some $j,k$ with $|j-k|\ge2$ satisfy

$$\Re\big(Z_{d,j}(t)\overline{Z_{d,k}(t)}\big)>1/(128J^2). \tag{3}$$

One and the same bin pair satisfies (3) for at least $\kappa D/(2J^2)$ shifts. At fixed $J$ all products here are on four distinct primes for large $X$, but with start separation $\gg X/J$, not $O(D^2)$ as in the local rectangle. The bin pair can depend on the center, from a fixed finite menu in the coordinate $p/X$. This identifies a signed test for peak exclusion: One need not bound all four-prime terms, nor treat a positive local square as evidence for agreement between two distant parts of the prime band. It would suffice to rule out (3) at that multiplicity for each of the nonneighboring bin pairs at actual named centers. Neither Ford's unsigned theorem nor the dispersion bound at an enlarged radius does so; aligned free twists pass even (3). This is a reduction for the peak implication, not a proof of the original $o(D)$ assertion at radius $bL_1$.

There is also a signed test for the **common dip itself**, without fixing a set of shifts in advance. Suppose the requested assertion fails. Then for some $\varepsilon>0$ along arbitrarily large named centers, at every $|u-t|\le T=bL_1$ at least $\varepsilon D$ of the specified moving sums exceed $1/16$. Enlarge to the full fixed range $cD\le d\le4D$ for the squared norm. Freezing costs $o(1)$ uniformly, so $\sum_d|Z_d(u)|^2\ge (\varepsilon/256-o(1))D$ on the interval. Now take $J>4096(C+1)/\varepsilon$ fixed. Equal and neighboring bins contribute at most $\sum_d N_d<\varepsilon D/1024$ eventually. Expand the rest, then average over the interval: Along a subsequence there must be a **fixed** nonneighboring bin pair $j,k$ such that

$$\sum_d\ \sum_{p\in I_j,\,r\in I_k} w_p w_r\cos(t(\lambda_p-\lambda_r))\,\operatorname{sinc}(T(\lambda_p-\lambda_r))>\frac{\varepsilon D}{512 J^2},\qquad \operatorname{sinc}(x)=\sin(x)/x. \tag{4}$$

Indeed the ordered far-bin sum averages to more than $\varepsilon D/512$, and there are fewer than $J^2$ ordered bin pairs. This time (4) is a **single signed sum over all shifts**, not a sum of absolute values, or a choice of a different pair of bins at each ordinate. In particular, nonpositive normalized limsup (normalize by $D$) for each fixed separated bin pair for arbitrarily large fixed $J$ would suffice for the requested every-center assertion. The sign in (4) matters: For the short radius the sinc factor has argument of size at most $4be^8$ throughout. It is not a frequency cutoff that removes distant starts. Positive spikes in the free-twist control force (4) with their own phases as well.
