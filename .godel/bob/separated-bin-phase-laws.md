# Which two-prime phase laws can see the signed separated-bin sum?

Write $F_{jk}(t)$ for the full signed left side of [(4)](prime-pair-dispersion.md), with $t=2q^{3/5}$, $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $T=bX/D$, and $J$ fixed. Use the shift range $cD\le d\le4D$ and the constants $b,c$ of the [pair implication](short-prime-pair-bias.md). There are two different tests here:

* At the **actual phases**, any order-$D$ positive value of $F_{jk}$ can be transported, with $o(D)$ loss, to a nearby named height at its own cutoff where all fixed two-prime harmonics of nonzero total degree cancel locally. This works down to start cells of width $H$ with $H/D^2\to\infty$. At $H=O(D^2)$ the simultaneous harmonic conclusion is false, regardless of height.
* Even adding cancellation of the remaining harmonics **after pooling neighboring shifts** does not determine the sign test. Two completely multiplicative controls on the same primes and named heights pass both tests; in one the signed bin sum is $\gg D$ for a fixed separated pair, and in the other it is $o(D)$ for every separated pair. These are not examples for untwisted primes.

## Transport without changing the signed test at leading order

Let $w_{d,p}=X^{-1}\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$ on pairs of primes in $I=[e^{-8}X,X]$, zero otherwise, and $A_d=\sum_p w_{d,p}$. For a cell $C$ of starting locations and integers $k,l$, put

$$M_{d,C}^{k,l}(u)=\sum_{p\in C}w_{d,p}\exp(iu[k\log p+l\log(p+d)]).$$

Initially freeze everything except the phases. Put $R=X/D^2$. At every large center $t$ one can select a named $v$ with $|v-t|\le R$ such that, now using **its own** weights, shift range, and regular start grid of width $H=H(X(v))\le X(v)$,

$$\sum_{d,C}|M_{d,C}^{k,l}(v)|=o(D)\qquad(k+l\ne0). \tag{1}$$

Here $H(x)/(\log x)^2\to\infty$ is prescribed; end cells may be clipped. One selection works for all fixed characters, not uniformly over unbounded degrees. The vanishing is weighted and summed over cells and shifts; it says the local pair law is insensitive in these tests to multiplying both phases by the same circle element. It says nothing of the ratio character $(-1,1)$.

Indeed, freeze a grid of width $h\le X$. For $k+l\ne0$, the derivative of $k\log x+l\log(x+d)$ is $(k+l)/x-ld/[x(x+d)]$. It has constant sign and absolute value $\ge |k+l|/(2X)$ throughout the band for large $X$. Average over $|u-t|\le R/2$ using a translated nonnegative squared-sinc majorant as in [(1) of the dispersion note](prime-pair-dispersion.md). Only frequencies within $O(1/R)$ can contribute to the upper bound on the mean square. These starts differ by $O_{k,l}(D^2)$. The collision bound (2) there gives

$$\operatorname{mean}\sum_{d,C}|M_{d,C}^{k,l}|^2\ll_{k,l}D^3/X,\qquad
\operatorname{mean}\sum_{d,C}|M_{d,C}^{k,l}|\ll_{k,l}D^2/\sqrt h.$$

The second inequality counts $O(DX/h)$ cells with shift labels. For $h/D^2\to\infty$ choose one $u$ where the sum of the normalized tests for a slowly growing finite prefix of the characters is $o(1)$: Each fixed prefix has mean $o(1)$. [Squarefree sampling](sampled-principal-peaks.md#rotate-first-then-sample) gives an admissible $v=2q^{3/5}$ within $O(t^{-1/9})$ of $u$. For each fixed character the change summed over the cells is $O_{k,l}(D^2|v-u|)$, since $\sum A_d\ll D$.

Here are details to handle moving grids, even if $H$ jumps. Throughout the window $X(u)=X+o(1)$. Take $B\le X$ a lower bound for the moving widths in the window, with $B/D^2\to\infty$, and use the frozen fine grid $h=\sqrt{D^2 B}$. For any regular grid with width at least $B$, the incomplete fine cells cut by its boundaries number $O(DX/B)$ counting shifts. The sum of **squared unsigned** fine-cell masses over all shifts and fine cells is $O(D(D^2+h)/X)$ by the same collision bound. By Cauchy the total cut-cell mass is $O(D\sqrt{(D^2+h)/B})=o(D)$; uncut cells combine by the triangle inequality. Thus grid motion needs no bound on $H'$. At common shifts moving band endpoints and weights cost $O(D^2/X)$ per shift in total variation as in the dispersion note. A changed shift endpoint costs at most $O(1)$ shifts, each with $A_d\ll G(d)^2\ll (1+\log(2d))^2$, $G(n)=n/\varphi(n)$, by the pair sieve (or Ford's two-form bound); $G(d)=\sum_{m\mid d}\mu^2(m)/\varphi(m)\ll1+\log(2d)$ by the prefix first moment of $G$ and partial summation. All these changes are negligible in (1).

To transport **the signed sum**, write $\delta=\log(1+d/p)-\log(1+d/r)$. Uniformly $|\delta|\le4e^8D/X$. At the frozen cutoff the change in $F_{jk}$ is at most $O(RD/X)\sum_d A_d^2=O(1)$, without taking absolute values in the definition of the test. Restoring the cutoff and the fixed-$J$ bins costs $O_J((D^2/X)\sum_d(A_d+D^2/X))$ on the common shifts: Each bin boundary crosses only $O(1)$ integers. The change of $T$ is $o(1)$, negligible by the bounded derivative of sinc and the same frequency bound. The exceptional endpoint shifts cost $O((1+\log(2D))^4)=o(D)$ in the product. Thus $F_{jk}(v)=F_{jk}(t)+o(D)$ at its own parameters. It suffices to test the normalized upper bound on sequences satisfying (1). This does **not** transport the ramp peak, nor prove a dip by changing the radius.

### Why a bounded multiple of $D^2$ does not work in (1)

For $H\le KD^2$ with $K$ fixed, put $w_*=D^2/X$ and $U_{d,C}=\sum_{p\in C}w_{d,p}$. Taking all phases one in the [pair extraction](separated-bin-shift-fibers.md) gives $aD\le\sum U_{d,C}\le a'D$ for fixed positive $a,a'$. The collision bound gives $\sum U_{d,C}^2\ll_K D w_*$. The two-form sieve and $\sum_{d\le4D}G(d)^2\ll D$ bound the **number** of entries by $O(X/D)$, without weights. Atoms of weight $<\tau w_*$ therefore cost $O(\tau D)$; cells with $U_{d,C}>P w_*$ cost $O_K(D/P)$. Take fixed $\tau$ small, $P$ large. At least $aD/2$ of the original mass remains, and on these atoms $w_{d,p}/U_{d,C}\ge\tau/P$ using the *original* cell mass. Consequently $\sum_{d,C,p} w_{d,p}^2/U_{d,C}\ge\beta D$ for some $\beta(K)>0$, omitting empty cells.

The nonnegative Fejér kernel $1+2\sum_{k=1}^N(1-k/(N+1))\cos(kx)$ equals $N+1$ at zero. Apply it to all pairs of phases in each cell with weights $w_{d,p}w_{d,r}/U_{d,C}$. The diagonal contributes at least $(N+1)\beta D$. The constant term costs at most $a'D$, and $|M^{k,0}|^2/U_{d,C}\le|M^{k,0}|$. Since the coefficients of the cosines sum to $N$, at every height

$$\max_{1\le k\le N}\sum_{d,C}|M_{d,C}^{k,0}|\ \ge\ \frac{(N+1)\beta-a'}{N}\,D.$$

A fixed sufficiently large $N(K)$ suffices. The maximizing degree may vary. This lower bound uses no phase approximation and is not evidence for positivity of $F_{jk}$.

## Restore the ratio characters in isolation: Two controls with different signed sums

Take the same sparse sequence of named heights for both controls, with disjoint bands $I$. Fix $J$ sufficiently large, any regular start grid of prescribed width $D^2\ll H\le X$ with $H/D^2\to\infty$, and a regular partition of the shift range into consecutive blocks $Q$ of prescribed length $L\to\infty$, $L\le D$, clipping ends. In each control there is a **single completely multiplicative twist** $\omega$. At a center write $z_p=\omega(p)p^{it}$ and replace the exponential in $M^{k,l}$ by $z_p^k z_{p+d}^l$. Both controls satisfy (1), and in addition

$$\sum_{Q,C}\left|\sum_{d\in Q}M_{d,C}^{k,l}\right|=o(D)\qquad ((k,l)\ne(0,0)). \tag{2}$$

These are the Fourier tests for two independent uniform phases after pooling shifts, in the sense of absolute errors summed over boxes, relative to total mass of order $D$. The zeroth coefficients in every box are identical in the two controls. No equidistribution in an individual box of vanishing mass is asserted. Nevertheless, on a common subsequence one control has a fixed nonneighbor pair with $F_{jk}\gg D/J^2$ and the other has $F_{jk}=o(D)$ for all such pairs. Here the cosine is replaced by the real part of $z_{p+d}\overline{z_p}\overline{z_{r+d}}z_r$; the sinc is **unchanged**, since away from the center the extra factors $\omega$ are fixed.

Assign $z_p=\exp(i\theta_j p)$ on bin $j$. Use one uniform slope on the circle shared across all bins in the first control, and independent uniform slopes in the second. Set $\omega(p)=p^{-it}z_p$ on each disjoint band, one elsewhere on primes, and extend multiplicatively. Split start cells additionally at the fixed bin boundaries; this costs only $O_J(1+X/H)$ cells per shift. In checking the estimates, delete pairs for which $p+d$ has left the starting bin. At most $O_J(D)$ integer starts per shift are affected, of total weight $O_J(D^3/X)$ per shift. Even in $F_{jk}$ the total deletion costs $O_J((D^3/X)\sum_d A_d)=o(D)$. One need not delete them at all in the first control.

For an uncut pair in bin $j$ the exponent of its character is $\theta_j((k+l)p+ld)$. For $k+l\ne0$ orthogonality at each fixed $d$ and subcell leaves only the diagonal in its expected square. The sum of those diagonals is $O(D^3/X)$ by the weight cap and $\sum_d A_d\ll D$. Cauchy as in (1) gives $O_J(D^2/\sqrt H)=o(D)$ in expected total absolute value. For $l=-k\ne0$ the exponent instead is $l d\theta_j$. After summing over $d\in Q$, orthogonality leaves $\sum_{d\in Q}U_{d,C}^2$ in each expected square (here $C$ is split by bins and the masses omit deleted pairs). By the collision bound the total is $O(D(D^2+H)/X)$. There are $O_J((D/L)(X/H))$ boxes, so the expected left side of (2) for these characters is $O_J(D\sqrt{(1+D^2/H)/L})=o(D)$. For the other characters (2) follows from (1). This argument uses the original consecutive shift labels, not a random reassignment of prime pairs.

In the shared-slope control the two ratios for the **same $d$** are both $e^{id\theta}$, and every four-phase product equals one. For the persistence choice of $b$, $4be^8\le1$, so the sinc is at least $1/2$. The all-one extraction gives $\sum A_d^2\gg D$; the entire equal- and adjacent-bin mass costs $O(D/J+D^3/X)$. Pigeonholing the remaining mass and passing to a subsequence give the stated fixed separated pair. In the independent-slope control, up to the uniform deletion error, its signed sum is exactly

$$\sum_d B_{d,jk}\cos(d(\theta_j-\theta_k)),\quad B_{d,jk}=\sum_{p\in I_j,\ r\in I_k}^{\rm uncut} w_{d,p}w_{d,r}\operatorname{sinc}(T\delta).$$

At $j\ne k$ the common angle difference is uniform, so the expected square is $\tfrac12\sum_d B_{d,jk}^2\le\tfrac12\sum_d A_d^4\ll D$ by the pair sieve and the prefix eighth moment of $G$. This is cancellation **after summing shifts**, not a small unsigned count. For each center select slopes by averaging the sum of the normalized errors in (1), (2), and, for the independent control, $|F_{jk}|/D$ for the finitely many separated bin pairs. A slowly growing prefix of character tests suffices as before. This constructs deterministic twists along the sequence. The controls concern (4), not the necessity or sufficiency of (4) for any zeta peak.

There is also an **exact invariance of the genuine test**, not just a property of the two examples. Start with the actual phases $y_p=p^{it}$ and replace them at all primes in the band by $z_p=e^{i\theta p}y_p$, using one common slope. The four factors for a given $d$ acquire $e^{i\theta(d-d)}=1$. Thus $F_{jk}$ itself, for every $j,k$, has exactly its untwisted value, with no change to $t$, $X$, or $T$. The preceding orthogonality estimates for the marginal tests still apply: For $k+l\ne0$ their surviving diagonal is unchanged by the fixed factors $y_p^ky_{p+d}^l$, and for $k+l=0$ the coefficient at each $ld$ has modulus at most $U_{d,C}$. Hence there is a common-slope representation of **every** actual $F_{jk}(t)$ satisfying both marginal bounds. It does not assert (2) for $p^{it}$ without the modulation. Unlike this representation, the nearby-height result (1) requires no freedom in the phases.

**Decision.** The disappearing degree-$k+l\ne0$ modes can be imposed on genuine named centers without losing a putative violation. Going on to prove the pooled law (2), even on very slowly growing consecutive blocks, would still miss the agreement of the ratio phases at *matching shift labels* in distant bins. For example, to keep those labels when using an additive Fourier variable, put $P_j(\theta,u)=\sum_d e^{id\theta} Z_{d,j}(u)$ with the true phases and frozen cutoff. Exactly,

$$F_{jk}(t)=\frac1{2T}\int_{t-T}^{t+T}\int_0^{2\pi}\Re(P_j(\theta,u)\overline{P_k(\theta,u)})\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\,du.$$

The slope must be the **same** in both bins. Each $P$ has squared norm at most $\sum A_d^2\le C_0D$ under this probability measure. If $F_{jk}>\eta D$, the part with $\min(|P_j|,|P_k|)\le\eta\sqrt{D}/(4\sqrt{C_0})$ costs at most $\eta D/2$ by Cauchy applied to each factor separately. Only the region where both exceed this threshold needs a signed upper estimate. This does not say that this region has positive limiting measure. In fact the controls have identical single-bin spectral distributions on the uncut terms: At every $u=t+s$, $P_j$ for a slope $\theta_j$ is exactly the zero-slope polynomial translated by $\theta_j$ in its angle variable. Even knowing every separate bin's large-value distribution would not see a *relative* translation. In the shared-slope example an arc of length $1/(16D)$ carries a positive contribution $\gg_J D$ by itself. To see this, for the selected pair $\sum_d A_{d,j}A_{d,k}\gg_J D$, where $A_{d,j}$ denotes bin mass. Discarding $A_d>P$ costs $O(D/P^2)$ by the fourth moment, so both $\sum_d A_{d,j}$ and $\sum_d A_{d,k}$ are $\gg_J D$ by choosing $P=P(J)$ large. On $|s|\le T/8$ and $|\theta+\theta_j|\le1/(32D)$ modulo $2\pi$, every term in both polynomials has argument of absolute value at most $1/4$, since $4be^8\le1$. Their real cross product is $\gg_J D^2$ there. Thus even overlap of measure tending to zero cannot be ignored at the required precision. A useful next test is to price its **product-weighted** contribution at genuine centers, not the measure of the large values of either marginal. No estimate here bounds that remaining integral, the signed (4), or the number of bad shifts at fixed radius for every center.
