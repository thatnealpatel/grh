# How many replacement-frequency intervals does a nonlocal pairing need?

Changing many starts avoids [local isolation](isolated-tuple-hits.md), but not if the replacement frequencies lie in a small list of predictable intervals. The list can depend on disclosed source primes; the choice of a partner from the list can use the *whole* source. The estimate counts sources, not pairs.

Use the fixed $t=2q^{3/5}$, shift $d$, mass floor $A_d>a_0>0$, and ordered product probability of the isolation note. Thus $D=\log X$, $L=\log t/D$, $n=CL+O(1)$, $C=4+2\gamma$, and $0<\gamma<1/2$ is fixed. Write $\nu=\sum_i\epsilon_i\lambda_{p_i}$ with the original balanced signs. Let $M$ be the probability of a separated source with $0<|t\nu|<1/2$, at either separation radius of that note. Put $k=2\lfloor\log(t/160)/(2D)\rfloor=L+O(1)$.

**What can depend on what.** Fix $\beta,\omega\ge0$. For each removed set of slot indices $J$, each target sign pattern on the retained indices $J^c$, and each disclosed set $E$ of at most $\beta L$ indices, allow a list of at most $t^\omega$ intervals of radius $1/t$. For these masks the list is a function of the labeled starts in $E$ alone (and of the fixed test). The functions, not their values, are fixed before drawing the source. Mark a separated source with $|t\nu|\le2\pi$ if there exist such masks and a partner satisfying:

* The two unexposed sizes $r=|J\setminus E|$ and $v=|J^c\setminus E|$ are both at least $k$.
* The partner uses the retained starts with the designated signs, plus $|J|$ signed occurrences at this same shift. Their total signed label $s$ lies in the chosen list. These extra starts need not be distinct, and partner signs need not balance.
* The partner frequency $\nu'$ satisfies $|t\nu'|\le2\pi$, a window containing the negative annulus.

The masks, interval, and partner can all be selected after seeing the source. Uniformly over the list functions and allowed shifts, the marked source mass is at most

$$ t^{\omega-\delta_\beta+o(1)} M,\qquad \delta_\beta=\frac{C/4-1-3\beta/4}{C-1}. \tag{1} $$

**Proof.** Fix the masks and expose the labeled values on $E$ under their original product law. For one resulting interval $[a-1/t,a+1/t]$, a partner hit confines the *hidden retained* sum with its **target** signs to an interval of half-width $(2\pi+1)/t$. Its center is determined by $a$ and the disclosed retained starts. This necessary condition does not involve the hidden removed starts. Once all retained values are known, the **source** hit confines the hidden removed sum, with its original signs, to a second interval of half-width $2\pi/t$. Its center may depend on all retained values. Do not charge a uniform probability to the partner or generate it. Dropping separation after taking these necessary conditions only enlarges the source event.

Use $U_m=\int K_{80,t}|f|^m$ from the isolation note with the same probability kernel at every order. For independent draws with arbitrary fixed signs, either interval condition costs at most $2U_m$ on its block of size $m$: Twice the translated triangle of half-width $80/t$ majorizes the indicator, and translation in the Fourier integral has modulus one. Conditional on the retained starts the second price is uniform. Integrating it over the first event gives $4U_r U_v$; the two events need not be independent.

Write $e=|E|$. Since $r+v=n-e$ and both sizes are in $[k,n]$, log convexity gives

$$ \frac{U_r U_v}{U_n}\le U_k^{(n+e)/(n-k)} U_n^{-(k+e)/(n-k)}. $$

At this $k$, $X^{-k}\ge160/t$, so the permutation diagonal gives $U_k\le t^{-1/4+o(1)}$. For $U_n$ use the **lower** bound $t^{-1+o(1)}$ from the origin on the right, and the **upper comparison** $U_n\le t^{o(1)} M$ on the left. The latter is the fixed-width cell comparison followed by full-to-separated comparison under the small nonnegative kernel, not an absolute upper bound at scale $1/t$. The saving before the interval count is $[(C+e/L)/4-(1+e/L)]/(C-1)+o(1)\ge\delta_\beta+o(1)$. Sum over the intervals *after* exposure; integrating the exposed product probabilities costs at most one per choice of masks. There are at most $6^n=t^{o(1)}$ mask choices (removed, retained positive, or retained negative, and disclosed or not). This proves (1) without counting orders or multiplicities of partner starts.

**Use and limits.** For example, remove $h=n/2$ starts. Even after revealing $\lfloor\gamma L/3\rfloor$ starts anywhere, both hidden groups exceed $k$. Here $\delta_{\gamma/3}=\gamma/[4(C-1)]>0$. Any method of pairing a fixed fraction of $M$ into the negative annulus this way must escape every such list of $t^{\delta_{\gamma/3}-\varepsilon}$ intervals, for fixed $0<\varepsilon<\delta_{\gamma/3}$. It is the number of resolvable **sums**, not the number of tuples yielding them, that this prices. With no disclosure the exponent is $\gamma/[2(C-1)]$. For example, even allowing *every* replacement block with $|s|\le t^{-1+\omega}$ gives only $O(t^\omega)$ intervals; no enumeration or probability law for the replacement primes is needed. These are restrictions on partial-overlap transports, not estimates for all partners on far supports. For instance, with no retained starts there is no first interval constraint on the source. A separate interval centered at the negative retained sum after reading *all* hidden retained starts would also make that constraint vacuous.

**Why not take all intervals?** Even with no disclosure, a list covering *every* separated replacement block changing half the starts is too large. To check this, take $j=2\lfloor h/2\rfloor$ signed draws with half of each sign. They can avoid all $R$-neighborhoods of up to $n$ prescribed starts and each other with probability $t^{-o(1)}$ or larger: With $b=\max\rho_p\ll D^2/X$, each successive forbidden mass is $O(n(1+R)b)=o(1)$, and the total logarithmic loss is $O(n^2(1+R)b)=O(1+R)=o(\log t)$. Each interval of radius $1/t$ carries unconditional probability at most $2U_j\le 2U_k\le t^{-1/4+o(1)}$. Thus a full cover needs $t^{1/4-o(1)}$ intervals, more than the budget even at $\beta=0$. This is **not** a lower bound for the list needed by a filtered family of successful comparisons.

The same two-block calculation prices a central donor proposal. Fix the receiving tuple and try splicing in a block of $j$ slots from a donor drawn independently from the separated core. For fixed masks, the receiving tuple fixes the frequency window for the donor's transferred sum. Given this donor block, its complement must still complete the donor hit. Under the unconditioned donor law this costs at most $4U_jU_{n-j}$; divide by $M$ only afterward. Union over the two slot masks costs at most $4^n$. For $k\le j\le n-k$ the conditional success probability is therefore at most $t^{-\delta_0+o(1)}$, even with the masks chosen after both tuples are seen and the transferred signs unchanged. This does not rule out a deliberately matched donor.

When proposing a nonlocal pairing, specify what chooses the replacement-sum intervals and how many unexposed slots must satisfy each hit. A larger or unpriced list gives no positive transport result: It still needs reverse weights or target multiplicities. For a direct signed estimate no list is required. The one-sided [shape integral](separated-signed-tuples.md) for the actual gated prime-pair tuples is unchanged; (1) is relative to $M$ and does not pay an absolute deletion at detection scale.
