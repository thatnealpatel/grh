# Review of the common-ordinate experiment

**Decision.** At the handoff from a persistent prime-pair bias to an exclusion test, negating the condition in witness order should cause Work to allow different low ordinates for different shifts instead of demanding one low ordinate for almost all shifts, because it is the *same positive-density set* that must persist. The logic is correct, but the replay exposed a more important fact: At the stated radius **every** center-selected shift already persists by its center value alone. Changing the order of the search is not a new source of cancellation. Keep the Work prompt and graph. Change the mathematical handoff, not the generic instructions.

**Bounded replay.** In improvement session `01a20833-8491-7011-b694-395c4dadf72c`, two fresh readers received the launch Work text, the same fixed theorem, phases, cutoff error, radius, and controls, but no later results. Both had one decision and no edits or delegates. Only the treatment had the proposed quantifier instruction. The comparison sought a weaker sufficient exclusion at the proved radius, not just a renamed common-height test; enlarging that radius without proof would be a regression.

Control `yYyfcWw` gave an exact frozen-height average via a sinc multiplier and used the positive part of the average at each shift: A peak forces the sum of their squares divided by $D$ to be at least $\kappa/256-o(1)$ on the mass-capped range. Treatment `fkYFFWS` correctly negated persistence separately for each shift, **then** noticed that $4Me^8(D/X)bX/D\le1/64$. Thus $C(t)>1/8$ gives $C(u)>7/64-o(1)$ everywhere in that interval. What one must rule out is already the count at the center. Its proposed full second moment is sufficient but also charges large negative values, unlike the control's positive-part test. Neither obtained the required small constant. Mixed result: A useful diagnosis, but no established weaker arithmetic estimate, speedup, or live prompt effect. Do not install the sentence on this evidence. Reconsider if on a different persistence proof the per-shift witnesses give an actually weaker verifiable bound without losing the radius or the sign.

**Change of experiment.** Instead of sampling the old interval more finely, localize a second square and charge the repeated primes. The [rectangle proof](prime-rectangle-persistence.md) keeps four distinct primes and uses the smaller *difference* of the pair frequencies. Dividing by local unsigned box mass makes the cost of varying the height bounded by $M$ times the largest frequency. This gives a radius $b_2X^2/D^3$ for the new statistic, not for the old one. Reader `tMZyKkO` checked the normalized formula, shared-prime deletions, and moving lengths. This is a paper implication, not evidence that a low value occurs. The explicit small phase swing in that proof rules out relying on window smoothing alone. The next useful question is a signed constraint for the actual four-prime phases at a named center, for enough of the eligible shifts to break persistence. A new necessary condition with no signed upper bound is not an endpoint estimate.
