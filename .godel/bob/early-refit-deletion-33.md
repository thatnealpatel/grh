# The early-score refit fails the quadratic test at 39/20

On the [1,257-knot comparison](joint-tail-deletion-33.md#full-negative-replacement-with-a-measurable-tail), refitting all original slabs and enforcing the score on $[0,1)$ does produce **positive surplus with every retained linear cut strict**. The resulting field is not a witness: Its full second inequality fails by more than $0.428$, and each continuous first cap fails at a single explicit frequency.

## Check the fitted law, not the optimizer

Keep $X=0$ before $-144/25$, and use the original knots up to $29/5$. In this trial (not a restriction in the upper bounds), allow independent tail steps of width $1/10$ through $36$, then return to the reference. The LP used all the retained first cuts, the retained second tangent, and the additional constraint that the early score exceed $2.01\cdot10^{-8}$ (implemented as a weak lower bound). The early score is the contribution of $[0,1)$ to the change in $M$ less the weighted changes of the cut functionals, using the tangent for the second cut. The dictionary included the reference law, endpoint atoms, local maxima of the sampled slab price, and, for positive slabs, a fixed-$(A,B)$ three-atom law. Only the returned profile matters, not optimality over the dictionary.

The [integer table](deletion-laws/delete-33-early-refit.txt) gives the left knot in units of $1/400$, followed by (site, weight) pairs. Normalize each row to mass one. Nonnegative sites are in units of $10^{-9}$; label $-1$ denotes the reference on that slab, and $-2$ its [fiber triple](deletion-laws/delete-33-tail-fiber.triples) with the weights obtained by solving the three exact mass, $A$, and $B$ equations. Past $5.8$ both labels use the last reference slab. Finally mix each row with $1/1000$ of the reference (weight $999/1000$ on the table). This last retreat pays for rounding and numerical LP tolerances.

For this rational law the surplus is $6.39498955\ldots\cdot10^{-8}$, the actual early score is $9.78167160\ldots\cdot10^{-8}$, and the smallest slack of the 192 retained unweighted inequalities exceeds $2.0743\cdot10^{-10}$. In the early-score calculation restrict the *field differences* to $[0,1)$ before taking jumps, including the jump back to zero at $1$.

For $V=V_B$, $V_*=V_{B_*}$, and the retained three-site direction, let $Q$ and $d$ be the quadratic form and threshold of the [joint price](joint-tail-deletion-33.md#joint-price). The checker evaluates the full lattice kernel on both jump vectors, with no new tangent. The affine test omits precisely $Q(V-V_*)$. This term is $0.42846251944\ldots$ and the **full $Q(V)-d=0.42846251543\ldots$**. This is not an artifact of the Laurent majorant $q$: With $q=p+10e$, the verified $p>0$, $|L|>0.1$, and $|p^2-|L|^2|\le e<10^{-8}$ give $0\le q-|L|<2\cdot10^{-7}$. Replacing $q$ by $|L|$ in the violation subtracts at most $2\cdot10^{-7}\int |V|^2\,d\mu$, even without assuming the cap. The checker evaluates that norm with the same kernel, constant polynomial $1$, and zero site coefficients. The unmajorized violation still exceeds $0.428$. Independently, $|V_A(36.202)|>5.92$ and $|V_B(34.121)|>2.52$ (both caps are one). No completion of the simultaneous second replies, or verification on outer frequency rays, can turn this trial into a witness.

## A necessary change on the remote tail

The early constraint is not the only regional one. With all original slabs free, $M\ge8/9$ and feasibility require **both $A>0.715$ and $B>-0.6828965$ simultaneously** on a subset of $[24,30]$ of Lebesgue measure greater than $0.00147$. The terminal reference has $A=0.6077479\ldots$ and $B=-0.8191010\ldots$. In particular a fixed-$B$ search at these knots cannot succeed, even if $A$ and all pre-tail moments are otherwise free and $B$ is held fixed only on this remote band.

Here is the comparison, using only the retained full-negative cut list. Write $m=te^{-t}$, and $a,b$ for the signed $A,B$ price densities. On $[24,30]$ replace $A$ by $A'=\min(A,s)$, $s=0.715$, leaving the signed residual integral $R=-\int_{24}^{30}a(A-s)_+\,dt$ separate. The clipped triple need not be a law; it lies in the box $0\le U\le1$, $-1\le A'\le s$, $|B|\le1$. At a grid node the old upper price before subtracting the reference is at least $D=\max(a+b,m-a-b)$ by testing the two endpoint atoms. The new box price is at most $N=m+a_++s(-a)_++|b|$. Thus replacing the old trapezoid by the box trapezoid reduces the upper bound by at least the trapezoid of $D-N$. No sign assumption on a node's reduction is needed. Use the band endpoints with half weight on the inside; on the outside retain the old price.

The chord allowance carries over **because the old derivative estimate used only the coordinate ranges**. For each *fixed* box triple subtract the reference density; its three coefficient differences are at most $1,2,2$ in absolute value. Hence its second derivative satisfies the identical uniform bound used in the original $1/2400$ chord estimate. Interpolate with the triple fixed before maximizing over the box, cell by cell. Never differentiate $A(t)$, $A'(t)$, or a maximizing selection. The remaining terms, including the remote remainder beyond $48$, are unchanged. Starting with the verified old total $<7.814\cdot10^{-8}$ gives $R>6.73\cdot10^{-10}$ if $M\ge8/9$. Using $A'=A_*^{\rm tail}$ instead gives $-\int_{24}^{30}a(A-A_*^{\rm tail})\,dt>5.70\cdot10^{-8}$ by the same argument with $N=m-aA_*^{\rm tail}+|b|$.

Positive contributions to $R$ require $a<0$ and $A>s$. On the band $|a|\le e^{-12}\sum_{j\text{ on }A}\sigma_j/C_t$ by the unit phase in each first cut. Dividing by this bound times $1-s$ gives the stated measure. Finally $A\le f(B)$ with $f(b)=\cos(\arccos(b)/3)$: Each $X\le f(T(X))$, and $f$ is concave as the inverse of the increasing convex branch of $T(X)=4X^3-3X$ on $[1/2,1]$. Thus $A>s$ implies $B>T(s)=-0.6828965$ on the very same set. This localization is necessary, not sufficient. It does not apply after changing the cutoff or releasing the negative knots.
