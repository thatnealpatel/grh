# Separation at the detection amplitude

A failed common dip forces a large signed moment even if all starts must be more than $(\log X)^2$ apart. At this radius there is a detector at the **same amplitude** $a=1/16$, with a constant loss depending on the failure density. The larger radius $X^\xi$ in the [full signed comparison](separated-signed-tuples.md) makes the arithmetic family smaller but does not give this constant by pointwise comparison. The distinction is already visible for the actual weights at height zero.

Freeze at the named $t=2q^{3/5}$, not at the bottom of a dyadic block. Use $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $0<\gamma<1/2$, $h=\lceil(2+\gamma)\log t/D\rceil$, and $n=2h\asymp\sqrt X/D$. For each shift use the same primes, weights $w_p$, log labels, and complex sum $Z_d(u)$ as in the full comparison. Let $J_r(d)$ be the original ordered, balanced, signed sum with multiplier $G(t\nu)$, retaining just tuples with every two starts at distance $>r$. Put $r=D^2$.

**Detector.** Fix $b,c,\varepsilon>0$. Suppose that at every $u\in F=[t-bX/D,t+bX/D]$ at least $\varepsilon D$ real pair sums in the moving range $cD(u)\le d\le4D(u)$ have absolute value greater than $a=1/16$ at their moving cutoff. Then for $\gg_\varepsilon D$ distinct shifts at the cutoff of $t$,

$$ J_r(d)\gg_{b,c,\varepsilon,\gamma} \frac XD\frac{a^n}{t}. \tag{1} $$

In particular it suffices to show that for each fixed $\delta>0$ only $o(D)$ shifts reach $\delta (X/D)a^n/t$ at large named centers. The distinction matters only without spare amplitude. For any fixed $a'<1/16$, the $X^\xi$ comparison already detects common-dip failure at scale $(X/D)(a')^n/t$: Use the whole window and any intermediate amplitude in $(a',1/16)$ to absorb $\exp(o(n))$. Likewise the [stronger shifts forced by a peak](short-prime-pair-bias.md) have fixed slack over $1/16$. What (1) adds is the endpoint $1/16$ for the common dip itself, without a peak or an assumed gap. It is not a constant-factor comparison of two arbitrary small signed sums.

**What the radius costs.** Write $C_d(r)=\sum_{|p-p'|\le r}w_p w_{p'}$ with both orders and the diagonal included. Equation (2) of the [dispersion estimate](prime-pair-dispersion.md) gives

$$ \sum_d n^2 C_d(r)\ll n^2(D^3+Dr)/X\ll D. \tag{2} $$

Here and below use the enlarged frozen range $cD/2\le d\le5D$: The proof there sums the diagonal and shared endpoints by the two-prime bound, and the other offsets by the four-form bound and moments of $j/\varphi(j)$ up to $5D+r$. Changing the fixed endpoint constants does not change (2). Take a fixed $K$ large enough that fewer than $\varepsilon D/4$ shifts have $n^2 C_d(r)>K$. This discards shifts, not a sign slice of tuples. For every $u\in F$ the moving large shifts lie in the frozen range eventually. Freezing at $t$ costs $O(D^2/X)=o(1/n)$ per shift on this window: The cutoff moves by $o(1)$, at most $O(1)$ starts cross its boundaries per shift, and the common weights have total change $O(D^2|X(u)-X|/X)$. These large shifts have $A\ge a/2$ eventually, enough for the earlier uniform estimates with a fixed mass floor. Thus on the incidence set under consideration $|Z_d(u)|\ge a-o(1/n)$, uniformly. The sum of the window fractions of those incidences on the retained shifts is at least $3\varepsilon D/4$. Since there are at most $5D$ labels, $\gg_\varepsilon D$ of them each have fraction at least $\varepsilon/20$.

**The quadratic coefficient pays the whole nonvanishing cost.** For a retained shift use $P,F_m,S,b_j$ from the full comparison on the graph with distance cutoff $r$. In particular $F_m=m![z^m]P$ for the independence polynomial with real activities $x_p=w_p\cos(\theta+u\lambda_p)$. Put $B=C(1+2r)w_*=O(D^4/X)$, $A=\sum w_p=O(D^2)$. In the coefficient bound $|b_j|\le4AB^{j-1}$, the degree-two case admits an exact improvement:

$$ b_2=2[z^2]P-S^2=-\sum_p x_p^2-2\!\sum_{\{p,p'\}\text{ edge}} x_p x_{p'},\qquad |b_2|\le C_d(r). $$

Flip all activities if necessary so $S=s=|S|$; $F_n$ is unchanged. For $s\ge a/2$, suppose all earlier consecutive ratios $F_i/F_{i-1}$ are at least $s/2$. The recurrence of the full comparison, divided by $sF_{m-1}$, has leading term 1. The $j=2$ error is at most $2mC_d(r)/s^2$, and all $j\ge3$ together cost at most $(4A/s)\sum_{k\ge2}(2mB/s)^k\ll_a A m^2 B^2$. The error is uniformly $o(1)$ since $n C_d(r)\le K/n$ and $n^3 A B^2=o(1)$. This proves the ratio hypothesis by induction from $F_0=1$. Summing the absolute log errors gives

$$ F_n\ge s^n\exp\{-O_a(n^2 C_d(r)+n^3 A B^2)\}\gg_{a,K}s^n. \tag{3} $$

Elsewhere a constant-factor comparison is unnecessary, but a **global lower floor** is essential. The original ratio and small-projection estimates apply with this graph too: $r\le X^\xi$ eventually, so their bounds in terms of the maximum degree only improve. For $s_0=t^{-(1+\gamma/8)/n}$ they give $F_n\ge0$ on $s\ge s_0$ and $F_n\ge-t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)}$ below it. This is not monotonicity of a signed coefficient in the graph.

Multiply the uniform angular average by $2^n/\binom nh\asymp\sqrt n$. At an incidence, angles $|\theta+\arg Z_d(u)|\le1/\sqrt n$ modulo $2\pi$ have $s^n\gg a^n$, since $a$ is fixed and the freezing loss is $o(1/n)$. Their angular measure cancels the normalization in order. Equation (3) gives a positive contribution $\gg_{a,K}a^n$ there. Over all other angles and all other heights the normalized lower floor costs at most $t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)}$. Integrate against the original nonnegative probability density $t^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(\pi(u/t-1))$, which is $\gg 1/t$ on $F$. Projection selects the balanced signs, and the real transform is exactly $G(t\nu)$. The resulting sum has no diagonal because $r>d$ and all endpoints are distinct. For each shift with the stated incidence fraction the floor is negligible compared with $(X/D)a^n/t=t^{-1+o(1)}$. This proves (1).

**Where the scale stops.** For $A\ge a$ set $u=0$ and choose $\cos\theta=a/A$. Then $S=a$ exactly, all $x_p$ are nonnegative, and $F_n/a^n$ is the probability of separation for $n$ iid starts of law $w_p/A$. Partition the band into $L=O(1+X/r)$ cells of diameter at most $r$. Separation requires distinct occupied cells. This probability is at most $(L)_n/L^n$: For $L\ge n$ the distinct-cell probability is $n!$ times the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $n$ in the cell masses, maximized at equal masses by Maclaurin's inequality. For $L<n$ it is zero. In the first case the product is at most $\exp(-n(n-1)/(2L))$. Thus for $r/D^2\to\infty$ there is **no** uniform constant lower comparison of $F_n$ to $|S|^n$ even at this single fixed amplitude on a mass-rich shift. In particular, at $r=X^\xi$ the loss can be at least $\exp(\Omega(X^\xi/D^2))$ in the inverse comparison. The aligned projection is not a counterexample at a named center: The translated height kernel vanishes at zero. This limitation is on the pointwise proof, not on every possible signed integral estimate.

**Arithmetic handoff.** In the constrained divisor count at the end of the [full comparison](separated-signed-tuples.md), change only the support radius to $D^2$. Distinct prime endpoints, squarefreeness, $N(0)=0$, the multiplicity $(h!)^2$, and the signed shape integral remain exact. The starts need *not* all be private at the old radius $X^\xi$. If using the old rounded-slot gate, intersect it with the new separation condition: Discarding tuples with too few private slots on the kernel support costs at most the original absolute error. Do not require **all** slots to be private at the old radius. Use (1) if the prospective arithmetic bound needs the identical amplitude and tolerates the larger family; keep the $X^\xi$ comparison if power separation is needed or a smaller fixed amplitude is available. Neither test estimates the signed shape at the named center.
