# A longer height test on four distinct primes

At a threshold peak, the [same selected prime gaps](short-prime-pair-bias.md) have a positive signed four-prime statistic throughout an interval of radius $b_2X^2/D^3$. This is longer than the pair interval of radius $bX/D$ by an unbounded factor. It is the **new statistic**, not the pair sum or the peak, that persists there. No upper bound for it at the named heights is known here.

Use the notation and constants of (1) in the linked proof. Fix a selected even $d$, write $m=1+M$, $H=8192mD^2$, and put $a_p=\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$ when $p,p+d$ are primes in $I$, and zero at all other integers. Only nonzero entries are used in phase expressions. Define

$$v_p(x)=\frac{a_p}{H}1_{[p-H,p)}(x),\qquad g(x)=\sum_p v_p(x),\qquad \lambda_p=\log(1+d/p).$$

Use normalized local pair weights $P_{pr}(x)=v_p(x)v_r(x)/g(x)$, zero when $g=0$. Define the signed statistic at frozen cutoff by

$$ R_{d,X}(u)=\frac1X\int_{\mathbb R}\sum_{p,r:\,|p-r|\notin\{0,d\}} P_{pr}(x)\cos\big(u(\lambda_p-\lambda_r)\big)\,dx. $$

The sum is ordered. For $r=p+h>p$, its four primes are $p,p+d,p+h,p+h+d$, distinct, with $h<H$. Its positive log frequency is exactly $\log(1+dh/[p(p+d+h)])$. The weights involve only primes and the cutoff, not the height phases. The local division by $g$ prevents a concentration of unsigned pairs in some boxes from shortening the radius.

**At the center.** Before deletions the integral is the square of $F(x)=\sum_p v_p(x)e^{it\lambda_p}$ in the form $X^{-1}\int |F|^2/g$. Since $X^{-1}\int g=A_d\le M$ and $\Re X^{-1}\int F=C_{d,X}(t)>1/8$, weighted Cauchy bounds this integral below by $1/(64m)$. At each $x$ the diagonal costs at most $\max_p v_p$, and the two orientations $r=p\pm d$ together cost in absolute value at most $2\max_p v_p$. Indeed on each such diagonal one factor can be bounded by the maximum and the other factors sum to at most $g$. The support has length at most $(1-e^{-8})X+H<X$ eventually. As $\max_p v_p\le8D^2/H$, the deleted cost is at most $24D^2/H=3/(1024m)$. Hence $R_{d,X}(t)>13/(1024m)$, with no four-prime sieve or asymptotic for prime tuples.

**On the longer interval.** The integrated unsigned mass after deletions is at most $X^{-1}\int g=A_d\le M$. The remaining frequencies have absolute value at most $e^{16}dH/X^2\le 4e^{16}(8192m)D^3/X^2$. Multiplying these bounds controls the frozen derivative at every ordinate. For example with $b_2=1/[1024m^2\cdot4e^{16}(8192m)]$ the loss from the center for $|u-t|\le b_2 X^2/D^3$ is at most $1/(1024m)$. This uses the exact frequency difference, not a Taylor approximation to its phase at $t$.

We may use the statistic at $Y=X(u)$, updating $I,D,H$ as well (primes on symbols below denote the updated weights and lengths). Uniformly on this interval $|Y-X|=o(1)$ since the derivative of $X(t)$ is $18\log(t/2)/t$ and the radius is polylogarithmic in $t$. For fixed $d$, only $O(1)$ integers enter or leave the pair support; their weights cost $O(D^2)$. For common entries the only changing factor in $a_p$ is $\log(X/p)$, with change $O(|Y-X|/X)$. Thus $\sum|a'_p-a_p|=O(D^2(1+|Y-X|))=o(X)$ uniformly in the selected $d$. Also $H'/H\to1$. The two normalized box indicators at a fixed $p$ have $L^1(dx)$ distance $2|H'-H|/\max(H,H')$. Using $\sum a_p\le MX$ gives $\int\sum_p|v'_p-v_p|\,dx=o(X)$. The map $v\mapsto v\otimes v/\|v\|_1$ on nonnegative vectors, extended by zero, is $3$-Lipschitz in $\ell^1$: Compare the two tensors first over the larger mass, then change the denominator of the smaller tensor. The first change costs at most $2\|v-v'\|_1$, the second at most $\|v-v'\|_1$. Keeping a subset of entries and multiplying by cosines cannot increase this error. Changing the outer normalization costs $o(1)$ as well. In particular all the same $\ge\kappa D$ shifts satisfy

$$ R_{d,X(u)}(u)>1/(128m)\qquad (|u-t|\le b_2X^2/D^3). $$

**Exclusion experiment.** At each sufficiently large named center it suffices that fewer than $\kappa D$ eligible shifts stay above this threshold on the longer interval. A low ordinate can depend on the shift; an upper bound for the signed average for that shift also suffices if it forces such an ordinate. One can restrict eligibility to $A_d\le M$ and $C_{d,X}(t)>1/8$ in the original range. Merely finding dips on still larger intervals does not suffice. The point is a longer interval and a different arithmetic sum, **not** cancellation from height smoothing alone. Indeed $b_2X^2/D^3$ times even the largest frozen rectangle frequency is at most $1/(1024m^2)$: Averaging $e^{i(u-t)(\lambda_p-\lambda_r)}$ against any probability on that interval changes $1$ by at most this much. A small signed average would have to use the phases at the center, not attenuation by that kernel alone. Free twists with a peak still pass the necessary test with their own four phases. It is cancellation of the actual curvature phases at the coupled height that remains to be shown.
