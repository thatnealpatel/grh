# A translated mesh for the one-copy test

At a fixed genuine center, one may choose the origin of the **sampling grid**, not the origin of the **prime-pair phases relative to the kernel**. In the notation and with the two radii, weights, and disjoint bands of the [height reconstruction](balanced-height-reconstruction.md), put $\omega_p=\log(1+d/p)$, $\Omega=\max\omega_p-\min\omega_p$, and $P=2\pi/\Delta$. For $P\ge h\Omega+t^{-1/2}+2\pi/t$, take any $b\bmod\Delta$ and replace both integrals $M_i^{\rm ht}$ by the sums of $\Delta K_t W_i$ at $\tau=b+j\Delta$ on their respective bands. Write $Q_b$ for their sum. Then, uniformly in $b$ and such meshes,

$$ |S-Q_b|\le o(1) Q_b+t^{-1-\min(\gamma/8,1/100)+o(1)}. $$

In particular an upper estimate with a fixed margin on **one chosen origin** suffices; if $|S|\ge B$ then **every origin** has $Q_b\ge(1-o(1))B$. It need not be the same band that pays on every grid. A common mesh of order $X/(hD)\asymp\log t$ suffices since $\max\omega_p\le5e^8 D/X$. Nothing here estimates either retained sum.

**Sample the signed coefficient first.** Write the untruncated real coefficient as $D_h(\tau)=\sum_\nu c_\nu e^{i\tau\nu}$, aggregating repeated frequencies. The coefficients are real with $c_{-\nu}=c_\nu$, and balance gives $|\nu|\le h\Omega$. With $H_t(x)=(1-t|x|/(2\pi))_+$ the exact formula is

$$ \Delta\sum_j K_t(b+j\Delta)D_h(b+j\Delta)=\sum_m e^{imPb}\sum_\nu c_\nu e^{it(\nu-mP)} H_t(\nu-mP). $$

Indeed the periodization on the left is continuous (the kernel decays quadratically); its $m$-th Fourier coefficient unfolds to the inner sum, using the shifted triangle transform. Only finitely many harmonics survive. If $P\ge \max|\nu|+2\pi/t$, only $m=0$ remains, giving exactly $J_r$. The same check with the constant function gives mass one for our grids. At $b=t+\theta\Delta$ the phase of each term is $e^{it\nu}e^{2\pi i m\theta}$, not a modulation of the underlying prime weights. Applying this identity directly to a thresholded $W_i$ would require a bandwidth it does not have in general.

**Price the omitted sample points.** On $T\le|\tau|\le2T$, $t^{1/2}\le T\le t^3$, use $k=2\lfloor\log(T/4)/(2D)\rfloor\le n$. A positive majorant for the positive shell is a constant times $\operatorname{sinc}^2((\tau-3T/2)/(2T))$, with a reflected copy for the negative one. Its transform has support $[-1/T,1/T]$ and its integral is $O(T)$. Its product with $s^k$ has bandwidth at most $k\Omega/2+1/T<P$, so its sampled sum times $\Delta$ **equals** its integral for every origin. In that integral only the permutation diagonal survives: A nonzero moment frequency $\log(N/M)$ with $N,M\le X^k$ has modulus at least $X^{-k}>1/T$. At zero use the [least differing start](dyadic-prime-pair-moments.md). Thus the sampled shell moment is $\ll T(k/2)!(A w_*)^{k/2}\le T^{3/4+o(1)}$, with $w_*=\max w_p$.

The remaining estimates use only positivity and pointwise bounds, so apply to these sums. Explicitly, $\Delta\le 2\pi t^{1/2}$ and the grid length charged to an interval of length $L$ is at most $L+\Delta$. With $|D_h|\le A^n=t^{o(1)}$, the central $|\tau|<t^{1/2}$ costs $t^{-3/2+o(1)}$ by the kernel zero, and dyadic shells beyond $t^3$ cost $t^{-2+o(1)}$ in total. Charge the pointwise floor term of the absolute bound once to kernel mass one. For $T=t^y$ in the remaining range, $s\le X^{-\beta}$ saves $X^{-\beta(n-k)}$ from $s^n$ (use $A^{n-k}=t^{o(1)}$ for unrestricted $s$). Multiplying by $O(T^2/t^3)$ below $t$ and $O(t/T^2)$ above gives exactly the piecewise exponent $f_\beta(y)$ in the reconstruction. For unrestricted $s$ outside the wider band its maximum is $-33/32$; for $\beta=1/10$ outside the narrower band it is $-101/100-\gamma/5$; for $\beta=\alpha$ everywhere it is $-1-\gamma/8$. At cut endpoints one may bound the truncated shell by a whole shell, changing $y$ by $O(1/\log t)$. Summing, applying the pointwise relative estimates on the two retained sets, and using the completed-gate error $|S-J_r|\le t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)}$ proves the claim. This does not assert separate quadrature to $o(B)$ for either discontinuous band weight.

**Fixing a prime phase.** For the least start let $\omega=\max\omega_p$. Set $L=\lceil(h\Omega+t^{-1/2}+2\pi/t)/\omega\rceil$. At $\Delta=2\pi/(L\omega)$ the mesh is the union of $L$ subgrids, on each of which $e^{i\omega\tau}$ is constant. Here $1\le L\le h$ eventually, since $\min\omega_p\gg1/X$. The test is the **mean** of their coarse sums, each normalized with step $2\pi/\omega$, not a test on one arbitrarily prescribed phase. This gives a discrete next target: Estimate this finite phase average of the two weighted sums at a chosen $b$ with the original $K_t$ and untouched prime phases. One cannot charge a translate containing the peak at zero as though the kernel there were of order $1/t$.

**One phase can alias even at a genuine center.** Run `python3 .godel/bob/check-height-mesh.py`. On the reconstruction's $q=100000007$, $d=26$, $r=D^2$, $h=4$ fixture, take the coarse mesh $P=\omega=\log(29/3)$. In the exact formula all $|m|\ge2$ vanish: The logarithmic range of *separated* quartet products, plus $2\pi/t$, is strictly below $2\omega$. For $m=1$ the checker encloses the imaginary part of the inner sum $C_1$:

$$ -2723 < 10^{12}\Im C_1 < -2722. $$

It sorts all 80,331 separated quartet products as rationals. For each smaller product $u$, search around $v=(29/3)u$ by exact bisection. Ratios with $|t\log(v/((29/3)u))|<2\pi$ lie in the searched multiplicative interval by $e^x\le1/(1-x)$ for $0\le x<1$. Reject intersecting or conflicting starts. Exactly 8,387 ordered choices of the two increasing quartets have positive triangle weight; multiply each by $(4!)^2$, the original prime weights, and the interval-enclosed triangle times sine. At physical origin $b=\Delta/4$ the deviation from $J_r$ is $-2\Im C_1>5444\cdot10^{-12}$. Staggering by half a coarse step cancels this alias in the mean **exactly**. This finite statement concerns the ungated signed integral, not an asymptotic obstruction, the completed $S$ at this fixture, or an exact two-phase quadrature for the positive band weights.
