# What can even the full set of short prime ratios certify?

A small [positive box mass](real-run-length.md) is sufficient to exclude a principal peak. What if it is large? First ask what the short edges determine *before* forming box sums. At one genuine named center they give a sharp small cap. But even knowing every short complex edge at every ordinate can be consistent with opposite principal outcomes at arbitrarily large named centers in the multiplicative-phase control class. This includes all positive masses, fixed-label runs, and products formed only from those edges.

## A sharp test with relative phases only

Take the prime ramp $S_Y(v)=\sum_{p<Y}w_p p^{iv}$, $w_p=Y^{-1}\log p\log(Y/p)$. On any graph of these primes suppose we know each oriented complex ratio $z_{p'}/z_p$ of a consistent unit phase assignment. Include unseen primes as isolated vertices. In a connected component $G$, multiply edge labels along paths from a root to recover $r_p=z_p/z_{\rm root}$. Write $a_G=|\sum_{p\in G}w_pr_p|$. The largest possible modulus consistent with the labels is exactly $\sum_G a_G$; the smallest is $\max(0,2\max_G a_G-\sum_G a_G)$. Indeed each component sum has just one free rotation, independently. The possible radii of a sum of freely rotated segments fill the interval between these endpoints (successively add a segment of length $b$ to one of radius $r$, obtaining $[|r-b|,r+b]$). Every choice extends from the primes to a unit completely multiplicative twist. No choice of auxiliary angle or averaging of box locations is needed. These extrema are over twisted assignments, not over ordinates for the original untwisted phases.

For $q=2^{127}-1$ take the **named center** $t=2q^{3/5}$, $Y=X(t)=(3\log(t/2))^2$. Give all termwise short ratios $e^{it\log(p'/p)}$ in the clip $[e^{-8}Y,Y]$ with $0<p'-p\le4\log Y$, not just one aggregate per shift. Successive primes connect exactly until a gap exceeds 40. There are four clipped components, with endpoints $(11,15683)$, $(15727,16141)$, $(16183,19609)$, and $(19661,25097)$, and four isolated primes below the clip. The sharp upper radius lies in $(0.04136603,0.04136604)$. For comparison, if every edge label were one **on this same support**, even the minimum radius would exceed $0.82034516$ (maximum $<0.98429172$). Here one component carries more than half the mass. These assertions use [a 160-bit interval checker](check-component-cap.py), runnable with SageMath 10.9 Python and assertions enabled. Evaluating $t\log(p/p_{\rm root})$ implements the telescoped path product, not a short-increment approximation. This is not another test of the offset cell; at a single specified ordinate a direct prime sum is simpler if the absolute phases may be used.

## At large cutoffs no component carries positive mass

Fix $C,\eta>0$. On primes in $[\sqrt X,2X]$ put edges of length at most $C\log X$. For all sufficiently large $X$ every component spans at most $X^\eta$. Here is an elementary covering argument; an isolated large prime gap somewhere in the band would not suffice.

Choose $0<c<\min(\eta/2,1/4)$, $z=c\log X$, and a fixed integer $B>C/c+2$. It suffices to cover $1,\ldots,\lfloor Bz\rfloor$ with one residue class per prime up to $z$. Use class 1 for primes $p\le K$ with $K$ a large *fixed* integer, and class 0 for $K<p\le z/2$. Any survivor is $K$-smooth or has form $a\ell$, $1\le a\le 2B$, $\ell>z/2$ prime. The smooth numbers cost $O_K((1+\log(Bz))^{\pi(K)})=o(z/\log z)$. For each $a$ the surviving primes $\ell\le Bz/a$ must avoid $a^{-1}\bmod p$ for every $2B<p\le K$. By the prime number theorem in progressions with the **fixed** product of those primes as modulus, their number is at most

$$\left(\prod_{2B<p\le K}(1-1/(p-1))+o_{B,K}(1)\right)\frac{Bz}{a\log z}.$$

The product tends to zero by divergence of the reciprocal primes. Fix $K$ so that after summing over $a$ its leading coefficient is $<1/4$. There are $(1/2+o(1))z/\log z$ reserved primes in $(z/2,z]$. Assign a distinct one to each survivor, using its residue. CRT now places a block of $\lfloor Bz\rfloor$ integers each divisible by a prime $\le z$, repeated with period $P=\prod_{p\le z}p=\exp((1+o(1))z)$. In or near the band these integers exceed $z$, hence are composite. No edge crosses such a block. Even the two boundary components have span at most $P<X^\eta$, by using the periodic blocks just outside the band. Implied onset depends on $C,\eta$; nothing predicts this fragmentation at $Y=25107.49\ldots$.

## Same short-edge data, opposite principal means

Choose arbitrarily sparse odd prime conductors with $t_j=2q_j^{3/5}$, $X=X_j=X(t_j)\to\infty$, and the above prime bands disjoint. Define two fixed unit completely multiplicative twists on primes in each band by $\omega_+(p)=p^{-it_j}$ and $\omega_0(p)=p^{-it_j}U_G$, one unit phase per component. Put both equal to one outside the union. For every edge the two twists have *identical* ratios, not just identical moduli. This equality survives multiplying by $(p'/p)^{iv}$ for any $v$ and taking powers of the twist. It remains true for any cut, weights, and varying ordinate for which the queried edges stay in the band and have length $\le C\log X$. For the usual moving clip near $kt_j$, $1\le k\le X^{1/4}$, take e.g. $C=5$ to cover lengths $\le4\log X(kt_j)$.

Nevertheless for $Y=X(kt_j)$ the two twisted principal means $\sum_{p<Y}w_p(Y)(\omega(p)p^{it_j})^k$ are respectively $1+o(1)$ and $o(1)$, uniformly for these integer $k$. Indeed $Y/X=1+O(\log X/\sqrt X)$ uniformly. Below $\sqrt X$ the absolute ramp mass is $O(X^{-1/2}\log^2 X)$ by counting integers; the full positive mass tends to 1 by PNT. For the second control take the $U_G$ independent and uniform on the circle on this band. With $m_G(Y)=\sum_{p\in G,p<Y}w_p(Y)$ we have $\max_G m_G(Y)\ll(1+X^{1/4})\log^2 X/X$ by the span bound with $\eta=1/4$. For each $k\ge1$ the mean square of the band sum is $\sum_G m_G(Y)^2\le O(1)\max_G m_G(Y)$. Summing over $k\le X^{1/4}$ gives $o(1)$. Choose one realization with the sum of squares no greater than its expectation on each band. This gives one $\omega_0$ for the entire sequence and uniform smallness, without independence between different harmonics. The $k$th statistic uses $\omega^k$, not a claim about harmonics of one twisted Dirichlet series with coefficients $\omega$ held fixed.

**Decision.** A cap from small short-edge data remains valid. The example rules out a converse interpreting alignment of even *all* such edges as forcing a principal peak in the control class. It does not rule out a uniform small-box bound for the genuine phases, or construct an untwisted large value. If exact short ratios have survived a screen, the component radii test is the sharp test from those data alone. If that cap is too large, a sharper inference at the same center needs information tying together the component rotations, for example an actual longer ratio between roots. Do not refine an aggregate of the same short edges expecting to recover a missing rotation.
