# The exceptional height set for prime ratios at the coupled cutoff

Does multiplicative independence at one shift remove the exceptional center in the [fixed-radius test](prime-pair-dispersion.md)? It gives a power saving on a dyadic interval, stronger than the second-moment density estimate there. It does not give the needed empty exceptional set. In fact the diagonal argument works for **every fixed unit prime twist**, including the [matched control](matched-prime-overlap.md) with persistent shifts.

**Claim.** Fix a threshold $a>0$, a radius constant $b>0$, and a fixed number of equal start bins (or use the whole band). For $v\in[U,2U]$, call a center exceptional if at *some* $|u-v|\le bX(v)/D(v)$ a complex pair sum in even one bin and shift $1\le d\le 4D(u)$ exceeds $a$ in modulus at its moving cutoff $X(u)=(3\log(u/2))^2$, $D(u)=\log X(u)$. Such centers are covered by $U^{3/4+o(1)}$ intervals of length one. In particular on $Q\le q\le2Q$ at most $Q^{17/20+o(1)}$ integer conductors can have such a center $v=2q^{3/5}$. The constants are uniform in a fixed system of unit-modulus coefficients on prime pairs independent of the varying ordinate (so a prime twist is allowed). Taking the whole band and $a<1/16$ prices a superset of the common-dip failures.

**Freeze the dyadic band.** Use $X=X(U)$ and $D=\log X$. On $[U/2,3U]$ the cutoff differs by $O(\sqrt X)$, so at each bin or band boundary and each shift only $O_J(\sqrt X)$ possible integer starts are affected. With $w\le O(D^2/X)$ this costs $O_J(D^2/\sqrt X)$ per sum. On common pairs differentiation of $X^{-1}\log(X/p)$ costs $O(X^{-2})$ per unit cutoff change, and the two logs are $O(D^2)$, again giving $O(D^2/\sqrt X)$ after summation. Use all $1\le d\le 5D$; changing the allowed shifts identifies a subset, not a separate error. Thus a moving large sum gives a frozen sum exceeding $a/2$ at the same height.

**Exact diagonal at growing order.** For a fixed shift and bin let $Z(u)=\sum_p w_p c_p e^{iu\lambda_p}$ be the frozen sum, $|c_p|=1$, $\lambda_p=\log((p+d)/p)$. On raising $|Z|$ to $2h$, each frequency is $\log(n/m)$ with $n,m\le X^{2h}$. A nonzero frequency has magnitude $\ge X^{-2h}$ by the mean value theorem. At zero the two $h$-tuples of starts must be permutations: If signed edge multiplicities differ, their least differing start has unequal valuation balance there, since its only possible incoming edge starts lower. Unique factorization is essential here; no disjoint matching or sieve removal is needed. The total diagonal is therefore $\le h!(\sum_p w_p^2)^h$, and the phases $c_p$ cancel exactly on it.

Take $h=\lfloor\log(U/4)/(2D)\rfloor$. A constant times $\operatorname{sinc}^2((u-3U/2)/(2U))$ majorizes the indicator of $[U/2,3U]$, has integral $O(U)$, and has Fourier transform supported on $[-1/U,1/U]$ in angular frequency. No nonzero frequency contributes. By the two-form bound $A_d\ll G(d)^2$ and the weight cap, $\sum w_p^2\ll D^2 G(d)^2/X\le D^{O(1)}/X$ uniformly here (odd shifts are empty). Hence the measure of $|Z|>a/4$ there is at most

$$ O(U)\left(\frac{hD^{O(1)}}{a^2X}\right)^h=U^{3/4+o(1)}\quad\text{as an upper bound}.$$

Indeed $D=2\log\log U+O(1)$, $\log h=D/2+O(\log D)$, and $hD=(1/2+o(1))\log U$. Union over the $O_J(D)$ tests costs $U^{o(1)}$. Deterministically $|Z'|\le 5e^8(D/X)A_d=o(1)$ on the frozen band. Thus every unit grid interval meeting a value $>a/2$ lies wholly in the $>a/4$ set for that same test, away from the outer endpoints, which do not arise for the specified centers and their short windows. There are at most $U^{3/4+o(1)}$ such unit intervals. Expanding by the allowed polylogarithmic radius and covering again by unit intervals changes only the $o(1)$ in the exponent. The spacing of $2q^{3/5}$ near $U$ is $\gg U^{-2/3}$, giving $O(U^{2/3})$ possible conductors per unit interval and the claimed exponent after $U\asymp Q^{3/5}$.

**The actual product-weighted overlap.** One may take $a=D^{-1}$ in the dyadic proof: Freezing and the unit-interval derivative cost $o(a)$, and the extra $O(h\log D)$ in the moment exponent is $o(\log U)$. Outside the same size exceptional cover, all frozen bin coefficients at a center $v$ and all heights $|u-v|\le bX(v)/D(v)$ are then $O(D^{-1})$, also when frozen at $X(v)$ by the dyadic cutoff comparison. For each such $u$, Parseval gives $\int |P_j(\theta,u)|^2\,d\theta/(2\pi)\ll D^{-1}$. Hence the **absolute** product integral on *any* same-height, same-angle overlap set, even before imposing the large-modulus thresholds, is $O(D^{-1})$ with the probability measures of the fixed-radius test. This conclusion is for genuine centers outside at most $Q^{17/20+o(1)}$ possible conductors, not merely an average signed cancellation between centers.

**Decision for the remaining centers.** Balancing moment order against the guaranteed rational spacing only buys a factor $U^{-1/4+o(1)}$ in this Markov bound. This is the output of the calculation, not a lower bound on the true exceptional measure. A single bad center with its whole required polylogarithmic interval fits easily, and the same diagonal is compatible with the twisted persistent example. If trying to exclude the remaining centers by taking higher moments, the first new input must control the nonzero frequencies that survive the averaging window, with the actual center phases. Increasing the order and continuing to count only exact permutations is not justified.

**A signed test beyond the diagonal range.** Here is the cost and frequency support if one does take that step. Freeze at the actual center $t$, use the untwisted sum for one shift, and suppose $|Z(t+s)|>a$ for some $|s|\ll X/D$. Fix $\gamma>0$ and take $h=\lceil(2+\gamma)\log t/D\rceil$. Write $W(\mathbf p)=\prod_{i=1}^h w_{p_i}$, $\nu=\sum_i(\lambda_{p_i}-\lambda_{r_i})$ for two ordered $h$-tuples on the same shift. Then necessarily

$$\sum_{0<|\nu|<2\pi/t} W(\mathbf p)W(\mathbf r)\left(1-\frac{t|\nu|}{2\pi}\right)\cos(t\nu)\ \gg\ \frac{aX}{hD A_d}\,\frac{a^{2h}}{t}. \tag{1}$$

Use the probability kernel $t^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(\pi(u/t-1))$ against $|Z(u)|^{2h}$. Since $|Z'|\le 4e^8 D A_d/X$, the sum stays above $a(1-1/(4h))$ in modulus within radius $aX/(16e^8 hD A_d)$ of $t+s$. This radius is at most $X/(16e^8 hD)$, since $A_d>a$, and is $\gg_a 1$ eventually by the two-form bound. The kernel is $\gg1/t$ there. Raising to $2h$ costs only a constant factor relative to $a^{2h}$. The transform gives exactly the displayed triangular multiplier with the center phase; the zero frequency costs $\le h!(D^{O(1)}/X)^h\le t^{-1-\gamma/2+o(1)}$, negligible even relative to $a^{2h}/t$. The kernel has a **zero at height zero**: For any fixed $B$ the entire $|u|\le X^B$ contribution, even with $|Z|\le A_d$, is $t^{-3+o(1)}$ as an upper bound. A dyadic majorant nonzero at the origin would instead see the aligned frozen sum there. After canceling starts common to the two tuples in (1), at least $(\log t-O(1))/(2D)$ starts must remain on *each* side, by the same rational spacing bound applied to the reduced tuples. Thus fixed-order nonexact relations cannot pay (1).

At a common-dip failure this necessary inequality holds on $\gg D$ shifts, using the whole band and any fixed $a<1/16$ at the center. The argument uses the actual ratio frequencies, not the marginal character laws. To use it for exclusion, it is enough to rule out that multiplicity, not to prove an upper bound for every single shift. This is an alternative to the matching-product test; passing to a single-shift modulus does not recover the relative sign for a specified pair of bins. No bound for the signed near relations in (1) is known here.

**An absolute near-relation bound fails on enough actual shifts.** At this order the central part of the sum is already much larger than the threshold, without any hypothesis on the center phases. For each $d$ with $A_d\ge a$, all one-sided tuple frequencies $\sum_i\lambda_{p_i}$ lie in $[0,4e^8hD/X]$. Partition this into $N\ll 1+thD/X$ cells of length $1/(2t)$. Their masses sum to $A_d^h$, so ordered same-cell pairs have mass at least $A_d^{2h}/N$. Remove the zero frequency at cost $t^{-1-\gamma/2+o(1)}$. On all remaining same-cell pairs the triangular cosine multiplier in (1) exceeds $1/2$, and all terms with $0<|\nu|\le1/(2t)$ have positive sign. Thus this central slice alone contributes $\gg X A_d^{2h}/(thD)$. Compared with the necessary lower-bound scale on the right of (1), this is a factor $\gg (A_d/a)^{2h+1}$. For $a<1/16$ the [consecutive-gap mass lemma](matched-prime-overlap.md) supplies $\gg D$ shifts with $A_d>1/4$ even for the **untwisted** prime supports at every sufficiently large center. The factor on these shifts exceeds a constant times $4^{2h}$, even after accounting for persistence of the high moment near a putative large value. In particular neither a bound on the absolute number with these weights nor just pricing the zero frequency can exclude the necessary multiplicity. It is the cancellation against the negative part of the multiplier ($\pi/2<t|\nu|<3\pi/2$), not a scarcity of nonidentical close tuples, that could make the full signed sum small.
