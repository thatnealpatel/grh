# How much net negative pool weight is available?

Work directly with the original integrated arithmetic arc difference $I$ from the [windowed harmonic test](windowed-harmonic-witness.md). Freeze $t=2q^{3/5}$, $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $L=\log t/D$, $0<\gamma<1/2$, $C=4+2\gamma$, $n=2\lceil(2+\gamma)L\rceil$. Use either separation radius $r=D^2$ or $R_*=X^{\gamma/(16(2+\gamma))}$ **inside each copy**. The two copies need not be jointly separated. Fix $1\le d\le5D$ with $A=\sum_p w_p>a_0>0$; starts have $p,p+d$ prime in $[e^{-8}X,X]$ and $w_p=X^{-1}\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$. All weights below are unnormalized. Write $M_2$ for the [jointly separated central mass](joint-pool-isolation.md) at this shift and radius. The results are asymptotic, uniform in these shifts for fixed $\gamma,a_0$.

For each unsigned **multiset** $V$ of $2n$ starts let $s(V)$ be the sum of the actual labeled terms on $V$. Each term imposes the two separate completed gates, both rational cuts $|y_i|\le8$, and the old distance cut: In each direction fewer than $\lceil(3+\gamma)L\rceil$ labels are within $R_*$ of the other copy. Its kernel is $T(a_1)T(a_2)\cos(a_1-a_2)$, with $T(z)=(1-|z|/(2\pi))_+$, $y_i=t(e^{\nu_i}-1)$, $a_i=y_i-y_i^2/(2t)$, and $\nu_i$ the balanced signed sum of $\lambda_p=\log(1+d/p)$. Count each ordered term once, with its product of weights, before forming $P=\sum_V s(V)_+$ and $N=\sum_V(-s(V))_+$. In particular $I=P-N$ exactly.

**Supply formula.** Use the one-copy completed log-window measure $\sigma$ of the harmonic test at the same $r$ and $d$, retaining privacy, separation, and the weight of the designated positive slot. Put $G(z)=T(z)\cos z$, $z=t\nu$, $u=\int G_+\,d\sigma$, and $v=\int G_-\,d\sigma$, where $G_-=(-G)_+$. There is a *common* number $0\le L_{\rm mix}\le t^{-\delta+o(1)}M_2$, $\delta=\gamma/[2(3+2\gamma)]$, such that

$$
 P=u^2+v^2-L_{\rm mix}+O(e),\qquad N=2uv-L_{\rm mix}+O(e),
 \qquad e=t^{-5/2+o(1)}+t^{-b'+o(1)},
$$

where $b'=1/2+(3+\gamma)(1/2-\gamma/[16(2+\gamma)])>2+\gamma/4$. In particular $I=(u-v)^2+O(e)$. This last signed identity by itself follows from the harmonic test. The separate supply formulas do not: Different signed splits could have canceled on the same multiset before the positive and negative parts were taken. In general the bound for $L_{\rm mix}$ is relative to central mass, not an additive detector-scale error.

**Average signs in the right order.** First replace the arithmetic coordinates by the log coordinates in the pair kernel. The pointwise Lipschitz error on the union of supports is $O(t^{-2})$ and requires two $|t\nu_i|\le9$ hits, of total product mass at most $t^{-1/2+o(1)}$ by the harmonic test. Hence even the sum of the absolute errors of the pool coefficients is $\le t^{-5/2+o(1)}$. Taking either positive part is 1-Lipschitz. In the exact log sum, reversing the signs in either copy preserves the pool. It also preserves the gates on the window for sufficiently large $t$: The actual designated slot of either orientation has rounding displacement $O(X^2/(dt))<1/2$ by the [recovery identity](last-slot-residues.md), and privacy and distance use no signs. Average the two orientations before taking any positive or negative part. On each pool the resulting sum is exactly the sum of $G(z_1)G(z_2)$ with the original product weights. This is **not** averaging an arithmetic orientation at a finite center where reversal may fail the gate.

Before the old distance cut the positive and negative masses of these products over ordered pairs are $u^2+v^2$ and $2uv$. The cut removes at most $t^{-b'+o(1)}$ absolutely by the two original compact windows in the harmonic test. For each remaining pool let $c_+,c_-$ be its two masses of opposite product signs. Grouping loses $\min(c_+,c_-)$ from each side. Define $L_{\rm mix}$ as the sum of these minima. To bound it one must price the **source** weight of admitting an opposite product, not the product probability of drawing a partner independently.

**An extra small sum is necessary.** Fix any source from a pool with both product signs. Write $x,y$ for the two *unscaled* source frequencies, and $x',y'$ for those of an opposite product on the very same multiset. All four have absolute value at most $2\pi/t$. Both target frequencies are nonzero: Each internally separated block has distinct starts, and the valuation at its least start in the balanced ratio cannot cancel. Of the two numbers $x'+y',x'-y'$, at least one, say $\mu$, is outside $\{\pm x\pm y\}$. Otherwise they have different classes up to overall sign (since $x'y'\ne0$), and must exhaust the two source classes up to sign. The addition identity would give $\cos(tx')\cos(ty')=\cos(tx)\cos(ty)$, impossible for opposite products with nonzero tapers.

Identify target occurrences with source occurrences of the same value, arbitrarily if a start was repeated. Every target occurrence in $\mu$ has a sign. In each source copy $i$ choose $g_i=\pm1$ so at most $n/2$ of the target signs disagree with $g_i$ times the original signs. If $m_i$ signs disagree, the signed sum $W$ of these minority labels with the oriented **source** signs satisfies

$$2W=g_1x+g_2 y-\mu,\qquad 0<|W|\le4\pi/t.$$

With $m=m_1+m_2$, each side of the ratio $e^W$ is an integer product at most $X^m$ before reduction. Spacing at one gives $m\ge\log(t/(4\pi))/D$. There is no union bound over permutations of the multiset. The existence of the partner implies the existence of two minority masks and two signs with the displayed properties.

**Price the ambiguity.** Use independent start probabilities $\rho_p=w_p/A$, $f(s)=\sum_p\rho_p e^{is\lambda_p}$, and the moments $U_j=\int K_{80,t}|f|^j$ with $K_{B,t}(s)=B(2\pi t)^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(Bs/(2t))$. For each fixed mask the minor group has a window of radius $4\pi/t$. After its disclosure the two majority groups have translated windows of radius $2\pi/t$ from the source cuts. Twice the translated tent of width $80/t$ majorizes each indicator, so a group of size $j$ costs at most $2U_j$ for any signs. Drop all other restrictions for this bound. The three orders $m,n-m_1,n-m_2$ sum to $2n$ and belong to $[k,n]$ for $k=2\lfloor\log(t/160)/(2D)\rfloor$. By Hölder the product of their moments divided by $U_n^2$ is at most

$$ U_k^{n/(n-k)} U_n^{-k/(n-k)}\le t^{-\delta+o(1)}.$$

Here the [same-pool moment budget](joint-pool-isolation.md) gives $U_k\le t^{-1/4+o(1)}$ from the permutation diagonal and $U_n\ge t^{-1+o(1)}$ from the origin. It gives $A^{2n}U_n^2\le e^{o(n)}M_2$ by the constant-offset cell bound and the joint small-tent comparison. These bounds are against the original product law, not a law conditioned on the gates. The masks, orientations, and constants cost $\exp(O(n))=t^{o(1)}$. This bounds even the full weight of sources of nonzero product sign on mixed pools, and proves the asserted bound for $L_{\rm mix}$. A cross-copy repetition introduces no new term in the argument.

**Decision.** The arithmetic pool supply differs from $2uv$ by $o(M_2)$, so a claim of macroscopic supply can be tested on two **one-copy** sign slices. The common mixing loss cancels from the signed deficit even when it cannot be bounded at $B^2$. The [distant allocation](distant-pool-allocation.md) shows how much of this net supply can be used without reusing capacity. Neither reduction proves $u-v$ small at a genuine center. A lower bound for $v$ by a fixed fraction of $u$ would give negative supply, but not the required $|u-v|\le (\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}+o(1))B$ when $u\gg B$. Do not assign an estimate of the mixing loss at scale $B^2$ as a prerequisite for that comparison.

If pursuing a sharp-window substitute for that one-copy estimate, distinguish the two families. The [finite common-window certificate](common-window-obstruction.md) rules out a pointwise implication for the jointly separated $H_r$, not the common-window witness for the original $I$.

For the signed difference itself, see the [balanced height reconstruction](balanced-height-reconstruction.md). It reduces the fixed-margin test to two positive height integrals at the original center, with relative rather than exponential comparison loss. Its finite checker measures $u-v$ directly and tests the rounded-slot gate in isolation. Neither integral has the needed arithmetic upper bound.
