# Search the original principal ramp on a short height block

At accepted `ce3add4fadc9af44b793218130c05c8200abe48a`, the [real-run/box test](real-run-length.md) gets a principal cap from shift data on one offset cell. If the absolute prime phases are available, test the ramp itself. For one specified height this is a direct sum. For a block there is a sublinear certified search: the direct prime-product expansion replaces the ratios in [the shift search](conductor-interval-search.md), and a second, higher moment only **counts** the leaves. No shift persistence, component alignment, or pressure cut is needed.

**Contract.** Fix $0<\delta<3/5$, a positive rational amplitude $a$, and a finite list of signed harmonic tests. Given integers $Q,J$ with $Q^{2/5+\delta}\le J\le Q$, search $v_j(q)=2j q^{3/5}$, $Q\le q\le Q+J$, for each fixed positive index $j$. The statistic is $\sigma_j\Re S_{X_0(q)}(v_j(q))$, $\sigma_j=\pm1$, where

$$ S_x(v)=\sum_p e^{-\log x}\log p\,(\log x-\log p)_+\,p^{iv},\qquad X_0(q)=((9/5)\log q)^2. $$

One may instead use the modulus, or $X(v)=(3\log(v/2))^2$ in place of $X_0(q)$; at $j=1$ the cutoffs agree. Put $N\asymp JQ^{-2/5}$ for the **physical height length** and $\ell=\operatorname{bitlength}(Q)$, $\eta=1/\lfloor\sqrt{\ell}\rfloor$. For all sufficiently large $Q$, the output per harmonic is a union of closed rational height cells, or its compressed inverse in integer $q$. It covers every hit at $a$, and every point of a returned cell in the target range passes $a-\eta$. The same cells may be constructed to satisfy both cutoff contracts. The expected bit cost is $N^{2/3+o(1)}$ and the number of cells is $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ **as an upper bound**, uniformly in the block, at fixed $\delta,a$, and harmonic list. The algorithm is Las Vegas; no probability of missing a peak is allowed.

## Freeze only the weights; use a fine lattice

Sieve to the integer $V$ obtained by flooring a certified rational lower approximation to $X_0(Q)$ of error $<1$. Use $F(v)=S_V(v)$ with all primes through $V$ (a prime equal to $V$ has weight zero). No lower clip, exact transcendental boundary test, or change of normalization is needed. With $r=\log x$ each weight is continuous across its entry point. The sum of absolute derivatives in $r$ is bounded a.e. by $e^{-r}\vartheta(e^r)+M_{e^r}$, where $M_x=\sum_p x^{-1}\log p\log^+(x/p)=O(1)$ by Chebyshev and partial summation, and $M_x=1+o(1)$ by PNT. Thus freezing costs $O(|\log(x/V)|)$ **uniformly in the phase**. Here that log difference is $O_j(1/\ell)$ at either cutoff, including neighboring nodes and the padded interval. A twist's own cutoff $((9/5)\log(q|d|))^2$ for $|d|\le X_0(q)^\theta$ differs by $O_\theta(\log\ell/\ell)$ in log, also within the buffer. In particular no moving ramp is inserted into a Fourier transform. For explicit error certification the derivative bound can even be replaced by $2r$ using $\vartheta(x)\le x\log x$.

Enclose the named interval by integer endpoints with room for one cell, using $v_j(q)^5=(2j)^5q^3$ and root comparisons. Let $N$ be its integer length, $u_0$ its left endpoint, $g=\ell^2$, and $P$ the least power of two at least $gN$. Use $u_i=u_0+i/g$, $0\le i<P$, and closed cells $[u_i,u_i+1/g]$. The mean value theorem gives $N\asymp_j JQ^{-2/5}$, so $\log N\asymp_\delta\ell$, $P=N^{1+o(1)}$. Write $D=\log V$. Since $|F'|\le DM_V$, from a target to its left node the variation is $O(D/g)$. Together with freezing the error is $<\eta/8$ eventually, also for signed real parts or moduli. To certify these allowances on a concrete input one can use the elementary bounds $2r$ above and $DM_V\le D^3$, with rational enclosures for the log cutoffs at the interval endpoints. Endpoints and ties do not require choosing the nearest node. This pitch also handles the **moment** bandwidth; unit sampling is not justified by the low bandwidth of a fixed-shift sum. Centering $F$ by $e^{-ivD}$ does not center away the differences in $|F|^{2h}$, nor remove the carrier from a signed real test.

## Products, not prime ratios

For a multiset $\alpha$ of $h$ primes of multiplicities $\alpha_p$, put $n_\alpha=\prod p^{\alpha_p}$ and $b_\alpha=(h!/\prod\alpha_p!)\prod w_p^{\alpha_p}$, where $w_p=V^{-1}\log p\log(V/p)$. The multinomial counts all ordered choices with that multiset. Unique factorization gives exactly one multiset per product; $F(v)^h=\sum_\alpha b_\alpha n_\alpha^{iv}$. The diagonal of $|F|^{2h}$ is

$$ B_h=\sum_\alpha b_\alpha^2\le h!\Big(\sum_p w_p^2\Big)^h\ll h!(C D^2/V)^h, $$

using one multinomial in the sum and bounding the other by $h!$, and $\sum w_p^2\le M_V\max w_p$. For $n\ne m$ the angular frequency has $|\log(n/m)|\ge |n-m|/\max(n,m)\ge V^{-h}$, **not** $V^{-2h}$. Equal nonzero differences need not be merged. Use one term for the diagonal and a doubled real part for each unordered distinct pair.

Take $h_1$ largest with $4^2V^{3h_1}\le N^2$, and $h_2$ largest with $4V^{h_2}\le N$. These exact integer choices have $hD=\gamma\log N+O(D)$ for $\gamma=2/3,1$, respectively, and $\log h=D/2+O_\delta(\log D)$ since $V\asymp\ell^2$. At either order the number of multisets is at most $\binom{V+h}{h}=N^{\gamma/2+o(1)}$ as an upper bound, by $\binom{V+h}{h}\le(e(V+h)/h)^h$. Taking $h$ distinct primes in $[V/2,V)$ gives the same exponent as a lower bound by PNT and $h=o(V/D)$. Thus explicitly enumerating the unordered pairs costs $N^{\gamma+o(1)}$ up to polylogarithms per pair. The diagonal bound is $B_h\le N^{-\gamma/2+o(1)}$; the full spectral coefficient mass (with doubling) is at most $M_V^{2h}=N^{o(1)}$ as an upper bound.

For the discrete mass $S_h=\sum_{i<P}|F(u_i)|^{2h}$ use $W(v)=4\operatorname{sinc}^2((v-u_c)/T)$, $T=P/g$, $u_c=(u_0+u_{P-1})/2$, $\operatorname{sinc}x=\sin x/x$. This is nonnegative and $\ge1$ on the nodes. Its angular transform is supported in $[-2/T,2/T]$. The product with the moment has bandwidth $\le hD+2/T<2\pi g$ at both orders. Poisson on $u_0+g^{-1}\mathbb Z$ therefore has only the integral term, with factor $g$ (summable sinc-square tails and compact transform). In the integral every nonzero product difference misses the support since $V^{-h}\ge4/N$ and $T\ge N$. Hence

$$ S_h\le g\int_{\mathbb R}W(v)|F(v)|^{2h}dv=4\pi g T B_h\le N^{1-\gamma/2+o(1)}. $$

This is an upper bound on a padded grid in a translated block, **not** an identity for the unweighted finite sum. Alternatively, directly summing each off-diagonal geometric progression bounds its modulus by $\pi g/|\log(n/m)|$ since $hD/g<\pi$. Use $|\log(n/m)|\ge|n-m|/V^h$ and $2b_nb_m\le b_n^2+b_m^2$ to get $S_h\le(P+2\pi gV^h(1+\log(V^h)))B_h$ by harmonic row sums over the integer products. This simpler estimate loses only a logarithm. The moment order is controlled by the block length, while the phase precision depends on its distance from zero.

## Construct with the first moment order; certify size with the second

For $h=h_1$ apply the [positive bucket search](moment-bucket-search.md) to $y_i=|F(u_i)|^{2h}$. It has $M=N^{2/3+o(1)}$ terms $\Re(c_e e^{ii\theta_e})$ with $\theta_e=g^{-1}\log(n/m)$ and exact phase $e^{iu_0\log(n/m)}$ in $c_e$. Take a dyadic $\tau$ between half and all of $(a/2)^{2h}$. The budget $1+S_h/\tau$ is at most $N^{2/3+o(1)}$ since $a$ is fixed. For clarity, the inherited construction charges:

* Compute $\widehat S_h$ to absolute error $\tau/8$ by geometric sums over all $P$ nodes, and set $K=\lceil1+(\widehat S_h+\tau/8)/\tau\rceil$. Split surviving dyadic blocks. For $O(K)$ candidates use an injective residue modulus when the full block count is small, or sample primes of size $\asymp K\log^2(2P)$ until the **checked** candidate-pair collisions are $\le K$. A candidate difference has at most $\log P/\log p$ prime divisors of that size, so the expected number of attempts is bounded. Check collisions by sorting residues; no transform for rejected primes. Keep candidates with residue mass estimated $\ge\tau/2$ to error $\tau/8$. The buckets include all blocks, not just candidates. Nonnegativity keeps ancestors of hits, and at most $8S_h/(3\tau)$ residues pass, plus at most $K$ extra candidates from collisions. This closes the traversal budget across $O(\log P)$ levels.
* At length $s$ and modulus $p$, residue $r$ has $l_r$ blocks, one of two lengths. For each spectral term use $c_eG_s(e^{i\theta_e})G_{l_r}(e^{ips\theta_e})e^{irs\theta_e}$, where $G_l(z)=\sum_{k<l}z^k$. Binary composition avoids division near resonance. Batch the two coefficient lists by the cited Taylor-FFT gridding: on an FFT of length $L\ge p$ round $Ls\theta_e/(2\pi)$ with residual $\le3/4$, using a fixed chosen integer even at a tie. The coefficient mass before Taylor is at most $P\max(1,M_V^{2h})$; $O(\log Q)$ terms give absolute tail $<\tau/32$. Balanced bin additions and FFT have depth $O(\log Q)$ and polynomial-in-$Q$ intermediate magnitudes. $O(\ell^2)$ bits of guarded precision suffice for error $\tau/8$, including reducing $u_0\log(n/m)$ modulo $2\pi$; $u_0$ is $Q^{O(1)}$, not $O(N)$. Integers in the products and multinomials have $O(\ell)$ bits. Sieve, elementary functions, dyadic threshold construction, each multiset and pair, and each FFT record cost polylogarithmically in $Q$. This uses expected $(M+K)\operatorname{polylog}Q$ bit operations and at most that order of storage without scanning the grid.
* At leaves apply the buffered test to the **individual** $y_i$, computed from the original $F$ to absolute error $\tau/8$; powering a bounded sum needs only polylogarithmic precision and work. This last check is essential: an untested singleton can inherit mass from another block. Every surviving leaf now has $|F(u_i)|\ge(3\tau/8)^{1/(2h_1)}\ge a/8$, and every $|F(u_i)|\ge a/2$ is found. By the proven $h_2$ mass, the number of nodes at $a/8$ is $S_{h_2}(a/8)^{-2h_2}\le N^{1/2+o(1)}$. This also counts true fixed-amplitude nodes of either moving-cutoff statistic, or nodes at a smaller positive fixed threshold by the same argument. **Do not form the $h_2$ pairs**: that enumeration alone is $N^{1+o(1)}$. The $2/3$ balance is for the operational first-order budget and spectrum, not a lower bound on output size.
* Estimate the actual requested statistic of $F(u_i)$ to $\eta/8$ on surviving leaves and mark the cell if the estimate is $\ge a-\eta/2$. With the cutoff and interpolation allowances this gives the two-sided contract, including endpoints. This final signed test cannot be replaced by the modulus count. Neither step decides equality at an exact transcendental threshold.

## Projection and limits

Invert each cell by binary search comparing fifth powers of the rational endpoints with $(2j)^5q^3$, then intersect with $[Q,Q+J]$. Sorting/merging and inverse searches cost only polylogarithms per cell. Named heights are spaced $\asymp_j Q^{-2/5}$, much closer than the mesh. Thus the compressed list costs no enumeration of conductors, but its integer population is at most $O((1+Q^{2/5}/g)N^{1/2+o(1)})=Q^{2/5}N^{1/2+o(1)}$. This bounds real primitive conductors by inclusion; it is not a sieve to identify them. The [odd-squarefree sampling result](sampled-principal-peaks.md) also explains why spacing alone is no uniform exclusion: a fixed-margin modulus peak can be rotated and sampled by many admissible conductors. For $J=Q$ the exponents in $Q$ are $2/5$ for construction and $7/10$ for population; for $J\asymp Q^{1/2}$ they are $1/15$ and $9/20$, respectively.

For one conductor (or a polylog-length height window), direct guarded summation (or a dense fine grid) is simpler. Stationary clipping via the positive part above suffices for changing supports, and a lower clip at $\kappa V$ costs $O(\kappa(1+\log(1/\kappa)))$, but neither is sublinear block traversal. A dense frozen scan still costs $N^{1+o(1)}$ in the stated regime. Using only a stationary *diagonal* local moment is not a substitute: on windows of length $s\asymp V^h$ where sinc-square kills all off-diagonals, the mass bound is the same at every center and $s B_h\ge s M_V^{2h}/\#\{n_\alpha\}=N^{\gamma/2+o(1)}$ as a lower bound, too large for a fixed-amplitude pruning threshold even with a polylog grid factor. Finer windows can have off-diagonals and require a cost argument to evaluate/resolve survivors. There is no optimality claim for the bucket search. Below the polynomial-length regime, $\log h\sim D/2$ and even $h\ge1$ need not hold; keep the actual binomial/moment bounds or scan directly, not the displayed exponents.

For the [eight signed modes](high-endpoint-harmonics.md) of a high endpoint failure use any fixed $a<0.58712243824$ sufficiently close to that threshold. Outside the union of the eight lists a failure at $y=q^{3/5}$ is excluded for sufficiently large $q$ by that implication. A listed principal peak is neither a failing twist nor necessarily an actual unbuffered hit. The lists are not proved empty, do not supply a strict uniform principal limsup gap, and do not address arbitrary endpoint heights. The [ambient character theorem](local-quadratic-twists.md) already gives $O_\varepsilon(Q^{1/2+\varepsilon})$ exceptional bases dyadically for the allowed polylog-radius twists by counting product characters (as in [the sampling note](sampled-principal-peaks.md#what-averaging-would-and-would-not-decide)). That is a different, stronger dyadic population bound for the Weil-floor exceptions, not a principal-peak list or a test at a specified exceptional base. The useful new output here is a certified local search of the original principal statistic, not a replacement density theorem for characters.
