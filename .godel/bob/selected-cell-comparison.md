# A buffered list on which only the direct screen passes

There was no list of coordinates in the handoff. Here is a reproducible small-input comparison, followed by a stronger asymptotic joint test and a [fully finite calibration at another cutoff](finite-successor-cap.md). Neither supplies a large-height margin.

Take all eight signed modes, $176\le q\le220$, $y=q^{3/5}$, $v=2jy$ for positive harmonic index $j$, $X=(3\log y)^2$, and $a=0.587$. Use the [principal cell grid](principal-block-search.md) with $g=64$, frozen $V=86$, and $\eta=1/2$. Scan every intersecting cell $[n/g,(n+1)/g]$, marking when $\sigma_j\Re S_V(n/g)\ge a-\eta/2$. This proves its own two-sided buffer contract for the named cutoff: The sum of the cutoff error and within-cell change is strictly less than $\eta/2$, and every node comparison is decisive. It does not invoke the asymptotic search algorithm at this input size.

Only the following three modes return cells, each in one connected cluster. Take the middle of the union as representative. $B$ is the gap-grouped positive budget at $H=40\log X$, with no rank taper.

The chord condition holds; the budget condition fails throughout. Even just $d=6,8$ contribute more than $0.0035$ together on every cluster, after summation by gap and before adding the other nonnegative terms. **None of the 60 cells pass the joint screen; the direct table rejects all 60.** These are continuous height cells, not 60 conductors. Inverting them on the integer grid gives only $q=187$, in the $-9$ cluster, by exact fifth-power comparisons. Both comparisons use absolute enclosure width below $0.001$ (for the direct table, on each of four subintervals per cell). This compares outcomes, not execution times. At these small cutoffs even the total weight is below $0.76$ everywhere on the clusters. Thus the direct rejections are genuine but require no arithmetic cancellation to beat $8/9$.

The [SageMath Python checker](check-selected-comparison.py) uses 120-bit outward-rounded intervals. The freeze bound sums absolute derivatives in $\log X$ of the individual ramp weights, allowing their entry at zero. For the successor tests the clipped prime list is constant on each cluster. The checker bounds $F$ by its midpoint value plus a derivative enclosure times the height offset (one-sided derivatives suffice at a kink). For $B$ it evaluates the gap pools with the whole cluster interval as argument, retaining the moving taper and cutoff. For the direct test it snaps both the offset and its carrier angle to a mesh at the cutoff frozen at the cluster midpoint $y_0$; the price is the sum of the two coordinate Lipschitz errors, the moving weight error, and the subinterval half-width times $\sum w_p\log p$. These are evaluations at the actual coupled heights, not translated free-phase controls. The loose small-input buffer is important: In fact the checker also bounds every signed statistic strictly below the unbuffered $0.587$ throughout the block.

## A block beyond trivial total mass

On the whole dyadic block $2^{19}\le q\le2^{20}$ the named ramp has mass **above** $8/9$, yet the buffered list at the prescribed marking level is empty. Here $V=561$, $\ell=20$, $\eta=1/4$. The [interval checker](check-empty-principal-block.py) tests all eight modes on the entire real harmonic intervals, padded by one height unit. It uses addition of angles on length-four blocks, rounding weighted base vectors and unweighted rotation vectors to a $10^{-6}$ lattice before integer dot products. The rounding cost is at most $2(\#\{p<V\}+M_V)10^{-6}$; the nearest $1/32$-spaced node costs at most $\sum w_p\log p/64$. The largest resulting uniform signed upper bound is below $0.391$ (mode $-14$). Even a node estimate erring upward by $\eta/8$ stays below $0.587-\eta/2$. Adding the separate moving-weight error gives a bound below $0.48$ for the named statistic throughout. The mass lower bound uses the numerator at $k$ and denominator at $k+1$ on each unit cutoff interval. This is not a comparison of rejection rates on a nonempty list: Here selection itself already rules out all cells.

## More room in the joint test at large heights

The [overlapping-triple bound](finite-successor-cap.md#large-cutoff-consequence) implies that with $F\le0.9005+o(1)$ and $B\le0.002389+o(1)$ the full signed modulus is at most $0.882315+o(1)$. Conversely, raw $8/9-o(1)$ hits with that chord bound must have $B>0.012825$ eventually. This does **not** reclassify the small cells using $0.012825$: Only the specified joint thresholds are being tested here. At the unrelated 127-bit fixture the finite allocation proof needs neither an unspecified $o(1)$ nor the actual angles once the two scalar bounds are known.

There is also a one-sided comparison that does not require finding more cells. Put $P(y)=\sum w_p|\cos(y\log p)|$. Choosing $s_p=\operatorname{sign}\cos(y\log p)$ shows that $P(y)$ is at most the uniform signed-modulus cap. Hence, for all sufficiently large cutoffs, **any** cell certified to pass both successor thresholds throughout must also be rejected throughout by a direct upper table of uniform excess at most $0.001$: Even $0.882315+0.001<8/9$, with room for the uniform $o(1)$. This is inclusion of rejection sets at that accuracy, not an assertion that the joint set is nonempty, or a comparison of the number of passes. At $25107\le X\le25108$ the same inclusion is already finite since $0.8877+0.001<8/9$.

**Decision.** On this list it is the gap budget, not propagation or numerical precision, that prevents the specified joint rejection. Do not extrapolate its failure rate to large conductors. The next informative input is a nonempty list at a cutoff where total weight cannot itself reject, preferably with a peak clear of the buffer. Use the direct projection as baseline and calibrate the finite triple allocation at the actual cutoff before using successor thresholds as a certificate.

**Replay.** Run `check-selected-comparison.py`, `check-empty-principal-block.py`, and `check-finite-successor-cap.py` with SageMath 10.9 Python, NumPy, and assertions enabled. `check-rank-box.py` supplies the independent measurements of the two scalars at the 127-bit fixture.
