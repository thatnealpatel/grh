# A ramp peak fixes the center of the angle test

The [full-circle product](separated-bin-phase-laws.md) sees only matching shifts. For **peak exclusion**, rather than the stronger common-dip assertion, there is a different test: Keep an arc centered at zero. Signed products with widely separated starts, macroscopically different shifts, and separated ratio frequencies must then contribute. This is a localization of the known box inequality, **not** a new lower bound for the box or an upper bound for the genuine signed sum.

Use the frozen data of the [pair dispersion note](prime-pair-dispersion.md) at $t=2q^{3/5}$: $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $I=[e^{-8}X,X]$, $T=bX/D$, $cD\le d\le4D$. For primes $p,p+d\in I$ put $w_{d,p}=X^{-1}\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$, $\lambda_{d,p}=\log(1+d/p)$, and $A_d=\sum_p w_{d,p}$. Use $J$ equal starting bins $I_j$ in $I$. All constants and $J$ below are fixed before $t\to\infty$.

**Test.** Along a sequence approaching the $47/81$ ramp threshold there must be a subsequence and a fixed ordered pair $|j-k|\ge2$ on which the following exceeds $\ell D/J^2$ for some $\ell>0$. Set $\Delta=\lambda_{d,p}-\lambda_{e,r}$ and $\operatorname{sinc}(x)=\sin(x)/x$ with value one at zero. Sum over eligible prime pairs with $p\in I_j$, $r\in I_k$:

$$\frac{R}{\pi D}\sum_{\substack{d,e,p,r\\ |d-e|>\alpha D,\ |\Delta|>\tau D/X}} w_{d,p}w_{e,r}\cos(t\Delta)\operatorname{sinc}(T\Delta)\operatorname{sinc}\!\left((d-e)R/D\right). \tag{1}$$

Here $R$ is sufficiently large, then $J$ sufficiently large, then $\alpha,\tau>0$ sufficiently small. In particular, the near-equality $d/p\simeq e/r$ is not required to support this lower bound. The frequency cut does **not** make the height sinc small: Even the maximal $T|\Delta|$ is at most $4be^8$.

**Locating the angle.** Write $Z_{d,j}(u)=\sum_{p\in I_j}w_{d,p}e^{iu\lambda_{d,p}}$, $P_j(a,u)=\sum_d e^{ida}Z_{d,j}(u)$, $P=\sum_jP_j$, and $d\mu=da/(2\pi)$ on the principal circle. By (2) of the [short-box argument](short-prime-pair-bias.md), with $H=4D$, a peak gives

$$\Re\sum_{d,j}(1-d/H)_+ Z_{d,j}(t)\ge .08D.$$

Indeed the original lower bound is $(H/2)(.0849-o(1))$ and the low end strip costs less than $(H/2)(.0849-1/16)/2$ with the source's choice of $c$. No further parity adjustment. Pair $P$ against the mass-one Fejér kernel $K_H$ with Fourier coefficients $(1-|d|/H)_+$. For noninteger $H$, mix the kernels of orders $\lfloor H\rfloor,\lceil H\rceil$ by linearly interpolating their **reciprocals** to $1/H$. The squared-sine formula gives $K_H(a)\ll 1/(Da^2)$ off zero, so its mass outside $A=\{|a|\le R/D\}$ is $O(1/R)$; also $\int K_H^2 d\mu\le4D+1$. By $\sum_d A_d^2\ll D$, $|P|\le\sum A_d\ll D$. Choose $R$ to spend at most $.02D$ on the tail of the **linear pairing**. Since $\lambda_{d,p}\le 4e^8D/X$, its full-circle pairing changes by at most $.02D$ on $|u-t|\le b'X/D$ for a sufficiently small constant $0<b'\le b$. Thus the real arc pairing is at least $.04D$ there. Cauchy gives $\int_A|P|^2d\mu\ge(.04D)^2/(4D+1)$. Averaging this nonnegative square over the full height interval retains at least the fraction $b'/b$ of this bound, hence at least $LD$ for some $L>0$. No bound on the quadratic complement was used.

Expand the square in ordered bin pairs. The equal and neighboring terms are bounded above by $3\sum_j|P_j|^2$ pointwise. Parseval on the full circle bounds the integral of this majorant by $3\sum_{d,j}(\sum_{p\in I_j}w_{d,p})^2\ll D/J+D^3/X$ by the near-start collision estimate (2) in the dispersion note. At sufficiently large fixed $J$ this costs less than $LD/2$. One separated pair therefore contributes more than $LD/(2J^2)$ to the full-height average on $A$. Its exact expansion is (1) **without** the two cuts: The prefactor is the circle mass of the arc, not a conditional probability on it.

**Pricing the cuts.** For fixed $h=d-e$, $\sum_d A_d A_{d-h}\le\sum A_d^2\ll D$ (extend by zero). Hence the cost in absolute value of $|h|\le\alpha D$ in any bin pair is $O(R(\alpha D+1))$. For the other cut, partition the frequency line into intervals $B$ of length $\tau D/X$. Put $M_{B,d}=\sum_{p:\lambda_{d,p}\in B}w_{d,p}$ and $M_B=\sum_d M_{B,d}$. At fixed $d$, $|\lambda'_{d,x}|=d/[x(x+d)]\ge cD/X^2$ between eligible starts. Two starts in the same $B$ therefore differ by at most $\tau X/c$. By collision estimate (2) and Cauchy in $d$,

$$\sum_B M_B^2\le 4D\sum_{B,d} M_{B,d}^2\ll D^4/X+\tau D^2/c$$

for $\tau<c$. Pairs with $|\Delta|\le\tau D/X$ lie in equal or neighboring frequency intervals (take half-open bins), at unsigned cost at most $3\sum_B M_B^2$ before the arc prefactor. These bounds apply even after other deletions. Fix $\alpha,\tau$ so the two costs together, including the prefactor on the second, are less than $LD/(4J^2)$ eventually. This proves (1) with $\ell=L/4$ by finite pigeonholing. All cuts are in the weights, with no Taylor approximation inside $\cos(t\Delta)$.

**Why not use it for a common dip?** The following twisted control can fail the common dip yet have all the sums (1) small. On a sparse sequence of disjoint prime bands, prescribe center phases $e^{i\beta p}$ by multiplying $p^{it}$ by $p^{-it}e^{i\beta p}$ and extending completely multiplicatively; pad the bands by one integer at either end for the moving cutoff. For uniform $\beta$ the complex expansion before taking the real part acquires $e^{i(d-e)\beta}$ and the center factor $e^{it\Delta}$ is removed. For **either** the raw or the cut test, each coefficient at $h=d-e$ has modulus at most $(R/(\pi D))\sum_d A_d A_{d-h}\ll_R 1$, irrespective of the frequency cut. Parseval in $\beta$ bounds its second moment by $O_R(D)$. At fixed $J$, all tests are $O(D^{3/4})$ with probability $1-o(1)$. One must do this after deletion, not infer smallness by deleting terms in a small signed sum.

The all-one box extraction gives $N\gg D$ interior shifts with $1/8<A_d\le M$. Each meets $\cos(d\beta)\ge3/4$ with probability $p_0=\arccos(3/4)/\pi$. A fraction at least $p_0/2$ succeeds with probability at least $p_0/(2-p_0)$ by the mean and the bound one on the fraction. No independence is needed. These shifts start above $3/32$ and lose at most $1/64$ on the **original** $T$ by the pair derivative bound, and $o(1)$ on unfreezing. Thus one can choose slopes with the same $\gg D$ shifts above $1/16$ throughout and every test (1) small. The common slope also cancels identically at matching shifts. This disproves an inference about phase information, not an estimate for genuine primes.

**Next decision.** For peak exclusion it suffices to upper-bound the signed, cut expression (1) below the displayed threshold for every fixed separated pair at genuine centers. Before introducing another height average, price its change on this statistic: On the retained terms the oscillation scale in height is comparable (up to fixed constants) to $X/D$. The larger-radius common dip does not transport that sign back. For the stronger fixed-radius assertion, return instead to [the matching-shift test](prime-pair-dispersion.md). Neither upper bound is known here.
