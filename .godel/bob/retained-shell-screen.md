# Screening the genuine shell before testing the signed sum

For one retained shift, the [pointwise cap](retained-shell-overshoot.md) has an equivalent test taking $O(1+t/(Ln^2))$ height evaluations, rather than $\asymp t\sqrt n/L$. The saving is in **where** the fine test is required. A small coarse value certifies an entire interval; a large one is not evidence that the cap fails. The test uses the original phases. It does not prove that it passes.

Keep the frozen data from the linked note: $t=2q^{3/5}$, $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $L=X/D$, $n=2\lceil(2+\gamma)\log t/D\rceil$ with $0<\gamma<1/2$, and $a=1/16$. For $1\le d\le5D$ both prime endpoints $p,p+d$ lie in $[e^{-8}X,X]$. Write $w_p=X^{-1}\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$, $\lambda_p=\log((p+d)/p)$, and $Z=\sum_p w_p e^{iu\lambda_p}$. Assume $n^2 C_d(D^2)\le K$ for fixed $K$, where $C_d(y)=\sum_{|p-p'|\le y}w_pw_{p'}$ is ordered and includes the diagonal. All bounds below are uniform in this shift for sufficiently large $t$, with constants allowed to depend on $K$ and $\gamma$.

**An upper bound that varies slowly.** Choose $C\ge\max(1,16K/a^2)$ fixed. Split the integers from $1$ to $\lfloor X\rfloor$ into $B=\lceil\lfloor X\rfloor/H\rceil$ bins of width $H=\lceil CX/n^2\rceil$, clipping the last. Let $Z_j$ be the part of $Z$ in bin $j$, $A_j=\sum_{p\in j}w_p$, and $s_j=\sum_{p\in j}w_p^2$. Put

$$T(u)=B\sum_j|Z_j(u)|^2\ \ge\ |Z(u)|^2,\qquad D_0=B\sum_j s_j\le (1/C+1/n^2)K\le a^2/8.$$

These bounds follow from Cauchy, $B\le n^2/C+1$, and retention. Here $X/n^2\asymp_\gamma D^2$. The same-bin mass satisfies $\sum A_j^2\le C_d(H)\ll_C K/n^2$: In cells of length $D^2$, the mass of pairs at each cell offset is at most the sum of squared cell masses, and only $O_C(1)$ offsets occur. Thus $B\sum A_j^2=O(1)$ and $\sum A_j=O(1)$. In $T$ each frequency difference has modulus at most $e^{16}dH/X^2\ll_C 1/(Ln^2)$ by differentiating $\lambda(x)$. Its total absolute coefficient mass is at most $B\sum A_j^2$. In particular $|T'|\ll 1/(Ln^2)$.

**Why few coarse cells need refinement.** On the whole shell $U=[t,5t/4]$,

$$\int_U|T(u)-D_0|^4\,du\ll t/n^4.$$

Indeed $T-D_0$ is real and has just the off-diagonal terms in the bin squares. In its fourth power every frequency is $\log(N/M)$ with $N,M\le X^8$; each of the four differences uses two prime ratios. Nonzero frequencies have modulus at least $X^{-8}$. A constant multiple of $\operatorname{sinc}^2((u-9t/8)/(2t))$, with $\operatorname{sinc}(x)=\sin(x)/x$, majorizes the shell indicator, has integral $O(t)$, and has angular-frequency transform supported in $[-1/t,1/t]$. Eventually $t>X^8$. Integrating the nonnegative fourth power against this majorant therefore retains only its constant coefficient. This is continuous integration, not a moment identity on any grid.

That coefficient is exactly the fourth moment obtained by replacing the edge phases by independent uniform unit phases. Indeed for signed start multiplicities $k_p$, a product of edge ratios equal to 1 has valuation $k_{v-d}-k_v=0$ at every prime endpoint $v$ (absent starts count as zero). A least start with $k_p\ne0$ contradicts this equation there. Thus the vanishing frequencies are precisely the monomials surviving independent phase averaging, even when bins share prime endpoints.

Under that average the bin variables $Y_j=B(|Z_j|^2-s_j)$ are independent, centered, and real. Permuting four starts gives $\mathbb E|Z_j|^8\le 4!s_j^4$, hence $\|Y_j\|_4\ll B s_j$. Since $s_j\le w_{\max} A_j$ and $w_{\max}\le 8D^2/X=O(n^{-2})$ (also valid as a weight bound when there are no starts),

$$\mathbb E\Big(\sum_jY_j\Big)^4=\sum_j\mathbb E Y_j^4+6\sum_{i<j}\mathbb E Y_i^2\,\mathbb E Y_j^2\ll \big(B^2\sum_js_j^2\big)^2\ll n^{-4}.$$

This proves the integral bound. Unlike an [exceptional measure estimate for $Z$](dyadic-prime-pair-moments.md), the combination with the derivative bound for $T$ locates cells to clear without first testing $Z$ on the fine grid.

**Two decisions, at different precisions.** Partition the inner shell $[t+\ell,5t/4-\ell]$, $\ell=L/\sqrt n$, into cells of length $cLn^2$ apart from a possible shorter last cell. Choose $c>0$ small enough that $T$ varies by at most $a^2/16$ from each midpoint. Evaluate $T$ there with certified absolute error at most $a^2/16$. If the estimate is at most $a^2/2$, the entire cell has $|Z|^2\le5a^2/8$. Otherwise flag it: Throughout that cell $T-D_0>a^2/4$. The fourth moment bounds the total flagged length by $O(t/n^4)$, and the number of flagged cells by $O(1+t/(Ln^6))$, including the possible clipped one.

In each flagged cell of length $v$, divide into $\lceil v/\ell\rceil$ equal subintervals. Test **both endpoints** of each subinterval for the original squared cap $a^2\exp((\log n+O(1))/n)$, using either $|Z|^2$ or the far, signed $Q_d$ from the linked cap test. The dense integer-gap version there also works. The ordinate must be the genuine one; the stated precision is for the final statistic value. The strip, optional sparse-group deletion, and a certified $O(1/n)$ evaluation error cost only $O(1/n)$ in total. On each subinterval $|Z|^2$ is at most its larger endpoint value plus $O(1/n)$ by linear interpolation: Its second derivative is $O(L^{-2})$ since its total coefficient mass is $O(1)$ and its frequencies are $O(1/L)$. Conversely the cap on the inner shell bounds every tested node up to the same errors. Thus these tests are equivalent **allowing a change of the bounded additive exponent constant**, not at an unchanged prescribed constant. Endpoints of clipped or isolated flagged cells must not be omitted.

The fine-node count is $O(1+t/(Ln^{7/2}))$ by the length and count bounds. Including all coarse midpoints gives the claimed $O(1+t/(Ln^2))$. This counts statistic evaluations per shift, not bit operations or elapsed time. It is asymptotic: At the [certified crest](check-retained-crest.py) $\sum w_p^2>0.011$ already exceeds $a^2/8$ even for $B=1$, and $t<X^8$. That crest is not a finite trial of the claimed saving.

**What the flags cannot decide.** There is a control on the whole coarse profile, not just its value at one node. On a retained support with $A=\sum A_j>1/4$ (the linked cap note gives such shifts for sufficiently large fixed $K$), fix an inner-shell ordinate $v$. Multiply each edge by $e^{-iv\lambda_p}$, giving $Z(v)=A$. In a second control also multiply all edges of bin $j$ by a sign $\epsilon_j$. Then $T(u)$ is identical in the two controls at **every** $u$. Yet one can make $|Z(v)|\le\max_j A_j=O(1/n)$: Add the real summands $\epsilon_j A_j$ successively with sign opposite to the running total. Both controls can be realized by vertex phases for this one shift, since each increasing edge chain can be assigned phases recursively. This is not a realization of arbitrary twists at untwisted shell ordinates, or a simultaneous construction across shifts. It shows exactly what the fine test must restore, even if the entire local-energy profile is known.

**Next arithmetic question.** It suffices to bound the dense signed gap sum at the fine nodes of flagged cells. Can the actual rational edge phases there constrain the *relative* bin phases? Pricing the absolute mass of the gaps again loses that information. Neither the flags nor their total length rule out one offending node. The per-shift budget also still needs the weighted sum of smaller excursions; the screen here is for the pointwise cap only.

The [within-gap cube screen](signed-gap-cube-screen.md) further reduces the node count, at greater cost per coarse evaluation. A single quadratic vertex twist tests the remaining phase ambiguity across shifts simultaneously.
