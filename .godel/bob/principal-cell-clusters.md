# Two short intervals cover the selected cells locally

The relevant unit for comparing prime passes is smaller than the [triple rejection window](triple-endpoint-cap.md). In *every* window of harmonic heights of length

$$H_X=\exp\big((\log X)^{3/2}/(\log\log X)^3\big),\qquad X=((9/5)\log Q)^2,$$

the target-range part of the [buffered principal list](principal-block-search.md) at any fixed $a\ge0.587$ is covered by at most **two intervals of length 8** per harmonic, for all sufficiently large $Q$. At $a=47/81$ use length 15 instead. Here $Q^{2/5+\delta}\le J\le Q$ as in the list contract, $Q\le q\le Q+J$, and the list tests a signed real part or a modulus at $v=2jy$, $y=q^{3/5}$, with fixed $j>0$. Divide the covering lengths by $2j$ before comparing different lists. The assertion concerns all points of the clipped cells, not just the conductors or unbuffered hits. It neither says that there are two peaks nor locates one without a list.

## The ramp at differences, not at the peak ordinates

Write $D=\log X$, $w_p=X^{-1}\log p\,(D-\log p)_+$, $M=\sum w_p$, and $S_X(u)=\sum w_p p^{iu}$. Uniformly for $|u|\le H_X$ we have

$$S_X(u)=X^{iu}/(1+iu)^2+o(1). \tag{1}$$

The proof of (1) uses the Vinogradov–Korobov zero-free region for zeta, **not** a fixed strip to the left of one. We detail the range since partial summation with an ordinary PNT error multiplied by $|u|$ would not suffice. Replace the prime sum by the von Mangoldt sum at cost $O(X^{-1/2}D^3)$ (bound the number of bases for each power by $\sqrt X$). By Mellin inversion of the positive log ramp the latter is the integral, divided by $2\pi i$, of $-(\zeta'/\zeta)(s-iu)X^{s-1}/s^2$ on $\Re s=1+1/D$. Cut at imaginary parts $\pm T$, $T=4H_X$; the tails are $O(D^2/T)$ by absolute convergence.

By [Mossinghoff, Trudgian, and Yang, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06867), choose $b=c(\log T)^{-2/3}(\log\log T)^{-1/3}$ with a sufficiently small absolute $c>0$ so that $\beta<1-2b$ for zeros up to $2T+2$ in absolute height. For bounded ordinates this follows also by compactness and nonvanishing on $\Re s=1$. Move the finite contour to $1-b$, crossing only $1+iu$. On the three new sides $|(\zeta'/\zeta)(s-iu)|\ll b^{-1}\log(2T)$. Indeed the standard local partial-fraction formula for $\zeta'/\zeta$ (Titchmarsh, *The Theory of the Riemann Zeta-function*, §9.6) is the sum of $1/(s-iu-\rho)$ over zeros within one of the shifted ordinate, plus $O(\log(2T))$ at absolute ordinates at least 3; the local count is $O(\log(2T))$, and each denominator has modulus at least $b$. Near bounded ordinates subtract the pole term; the remainder is bounded there on this narrowing strip, and the pole is at distance at least $b$ on the new sides. Thus the vertical costs $O(X^{-b} b^{-1}\log(2T))$ and the horizontals $O(T^{-2}b^{-1}\log(2T))$. Both vanish: $bD\gg(\log D)^{5/3}$ while $b^{-1}\log(2T)$ grows only polynomially in $D$. The pole residue proves (1), including $M=1+o(1)$ at zero. This computation gives no decay estimate at the absolute selected height $2jy$.

## A packing threshold with room for the search buffer

Freezing all cutoffs on the block at $X$ costs $o(1)$ uniformly in the phase, by the [log-cutoff variation bound](principal-block-search.md). Suppose three points $v_i$ of one list in the window are pairwise at distance at least $K$. Use the three vectors $(\sqrt{w_p}\,p^{iv_i})_p$ against $(\sqrt{w_p})_p$. Their Gram matrix has diagonal $M$ and off-diagonal moduli at most $(1+K^2)^{-1}+o(1)$ by (1). Duality and the maximum absolute row sum give

$$3(a-o(1))^2\le \sum_{i=1}^3|S_X(v_i)|^2\le M\left(M+2/(1+K^2)+o(1)\right).$$

The left bound uses the search guarantee at **every** returned point, since its slack tends to zero. For $(a,K)=(587/1000,8)$ and $(47/81,15)$ the excess $3a^2-1-2/(1+K^2)$ is respectively $38191/13000000$ and $299/247131$, both strictly positive. Thus a third such point cannot exist. For the cover, start at the leftmost selected point and take length $K$ to the right; if anything remains start the second interval at the infimum of the remainder (it too is selected for the closed clipped list). An uncovered third point would contradict the packing bound. Enclosing the cells this way after sorting them requires no prime sums. There is no factor counting the mesh points in either the contradiction or the number of covering intervals.

## What one successor pass buys on the supplied list

Both observables in the [adjacent-chord alternative](adjacent-chord-deficit.md) vary by $O_C(D/X)$ per unit of $y$ at frozen cutoff. For the chord mean $F$, its angle integrand is the maximum of $|\cos(u/2)|$ and $|\sin(u/2)|$, hence is $1/2$-Lipschitz. On the clip $e^{-8}X\le p_i\le X$, the right log weights $z_i=\log(X/p_i)$ are at most 8, the adjacent log gaps telescope to at most 8, and $Z=\sum z_i\asymp X/D$. For the gap-grouped rank-two budget $\sum_{d<H} b_d(E_{2,d})_+$ at $H=CD$, positive part is 1-Lipschitz. Each contributing cosine has $y$-frequency $2\log(p_j/p_{j-2})=O_C(D/X)$ by the clip and gap restriction. The sum of absolute coefficient weights before grouping is $O_C(1)$: There are $O(X/D)$ edges and each costs $O_C(D/X)$ including $b_d$. This proof also covers the rank-one and rank-two triangular terms at $C=400$.

Freezing these clipped tests on the dyadic block costs $o(1)$ independently of the center. Indeed $X(q)/X=1+O(1/\log Q)$. At either clip end $O(1+X/\log Q)$ integers change membership, costing $O_C(D/X)$ each, including normalization for $F$. A prefix or suffix change only inserts or deletes edges at the ends. On common edges $z_j$ changes by $O(1/\log Q)$, $X^{-1}$ changes relatively by $O(1/\log Q)$, and $b_d=2(1-d/H)_+/H$ changes by $O_C(|\Delta H|/H^2)$ uniformly in $d$, including at entry. These variations sum to $o(1)$ by the same edge count; for $F$ use $Z\asymp X/D$ also for the changed cutoff. Thus evaluating both observables at **one actual list point per covering interval** bounds them everywhere on its selected cells with additive $o(1)$ error. In particular two passes suffice per harmonic per window, irrespective of whether either pass finds a rejection margin. The costs of proving a margin and of extending it are different.

There is a conditional cell count for the *specific marking algorithm*, not for an arbitrary list satisfying the buffer contract. A true hit at $a$ forces $\gg_a g\eta/D$ marked nodes within harmonic distance $O(\eta/D)$ of it, even at one end of the target block. Indeed freezing costs $o(\eta)$ and the frozen derivative is $DM$; on the inward side for distance $\eta/(8D)$ the tested statistic is at least $a-\eta/3$ eventually. All these nodes pass the preceding modulus search at $a/2$, and an evaluation with error $\eta/8$ clears the final $a-\eta/2$ test. Use the longer inward side if the hit is interior. The block length grows polynomially, and $g\eta/D\to\infty$. So one successor evaluation can cover many distinct cells if the list contains an unbuffered hit; this is not guaranteed from a nonempty buffered list alone. It does not imply that this evaluation rejects them.

## Direct screening also reuses work on these intervals

At fixed additive accuracy $\epsilon>0$, one can price the **one-prime real projection with unrestricted signs** on every supplied cell in the window with $O_{\epsilon,j}(1)$ full prime passes per harmonic, plus work per listed cell, not one prime pass per cell. This applies even if the triple cap has no margin at the chosen representatives. It gives an upper test, not a computation of the arithmetically prescribed character signs.

In a covering interval in $y$ let $y_0$ be its left endpoint and $h=y-y_0\in[0,K/(2j)]$. Form a table of the frozen sums

$$A(h,\theta)=\sum_p w_p |\cos(y_0\log p + h\log(p/X)+\theta)|.$$

At $\theta=hD\pmod\pi$ this equals the direct sum $\sum w_p|\cos(y\log p)|$, without optimizing over or losing the carrier rotation. The two coordinate Lipschitz costs are $\sum w_p\log(X/p)=2+o(1)$ and $M=1+o(1)$: For the first, integrate $X^{-1}\log^2(X/u)$ against $d\vartheta(u)$ by parts, then use $\vartheta(u)\sim u$ and $\int_0^1\log(1/z)\,dz=1$. Hence a rectangular table of pitch $\epsilon/20$ or smaller on this bounded cylinder suffices, with $O_j((1+1/\epsilon)^2)$ entries. Compute entries with error at most $\epsilon/10$; the Lipschitz constants can also be bounded by summing their positive weights. For a cell take its intersected midpoint, locate a nearby entry in each coordinate (one pitch suffices), and add interpolation, entry, cutoff, and within-cell errors. The latter two are $o(1)$ uniformly: Use the variation of the weights, and the direct derivative bound $DM$ in $y$ times the mesh $1/(2jg)$, $g=\operatorname{bitlength}(Q)^2$. These allowances give a uniform upper enclosure within $\epsilon$ of the moving direct sum everywhere in each such cell for sufficiently large $Q$ (refine arithmetic enclosures within the slack). Cells straddling a cover boundary may be split. This is a fixed-accuracy count of prime passes, not a constant bit cost, and there is still the cost of producing and reading the list. The direct test can reject every cell on which it certifies a fixed margin, without requiring *all* intervening heights to have margin.

Two clusters cannot be collapsed to one just from this difference estimate and a free base phase. For example take $u=D$, $r_p$ the representative of $u\log p$ in $[-\pi,\pi)$, and set the initial raw phase at $p$ to $-r_p/4$. At offset $u/2$ its angle modulo $\pi$ becomes $r_p/4$. Both direct unsigned-sign projection caps tend to $2\sqrt2/\pi>8/9$ and both first doubled real means tend to $2/\pi>0.587$. Indeed the $w_p/M$ law of $r_p$ tends to uniform on the circle by (1) at each fixed nonzero multiple of $u$ and trigonometric approximation. The two means integrate $\cos(r/4)$ and $\cos(r/2)$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$ with normalized measure. A single assignment on primes suffices; the offsets use the actual logs. This is a free initial phase **control**, not a selected center at its own cutoff, nor a realization of the character signs.

## If both clusters contain actual signed hits

There is a stronger arithmetic test than the unsigned packing statement when two raw hits have $u=|y_2-y_1|\to\infty$ in the same window. Let $s_{i,p}=\xi_{q_i}(p)\chi_{d_i}(p)$ be their [actual signs](principal-list-sign-thinning.md), with $|d_i|\le(\log Q)^B$ for fixed $B$. Write $\mathbb E$ for averaging with $w_p/M$. Then

$$\mathbb E\,s_{1,p}s_{2,p}\cos(u\log p)\ \ge\ \frac{2}{\pi}-2\sqrt2\left(\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi}-\frac89\right)-o(1)>0.60429\quad\text{eventually}. \tag{2}$$

This concerns the **product character at the difference height**, not either original projection and not a necessary test for two merely buffered cells. The primes dividing $q_1 q_2 d_1 d_2$ have weight $O(D\log(q_1 q_2|d_1d_2|)/X)=o(1)$, so fill zeros with arbitrary signs during the proof. Put $c_p=|\cos(u\log p)|$. The chord inequality bounds the average of the two signed real projections at $p$ by $\sqrt{(1+s_{1,p}s_{2,p}\cos(u\log p))/2}$. Its free maximum is $f_p=\sqrt{(1+c_p)/2}$. On a sign mismatch the loss is at least $c_p/\sqrt2$, by rationalizing the difference of the two square roots. Now $u\log p$ is equidistributed modulo $2\pi$ in the weighted limit: (1) holds also up to any fixed multiple of $H_X$ by increasing $T$ by that factor, so all fixed nonzero Fourier coefficients vanish as $u\to\infty$. Thus $\mathbb E f_p\to2\sqrt2/\pi$ and $\mathbb E c_p\to2/\pi$. Two raw hits need mean projection at least $8/9-o(1)$ even after freezing. Subtract twice the mismatch mass weighted by $c_p$ from $\mathbb E c_p$ to get (2). The constant simplifies to $16\sqrt2/9-6/\pi$; the lower bounds $\sqrt2>1.4142135$ and $\pi>3.1415926$ suffice for the decimal comparison. Simply squaring the chord bound and using Jensen would give only $47/81-o(1)$. A principal product character cannot pass (2) by (1); for a nonprincipal product, the zeta zero-free region used here is **not** a bound on (2). The ideal free-phase example above would choose the signs separately at the two heights; their product precisely tracks the sign of this difference cosine.

**After Work.** Two actual raw hits also [force a positive real, untwisted peak at twice their sum](two-hit-sum.md), even for cross-distance only known to exceed 6. The test restricts joint occupancy of two surviving groups; it does not apply to a mere pair of buffered cells. Use the original single-group comparison first.

**Decision.** A radius of order $X/D^3$ on which a ratio cap has margin cannot by itself imply an asymptotic saving of full prime passes over direct projection at fixed accuracy on a supplied list: Even a much longer window costs boundedly many direct tables. The triple test may still be cheaper in constants, avoid per-cell lookups on a whole rejected interval, or be useful when only ratio data are accessible. To demonstrate an advantage at shrinking margin, compare against a direct table on the two short clusters with its accuracy cost included. For actual exclusion the missing estimate is still a margin for the moving projection at genuine selected heights, not a bound on the number of sample points in one cluster.
