# Testing overshoots on retained shifts

There are two different arithmetic questions here. A unit budget for one shift requires a precise cap everywhere on the inner shell. A failure of the common dip, however, forces a **fixed** overshoot on many shifts at one and the same height. It cannot be sustained by switching between shifts whose amplitudes only graze the detection level. The fixed overshoot absorbs a subexponential loss at the power separation radius.

Use the notation of the [genuine shell](genuine-high-shell.md): $t=2q^{3/5}$, $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $L=X/D$, $n=2\lceil(2+\gamma)\log t/D\rceil$, $0<\gamma<1/2$, $a=1/16$, $B_{\rm unit}=La^n/t$. Freeze the starts $p,p+d$ prime in $[e^{-8}X,X]$, $w_p=X^{-1}\log(X/p)\log p\log(p+d)$, $\lambda_p=\log(1+d/p)$, $Z_d(u)=\sum_p w_p e^{iu\lambda_p}$, and $s=|Z_d|$. Write $C_d(y)=\sum_{|p-p'|\le y}w_pw_{p'}$, ordered with diagonal. Retention means $n^2 C_d(r)\le K$ at $r=D^2$, for a fixed $K$. Constants below may depend on it and on the fixed parameters.

## What a single crest tests

For a retained shift form only the far, signed four-prime sum

$$Q_d(u)=2\sum_{p'-p>nr} w_pw_{p'}\cos\!\left(u\log\!\left(1+\frac{d(p'-p)}{p(p'+d)}\right)\right).$$

Then, at **every** height, $|s^2-Q_d|\le(2n+3)K/n^2$. Indeed, partition the start line into half-open cells of length $r$ with masses $b_j$. The same-cell sum $\sum b_j^2$ is at most $C_d(r)$. Any pair within $nr$ has cell indices differing by at most $n+1$, and $\sum b_j b_{j+k}\le\sum b_j^2$ for each offset. The far cosine is the exact real cross term in $s^2$; its four endpoints are distinct for large $X$. Consequently the necessary pointwise cap from the shell note is **equivalent**, up to its unspecified additive constant in the exponent, to

$$Q_d(u)\le a^2\left(1+\frac{\log n+O(1)}{n}\right)$$

uniformly on the same inner shell. Indeed $\log^2(n)/n^2=o(1/n)$. This discards a growing strip of width $nD^2$ without spending the coefficient of $\log n/n$. The deletion is for testing the cap, **not** for computing the quadratic correction in $W_2$. An unsigned upper bound on this remaining support is the wrong estimate: Its unsigned mass is $A_d^2-O_K(1/n)$, $A_d=\sum w_p$. The [matching mass bound](matched-prime-overlap.md) gives $A_d>1/4$ for $\gg D$ shifts, and the summed collision bound discards only $O(D/K)$ of these for sufficiently large fixed $K$.

There is a further sparse part one need not estimate. Group $Q_d$ by the *integer* gap $g=p'-p$. Delete each group with at most $M=X/(nD^4)$ lower starts. The cost, in absolute value even at a crest, is at most $2X M(8D^2/X)^2=128/n$, since there are fewer than $X$ positive gaps. In particular the pointwise cap is also equivalent to its version on just the groups with more than $M\to\infty$ genuine four-prime starts. The phase on such a group is exactly $u\log(1+dg/[p(p+g+d)])$; rounding this rational defect at an ordinate of order $t$ is not justified by the deletion. This is a restriction within one shift, not a density assertion for shifts.

**At fixed amplitude the far sum needs only the persistence grid.** Partition the inner shell into intervals of length at most $\ell=L/\sqrt n$, including its endpoints as nodes. In the coordinate $h=u/L$, $|(d/dh)^2 Q_d|\le (10e^8)^2 A_d^2=O_{K,\gamma}(1)$, by the same-cell Cauchy bound below. The maximum of the two node values is at least $Q_d(u)-O_{K,\gamma}(1/n)$ everywhere in that cell, by linear interpolation. Hence it is necessary **and sufficient for the pointwise cap** to test the displayed upper bound for $Q_d$ only at those nodes, without changing the coefficient of $\log n/n$. The same argument works after the sparse-group deletion, increasing the constant in the $1/n$ error. No translated phases enter. This is coarser than the grid for relative $1+o(1)$ reconstruction all the way down to the vanishing high cutoff.

It also prices the fixed-amplitude part to within constants with a threshold buffer. On $U=[t,5t/4]$, use this grid of step $\ell$ with a possibly shorter last cell and include both endpoints. Fix $0<b_0<a$ and define $H(b_0)=B_{\rm unit}^{-1}\int_{U\cap\{s\ge b_0\}}K_t W_2$. Write $Q_j=Q_d(u_j)$, $w_j=(\max(Q_j,0)/a^2)^{n/2}$. For a sufficiently large fixed $C=C(K,\gamma)$,

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\!\sum_{\substack{\operatorname{dist}(u_j,\partial U)\ge\ell\\Q_j\ge b_0^2+C/n}} w_j
\ \ll_{K,\gamma,b_0}\ H(b_0)\ \ll_{K,\gamma,b_0}\ \frac{1}{\sqrt n}\!\sum_{Q_j\ge b_0^2-C/n} w_j.$$

For the upper bound charge each cell to its larger far node. At any contributing point $Q_j\ge s^2-C/n$; this additive error costs only a bounded factor in the $n/2$ power since $s\ge b_0$. There are at most two charges per node, and $K_t\asymp1/t$ on $U$. For the lower bound use the side on which the first derivative of $Q_d$ points up, taking length $\ell/2$. Taylor's bound and the deleted-strip estimate give $s^2\ge Q_j-C/n\ge b_0^2$ there. The intervals have disjoint interiors because the included nodes are at least $\ell$ apart. On either side of the estimate the correction is bounded between $\exp(\pm K/(2b_0^2))$ on the integrated set; it is **not** $1+o(1)$. This does not price the growing range $X^{-1/10}\le s<b_0$. Nor would an $O(\sqrt n)$ count alone suffice for the displayed weighted sum when node amplitudes vary.

**A finite test on the actual shell.** Use $q=2^{127}-1$, $\gamma=1/4$, $d=18$, $n=24$. This shift satisfies $D<d<4D$ and $60.99<n^2C_d(r)<61$. On the whole interval of radius $1/32$ about $9t/8+1296287232$, one has

$$0.4286<s<0.4311,\qquad 44.6<n\log(s/a)-\tfrac12\log n<44.8,\qquad 0.0966<Q_d<0.0982.$$

Thus even the separated part is large at this genuine interior height. The interval clears the actual high cutoff $X^{-1/10}$. Its corrected high-branch integral alone lies between $1.55\cdot10^8$ and $1.24\cdot10^9$ unit budgets. Run [check-retained-crest.py](check-retained-crest.py) with a Sage Python interpreter without `-O`. A numerical search selected the height; the certificate needs no search or floating-point phase recurrence. It encloses the logarithms, all rotations over the interval, the original translated sinc square, and the full ordered correction including the diagonal. The bound is on this interval, not the entire shell. One finite excess does not refute a cap with an unspecified $O_K(1)$ or an eventual budget, and is not a finite computation of completed $I$.

**Follow-up for the per-shift cap.** The [two-resolution screen](retained-shell-screen.md) clears whole height cells before applying this signed gap test. Only the flagged cells need fine nodes. It does not bound the signed sum there or price the smaller excursions.

## A retained shift cannot stay just at threshold

Fix $c>0$ and $cD/2\le d\le5D$. In the scaled coordinate $h$ write $F(h)=Z_d(t+Lh)$, $g=|F|^2$, and $W=X/D^2\asymp_\gamma n^2$. There exist constants $C_0,\beta>0$ such that on **every** interval $J$ of length at most $W$ and for all sufficiently large $X$,

$$\big|\{h\in J: |g(h)-a^2|\le\sigma\}\big|\le C_0\sigma^\beta\qquad(0<\sigma\le a^2/4).\tag{1}$$

Lebesgue measure here is in units of $L$, not the fraction of $J$. The estimate is uniform in the translate and the retained shift.

First, $A_d\ll1$ by Cauchy on the $O(W)$ cells of length $D^2$. All scaled frequencies $L\lambda_p$ are bounded by $\Omega=5e^8$, and their gaps are at least $c|p-p'|/(2X)$ by differentiation. Majorize an interval of radius $V=2W/c$ about $H$ by a constant times $\operatorname{sinc}^2((h-H)/(2V))$, with $\operatorname{sinc}(x)=\sin(x)/x$. Its angular-frequency transform has modulus $O(V)$ and vanishes outside $[-1/V,1/V]$. Only pairs within $D^2$ can contribute. Thus $\int_{H-V}^{H+V}g\ll V C_d(r)\ll 1$, uniformly in $H$. Boundedly many such intervals cover an interval of length $W+O(1)$. No orthogonality at the named center has been assumed.

Now $|g^{(j)}|\le A_d^2(2\Omega)^j$ by the ordered expansion. From any $x$ with $g(x)\ge a^2/2$ one can find $y\in[x,x+T]$ with $g(y)\le a^2/4$, for a sufficiently large **fixed** $T$: Otherwise the preceding energy bound fails for large $W$. Choose a fixed $k\ge1$ so that $A_d^2(2\Omega T)^{k+1}/(k+1)!<a^2/16$, using the uniform bound for $A_d$. Taylor's theorem then forces $|g^{(j)}(x)|\ge\eta$ for some $1\le j\le k$, with $\eta>0$ uniform: Take $\eta\sum_{j=1}^k T^j/j!<a^2/8$.

Partition into cells of a sufficiently small fixed length. In each cell meeting the set in (1), the derivative selected at a meeting point stays of modulus $\ge\eta/2$ throughout the cell, by the next derivative bound. Also $g\ge a^2/2$ throughout, by the first derivative bound. Only $O(1)$ such cells meeting $J$ are possible by the energy bound, including the two end cells. In a cell where the selected order is $j$, a set of measure $v>0$ supplies $j+1$ points separated by $v/(3j)$ (greedily remove neighborhoods of that radius). On the set in (1) the $j$th divided difference at those points is at most $(j+1)\sigma(3j/v)^j$ in modulus by subtracting $a^2$. It equals $g^{(j)}(z)/j!$ at an intermediate point. Hence $v\ll\sigma^{1/j}$, proving (1) with $\beta=1/k$. The same proof gives (1) for $g=(\Re F)^2$: Its integral is bounded by that of $|F|^2$, and its Fourier expansion has total absolute coefficient mass at most $A_d^2$ and frequencies bounded by $2\Omega$. No useful numerical size of $\beta$ is claimed.

## Amplification of a failed common dip

Use the [common-dip failure hypothesis](detection-radius.md): For fixed $b,c,\varepsilon>0$ at every $|u-t|\le bL$ at least $\varepsilon D$ real pair sums have absolute value $>a$ at the moving cutoff on $cD(u)\le d\le4D(u)$. Then there are constants $\delta,\kappa,\rho>0$ and **one** interval of length $\rho L$ inside $[t,t+bL]$ on which $s\ge a+\delta$ for the same $\kappa D$ or more retained shifts. Here $K$ is fixed sufficiently large in terms of the failure parameters. The argument needs only incidences in modulus.

Indeed, take $K$ as in the detection proof: Fewer than $\varepsilon D/4$ shifts are removed and the remainder lie in the enlarged range above. Freezing costs $O(D^2/X)$ per shift. On $J_0=[b/4,3b/4]$ the frozen $s\ge a-o(1)$ incidences therefore have total measure at least $3\varepsilon D|J_0|/4$, summed over shifts. For any small fixed $\delta>0$, those with $s\le a+2\delta$ lie in $|g-a^2|\le (4a+5)\delta$ eventually. Their total measure is $\le 5D C_0((4a+5)\delta)^\beta$. Choose $\delta$ *after* $K$ and small enough to make this less than $\varepsilon D|J_0|/4$. Some common $h$ now has at least $\varepsilon D/2$ overshoots of $a+2\delta$. Since $|F'|\le A_d\Omega$, all those shifts persist at $a+\delta$ on one common interval of the stated length, still in $0\le h\le b$. For the stated real hypothesis one can in fact use $g=(\Re F)^2$ throughout and get $|\Re F|\ge a+\delta$ on this interval. This conclusion concerns a window of failures; it does not amplify an isolated hit at $a$.

For each selected shift use either separation radius $R=r$ or $R=X^\xi$, $\xi=\gamma/[16(2+\gamma)]$. The window just found is in the genuine shell eventually, $s>X^{-1/10}$, and $K_t\gg 1/t$. The absolute quadratic exponent of $W_2$ there is at most $n^2 C_d(R)/(2s^2)$. At $r$ this is bounded. At the power radius use $C_d(R)\le A_d(1+2R)8D^2/X$, giving $O(X^\xi)=o(n)$. The spare amplitude absorbs this loss. Thus even the high-branch shell integral is $\gg L(a+\delta/2)^n/t$ at **both** radii. By [balanced reconstruction](balanced-height-reconstruction.md) the completed one-copy $S$ is at least $(1-o(1))$ times the positive height mass minus $t^{-1-\min(\gamma/8,1/100)+o(1)}$; $A_d\ge s>a$ supplies the required mass floor. The [completed comparison](windowed-harmonic-witness.md) transfers the lower bound to $J_R$ with additive error $t^{-1-\gamma/8+o(1)}$. At $D^2$ this retains the low-private exclusion. The [two-copy identity](net-pool-supply.md) gives $I=S^2+o(B_{\rm unit}^2)$, not joint separation. Since $B_{\rm unit}=t^{-1+o(1)}$, these errors do not consume the excess.

**Decision.** For the common dip one can now work at the power radius and identical amplitude $a$: Even a one-sided $J_R\le B_{\rm unit}\exp(o(n))$ outside $o(D)$ retained shifts, uniformly for each fixed trim, would suffice. Alternatively it suffices to exclude simultaneous fixed overshoots of $Q_d$ above $a^2$ in the one local shell window. Neither arithmetic bound follows from (1). The [available permutation moment](dyadic-prime-pair-moments.md) prices a set of ordinates, not its supremum, and the [four-form sieve](prime-pair-dispersion.md) prices unsigned mass, not $Q_d$ at an individual ordinate. For the stronger per-shift unit shell budget, the precise far-pair cap, even at a single overshooting height, remains necessary; proving it would still leave the weighted count of smaller excursions.
