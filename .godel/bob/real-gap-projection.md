# Complex gap profiles do not determine the real common dip

Keep the [cube screen](signed-gap-cube-screen.md) for the **modulus cap**. For the common dip the observable is different: The [persistent failure implication](retained-shell-overshoot.md) holds for $|\Re Z_d|$ itself. Absence of sufficient modulus overlap on the named window clears the real test, but the converse fails even with *all* complex gap profiles at *every* angle and height. Here is a control at the density and window scales of that implication, followed by a finite test starting from untwisted phases.

## Two controls on the same prime support

Use $t=2q^{3/5}$, $X=(3\log(t/2))^2$, $D=\log X$, $L=X/D$, $a=1/16$, and the frozen weights, $n$, and pressure $n^2C_d(D^2)$ of the screen. For a unit completely multiplicative $\omega$, let $z_{d,p}(u)=w_p\omega(p+d)\overline{\omega(p)}e^{iu\lambda_p}$ and $Z_d=\sum_p z_{d,p}$. The complex gap data are $P_{d,g}=2\sum_p z_{d,p}\overline{z_{d,p+g}}$, $g>0$. Multiplying the value at each prime $p$ by $e^{i\alpha p}$ and extending completely multiplicatively multiplies $Z_d$ by $e^{i\alpha d}$. It leaves **each complex $P_{d,g}$ unchanged** at every height, even before pruning. In particular all dense signed polynomials $B_d(\beta)$ in the cube note are identical, not just translated. This linear phase $\alpha$ is not the quadratic phase $\beta$ in that test.

There are fixed $b,c,\epsilon>0$ and $K<\infty$, two such controls related by a single $\alpha$, and arbitrarily large named centers with the following contrasting outcomes:

* In the first control the same $\ge\epsilon D$ retained shifts have moving-cutoff real sum $>1/8$ on **all** of $|u-t|\le bL$, inside the trim $cD(u)\le d\le4D(u)$.
* In the second, $\max_{1\le d\le 5D}|\Re Z_d(t)|=o(1)$. One dip at $t$ suffices. No uniform smallness on the window is needed.

**Proof.** Take named centers tending to infinity with disjoint frozen prime bands. The [matching estimate](matched-prime-overlap.md#a-matching-large-enough-for-a-full-support-control) gives a single vertex-disjoint matching of consecutive primes at each center. On at least $D/(16M)$ shifts strictly inside the trim (with a unit margin), the matching mass exceeds $1/4$ and $A_d=\sum w_p\le M$. By [the summed pressure bound (2)](detection-radius.md) discarding shifts with pressure $>K$ costs $O(D/K)$ shifts. Fix $K$ large enough to leave at least $D/(32M)$. Discard multiples of $2^J$ for fixed $J$ with $4/2^J<1/(64M)$. Pigeonhole the remaining $J$ possible values of $v_2(d)$: At least $\epsilon D$ have the same value $j$, for some fixed $\epsilon>0$. Pass to a subsequence so $j$ is fixed across centers.

Keep only the matching edges belonging to these selected shifts. Make each kept pair one block, and all remaining vertices singletons. At the center assign $\omega(p)p^{it}$ one common independent uniform unit phase per block. Each kept edge now has phase 1. Every other oriented prime edge connects two distinct blocks and carries the character end phase divided by start phase. Different ordered block pairs are orthogonal, including reversed pairs; at most four edges have a given ordered pair. For $E_d$ the sum over the other edges, Cauchy in each character gives

$$\mathbb E\sum_{1\le d\le5D}|E_d(t)|^2\le4\sum_{d,p} w_p^2\ll D^3/X.$$

Indeed $\max w_p\le 8D^2/X$ and $\sum_{d\le5D}A_d\ll D$ by the same pair-sieve moment bound as for the matching (with enlarged endpoint). Choose one realization per band where the sum is no larger than its expectation. The bands are disjoint, so all the prime prescriptions extend to one $\omega$. In particular the errors are uniformly $o(1)$, **including on unselected shifts**. At $t$ the first control has $Z_d$ equal to the kept mass plus $E_d$. On the selected shifts $L|Z'_d|\le 5e^8 M$ at all frozen heights. The moving-cutoff correction on the stated window costs $O(D^2/X)$ independently of phases, as in the detection proof. A sufficiently small fixed $b$ therefore leaves the real sum above $1/8$ on these same shifts; their trim margin absorbs the cutoff motion. In the second control use $\alpha=\pi/2^{j+1}$. All kept masses at $t$ are rotated by $\pm i$, and there is no kept mass on any other shift. This proves the contrast. Neither control is asserted to be untwisted.

## A full angle sweep from a genuine center

At $q=2^{127}-1$, $\gamma=1/4$, $n=24$, $K=61$, consider **all** retained $1\le d\le4D$ at $u=t$. In the untwisted sum $|Z_d|>a$ exactly for $d=14,18,26,34$, but $|\Re Z_d|>a$ exactly for $26,34$. The [192-bit Sage interval certificate](check-real-slope.py) varies one shared $\alpha$ over $[0,\pi)$, a full period for the absolute real counts. The modulus list and complex gap profiles stay fixed. The number of real hits ranges from **zero to four**; two at $\alpha=0$ is not an extremum. The certificate orders all 184 threshold crossings with disjoint interval enclosures, not a mesh of sampled rotations. At each isolated crossing the strict count equals the smaller adjacent count. Run with `sage -python .godel/bob/check-real-slope.py` and assertions enabled. This is a center test, not a trial of the large-$t$ cube saving, of persistent failure, or of an eventual cap with an unspecified constant.

**A genuine common node.** At the same conductor take $u=t+5L/8$, now with the moving cutoff $X(u)$ and **no retention restriction**. Among all $D(u)\le d\le4D(u)$ the same certificate finds 11 modulus hits, but only $d=12,14$ have absolute real part above $1/16$. Every other absolute real part is below $0.0562$. Thus for the finite parameters $b=c=1$ and $\varepsilon=1/4$ this single ordinate witnesses fewer than $\varepsilon D$ real hits, even though the modulus count exceeds that allowance there. No twist is used. The node was found by search, not specified by the asymptotic theorem. It does not establish a dip for another conductor, trim, density, or window.

**An entire window where only the real test gives a dip.** Take instead $b=1/16$, $c=1$, and $\varepsilon=1/2$ at that conductor. Now $5<\varepsilon D<6$. Exactly five shifts ($12,24,26,34,36$) have $|\Re Z_d(t)|>a$ in the full trim, but **every** $u$ in $[t-bL,t+bL]$ has at least six modulus hits. These need not be the same six shifts. All trim shifts satisfy the frozen pressure cut with $K=128$. For the moving cutoff throughout, the integer support band is $[9,25107]$ and the integer trim is $[11,40]$. The checker encloses the weights for this whole window. For each possible weight vector held fixed, on a cell of midpoint $m$ and radius $h$ the modulus is at least

$$|Z_d(m)|-h|Z'_d(m)|-\tfrac12 h^2\sum_p w_p\lambda_p^2.$$

All three terms are evaluated with the common weight enclosure, so this works for the weights at *any target point* of the cell, without differentiating moving weights. Bisect a cell unless six lower bounds clear $a$; no terminal cell may remain undecided. This certifies the absence of a modulus dip on a continuum, not just on trial nodes. Both the dip at the center and its absence for the stronger observable concern this particular finite slice.

**Arithmetic handoff.** At a height left by the modulus screen, for the real problem restore the *sum* of the two log labels, not another difference. Precisely, use $2(\Re Z_d)^2=|Z_d|^2+\Re Z_d^2$. Alongside $Q_*=\sum_{g\ {\rm dense}}\Re P_{d,g}$, on the very same dense far pairs form $U_*=2\sum_{g\ {\rm dense}}\sum_p \Re(z_{d,p}z_{d,p+g})$. For each quadratic sum the deleted terms have the same absolute weights, so $|(\Re Z_d)^2-(Q_*+U_*)/2|\le(2n+3)K/n^2+128/n$. Unlike $P$, the complex sum behind $U_*$ acquires $e^{2i\alpha d}$, precisely the missing phase. For the common dip, try to exclude simultaneous *fixed-margin* exceedances of $(Q_*+U_*)/2$ by the same positive density of retained shifts on one local interval, using the untwisted sum frequencies. The displayed error tends to zero, so it does not eat a fixed margin. Do not require $U_*$ separately small: Cancellation against $Q_*$ counts. Direct real evaluation is cheaper when only a finite node value is needed. The identity gives an arithmetic target, not its uniform bound. The stronger per-shift modulus budget still requires the original cap and control of smaller excursions.
