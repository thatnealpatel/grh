# Which part of the Euler band distinguishes a peak?

Keeping the von Mangoldt weights does not by itself distinguish the [synthetic limit (2)](individual-projection-model.md#all-fixed-harmonics-share-one-limiting-phase-law). Two controls separate the hypotheses. One keeps the named height and moving cutoff but twists the phases; the other uses genuine untwisted phases at admissible heights but loses the moving cutoff. Neither is a zeta peak at its own cutoff.

Write $t_j=2q_j^{3/5}$, $X_j=X(t_j)=(3\log(t_j/2))^2$, $D_j=\log X_j$, $z_n=D_j-\log n$, $B=7/8$, $a=1/4$, $r=127/128$, and $g(z)=\exp((1-B)z-a|z|)$. There are arbitrarily sparse sequences of admissible conductors and a **single** completely multiplicative $|\omega|=1$ such that, for every fixed integer $k\ge1$ and continuous compactly supported $W$, locally uniformly in real $v$,

$$ \frac1{X_j}\sum_n\Lambda(n)W(z_n)(\omega(n)n^{-it_j})^k e^{-ivz_n}\ \longrightarrow\ \int W(z)e^{-z}r^kg(z)e^{-ivz}\,dz. \tag{1} $$

This also holds for $W(z)=z_+$. Thus the first real ramp moment tends to $r/(B+a)^2=127/162$, exceeding $47/81$ by $11/54$. The positive coefficients and their prime-power support are exact; the twisted complex coefficients are **not** those of $-\zeta'/\zeta$. In particular the $k$th harmonic uses $\omega^k$, not the log derivative of one fixed twist evaluated at all $kt_j$.

**Construction.** Choose odd prime conductors so the bands $|D_j-\log n|\le j$ are disjoint and $\sum_j D_j/X_j<\infty$. They may also satisfy the growth conditions for the synthetic blocks. Independently at each prime $p$ in band $j$, draw a circle variable $U_p$ from the mixture of uniform measure and Poisson measure at $r$, with respective weights $1-g(z_p)$ and $g(z_p)$. Put $\omega(p)=p^{it_j}U_p$, and set it to one at other primes before multiplicative extension. Then $\mathbb E U_p^k=r^kg(z_p)$ and at $p^m$ the demodulated multiplicative phase is $U_p^{mk}$ if $p$ is in the band.

For fixed compact support $[-R,R]$ the band eventually covers every contributing prime. PNT gives vague convergence of the positive measures $X_j^{-1}\sum_p\log p\,\delta_{z_p}$ to $e^{-z}dz$ (first on intervals). The expectation therefore has the desired limit. The variance is $O_{W,R}(D_j/X_j)$: The maximum normalized prime weight there is $O_R(D_j/X_j)$ and the total weight is bounded. Chebyshev and summability give almost sure convergence on a countable sup-norm dense collection of tests on each compact interval, for every fixed positive integer $k$. Uniform boundedness of the masses extends convergence to all compactly supported continuous tests. Apply this to $W(z)e^{-ivz}$, a sup-norm compact family as $v$ varies over a compact interval, to get local uniformity for one realization. For $z_+$ the prime tail $z>L$ costs $O((1+L)e^{-L})$ by partial summation and $\vartheta(x)\ll x$, uniformly in the phases. Up to $e^R X_j$ there are $O_R(\sqrt{X_j}D_j)$ higher prime powers, each costing $O_{W,R}(D_j^2/X_j)$ even for the ramp (where $n\le X_j$). Hence their full contribution vanishes. No convergence rate uniform in growing $k$ is asserted.

**Frozen-cutoff control.** With the same $X_j$ one can instead take $\omega=1$ in (1) and replace $t_j$ in the phases by $t'_j=2(q'_j)^{3/5}$, with $X(t'_j)/X_j\to\infty$. For each $j$ fix the realized phase vector on the finite prime band above. Continuous-time Kronecker approximation gives arbitrarily late $u$ with $|p^{-iu}-U_p|<1/(2j)$ simultaneously there: No nonzero integer combination of these prime logs is zero by unique factorization. By the [odd-squarefree sampling estimate (2)](sampled-principal-peaks.md#rotate-first-then-sample), some admissible $t'_j$ differs from $u$ by $O((u/2)^{-1/9})$ (put $x=(u/2)^{5/3}$ and use $O(x^{-1/15})$). Take $u$ sufficiently late that $(D_j+j)|t'_j-u|<1/(2j)$ and $X(t'_j)>jX_j$. The coordinate errors are then at most $1/j$, costing at most $k/j$ in each $k$th power. For every fixed compact test the mass multiplying this error stays bounded, and the same absolute ramp tail and prime-power bounds apply. This proves the claim with the **actual** prime phases, but at the wrong cutoff. No effective hitting time near $t_j$ is supplied.

**What the Euler line adds.** In the $n^{-it}$ convention the Mellin ramp integral of $(-\zeta'/\zeta)(s+it)X^{s-1}/s^2$ on $\Re s=\sigma=1+1/D$, divided by $2\pi i$ and integrated upwards, is exactly $X^{-1}\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-it}(D-\log n)_+$. This follows by absolute convergence and inverse Mellin transformation, and differs from the corresponding prime sum by $o(1)$ uniformly in $t$. Its real part is unchanged by conjugating the phase convention. On compact bands $X^{-1}=e\,e^{-\sigma z_n}n^{-\sigma}$, so replacing the normalization by the Euler-line weight on such bands is just a compact-test reweighting (the factor and its inverse converge uniformly there). This is a statement about the band, not the whole unfiltered Euler series.

There is also a transfer for a smooth window **in the ordinate** on the Euler line. Fix $a\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, $\check a(z)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int a(u)e^{iuz}du$, and put $F_k(s)=\prod_p(1-\omega(p)^k p^{-s})^{-1}$ for $\Re s>1$. At $h=t_j$ use this $F_k$; at $h=t'_j$ use $F_k=\zeta$ and no twist. Termwise integration gives

$$ e^{-ivD}\frac{X^{\sigma-1}}{2\pi}\int \frac{-F'_k}{F_k}(\sigma+i(kh-v+u))\,a(u)e^{iuD}\,du $$

exactly equal to the prime-power sum in (1) at the respective height with $W=W_\sigma=e^{\sigma z}\check a(z)$. Here $X,D$ are frozen at $X_j,D_j$ in **both** cases. For the tails, $e^{z_n/D}\le e$ for $n\ge1$, and the $X^{-1}\sum \Lambda(n)e^{z_n}=\sum\Lambda(n)/n$ mass on each unit log band is $O(1)$ by Chebyshev. The rapid decay of $\check a$ now gives uniformly summable tails; on compacts $W_\sigma\to W_1$. Hence the integrated line statistic has limit (1) with $W_1$, locally uniformly in $v$. This uses absolute convergence on the Euler line, not analytic continuation or a contour shift. The two controls match the limiting statistics of a zero model on this observable, not a joint explicit formula for its zeros and primes at finite height.

**Remaining test.** To exclude the first-harmonic peak it suffices to prove, for some fixed $R,\delta>0$ at **every** sufficiently large admissible $q$, with $t=2q^{3/5}$ and $X=X(t)$,

$$ X^{-1}\!\sum_{Xe^{-R}\le p<X}\!\log p\,\log(X/p)\,[1-\cos(t\log p)]\ \ge\ 34/81+\delta. $$

The omitted deficit is nonnegative and the whole mass is $1+o(1)$. A fixed gap in the full ramp likewise implies a gap in a sufficiently wide fixed band by the absolute tail estimate. A new estimate for this route must use more than either (1) with free multiplicative phases or genuine phase recurrence with an unconstrained cutoff. In particular, test the **height range** of any recurrence or nonconcentration estimate against $\log(t/2)=\sqrt X/3$ before applying it. This deficit is a sufficient test, not the only way to exclude an endpoint failure. [Direct cancellation in the zero projection](adjacent-zero-counts.md#move-the-difference-onto-the-zero-counts) is still open, as is the [weaker required maximum-modulus gap for failure](sampled-principal-peaks.md).
