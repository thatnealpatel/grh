# Proof frontier

This is a working map of manuscript obligations, currently concentrated on the trace-to-zeros argument, not a complete inventory of the paper. Paragraphs and page numbers are navigation into the [pinned edition](sources/masullo/README.md); follow the handoffs for the evidence and its limitations. The finite-place, compact-test, and abstract cutoff-majorant branches have Lean declarations; the trace and divisor endpoints remain open.

The geometric and divisor branches have independent runnable obligations. The finite-place support branch now supplies the vanishing of the omitted linear restrictions. On the geometric branch, the [fixed-annulus nuclear factorization](obligations/trace-polar-free-q.md#a-fixed-smooth-annulus-gives-a-nuclear-lift) now transfers the trace of the actual local level operator through a fixed-support polar-free space in the rational real model. A global comparison still needs to account for the non-Mellin defect of the local multiplier replacement on tests below norm one. On the divisor branch, the [conditional uniqueness proposition](obligations/trace-divisor-resolvent.md#assembled-conditional-uniqueness) now gives noncentral vanishing in Lean from annihilation on the common tests. The [shifted zeta family now satisfies the geometric hypotheses](obligations/trace-divisor-uniqueness.md#checked-separation-of-the-shifted-zeta-zeros) in Lean. The next independent arithmetic step is to apply Jensen's inequality to the entire function with the same critical-strip orders. Equality of the full and capped distributions is still open.

## Global theta inversion (Theorem 2.2.3.2, p. 82)

The [rational unramified lattice theorem](../Proofs/Theorems/RationalThetaInversion.lean) proves theta inversion on the real norm section, including both zero-mode corrections for arbitrary complex Schwartz input. The [next runnable step](obligations/theta-inversion-q.md#next-runnable-step-prove-the-fixed-function-two-tail-estimates) is to bound the outer tail by Schwartz decay and use inversion to bound the small-scale remainder. This is a Chapter 2 endpoint independent of the trace comparisons and a foundation for the two-tail estimates used by the rational theta lifts. The adelic reduction, bounded-family uniformity, and the trace's Mellin subtractions are not established here.

## Toward the trace identity (Theorem 12.5.1.1, pp. 519–520)

The [final scalar comparison](obligations/trace-assembly.json) has a visual reading. It needs both evaluations of the **same** diagonal, on the same tests, with the same measure and limit. The algebra does not certify those inputs.

- **Geometric evaluation:** First justify the integrated kernel decomposition and the orbital tail for the two-sided test class; separately identify the normalized $S$-local cutoff asymptotic with the global $S$ contribution, including the two Mellin corrections. The [Chapter 5 citation check](obligations/trace-polar-free-q.md#what-chapter-5-supplies) separates the existence of flat lifts from the nuclear factorization needed on p. 515. For a one-sided cutoff equal to one near zero, a genuine two-moment real Paley–Wiener input can retain a nonzero second derivative after convolution and truncation, even on tests with both Mellin moments zero. A fixed smooth annulus repairs the range and gives the nuclear lift needed for the fixed-cutoff trace transfer in this model. Neither repair replaces the level projection with a multiplier or identifies the global $S$ contribution. The [geometric handoff](obligations/trace-geometric.md) distinguishes these from the vanishing of the omitted linear local terms. Its [scalar cutoff comparison](obligations/trace-geometric.md#smooth-radial-bands-on-the-extended-domain) extends the norm-one domain radially and controls monotone smooth bands by averaging sharp endpoints. The bound vanishes when the product of the position lower and Fourier upper endpoints tends to zero (also the reverse product if both profiles are annular), and the upper transition ratios tend to one. The [fixed-cutoff quotient kernel](obligations/trace-geometric.md#a-fixed-cutoff-quotient-kernel-and-its-haar-diagonal) gives that scalar sum as its normalized Haar diagonal, with locally uniform unit summation and the additive/multiplicative density conversion. This is a project operator on periodized tests, not an identification with the manuscript's nuclear operator. The [Paley-Wiener comparison](obligations/trace-geometric.md#the-fourier-multiplier-is-not-the-invariant-paley-wiener-projection) rules out a literal substitution on the real local model; at $s=0$ a rank-one trace difference survives the joint cutoff limit. The positive-degree comparison of moving-cutoff traces and the global projection are not supplied by that model. The [repaired scalar asymptotic](obligations/trace-scalar.md) evaluates the normalized constructed diagonal: The joint cutoff gives logarithmically normalized local integrals, but taking the Fourier limit at a fixed annular position cutoff first gives zero on the norm-one-vanishing class. Its norm-preserving quotient comparison matches the raw Haar measures under the hypotheses of Theorem 12.4.1.1, hence also their normalizations, but does not identify a kernel or supply a Mellin trace defect. The [integrated stratum split](obligations/trace-geometric.md#integrating-the-stratum-split-is-not-evaluating-its-parts) is absolutely summable given a common integrable diagonal, but its parts depend on the chosen lift. The literal extracted orbital indentation in (11.3.2.2) appears to have zero Haar mass. The common diagonal, global $S$-piece identification, and two Mellin subtractions remain open.
- **One-prime orbital trace:** The [rational trivial-isotype model](obligations/trace-orbital-q.md) gives genuine global theta lifts and a section-based trace-norm limit for tests on both sides of norm one. It distinguishes the exact Chapter 7 quotient from the Chapter 11 Hilbert quotient, which needs closure of the difference range. In this model the literal cutoff closures (11.1.2.6) coincide, and the Mellin sum (11.3.2.5) gives twisted compact averages rather than the unweighted point samples in (11.3.2.1). The [angular character sum](obligations/trace-orbital-q.md#angular-poisson-summation-at-one-prime) restores point evaluation for $p\notin S$, but retains the half twist. Both the corrected comb and the fixed-character sum still vanish for the Chapter 12 support bound $p>T$. For $S=\{\infty\}$, the [standard integer-ring model of (11.2.1.8)](obligations/trace-orbital-q.md#a-zero-line-descent-obstruction-for-the-ring-transition-projector) fails to preserve the zero-line periodization kernel at every finite level, even on genuine theta lifts. The identification with the global level named on p. 489 remains provisional: The literal auxiliary unit factors in the Chapter 7 extraction can change descent. A specified bounded section gives trace-norm convergence in the manuscript order when the level limit precedes removal of the position cutoff, at $a=0$. The [half-weight comparison](obligations/trace-orbital-q.md#half-weight-untwisting-and-the-iterated-limit-on-a-section) bounds untwisting followed by periodization at fixed compact position support, but not without it, even on genuine lifts. At $a=1/2$ a section of shifted theta modes gives a trace-norm limit in the manuscript order, with the level removed first, to the half-twisted comb. The [coupled limit](obligations/trace-orbital-q.md#a-coupled-level-and-position-limit-at-half-weight) gives trace-norm convergence when the band level grows sufficiently fast as a power of the position cutoff. Half-line support removes the rate restriction on any finite set of modes; the argument does not remove it for the full trace. The [shell cutoff comparison](obligations/trace-orbital-q.md#the-right-cutoff-erases-a-fixed-shell-in-the-literal-chain) shows that the right cutoff in the extracted (11.3.2.3) kills a fixed shell if it uses the same decreasing chain. Exhausting places outside $S'$ instead preserves an admissible rational lift, but its shell diagonal is a lift-dependent scalar multiple of the common diagonal, not the circle trace by normalization alone. Continue this branch when there is an independent identification of the selected shell operator with the orbital quotient; the unrestricted joint limit also remains open. The spectral order check below can run independently.
- **Spectral evaluation:** The [operator-order check](obligations/trace-spectral.md#which-operator-does-the-series-represent) shows that the projected-output series gives $PA$, not the required $AP$. The [canonical-lift check](obligations/trace-spectral.md#why-the-canonical-lift-norm-bound-fails) disproves the uniform upper bound in (10.4.2.24), already for the rational trivial isotype: Two close critical-line ordinates give close coefficient functionals, forcing large biorthogonal vectors. The [rational singleton repair](obligations/trace-spectral-q.md) uses local finite rank away from ordinates and trace-norm summation to prove $AP$ is trace class and compute its Sobolev-capped trace for $s=2r\ge2$. It gives the cutoff limit in the required order in that case only. At almost every positive width the [rational jet-quotient repair](obligations/trace-spectral-strip-q.md) gives trace class in the required order $AQ$ and controls $(1-Q)AQ$ for the projection $Q$ onto the span closure of the zeta jets. It groups high zeros by a strip product and uses a coercive smoothing multiplier, without any individual lift bound. The [cited totality bridge](obligations/trace-spectral-strip-q.md#source-interface-a-missing-totality-step) reverses the nesting of finite common kernels in the extraction. The [Gaussian-division repair](obligations/trace-theta-density-q.md) proves equality of the weighted theta closure and the common jet kernel at the same full-measure choice of widths, using moment-zero compact log approximations. This transfers $AQ$ to $AP$ on the rational trivial isotype only. The [level-first comparison](obligations/trace-diagonal-q.md) rules out the normalized local invariant-level replacement in the fixed-position-first order: Its iterated diagonal is zero, while the global companion has nonzero trace on some tests strictly between norms $1$ and $2$ with both Mellin moments zero. Neither this obstruction nor the jet trace evaluates the joint-cutoff local projection. The [joint-cutoff real-place comparison](obligations/trace-joint-local-q.md) computes the local projection-minus-multiplier defect for positive widths and specific scaled cutoff profiles. It is a single Mellin moment above norm one. [Below norm one](obligations/trace-joint-local-q.md#below-norm-one-the-defect-is-not-a-sum-of-mellin-moments), transposing the dilation gives a weight-dependent defect outside the span of the two Mellin terms. Thus even on tests with both moments zero the local multiplier cannot generally replace this projection in the joint limit. A global comparison must use the actual levels or identify additional contributions; the orbital shell is still open. The independent divisor uniqueness lemma below is available for interface work without that bridge. The [Haar diagonal repair](obligations/trace-spectral.md#from-the-weighted-kernel-to-the-haar-diagonal) requires [genuinely shrinking averaging boxes](obligations/trace-spectral.md#the-averaging-boxes-must-actually-shrink); in the provisional reading, the boxes in the manuscript retain the Archimedean angular subgroup. Use a common repaired diagonal for the two evaluations, or prove compatibility. Exclude strip-boundary zeros only if the same width is available throughout the argument.

## Idele norm and finite-place support (Appendix A and Chapter 12)

The [single-place norm](obligations/single-place-norm.md) and [idele-class support lemma](obligations/finite-place-support.md) are in Lean. The latter descends the norm by the product formula and proves that the entire single-place restriction vanishes given the support-band, residue-norm, and norm-one vanishing hypotheses. This applies to omitted linear local terms in (12.5.1.2), not the two-sided orbital trace.

The [finite exceptional set argument](obligations/finite-place-support.md#choosing-the-finite-exceptional-set) is now in Lean. The descended norm is continuous and positive, and the topological support of a compactly supported test lies in a strict norm band. Bounded ideal norms give a finite exceptional set, so no separate band or residue-norm premise is needed for vanishing outside that set. This does not supply the orbital trace comparison or the additional conditions on $S$ in the proof on p. 520.

## From trace to zeros (Chapters 1, 13, and 14)

Neither a trace on a restricted test class nor the empty [divisor heading](obligations/divisor-heading.json) supplies a divisor theorem. The [trace-to-zeros handoff](obligations/trace-to-zeros.md) distinguishes the two trace statements and two proposed routes. The [fixed-parameter subtraction](obligations/trace-to-zeros.md#fixed-parameter-subtraction-over-the-rationals) recovers the shifted logarithmic derivative up to a constant, **conditional** on the rational narrow-strip trace. Exclusion down to the critical line by this route needs widths tending to zero and does not bound orders.

Separately, [one-sided moment uniqueness](obligations/trace-divisor-uniqueness.md) recovers every noncentral order from agreement of the full and capped sums on the shared radial tests over $\mathbb Q$. The capped trace and its geometric comparison are not proved. The [raw Haar pole test](obligations/trace-divisor-uniqueness.md#a-pole-test-for-the-raw-haar-reading) rules out that comparison as literally extracted, under the common finite-place index convention. Verify the real-place normalization in (13.3.1.4) on rendered p. 522 once visual review reopens.

Independently of that gate, the [subtracted-resolvent lemma](obligations/trace-divisor-resolvent.md) gives a checked Lean endpoint from a neighborhood identity to vanishing of noncentral weights. The [cutoff interface](obligations/trace-divisor-cutoff.md) maps its input to pinned Mathlib. The [compact-test estimate](obligations/trace-divisor-compact-test.md) proves in Lean the two integration-by-parts identities and a weighted quadratic bound, without using the zero-moment premise. The [pointwise majorant](obligations/trace-divisor-cutoff-pointwise.md) and [integral bound](obligations/trace-divisor-cutoff-integral.md) are in Lean and have compatible interfaces. They assume profile bounds and do not construct admissible tests. The [profile specialization](obligations/trace-divisor-cutoff-profiles.md) proves in Lean that the tests are admissible before the mass correction, gives a uniform weighted integral bound at real scales $N\ge2$, and proves exact agreement with the exponential difference at each positive point for sufficiently large scales. The same module proves the scalar transform and mass limits using a fixed exponential envelope. The [mass-corrected series step](obligations/trace-divisor-cutoff.md#lean-endpoint) now assembles these bounds and limits in Lean: It constructs a fixed mass-one bump, applies annihilation only to the corrected tests, and gives an absolutely summable half-plane identity with a coefficient independent of the half-plane parameter. Equality of the full and capped distributions remains a premise: The [upstream check](obligations/trace-divisor-cutoff-profiles.md#what-the-cited-spectral-cutoff-can-change) separates the fixed-strip position-cutoff limit from the zero-width Sobolev boundary cap. At $s=2$ the latter gives the rational cap on the spectral side, but the cited geometric kernel definition requires $s\ge3$. The [singleton diagonal audit](obligations/trace-divisor-singleton-diagonal.md) finds that the cited singleton kernel theorem uses the opposite order and does not supply the $s=2$ Haar diagonal. A project repair now constructs that diagonal over $\mathbb Q$ in the trivial compact isotype, with $L^1$ control uniform over bounded output cutoffs. The [assembled proposition](../Proofs/Props/ZeroMassUniqueness.lean) now applies the cutoff series and residue lemmas on the common positive-log tests. The half-plane bounds give the anchor gap and local agreement. The [shifted zeta zero lemmas](../Proofs/Lemmas/ShiftedZetaZeros.lean) supply countability, injectivity, the strip bound, and separation at every point. To apply the proposition to the residual zeta weights, weighted summability and test-class annihilation remain to be proved.

For [weighted summability](obligations/trace-divisor-summability.md), Lean now bounds the pole-subtracted completed zeta function by a constant times $n!$ uniformly on $|\Re s|\le n$ using the nonnegative modified theta kernel. The [order transfer](obligations/trace-divisor-summability.md#checked-transfer-of-orders-to-an-entire-function) gives the positive zeta multiplicities as coefficients of an entire divisor. The next step is to [bound a disk count with Jensen](obligations/trace-divisor-summability.md#next-runnable-step-bound-a-disk-count-with-jensen), then use dyadic shells to get the required quadratic weight. This is independent of the full-versus-capped comparison.

The [singleton local control](obligations/trace-divisor-singleton-diagonal.md#a-singleton-test-of-the-local-replacement) matches the weight $s=2$ at width zero: On radial tests strictly between norms $1$ and $2$, the specified joint-cutoff local projection already subtracts the Mellin moment at $-1/2$. A direct replacement would still be missing the moment at $+1/2$, even on the Chapter 13 mass-zero tests. Use this control when comparing the actual global operator with the local and orbital contributions. The global comparison and shell identification remain open, and Hilbert–Schmidt convergence alone does not supply them.

For the full Chapter 13 test class, the [identity-term audit](obligations/trace-divisor-singleton-diagonal.md#passing-from-norm-one-vanishing-to-zero-mass-requires-subtraction) shows that zero mass does not give norm-one vanishing. The rational singleton trace has an explicit divergent identity term on such tests. Subtract it before comparing finite parts, or track its cutoff against the local logarithmic term. This additional obligation does not affect the common positive-log tests used for conditional uniqueness.
