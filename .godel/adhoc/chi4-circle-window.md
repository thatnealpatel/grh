# A finite certificate for the whole chi4 window across the first prime square

Use the conductor-normalized $\chi_{-4}$ Weil form, autocorrelation, and Fourier conventions of [the endpoint note](chi4-window.md). The following is a computer-assisted inequality, not a Lean theorem or GRH. Write $\lambda_D$ for its floor on unit complex tests supported on an interval of length $D$.

**Result.** The brackets are

| Support diameter $D$ | Strict lower bound on $\lambda_D$ | Strict upper bound on $\lambda_D$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| $2$ | $5.929\,10^{-7}$ | $8.184\,10^{-7}$ |
| $11/5$ | $4.368\,10^{-9}$ | $5.800\,10^{-9}$ |
| $9/4$ | $1.100\,10^{-9}$ | $3.797\,10^{-9}$ |

The second window exceeds $\log9$, where the first nonzero prime-square term enters. Run [chi4-circle-cert.py](chi4-circle-cert.py) with the Python interpreter from SageMath 10.9 (assertions enabled): The default certifies diameter two; `--period 16` sharpens its lower bound; `--period 17` certifies diameter $11/5$; `--period 17 --nine-fourths` certifies diameter $9/4$. The upper bounds each use one fixed test in [chi4-trial-upper.py](chi4-trial-upper.py). Neither script requires a zero table.

## Why the finite lower certificate controls arbitrary tests

Center the interval $I=[-D/2,D/2]$, initially with $D=2$. Put $m(y)=\log(4/\pi)+\Re\psi(3/4+iy/2)+2\sum_{n=3,5,7} s_n (\log n)\cos(y\log n)/\sqrt n$, with $s_3=s_7=1$, $s_5=-1$. Embed $I$ by zero extension in a circle of circumference $P=14$. Define its form by the multiplier $m(y_j)$ on the orthonormal modes $P^{-1/2}e^{iy_jx}$, $y_j=2\pi j/P$. Because $P>D+\log7$, the three periodic prime correlations agree exactly with the line correlations.

For the gamma part use $k(t)=e^{-3t/2}/(1-e^{-2t})$. The normalized line and circle archimedean forms both equal $m_{\rm arch}(0)\|h\|^2+2\int_0^\infty k(t)(\|h\|^2-\Re A(t))dt$, using the corresponding correlation. The periodic correlation is the sum of translates by $\ell P$ of the line correlation. Hence **line minus circle** is the form on $I$ with kernel

$$R(x,z)=\sum_{\ell\ne0}k(|x-z+\ell P|).$$

In particular $\|R\|\le 2D k(P-D)/(1-e^{-3P/2})<6.1\,10^{-8}$ by the row-sum bound. One can subtract this error on the whole space and still prove positivity. For the stated constant note also that $R$ is positive semidefinite on *even* functions: for each exponential $e^{-r t}$ in $k$ and each $\ell>0$, the pair of images contributes $2e^{-r\ell P}\cosh(r(x-z))$. Its form is $2e^{-r\ell P}(|\int\cosh(rx)h(x)dx|^2-|\int\sinh(rx)h(x)dx|^2)$, and the second square vanishes there. The series converges uniformly on $I^2$. Reflection commutes with both forms; the even and odd bounds thus combine for arbitrary complex tests.

Put $c=5$. At each frequency choose nonnegative dyadic $v_j=v_{-j}$ with $m(y_j)\ge c-v_j^2$. Only a finite list $J$ needs nonzero weights. Indeed the digamma increment above its value at zero is the sum over $u=3/4,7/4,\ldots$ of $(y/2)^2/[u(u^2+(y/2)^2)]$. This is decreasing in $u$, so integration gives the lower bound $\frac12\log(1+4y^2/9)$. Together with $m_{\rm arch}(0)=\pi/2-\gamma-\log(2\pi)$ and the sum of absolute prime amplitudes, this exceeds $5$ at $|j|=80001$ and beyond. Up to $80000$ the script checks each comparison with balls. The finite-frequency comparisons use Arb's complex digamma enclosure at $3/4+iy_j/2$.

For the restricted functions define $T:L^2(I)\to\mathbb C^{J}$ by $(Th)_j=v_j\int_I h(x)e^{-iy_jx}dx/\sqrt P$. Parseval gives the *lower* form bound $c\|h\|^2-\|Th\|^2$. The nonzero singular values can be computed on the finite side: $(TT^*)_{jl}=v_j v_l g_{j-l}$, where $g_0=D/P$, $g_k=\sin(\pi Dk/P)/(\pi k)$ otherwise. This is not a trial subspace argument: every mode not on the list already earns at least $c$. In frequency coordinates $(e_j\pm e_{-j})/\sqrt2$ the nonzero-index entries use $g_{j-l}\pm g_{j+l}$, with $\sqrt2g_j$ for the even-to-zero cross and $g_0$ at the zero diagonal. Bounding $TT^*$ above separately in these two blocks yields lower bounds on the corresponding spatial parities.

The numerical acceptance step uses $S=2^{50}$. The balls enclose a dyadic approximation to each $g_k$ with error $<1/S$, also for $\sqrt2$. The weights $v_j<3$ are *exact dyadics majorizing the required defects*. Thus rounding $(c-\sigma)I-TT^*$ to $U/S$ costs $<20/S$ in each entry: the two-$g$ entries cost $<18/S$ before the final half-unit roundings of the product and the diagonal; the zero cross uses $|g_j|\le D/P$ and $|\operatorname{round}_S(\sqrt2)|<3/2$. SciPy proposes a factor; the dyadic numerator $L$ obtained from it is accepted only after computing $H=\max_{j,l}|(SU-LL^*)_{jl}|$ **over the integers**. In a block of order $n$ this proves a circle lower bound $\sigma-n(20/S+H/S^2)$, by $LL^*\succeq0$ and the row-sum norm. It does not presume floating-point Cholesky is accurate.

At $P=14$ the even/odd block orders are $1922,1921$. The resulting circle floors exceed $5.817\,10^{-7}$ and $1/6000$, respectively. On the line the even bound is unchanged and the odd bound still exceeds $1/6000-6.1\,10^{-8}$. Inward dilation and mollification give the same window infimum in the smooth interior and finite logarithmic-energy definitions. At $P=16$ the verifier uses $c=11/2$, cutoff $150000$, and $S=2^{52}$. Its even block of order $3661$ at $\sigma=59295/10^{11}$ gives a floor above $5.929\,10^{-7}$. The odd block of order $3660$ at $\sigma=1/5000$ again gives a floor above $1/6000$, and the image norm is below $3.1\,10^{-9}$.

## Crossing $\log 9$

At $D=11/5$ add $-(2\log 3/3)\cos(y\log9)$ to the symbol. The numerator is $\Lambda(9)=\log3$, not $\log9$, and $\chi_{-4}(9)=+1$. No other atom enters before $\log11$. Use $P=17$, $c=263/50$, $S=2^{52}$, and cutoff $247000$. The same tail inequality, now subtracting the absolute values of **four** amplitudes, exceeds $5.26057$ at the cutoff's successor, above $c=5.26$. The finite restricted Gram uses the new length $D$ in *every* overlap. At shifts $\sigma=4.4\,10^{-9}$ (even) and $3\,10^{-6}$ (odd), the verifier checks errors below $3.2\,10^{-11}$ and $3.0\,10^{-11}$ in blocks of orders $3837$ and $3836$. Even subtracting the absolute image bound of $1.005\,10^{-9}$ on both parities leaves a strictly positive floor. Using its even sign retains a floor above $4.368\,10^{-9}$; the odd penalty fits below the odd margin. This is a new finite certificate, not a deduction from continuity at $\log9$. Comparing the lower bound at $2$ with the upper bound at $11/5$ shows that the actual floor drops by a factor greater than $102$. This comparison does not isolate how much of that drop comes from the new atom rather than the larger test space.

## Independent direction of comparison and what remains

For the upper comparison use the normalized real sum of eight odd-index Dirichlet sine modes specified in `chi4-trial-upper.py`. The zero extension is in $H^1$, hence in the log form domain. The script integrates its explicit normalized autocorrelation $A$ against $2k(t)$ in the combination $1-A(t)$, with a Taylor bound near zero and an exponential series bound beyond $D$. Its ball enclosure at $D=2$ is centered at $8.1834717849\,10^{-7}$ with displayed radius $2.89\,10^{-18}$. At $D=11/5$ a sixteen-mode fixed trial gives a quotient below $5.800\,10^{-9}$. At $D=9/4$ the same construction with another test, including the $-2\log3/3$ coefficient at $\log9$, gives an upper bound $3.797\,10^{-9}$. The independent lower certificate retains $P=17$, the cap, the frequency weights, and the cutoff, but recomputes every overlap and the image penalty at diameter $9/4$. The even shift $57/50000000000$ loses less than $2.970\,10^{-11}$ to the verified residual and rounding; the odd shift $10^{-6}$ loses less than $3.192\,10^{-11}$. The line image costs nothing on even tests and less than $1.12\,10^{-9}$ on odd tests. In particular the diameter-$9/4$ floor is strictly below the diameter-$11/5$ floor, without any new prime atom entering.

See [the exterior-correction note](chi4-exterior.md) for what this slack in support length buys, and two certified obstructions to small corrections.

The circle reduction itself works on every fixed window: include exactly the active prime powers, and take $P>D+\max\log n$ over active atoms (and $P>D$). For fixed $P$, clipping the symbol from above by $c$ gives increasing supported floors tending to the circle floor as $c\to\infty$. Indeed, a unit sequence of near minimizers with bounded clipped energy is Fourier-tight: once $c\ge M$, all sufficiently high modes cost at least $M$, since the unclipped symbol tends to infinity. Its strong $L^2$ limit is still supported on $I$; lower semicontinuity first with any fixed clip, then monotone convergence, proves the claim. Taking large $P$ first makes the image error arbitrarily small. This is a way to detect a *strictly positive* fixed-window floor, with no useful complexity bound. It does not ensure the floors stay positive as $D$ grows.

**Link to the Pick blocks at fixed conductor.** For the standard completed $L(s,\chi_{-4})$ the right-hand log derivative at $s=1/2+w$ has the [Pick note's](pick-block-weil-window.md) Laplace decomposition with $L(w)=\tfrac12\log(2(w+16)/\pi)$ and real measure $-G_{3/2}(t)dt-\sum_n\chi_{-4}(n)\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\delta_{\log n}$. This follows from the same digamma identity and the absolutely convergent Euler product on $\Re w>1/2$. The surrogate multiplier is now $\ell+\log4$, positive and coercive, and $\int e^{-2t}d|\mu|<7$ by the density and atom bounds in that note. These are precisely the two estimates needed in its variational passage: the weighted $H^1$ recovery controls the measure term, and the shift tail bound controls that term uniformly on optimized vectors. Bounded total energies then bound the log energy; Fourier and spatial tightness yield strong $L^2$ convergence on subsequences, uniform correlation convergence handles compact sets of shifts, and lower semicontinuity and form density finish the liminf. The projection identity and shift estimates depend only on the nodes. Thus at reciprocal budget $b=1$ the limiting generalized minimum is $\lambda_2$; for example every sufficiently far $[N,eN]$ block exceeds $5.8\,10^{-7}$ times its Cauchy Gram matrix. No effective starting $N$ or GRH implication from this single budget follows.

The same limit at $b=11/10$ is $\lambda_{11/5}$, so the positive margin persists for sufficiently far Pick blocks whose reciprocal sums tend to $11/10$ (for instance the integers in $[N,e^{11/10}N]$ as $N\to\infty$). The argument does not give a bound as $b\to\infty$.
