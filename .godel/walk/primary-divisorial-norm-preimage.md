# The primary unit stratum is not an ambient norm preimage

**Verdict.** Masullo, *The Music of the Primes*, §1.3.2, PDF p. 26,
after (1.3.2.2), p. 25, identifies two different subsets of the
full idèle group. The identification fails already at the first
rational prime in an admissible stage of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{17})$.
On the outside-$S$ integral tensor, the two indicators give the
same pointwise product. This is ordinary mathematics, not Lean.

## Separating the two predicates

Take $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{17})$, the trivial character, and $S$ the
real places together with the place over $17$. The integer ring
is generated by $\omega=(1+\sqrt{17})/2$, of polynomial $X^2-X-4$
and discriminant $17$. Thus $17$ is the only ramified prime and
has one place over it. The polynomial is $X(X-1)$ modulo $2$ with
distinct roots, so $2$ has two places $v,w$ with completions
$\mathbb Q_2$. Hence $S$ is saturated and contains no place over
$2$. The real quadratic Minkowski bound $\sqrt{17}/2<3$ supplies
an integral ideal of norm $1$ or $2$ in each class. The element
$(5+\sqrt{17})/2$ and its conjugate have norm $2$ and generate the
two primes over $2$ (their reductions $\omega$ and $1-\omega$
vanish at opposite roots), so even $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_F)=1$.
In particular $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_{F,S})=1$, and the
trivial character is unramified at all finite places. Here
$T_1=\{2\}$ under either ordering of the rational primes (all, or
only fibers disjoint from $S$), and $P_1=\{v,w\}$ is saturated.
One can obtain it by cutting at $v$ and then $w$ in a single-place
chain; neither intermediate singleton is saturated.

Let $g_v=2$, $g_w=2^{-1}$, and $g_u=1$ elsewhere. This is an actual
idèle. The explicit coordinate predicate fails: The normalized
valuations are $1$ and $-1$, so neither component is a unit. In
the norm route, the two degree-one local norms are identities and
multiply to $1$ at $2$. All other components of $N_{F/\mathbb Q}g$
are also $1$. Thus the rational unit predicate is satisfied, even
by an idèle in the kernel of the idèle-to-idèle norm.

More generally, for any finite prime $p$, use additive valuations
with a uniformizer of valuation one. If $f_v$ denotes the residue
degree, the local norm sends a uniformizer to an element of
$p$-valuation $f_v$ and sends units to units. Therefore

$$\operatorname{ord}_p((N_{F/\mathbb Q}g)_p)
  =\sum_{v\mid p}f_v\operatorname{ord}_v(g_v).$$

The coordinate unit locus is contained in the norm preimage, and
the latter imposes just the vanishing of this weighted sum at each
tested $p$. On the ambient group the inclusion is strict whenever
one tested fiber has two distinct places: Give those components
valuations $f_w,-f_v$ and set the rest to one. If all tested fibers
are singletons, the two predicates agree on the ambient group.

## What still agrees on the tensor

On the idèles where all coordinates in the tested fibers are
integral, all summands in the valuation formula are nonnegative
and all weights are positive. Thus the weighted sum vanishes
exactly when every component is a unit. In particular, if the
selected fibers are disjoint from $S$, this condition holds wherever
the supplied outside-$S$ integer-ring tensor is nonzero. The two
indicators multiplied by that test agree everywhere on idèles,
including where the test vanishes. At the witness the test is zero
because of the $2^{-1}$ coordinate. This works for any number field
and any finite set of tested fibers disjoint from $S$; it does not
need class number one for the support argument itself.

Use the explicit coordinate cut on the full idèle group, or state
the norm-preimage description only on the integral locus. These
are scope corrections to the printed identification. They do not
establish support of an arbitrary lifted kernel, identify an orbit
sum, or justify Haar or trace limits.
