## Classical RH for the Riemann zeta function

`Proofs/Theorems/RationalIdeleRadialExplicitFormula.lean:34–75`
unconditionally evaluates the absolutely summable full-order zeta zero
sum on normalized rational quotient Haar as the two polar Mellin moments
minus the normalized real and finite terms. This is a scalar project
repair, not a trace. Its alignment with PDF p. 623, (B.4.4.16), is
provisional. `Proofs/Theorems/RationalFixedAnnulusTrace.lean:21–41`
gives a norm-summable rank-one presentation and a basis-independent trace
for the actual projected local convolution at fixed cutoffs, retaining
the right projection. Neither identifies the common global diagonal.

Main reported the first obstruction at
`1f13433251809d04c28918a7c21a53fa3078406f`
(`.godel/obligations/trace-orbital-q.md:269–379`). On the provisional
literal Haar reading of PDF p. 495, (11.3.2.2), the first exact-section
indicator forces the compact variable into a Haar-null singleton for
each rational translate. The proposed lift has zero row mass instead of
unit row mass. In (11.3.2.3), the same-chain right cutoff independently
erases the fixed shell. Independent review (`eorRcBP`, Yah session
`01a20423-6e7a-7d64-a52b-fd38fb5c5973`) checked the deductions on that
reading, not an identification of the operator or a Lean result. The
literal zero is not an orbital evaluation. The inherited rendered
reviews in `.godel/obligations/trace-assembly.json` and
`decision-01a20240-39f6-7778-8369-892e00fbbf16.md:42–60` verify the final
algebra at PDF pp. 519–520, Thm. 12.5.1.1, not its analytic inputs; they
also record the insertion marker (p. 514, §12.3) and missing terminal
right projection in the printed limits (pp. 488–489, (11.2.1.4),
(11.2.1.9)). The local-limit alignment at pp. 517–519,
(12.4.1.8–12), (12.4.1.23–28), remains provisional.

**Reopen RH** when nonduplication is confirmed and a Haar-compatible lift
recovers the quotient kernel, identifies the global $S=\{\infty\}$ piece,
and retains a shell-preserving right projection with identified orbital
cutoffs on compatible levels at one common admissible positive half-width.
Then run the untried test in `.godel/obligations/trace-to-zeros.md:84–134`:
Take the derivative of a nonzero nonnegative smooth bump supported in
$(0,\log 2)$. Its mass and prime samples vanish, but its $+1/2$ Mellin
moment is nonzero. Take the global level limit before position exhaustion,
and compute the actual orbital term separately from the Mellin moment.
Even agreement on that test would not meet the remaining Lean premise
(`Proofs/Props/ZetaTestSumComparison.lean:30–39,77–109`): For arbitrarily
small positive half-widths, one strip-supported family dominated by full
orders per width must agree with the full sum on every smooth compactly
supported positive-log mass-zero test.

## Full Masullo GRH

The target is $\Re s=1/2$ for every nontrivial Hecke zero of every
continuous unitary idele-class character over every number field,
including ramified characters. This scope is attributed to the rendered
review in `decision-01a202cd-7acf-7edb-97ce-044d9614d43e.md:78–87` of
PDF p. 6, Acknowledgements, and p. 528, §14.1.2. The multiplicity assertion
and broader automorphic conjecture are separate. The inherited two-reader
`.godel/obligations/divisor-heading.json` review of PDF pp. 522–523 finds a
missing reference and insertion notes at Thm. 13.3.1.2, not a divisor
theorem. Thm. 13.3.1.1 gives a restricted capped critical-line sum, not
full-order equality.

- General-field Lean supplies large-norm Gaussian slices and the normalized
  radial Euler-product identity only for $\Re s>1$
  (`Proofs/Theorems/IdeleClassGaussianRadial.lean:96–105,150–180`). The
  finite Haar calibration, Fourier square on the integer-ring indicator,
  and finite-unramified reflected Mellin pairing landed at
  `51734244d7496bd244430e2c909b1f4f8883c99c`. Finite zero modes and the
  Archimedean ideal dual and covolume landed at
  `253ee3543f5f465600ff1b64be0d559a3231d22a`. These do not assemble
  pointwise adelic inversion with compatible signs and both zero modes
  retained until averaging. Manuscript interfaces at PDF p. 82,
  (2.2.3.2), pp. 97–98, Thm. 2.2.5.4, and pp. 126–127,
  Thm. 3.2.2.1, remain provisional.
- `Proofs/Theorems/PadicRamifiedThreeFourier.lean:29–126` and
  `Proofs/Theorems/DirichletContourReflection.lean:24–59` give local
  Fourier and tested vertical-reflection inputs to a ramified conductor-3
  control, not its identified idelic completion, full-order residues,
  or horizontal exhaustion. General contour alignment at PDF pp. 135–141,
  §3.3, and pp. 614–615, (B.4.2.1–4), remains provisional. Nor is there
  a normalized common positive-width diagonal for that same control.
- Companion's reviewed ordinary mathematics at
  `e46ddc2d60607575a47f38b7e9bd1fe340192a2e`
  (`.godel/obligations/trace-spectral-sum-qi.md:3–23,141–184`) gives a
  convergent capped sum for the actual convolution-first operator in one
  weight-four Gaussian isotype, using the conjugate character at width
  zero. It is not a Lean evaluation, a positive-width diagonal, or a
  verification of the printed kernel (inherited rendered readings: PDF
  pp. 448–449, (10.3.1.3–5), and p. 459, (10.4.2.5)). The reviewed
  numerator and local range results for the finite-unramified example over
  $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$ are also not Lean. The latter independently
  reviewed the rendered local image in PDF pp. 265–266, Thm. 5.4.1.1(1),
  allowing both zero modes (`.godel/obligations/isotypic-inverse-range-q-sqrt-minus-five.md:152–249`).
  The completed factor and entireness at PDF p. 123, (3.2.1.2),
  (3.2.1.5), Prop. 3.2.1.3(1), and literal gluing at pp. 305–306,
  (6.3.2.10–11), remain provisional. Neither example supplies a ramified
  contour comparison.

**Reopen full GRH** when nonduplication is confirmed and both a completed
contour and a normalized positive-width diagonal are identified for the
same admissible ramified control, such as the odd quadratic character of
conductor 3. Compare them using full-order residues and justified
horizontal exhaustion, separating conductor terms, identity terms, poles,
and central zeros. Neither the rational specialization nor a conditional
GRH implication meets the full target.
