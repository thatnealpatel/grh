## Classical RH

The target is Mathlib `RiemannHypothesis` for the Riemann zeta function.
The accepted `Masullo.rationalIdeleRadial_explicit_formula`
(`Proofs/Theorems/RationalIdeleRadialExplicitFormula.lean:66`) evaluates
the absolutely convergent full-order zero sum on smooth compact log
profiles, with both polar moments and the real and finite local terms.
It is a scalar formula on normalized rational idele-class Haar, not a
global operator trace. The accepted
`Masullo.riemannHypothesis_of_strip_test_agreement`
(`Proofs/Props/ZetaTestSumComparison.lean:30,94`) concludes actual RH if,
below every positive bound on the half-width, there is one common
positive half-width and one family of natural weights bounded by full
orders and vanishing off the corresponding closed strip, such that the
full and weighted sums agree on **every** smooth compact positive-log
test of integral zero. Neither a zero-width trace nor a multiplicity cap
is needed for this implication.

The missing bridge is two evaluations of the same global Haar diagonal
on the same tests at arbitrarily small common positive widths. The
independent rendered review in `.godel/obligations/trace-assembly.json`
verifies the final algebra, not the analytic evaluations or their
common admissibility (PDF pp. 519–520, Thm. 12.5.1.1). The prior Blitz
note records that rendered p. 514, §12.3, has only a heading and an
insertion footnote. The exact interfaces for level-before-position
exhaustion with the terminal right projection, the shell and right
orbital cutoff, and the local multiplier versus global $S$-piece remain
provisional source readings (PDF pp. 488–489, (11.2.1.6–10);
pp. 492–496, (11.3.1.2), (11.3.1.4), and §11.3.2; p. 517,
(12.4.1.8–12)). The reviewed rational spectral repairs are ordinary
mathematics at almost every positive width in the trivial compact
isotype, not the missing geometric evaluation.

A derivative of a nonzero nonnegative bump supported in $(0,\log 2)$
has zero mass, no linear prime samples, and a nonzero $+1/2$ Mellin
moment. The documented local joint-cutoff replacement retains the
$-1/2$ subtraction but misses the $+1/2$ subtraction
(`.godel/obligations/trace-joint-local-q.md` and `trace-to-zeros.md` in
the same directory). This rejects the replacement, not the actual
global trace. The lack of prime samples does not evaluate the orbital
trace. The newer reviewed fixed-band Gram calculation at
`1520ccc0cb8b5c37c6c89fc8b8c1f0ad6ac50f24`
(`.godel/adhoc/rational-theta-gram/handoff.md`) retains the right
projection and controls the whole-line Gram tail in the **unsplit**
weighted rational model. It is not a Lean Gram theorem or a
projection-error bound for growing observation windows. It identifies
neither the global $S$-piece nor the right orbital cutoff, so repeating
the control there would not test the disputed shell. RH is the closer
target, but the conditional theorem's comparison is still missing.

**Reopen RH** when a distinct global $S$-piece and right orbital cutoff
are identified with the terminal projection intact. At one common
positive width, take the level limit at fixed position support and use
the short bump derivative to check both Mellin subtractions and the
actual orbital contribution. One successful comparison would justify
further work, not prove the required agreement on all tests at
arbitrarily small widths.

## Full Masullo GRH

The target is location on $\Re s=1/2$ of all nontrivial Hecke zeros for
every number field and every continuous unitary idele-class character,
not just finite-order characters. The prior decision attributes the
scope reading to independent rendered inspection of PDF p. 6,
Acknowledgements, and p. 528, §14.1.2. The noncentral order bound and
broader automorphic conjecture are separate (PDF pp. 528–529, §14.1.2,
Conj. 14.1.2.1). A rational specialization, a single isotype, or a
conditional endpoint cannot discharge the target.

The accepted Archimedean character classifications and scalar Gaussian
integrals are prerequisites. The accepted actual-character product in
`CompletedHeckeEulerProduct.lean:98`
(`6452ac9c56d2c28caec6264ae4c0c68a979adef8`) gives nonvanishing,
differentiability, and a split log derivative only on $\Re s>1$.
`FiniteHeckeLocalZeta.lean:66,121`
(`27c8b9bc1d779e7988cf1cbc19e24271bdb94d3d`) gives integrability and
$M_vL_v$ for the unramified test on $\Re s>0$, and integrability and
$M_v$ for the ramified inverse-character test for every $s$. It retains
the actual finite Haar unit mass $M_v$ without computing the
discriminant normalization or identifying the normalized global zeta
integral. The exact comparison of the mass factors remains provisional
(PDF p. 130, (3.3.1.7); pp. 152–153, (3.4.3.7–13); p. 560,
(A.3.2.2), (A.3.2.5)).

The companion's accepted rational results check the reflected spatial
sign on finite box sums (`bc42366a909bae97277a6a159e45bd7d02493a91`,
`RationalAdeleSameSignTheta.lean:157`), and show nonintegrability of the
calibrated Gaussian tensor upstairs at every $\Re s=1$, including
$1+2i$ (`34f1711389a0e51e767326ceb9b39e22bb6be69c`,
`RationalIdeleRelativeOneLine.lean:208`). The inherited rendered reviews
locate the theta identity at PDF p. 82, Thm. 2.2.3.2, and distinguish
p. 145, Prop. 3.4.2.1, from the norm-ordered limit at p. 653,
(B.7.2.24). The sign result is a repair, and the nonintegrability result
does not refute the ordered product or meromorphic continuation.
Neither supplies a general-character identity. The reported checked
modulus-three Fourier shell draft (`674ae752f7fbcc647c9e93bd55b7dd6f582a838a`,
preserved at `1080bf8534b18b6574115607db70f09046e97d73`) is not in
the accepted ancestry. The reported conductor control is local and
does not determine the reflected phase or assemble the global conductor.

unadopted transport of the Gaussian pairings to completion-unit

Still missing are the normalized global integral identification,

identity and right-line integrability. The manuscript linkage from

not furnish the missing deduction by identification.

**Reopen full GRH** when an accepted general-character completed-contour
interface and an explicit common-diagonal or divisor identity permit a
comparison at one positive width in a ramified isotype. Compare full
residue weights and strip-supported weights on the same admissible test,
accounting for the central and identity terms. This would justify
further work, not prove the full assertion.
