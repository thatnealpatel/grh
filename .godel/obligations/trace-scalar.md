# The repaired scalar asymptotic

**Status: Project calculation, not a Lean result or an identification of operator traces.** This evaluates the sharp scalar integral on the radially extended domain and, by the [smooth-band estimates](trace-geometric.md#smooth-radial-bands-on-the-extended-domain), the corresponding normalized diagonal of the [constructed quotient kernel](trace-geometric.md#a-fixed-cutoff-quotient-kernel-and-its-haar-diagonal). The Paley-Wiener trace and the global diagonal are different interfaces.

## Sum the logarithmic terms before taking the limit

Use the notation of the geometric handoff: $G=\prod_{v\in S}F_v^\times$, $U=\mathcal O_{F,S}^\times$, $r=\prod_{v\in S}|\cdot|_v$, and $dw=d\nu_S(w)$ self-dual for the product character $\psi_{S,\mathrm{std}}$. Take a compactly supported Bruhat-smooth lift $\widetilde\varphi$ on $G$ and its counting-measure periodization $\Phi$ on $G/U$. Extend $h(z)=r(z)^{-1/2}\widetilde\varphi(z^{-1})$ by zero and put $\eta_\xi(z)=h(\xi^{-1}(1+z))$. In the plus-sign Fourier convention, the sharp **normalized** scalar at cutoff product $K$ is

$$T_K=\sum_{\xi\in U}\int\widehat{\eta_\xi}(w)\log_+\!\frac{K}{r(w)}\,dw.$$

The unnormalized quotient Haar diagonal contains the additional factor $V_S=\mu_{S,1}(J^1_{F,S})$. The normalization divides the entire expression by $V_S$, not just its logarithmic term.

For $r>0$, split the weight as $\log K-\log r+\log_+(r/K)$. Each per-unit integral in the resulting expression exists by Schwartz-Bruhat decay and local integrability of the logarithms. The coefficient of $\log K$ is exactly $\sum_\xi \eta_\xi(0)=\Phi(1)$ by Fourier inversion, the product formula, and compact support. The upper tail is summable with a bound tending to zero: Change variables to $y=\xi w$ and write $\log_+(r(y)/K)=\int_K^\infty \mathbf 1_{r(y)>k}\,dk/k$. For fixed $\xi$, the tail interchange is absolutely integrable. Its inner signed integral is bounded both by $C H_\xi^{-1/2}$ and by $C'k^{-2}$, where $H_\xi=\max_v|\xi_v^{-1}|_v$. The first bound subtracts the [uniform upper-edge integrals](trace-geometric.md#smooth-radial-bands-on-the-extended-domain) from their whole-space value, and the second uses $\int |\widehat h(y)|r(y)^2\,dy<\infty$. Equality levels in $k$ do not affect the layer integral. Taking the geometric mean gives $O(H_\xi^{-1/4}k^{-1})$. Integrating and summing by the $S$-unit log lattice count gives $O(K^{-1})$ for the sum of absolute values of the *integrated* tails. This is not an interchange with an absolute unit sum of Fourier integrands.

For the finite term, split $-\log r(w)=\sum_{v\in S}-\log |w_v|_v$. Partial Fourier inversion in the other coordinates reduces the $v$ term for $\xi$ to

$$D_v(f_{\xi,v})=-\int_{F_v}\widehat f_{\xi,v}(\omega)\log|\omega|_v\,d\nu_v(\omega),\qquad f_{\xi,v}(t)=\eta_\xi(0,\ldots,t,\ldots,0).$$

For each $v$, only finitely many $\xi$ give a nonzero slice: The coordinates $\xi_u^{-1}$ for $u\ne v$ must lie in the projections of the fixed compact support of $h$ in $G$. Their log moduli are therefore bounded, and the remaining log modulus is bounded by the product formula. The log lattice has finite kernel. Consequently the assembled finite term is $\sum_{v\in S}D_v(F_v^{\Phi})$ with the compactly supported Bruhat-smooth slice sum

$$F_v^{\Phi}(t)=|1+t|_v^{-1/2}\,\Phi([i_v((1+t)^{-1})]),$$

extended by zero near $t=-1$; $i_v$ inserts its argument in the $v$ coordinate and $1$ elsewhere. The change of variables uses the **inverse** of $1+t$ as the test argument. Thus $T_K=\Phi(1)\log K+\sum_v D_v(F_v^\Phi)+O(K^{-1})$ before imposing vanishing. Neither the sum over places nor the finite sum of slices needs a principal value interchange.

## Ordinary local integrals on the trace test class

Normalize the local multiplicative Haar $d_v^\times t$ by the logarithmic growth of the mass of $1\le |t|_v\le R$ (coefficient one in $\log R$). Equivalently, the norm pushforward is $dr/r$ at infinity and has mass $\log q_v$ at each norm level at a finite place. In particular, a finite-place measure of unit mass on $\mathcal O_v^\times$ is **not** the measure here: It must be multiplied by $\log q_v$.

The coefficient in the local distribution can be checked directly. Change Fourier variables in $D_v$ to obtain $D_v(f(a\cdot))=D_v(f)-(\log|a|_v)f(0)$. Off zero this distribution is dilation invariant, so it is a scalar multiple of $d_v^\times t$. For a compactly supported test $\chi$ equal to one near zero, apply the scaling identity to $\chi(a\cdot)-\chi$. Its Haar integral is $-\log|a|_v$, as is seen by replacing $\chi$ by the unit-ball indicator in that difference (the change has integral zero). This gives coefficient $+1$. The difference between $D_v$ and the extension $f\mapsto\int(f-f(0)\chi)\,d_v^\times t$ is supported at zero and dilation invariant, hence a multiple of evaluation at zero. At an Archimedean place this follows because derivatives of a delta of positive order are not invariant under positive dilations; at a finite place tests are locally constant. The choice of additive character can change the point term, but not the off-zero coefficient.

If $\Phi(1)=0$, the slice vanishes at $t=0$, and $D_v(F_v^{\Phi})$ is the ordinary integral against $d_v^\times t$: At a finite place the slice is zero near zero; at an Archimedean place it is $O(|t|_{\rm Euc})$, integrable against the radial singularity $d\rho/\rho$.

Put $x=(1+t)^{-1}$. Writing $d_v^\times t=c_v\,d\nu_v(t)/|t|_v$, translation followed by multiplicative inversion gives $d_v^\times t=d_v^\times x/|1-x|_v$ in the integral. The factor $|1+t|_v^{-1/2}$ becomes $|x|_v^{1/2}$, so for $\Phi(1)=0$ the result is

$$T_K=\sum_{v\in S}\int_{F_v^\times}\frac{|x|_v^{1/2}\Phi([i_v(x)])}{|1-x|_v}\,d_v^\times x+O(K^{-1}).$$

The stronger vanishing on norm one in Theorem 12.5.1.1 suffices and makes the local test zero on all local units at finite places. Compact support on both sides of norm one causes no problem for this computation. For the smooth-band comparison use $K=B_+D_+\to\infty$ with the endpoint conditions in the geometric handoff; add its profile error to $O(K^{-1})$. This gives a limit for the repaired normalized diagonal, not for all annular cutoffs or for a different order of limits.

## What matches the cited sources

**Masullo locators below are provisional readings of the digest-matched PDF text, not visual verification.** An independent Yah reader checked the correspondence against the extraction and [Connes, arXiv:math/9811068v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811068v1), §VII, Theorem 4 and its proof. Connes's test on $C_S=G/U$ is compactly supported Bruhat–Schwartz with no one-sided support or identity-vanishing assumption. Use the twisted test $H(g)=r(g)^{-1/2}\Phi(g)$ and lift $f(g)=r(g)^{-1/2}\widetilde\varphi(g)$. Since $r(\xi)=1$, Connes's $g_\xi(z)=f(\xi(1+z)^{-1})r(1+z)^{-1}$ in (26) is exactly $\eta_\xi(z)$. With $\Lambda=\sqrt K$, the scalar $T_K$ is the integral sum on the right of his (30). In the number-field case $2\log'\Lambda=2\log\Lambda$: The radial band is measured on the **quotient** with $dt/t$ and norm-one mass one (§VII (29)–(30), Appendix II (1)–(8)). Connes's (31)–(33) have the same log, local, and tail decomposition. This matches the repaired scalar, not Masullo's operator. In particular Connes uses $\widehat P_\Lambda P_\Lambda U(H)$ and the transpose cutoff kernel (§VII (12)–(13), (20)–(22)); he does not identify the smooth Paley-Wiener product on Masullo p. 517 or justify cycling its factors.

For Masullo's measures, the raw expression (12.4.1.23)–(12.4.1.28), pp. 518–519, has a quotient factor $V_S$, removed by the normalization in Theorem 12.4.1.1, p. 514, and (D.2.1.7), p. 794. The local factors multiplying $d\mu_v$ in the normalized result are $1/2$ at a real place, $1/(2\pi)$ at a complex place, and $\log q_v/\mu_v(\mathcal O_v^\times)$ at a finite place. These follow from the masses of the fibers of the norm: Two signs at a real place, $2\,dA/|x|_{\rm Euc}^2$ and squared Euclidean norm at a complex place, and multiplicative translates of $\mathcal O_v^\times$ at a finite place. The product factor $c_S$ in the additive-to-multiplicative conversion (12.4.1.17) cancels in the normalized quotient volume calculation; it is not another local multiplier. The extracted complex domain in (12.4.1.26) appears as $F_v$ rather than $F_v^\times$; the repaired formula uses $F_v^\times$ and the local modulus throughout.

Connes writes $H(u^{-1})$ in the numerator. Inverting $u$ gives the form above, since $|1-u^{-1}|_v=|1-u|_v/|u|_v$ and multiplicative Haar is inversion invariant. No inversion symmetry of the test is assumed. For a nonvanishing test the appropriate normalization is the Fourier-log distribution, not just an off-identity density. In Connes §V (34)–(36) and §VII (34), the extension uses the logarithmically normalized Haar density. A change of character $\psi_v(t)\mapsto\psi_v(at)$ changes $D_v(f)$ by $\log|a|_v\,f(0)$, by a Fourier change of variables (compare Connes Appendix II, Lemma 3). Thus one must match characters before matching the point term. The trace-standard character used here need not be conductor-normalized at a ramified finite place (compare Masullo (B.4.4.14)–(B.4.4.15), pp. 622–623). The point term disappears for the tests used above.

## The cutoff order can erase the local term

Keep the annular position profile fixed, so $A_->0$, and take the one-sided Fourier profiles with $D_-\to\infty$. The smooth diagonal is the average of $T_{BD}-T_{AD}$. Both arguments now tend to infinity uniformly over the endpoint choices. The finite terms cancel, and the remainder is $O((A_-D_-)^{-1})$. The limit is $\Phi(1)\,\mathbb E[\log(B/A)]=\Phi(1)\int p(s)\,ds/s$. In particular, on the norm-one-vanishing test class, taking any position expansion **after** this Fourier limit still gives zero. The joint limit above need not give zero: For $F=\mathbb Q$ and $S=\{\infty\}$, take a nonzero nonnegative periodized test on $\mathbb R_{>0}$ supported in $(2,3)$. Its ordinary local integral is positive. Thus even at the scalar level a fixed annular position level cannot be used to infer the joint-cutoff value. This does not refute the fixed-position theorem (Theorem 11.2.1.4, p. 489) for the manuscript's different operator. The [positive-width level-first comparison](trace-diagonal-q.md) proves that the actual orthogonal projection in the real local model also gives zero in this order, and separates it from the global companion trace even on tests with both Mellin moments zero.

## Compare the measures on local and global tests

A separate group-measure fact fixes the normalization of the *candidate* Mellin characters. Suppose $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_{F,S})=1$ and let $U^S$ be the image in $C_F$ of the product of local unit groups outside $S$. The insertion map induces a norm-preserving topological group isomorphism $G/U\simeq C_F/U^S$: The valuations of any idele outside $S$ determine a fractional ideal of $\mathcal O_{F,S}$, and a generator of that ideal removes those valuations. After ignoring outside units the residual principal ambiguity is exactly $U$. The map is open because $G$ times the product of outside unit groups is open in the restricted product. For a test $\varphi$ invariant under $U^S$, this identifies its descended test with $\Phi$ (if $\widetilde\varphi$ is a lift of that test) and matches their single-place restrictions for $v\in S$.

Use the measure of mass one on both norm-one subgroups and $dt/t$ on the modulus. The pushforward of the normalized global measure to $C_F/U^S$ then agrees with $dm/V_S$ on $G/U$: $U^S$ is compact in norm one, the compact norm-one probability measures push forward to one another, and the radial factor is unchanged. Consequently, with $V_F=\mu_1(C_F^1)$, the **unnormalized** Mellin values from (12.5.1.3), p. 519, satisfy

$$M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{\pm1/2})=V_F\int_{G/U}\Phi\,r^{\pm1/2}\,dm/V_S.$$

Indeed, under the additional ramification hypothesis of Theorem 12.4.1.1, the **raw** measures also push forward as $q_*\mu=m$ for $q:C_F\to G/U$. Let $K=\prod_{v\notin S}\mathcal O_v^\times$. With trivial $S$-class group, $C_F$ is the quotient of the open subgroup $G\times K$ by the diagonal $U$, and the restricted idele Haar is the product of the $S$-idele Haar and the probability Haar on $K$. The latter assertion uses (A.3.2.2), (A.3.2.5), and (A.3.4.2), pp. 560–561: All unit masses outside $S$ are one. Inflate a compactly supported test on $G$ to $G\times K$ by making it constant on $K$. Its orbit sum over $U$ is the pullback along $q$ of the local orbit sum, and Weil integration with counting measure on $U$ equates the two integrals. Such orbit sums exhaust the compactly supported quotient tests by local lifting and partition of unity. Thus $V_F=V_S$, by the common radial factor $dt/t$. This is a measure comparison for pullbacks, not a reason to set either volume to one or to identify the cutoff diagonals. In particular, p. 520 integrates the global smoothed kernel against $\mu$ as written, not $\mu/V_F$.

An independent Yah text audit found the manuscript's own global moment interface in (5.5.2.1)–(5.5.2.3), pp. 280–281. The two moments descend through the global theta image, have weights $1$ and $|\cdot|_F$, and vanish on the nontrivial $C_F^1$-types. The half twist gives weights $|\cdot|_F^{-1/2}$ and $|\cdot|_F^{1/2}$ on the candidate quotient. The same audit confirmed that §12.3, p. 514, consists in the extraction of a heading and the footnote “Include section.” The trace in (12.4.1.1)–(12.4.1.3) is normalized on $J_{F,S}$ and has neither Mellin term. Theorem B.4.4.7, (B.4.4.16), p. 623, displays both polar terms of a scalar explicit formula, but divides them and the unrestricted zero sum by $V_F$. It does not identify either cutoff kernel or give the strip-restricted, raw-$\mu$ comparison asserted on p. 520. These are provisional source readings; they support the candidate characters, not their subtraction as a trace defect.

**Field test of the resulting formula.** For $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$, [translated radial tests](trace-window-q-sqrt-minus-five.md) separate the pole order on the finite local side from the norm-one volume on the raw Mellin side. The restricted window is too small in exponential growth to remove the discrepancy.

**Remaining global obligation.** The [conditional stratum split](trace-geometric.md#integrating-the-stratum-split-is-not-evaluating-its-parts) integrates (11.3.1.4) once there is a common integrable diagonal, but does not identify the resulting $S$ term with the local trace. Identify the measure, cutoff order, and operators responsible for the two Mellin subtractions before comparing that term with the repaired scalar. Separately, p. 520 invokes Theorem 11.3.2.1 (p. 495) for the omitted orbital traces, but its extracted hypothesis is support *strictly above* norm one. Vanishing of the omitted finite-place local functionals does not extend that orbital-trace theorem to the below-one component. Neither gap is closed by Connes's scalar argument.
