# The level-first local diagonal is not the global geometric diagonal

**Scope:** Project calculation for $F=\mathbb Q$, $S=\{\infty\}$, the trivial compact isotype, $a=1/2$, $s=2r$ with integer $r\ge1$, and the common full-measure set of widths $0<\alpha<1/2$ in the [theta density repair](trace-theta-density-q.md). It compares two iterated limits at the *same* width, even on tests with both Mellin moments zero. It does not evaluate orbital traces, the joint-cutoff limit of the orthogonal projectors, or the manuscript's full trace formula.

## The orders being compared

**Provisional readings of the digest-matched layout extraction.** The invariant local and global levels are distinct in (11.2.1.6)–(11.2.1.8), pp. 488–489. Theorem 11.2.1.4(2), (11.2.1.9)–(11.2.1.10), p. 489, holds the position cutoff fixed while taking a global level limit; it assumes the test is supported over norm at least one. The calculation (12.4.1.8)–(12.4.1.12), p. 517, instead uses a local Fourier multiplier in its cutoff kernel. On p. 520 the proof uses the local normalized diagonal of (12.4.1.1)–(12.4.1.3), p. 514, to evaluate the global $S$ piece after the two Mellin subtractions. The test and strip in (12.5.1.1)–(12.5.1.4), pp. 519–520, do not by themselves identify the orders of limits or those operators.

Use $x=\log|g|_{\mathbb Q}$, $H=L^2(w(x)\,dx)$ with $w=e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2)^r$, and normalized Haar $dx$ on the radial quotient. Let $A$ be convolution with a smooth compact log test $\phi$, $P$ the global spectral projection, and $M_p$ multiplication by a smooth compact log position cutoff. Use cutoffs $0\le p\le1$ exhausting the line. In the local model let $L_Y$ be the orthogonal projection onto the weighted closure of the half-twisted periodizations of even real-place Schwartz tests with Fourier support in $[-Y,Y]$, value zero at zero, and integral zero. This is the local invariant-level model of (11.2.1.6), using the [real-place description](trace-geometric.md#the-fourier-multiplier-is-not-the-invariant-paley-wiener-projection), not the global projection of (11.2.1.8) or the local raw Fourier multiplier. Compare $M_p A L_Y$ with $M_p A(1-P)$, taking the level limit *before* exhausting position.

## Local level exhaustion and the cutoff trace

Under the even additive-coordinate map $f(u)\mapsto 2e^{x/2}f(e^x)$, the local Hilbert norm is a fixed multiple of $L^2(w(\log|u|)\,du)$ on even functions. The weight is locally integrable at $u=0$ and of at most polynomial growth at infinity. Smooth even functions compactly supported away from zero are dense there by truncation and ordinary smooth approximation on compact sets away from zero. Starting with such an $f$, subtract a multiple of $L^{-1}\beta(u/L)$ with $\beta$ even, smooth, supported in $1<|u|<2$, and of integral one. This restores integral zero without changing $f(0)$. For a fixed $f$ the norm of the correction tends to zero as $L\to\infty$, since its squared norm is $O(L^{2\alpha-1}(1+\log L)^{2r})$. Thus smooth compact tests with both moments zero are already dense.

Given one of those tests, multiply its additive Fourier transform by smooth compactly supported cutoffs equal to one on growing centered intervals. This converges in the Schwartz topology, and preserves the zero of the transform at frequency zero. Correct the *value* of the inverse transform at zero by subtracting a scalar multiple of a fixed even bandlimited Schwartz function with integral zero and value one at zero (take its Fourier transform to be a smooth even bump away from zero of integral one). The scalar tends to zero by Schwartz convergence. The resulting Fourier support is compact and both moments are exact. Schwartz convergence implies weighted additive $L^2$ convergence by the two bounds on the weight. Hence $L_Y\to1$ strongly: Its ranges are increasing and have dense union. The global theta closure, in contrast, is a common jet kernel, not this local closure.

The operator $M_p A$ is trace class, with zero trace when $\phi(0)=0$. Indeed, its Haar kernel $p(x)\phi(x-y)$ is smooth and supported in a compact rectangle. On a larger interval that strictly contains both coordinate supports, expand in a periodic Fourier series in $y$. Repeated integration by parts gives rapidly decaying coefficient functions in the supremum norm. Each term contributes a rank-one operator, with summable trace norms: On the interval both $w$ and $w^{-1}$ are bounded, so the output coefficient and the input Fourier functional have the corresponding weighted bounds. The same expansion is uniformly absolutely convergent on the rectangle, and the sum of the rank-one traces is the integral of $p(x)\phi(0)$ against $dx$. No smoothness of $w$ at zero is needed.

Right composition of a trace-class operator with the bounded self-adjoint $L_Y$ converges in trace norm when $L_Y\to1$ strongly, by finite-rank approximation. Thus $\lim_{Y\to\infty}\operatorname{Tr}(M_p A L_Y)=0$ for every fixed $p$ if $\phi(0)=0$. Exhausting $p$ afterward still gives zero. This is a trace (equivalently the integrated trace-class Haar diagonal by the [shrinking-average repair](trace-spectral.md#from-the-weighted-kernel-to-the-haar-diagonal)) at positive width, not just an iterated-limit calculation with the raw multiplier. For the raw multiplier the same zero limit was obtained [directly at the scalar level](trace-scalar.md#the-cutoff-order-can-erase-the-local-term).

For the global companion in the same Hilbert space,

$$\operatorname{Tr}(M_p A(1-P))=-\operatorname{Tr}(M_p AP)\ \longrightarrow\ -\operatorname{Tr}(AP),$$

using the vanishing of the total cutoff trace, the [trace-class spectral transfer](trace-theta-density-q.md#transfer-and-formalization-boundary), and trace-norm convergence under exhausting position cutoffs. Any family of orthogonal global level projections converging strongly to $1-P$ gives this same iterated trace when inserted on the right of $M_p A$, by the same trace-ideal argument. This statement does not need a new identification of the levels of (11.2.1.8).

## A test separating the two limits with no polar terms

Take a nonnegative, nonzero $b\in C_c^\infty((1,2))$ and put $\phi_\delta(x)=b(x/\delta)$ for $0<\delta<\tfrac12\log 2$. It is supported above norm one, strictly below norm $2$, and vanishes on norm one. Write $m_\delta(z)=\delta\widehat b(\delta z)$ with $\widehat b(t)=\int b(x)e^{itx}\,dx$. On the given width the global spectral trace satisfies

$$\operatorname{Tr}(AP)\big|_{\phi=\phi_\delta}\ \longrightarrow\ -\frac12\int_1^2 \frac{b(u)}{u}\,du \qquad (\delta\downarrow0).$$

To see this, use the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula and the fixed-distance density estimate, for example [Rodgers, *Bootstrapped zero density estimates*, Theorems 1.1 and 2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.3080v2). The latter, applied at $\sigma=1/2+\alpha/2$ and reflected by the functional equation, gives $o(T)$ zeros with $|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\le T$ outside the open strip of width $\alpha$, with multiplicity. For any $N>2$, $m_\delta(z)=O_N(\delta(1+\delta|\operatorname{Re}z|)^{-N})$ throughout $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le1/2$, by integration by parts on the fixed support of $b$. Including the omitted zeros changes the sum by $o(1)$: Split heights at $1/\delta$, then into dyadic intervals above it, and use $o(T)$ uniformly for $T\ge1/\delta$. Replacing each complex zero coordinate $z$ by $\operatorname{Re}z$ changes the full sum by $O(\delta\log(2/\delta))$, using the same derivative bound with $\delta^2$ in place of $\delta$ and the total $O(T\log(2+T))$ count.

On positive ordinates integrate against $N(T)=\tfrac{T}{2\pi}(\log(T/(2\pi))-1)+O(\log(2+T))$, and use the reflected ordinates for the negative side. Partial summation against the error costs $O(\delta\log(2/\delta))$ by the derivative bound; the explicit main term tends to zero at $T=0$, and its derivative is integrable there. The real-ordinate sum is therefore, up to $o(1)$,

$$\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R} \delta\widehat b(\delta t)\log\!\frac{|t|}{2\pi}\,dt
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat b(v)\log|v|\,dv.$$

The constant removed by the change of scale multiplies $b(0)=0$. Off the origin the inverse Fourier transform of $\log|v|$ in this normalization is $-1/(2|u|)$. For a direct check, truncate the representation $\log|v|=\int_0^\infty(e^{-s}-e^{-|v|s})\,ds/s$. Its truncated absolute value is bounded by $|\log|v||$ (the integrand has constant sign for each $v\ne0$), so dominated convergence applies against $\widehat b$. The $e^{-s}$ term integrates to zero, and the other inverse transform is the Cauchy density $s/(\pi(s^2+u^2))$. Integrating this against $ds/s$ gives $1/(2|u|)$ on the support of $b$. This proves the nonzero limit without assuming the manuscript's geometric calculation.

To impose both polar moments *exactly*, choose two fixed nonnegative smooth nonzero log bumps $h_1,h_2$ with disjoint ordered supports in $(0,\log 2)$. The two moment vectors $(\int h_j e^{x/2}\,dx,\int h_j e^{-x/2}\,dx)$ are independent, since the ratio of the first to the second is an average of $e^x$ over each support. The moment vector of $\phi_\delta$ is $O(\delta)$. Subtract the appropriate linear combination of $h_1,h_2$; its coefficients are $O(\delta)$, so the two fixed, finite spectral traces it contributes change the limit by $O(\delta)$. For sufficiently small fixed $\delta$ the resulting admissible test has both Mellin corrections zero but a nonzero spectral trace. Its radial lift is in the trivial compact isotype, and no finite-place norm level lies in its support. This last observation kills the ordinary single-place local integrals, not a priori the orbital traces.

## Consequence for the missing identification

The local and global iterated Haar diagonals already differ on one-sided admissible tests at positive width, even after imposing the two Mellin moment conditions. The fixed-position theorem on p. 489 cannot therefore justify equating the level-first local replacement with the global geometric diagonal. This does not contradict that theorem for its stated global levels. The [joint-cutoff scalar](trace-scalar.md) need not vanish, and this comparison does not measure the difference between the joint-cutoff orthogonal projection and the joint-cutoff raw multiplier. Nor does it identify any orbital shell with a local operator. A remaining joint-limit bridge must use quantitative control in the cutoff coupling or an independent trace identity for the actual local and global operators, not just their strong limits at fixed position. The separate [joint-cutoff real-place calculation](trace-joint-local-q.md) quantifies one local moment defect for tests strictly above norm one; it does not identify a global diagonal.
