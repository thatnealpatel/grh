## Narrowing the bridge: Project reasoning, not a source reading

The finite-place extension and the kernel comparison are separate. Assume the normalized single-place map $i_v:F_v^\times\to C_F$ has $|i_v(a)|_F=|a|_v=q_v^{-\operatorname{ord}_v(a)}$, where $q_v=N(\mathfrak p_v)$. Take $T>1$, $q_v>T$ outside $S$, $|\operatorname{supp}\varphi|_F\subset (T^{-1},T)$, and $\varphi|_{C_F^1}=0$. For any $a\in F_v^\times$ at $v\notin S$:

- If $\operatorname{ord}_v(a)>0$, then $|i_v(a)|_F\le q_v^{-1}<T^{-1}$.
- If $\operatorname{ord}_v(a)<0$, then $|i_v(a)|_F\ge q_v>T$.
- If $\operatorname{ord}_v(a)=0$, then $i_v(a)\in C_F^1$.

Thus the whole local restriction $\varphi\circ i_v$ is zero, not just its values on uniformizer powers. If each omitted term is a linear local functional of that restriction, it is zero, so extending the finite-place sum from $S_f$ to all finite places adds only zero terms. This also kills the discrete sum in obligation 2 if the arguments are the indicated uniformizer powers; it does **not** show that the two-sided orbital trace is computed by that sum. Matching the normalizations, local functional, and hypotheses to the text and Mathlib remains to be done.

Write $P_S$ for the limit of the normalized local Paley-Wiener diagonal on p. 514 (if established for the needed cutoffs) and $D_S$ for the global $k_S$ diagonal contribution used on p. 520. Under the support argument above, and if the cited local asymptotic applies, $P_S=L_{\mathrm{loc}}$: The logarithmic term vanishes since $1\in C_F^1$. The target comparison is then

$$D_S+M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{1/2})+M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{-1/2})=P_S.$$

This isolates what the argument still needs. Vanishing on the norm-one subgroup removes the logarithmic term, not the Mellin corrections. Neither the normalization nor the cutoff identification, nor this comparison itself, has been proved here.

## Where the two Mellin corrections could come from

**Project calculation, not a derivation of the bridge.** In the pinned PDF extraction, §12.4.1 on pp. 515–517 uses a lift of the test as in (12.4.1.4) and the factor $|g|_{F,S}^{-1/2}$ as in (12.4.1.5)–(12.4.1.7). This suggests the following check on the sign and powers of the two corrections. Compare [Connes, *Trace Formula in Noncommutative Geometry and the Zeros of the Riemann Zeta Function*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811068), §III (6), (9)–(11) for the two characters and §VIII after (26) for the contribution from removing the two moment conditions in his own cutoff construction. His §VII Theorem 4 is an $S$-local asymptotic; it does not identify Masullo's global $k_S$.

Let $V$ be the full Schwartz-Bruhat space on $A_{F,S}$, with additive Haar measure $dx$, and let $L(f)=(f(0),\int f\,dx)$ and $K=\ker L$. The two functionals are independent: Take one test nonzero at zero and another supported away from zero with nonzero integral. If $U(g)f(x)=f(g^{-1}x)$ for an $S$-idele $g$, change of variables gives

$$L(U(g)f)=\operatorname{diag}(1,|g|_{F,S})L(f).$$

The two characters descend through the $S$-units. Thus, if the twisted convolution $A$ uses the lift of $\varphi$ weighted by $|g|_{F,S}^{-1/2}$, and the periodization pushes its integration to the same normalized multiplicative Haar measure as in the global Mellin transform, the induced operator on $V/K$ has the two diagonal entries $M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{-1/2})$ and $M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{1/2})$. These terms are subtracted when going from the ambient trace to the trace with the two moment conditions imposed. This explains the candidate sign and powers without assuming $\varphi(1)=0$. The independent Yah text review confirmed the powers and flagged the measure conversion: (12.4.1.1) on p. 514 uses the canonical $S$-local measure, while p. 519 writes $d\mu$ in the Mellin transform. Equations (D.2.1.6)–(D.2.1.7) on p. 794 distinguish those normalizations by the corresponding norm-one volumes.

For cutoffs, a possible interface is to choose a fixed section $s:\mathbb C^2\to V$ of $L$ and use the compression to $K$ given by $p=1-sL$. In a realization where the cutoff operator $B$ and its compression have traces with the usual finite-rank trace property,

$$\operatorname{Tr}_V B-\operatorname{Tr}_K (p B|_K)=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb C^2}(L B s).$$

This follows by taking the diagonal blocks for $V=K\oplus s(\mathbb C^2)$; the off-diagonal blocks are finite rank with zero trace. It does not require $B(K)\subset K$. If $L B s$ tends to $L A s$ on the two fixed lifts, the right side tends to the two Mellin values. This leaves the comparison of the *infinite-dimensional* compressed traces to be proved, not just convergence on those lifts.

The extraction gives two reasons not to identify those traces by notation. First, pp. 515–517 use a polar-free $\mathcal S_{00}(A_{F,S})$ source, not $V$ or $K$. The Archimedean flatness conditions in (5.3.3.10) on p. 252 and the tensor factors in (5.3.4.1) on p. 259 do not express the two moments. Already for $F=\mathbb Q$ and $S=\{\infty\}$, a bump supported away from zero can be polar-free with nonzero integral; an odd test $x e^{-x^2}$ lies in $K$ but is not flat at zero. This comparison concerns the source spaces, not their images after periodization. Second, §12.4.1 uses a position cutoff and a Fourier cutoff, while Definition 9.3.3.1 on p. 427 and the §12.5 introduction on p. 519 refer to annular cutoffs. A raw annular multiplication on the adelic source would kill the evaluation-at-zero row of $L B s$ at every level. It cannot be substituted into this quotient calculation without a separate comparison to the adelic cutoffs. One must show that the local value is the ambient trace in this calculation and that its compressed counterpart gives the global $S$-piece, with the correct pushforward of Haar measure and an error tending to zero. Neither identification is obtained here.
