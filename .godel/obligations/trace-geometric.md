## Integrating the stratum split is not evaluating its parts

**Provisional source reading.** The indicators in §11.3 (pp. 492–494) impose the unit condition at progressively more finite places, so the strata in that construction are *decreasing*. Proposition 12.2.2.4 (pp. 512–514) concerns the complementary tail of an *increasing* exhaustion and assumes rank-one steps. A block that adds the places over one rational prime can have more than one valuation coordinate. Do not cite that proposition for the primary stratification without changing the filtration or proving the relevant limit directly. The direct limit below does not need a rank condition.

**Conditional project calculation.** Suppose the common Haar diagonal $d$ is already defined and in $L^1(C_F,\mu)$. Put $G=\mathbb A_F^\times$, $H=F^\times$, and let $G_S$ be the open subgroup of ideles that are units outside $S$. With $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_{F,S})=1$, $G_S$ surjects onto $G/H$ by removing outside valuations with a principal idele. Use a measurable set of representatives inside $G_S$. For a nonnegative indentation $a$ as in Lemma 12.2.1.1 (pp. 504–505), write $q=[x]$ and set

$$w(q,h)=\sum_{j\in H}a(j^{-1}x)a(j^{-1}h^{-1}x),\qquad h\in H.$$

This is the weight from (12.2.2.1) on the pair $(x,h^{-1}x)$. It has $\sum_{h\in H}w(q,h)=1$ by the two applications of the orbit-sum identity (12.2.1.2). The lifted diagonal integrand of (12.2.2.7) is $d(q)w(q,h)$, with total absolute integral $\|d\|_{L^1(\mu)}$ using the quotient Haar normalization and counting measure on $H$.

Now exhaust the finite places outside $S$ by finite blocks and let $\alpha_k$ require units at the first $k$ blocks ($\alpha_0=1$). The limit is $\alpha_\infty=\mathbf 1_{G_S}$ and pointwise

$$1=\mathbf 1_{G_S}+\sum_{k\ge0}(\alpha_k-\alpha_{k+1}).$$

Evaluate at $h^{-1}x$, multiply by the lifted diagonal, and integrate. The disjoint indicator summands are nonnegative and sum to one, so all integrated pieces are absolutely summable with sum of absolute values at most $\|d\|_1$. If one uses the sign $\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha_k$ of (11.3.1.4), the signed pieces must instead be *subtracted* from the $S$ piece in this convention. The sign is immaterial if those pieces vanish, but the telescoping does not prove their vanishing. If cutoff diagonals $d_\rho$ converge in $L^1(\mu)$ to $d$, the sum of absolute changes in these integrated pieces is at most $\|d_\rho-d\|_1$ for the same indentation and representatives. No such convergence is inferred here.

This is a split of an already identified diagonal, not an evaluation by the periodic orbital kernels. In fact, the $S$ part weights $d(q)$ by $\sum_{h\in H\cap G_S}w(q,h)$. Group the numbers $a(j^{-1}x)$ by cosets in $H/(H\cap G_S)$: The weight is the sum of the squares of the coset masses. For an indentation on $G_S$ relative to $H\cap G_S$, extension by zero gives an indentation on $G$, because $G_S/(H\cap G_S)\simeq G/H$ and $G_S$ is clopen. Its entire orbit mass is in one coset, giving $S$ weight one. Averaging it with a translate by a principal idele outside $G_S$ gives two distinct cosets of mass $1/2$, hence $S$ weight $1/2$. Thus a general indentation can change the partition without changing the total. Choosing a lift with no orbital tail by construction does not show that its $S$ part is the Paley–Wiener trace or explains the Mellin corrections.

**Specific orbital interface to check.** The extracted (11.3.2.2), p. 495, appears to use the product of two indicators of the exact section $\tau(\mathbb R_{>0})$, averaged over $\xi\in F^\times$ and $g\in E_F^1$, with the latter fundamental domain as in (A.2.3.14), p. 549. If interpreted literally as integration with norm-one Haar on this domain, even the first indicator has integral zero for each $\xi,x$: $\xi g x$ must equal $\tau(|x|_F)$, so at most one $g$ contributes. Norm-one Haar is nonatomic, since the group contains infinite compact local unit subgroups. The countable sum is then also zero, not a normalized lift. This is a reading to verify on the rendered page, not a verdict on the intended orbital formula. For a text-based next step, use the periodic model of Theorem 7.4.2.1 at a single rational prime for $F=\mathbb Q$ and look for a genuine lift and cutoff trace that works for tests on both sides of norm one. A periodic scalar alone does not identify that trace with the stratum piece above.

## Narrowing the bridge: Project reasoning, not a source reading

The finite-place extension and the kernel comparison are separate. Assume the normalized single-place map $i_v:F_v^\times\to C_F$ has $|i_v(a)|_F=|a|_v=q_v^{-\operatorname{ord}_v(a)}$, where $q_v=N(\mathfrak p_v)$. Take $T>1$, $q_v>T$ outside $S$, $|\operatorname{supp}\varphi|_F\subset (T^{-1},T)$, and $\varphi|_{C_F^1}=0$. For any $a\in F_v^\times$ at $v\notin S$:

- If $\operatorname{ord}_v(a)>0$, then $|i_v(a)|_F\le q_v^{-1}<T^{-1}$.
- If $\operatorname{ord}_v(a)<0$, then $|i_v(a)|_F\ge q_v>T$.
- If $\operatorname{ord}_v(a)=0$, then $i_v(a)\in C_F^1$.

Thus the whole local restriction $\varphi\circ i_v$ is zero, not just its values on uniformizer powers. If each omitted term is a linear local functional of that restriction, it is zero, so extending the finite-place sum from $S_f$ to all finite places adds only zero terms. This also kills the discrete sum in obligation 2 if the arguments are the indicated uniformizer powers; it does **not** show that the two-sided orbital trace is computed by that sum. Matching the normalizations, local functional, and hypotheses to the text and Mathlib remains to be done.

Write $P_S$ for the limit of the normalized local Paley-Wiener diagonal on p. 514 (if established for the needed cutoffs) and $D_S$ for the global $k_S$ diagonal contribution used on p. 520. Under the support argument above, and if the cited local asymptotic applies, $P_S=L_{\mathrm{loc}}$: The logarithmic term vanishes since $1\in C_F^1$. The target comparison is then

$$D_S+M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{1/2})+M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{-1/2})=P_S.$$

This isolates what the argument still needs. Vanishing on the norm-one subgroup removes the logarithmic term, not the Mellin corrections. Neither the normalization nor the cutoff identification, nor this comparison itself, has been proved here.

## Where the two Mellin corrections could come from

**Project calculation, not a derivation of the bridge.** In the pinned PDF extraction, §12.4.1 on pp. 515–517 uses a lift of the test as in (12.4.1.4) and the factor $|g|_{F,S}^{-1/2}$ as in (12.4.1.5)–(12.4.1.7). This suggests the following check on the sign and powers of the two corrections. Compare [Connes, *Trace Formula in Noncommutative Geometry and the Zeros of the Riemann Zeta Function*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811068), §III (6), (9)–(11) for the two characters and §VIII after (26) for the contribution from removing the two moment conditions in his own cutoff construction. His §VII Theorem 4 is an $S$-local asymptotic; it does not identify Masullo's global $k_S$.

Let $V$ be the full Schwartz-Bruhat space on $A_{F,S}$, with additive Haar measure $dx$, and let $L(f)=(f(0),\int f\,dx)$ and $K=\ker L$. The two functionals are independent: Take one test nonzero at zero and another supported away from zero with nonzero integral. If $U(g)f(x)=f(g^{-1}x)$ for an $S$-idele $g$, change of variables gives

$$L(U(g)f)=\operatorname{diag}(1,|g|_{F,S})L(f).$$

The two characters descend through the $S$-units. Thus, if the twisted convolution $A$ uses the lift of $\varphi$ weighted by $|g|_{F,S}^{-1/2}$, and the periodization pushes its integration to the same normalized multiplicative Haar measure as in the global Mellin transform, the induced operator on $V/K$ has the two diagonal entries $M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{-1/2})$ and $M(\varphi)(|\cdot|_F^{1/2})$. These terms are subtracted when going from the ambient trace to the trace with the two moment conditions imposed. This explains the candidate sign and powers without assuming $\varphi(1)=0$. The independent Yah text review confirmed the powers and flagged the measure conversion: (12.4.1.1) on p. 514 uses the canonical $S$-local measure, while p. 519 writes $d\mu$ in the Mellin transform. Equations (D.2.1.6)–(D.2.1.7) on p. 794 distinguish those normalizations by the corresponding norm-one volumes.

For cutoffs, a possible interface is to choose a fixed section $s:\mathbb C^2\to V$ of $L$ and use the compression to $K$ given by $p=1-sL$. In a realization where the cutoff operator $B$ and its compression have traces with the usual finite-rank trace property,

$$\operatorname{Tr}_V B-\operatorname{Tr}_K (p B|_K)=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb C^2}(L B s).$$

This follows by taking the diagonal blocks for $V=K\oplus s(\mathbb C^2)$; the off-diagonal blocks are finite rank with zero trace. It does not require $B(K)\subset K$. If $L B s$ tends to $L A s$ on the two fixed lifts, the right side tends to the two Mellin values. This leaves the comparison of the *infinite-dimensional* compressed traces to be proved, not just convergence on those lifts.

The extraction gives two reasons not to identify those traces by notation. First, pp. 515–517 use a polar-free $\mathcal S_{00}(A_{F,S})$ source, not $V$ or $K$. The Archimedean flatness conditions in (5.3.3.10) on p. 252 and the tensor factors in (5.3.4.1) on p. 259 do not express the two moments. Already for $F=\mathbb Q$ and $S=\{\infty\}$, a bump supported away from zero can be polar-free with nonzero integral; an odd test $x e^{-x^2}$ lies in $K$ but is not flat at zero. This comparison concerns the source spaces, not their images after periodization. Second, §12.4.1 uses a position cutoff and a Fourier cutoff, while Definition 9.3.3.1 on p. 427 and the §12.5 introduction on p. 519 refer to annular cutoffs. A raw annular multiplication on the adelic source would kill the evaluation-at-zero row of $L B s$ at every level. It cannot be substituted into this quotient calculation without a separate comparison to the adelic cutoffs. One must show that the local value is the ambient trace in this calculation and that its compressed counterpart gives the global $S$-piece, with the correct pushforward of Haar measure and an error tending to zero. Neither identification is obtained here.

See also the [Chapter 5 citation and range check](trace-polar-free-q.md): The cited flat-lift result does not provide a nuclear factorization, and the one-sided upper cutoff can leave a genuine local Paley–Wiener input nonflat after convolution. This is independent of the comparison with the raw Fourier multiplier.

## Independent lower cutoff: Estimate for the sharp logarithmic integrals

**Provisional source reading.** An independent Yah text review matched the pinned digest and checked the layout and raw extractions. Page 514 n. 2 asks to change Definition 9.3.3.1 and study a smoothening of $\mathbf 1_{[1/\lambda'',\lambda]}$, with $\lambda''$ not tied to $\lambda$. It does not give a limit estimate or prescribe the relative rate of $\lambda'$ and $\lambda''$. The note cites (12.4.1.23), the volume integral; the explicit logarithm is in (12.4.1.24), both on p. 518. The text works per $S$-unit through that volume step, then on p. 519 asserts an asymptotic for the **sum** of the integrals. Separately, the passage from (12.4.1.12) on p. 517 to (12.4.1.22) on p. 518 replaces displayed smooth weights by sharp ranges without a local error estimate. The argument below compares only the resulting sharp logarithmic integrals, not that replacement.

**Project calculation.** Write $r(w)=|w|_{F,S}$, $dw=d\nu_S(w)$, and $V_S=\mu_{S,1}(J^1_{F,S})$. Take the $x$-volume rule of (12.4.1.24) as the interface: A sharp band $a\le r(x)\le b$ on the $S$-idele class group (or its representatives with the corresponding measure) has volume $V_S\log(b/a)$. The quotient measure conventions in Definition A.3.4.1(3), p. 561, use $dt/t$. There is a domain issue in the extracted text: (12.4.1.22)–(12.4.1.23) write $x\in E_{S,0}$, but (A.2.4.5) and Proposition A.2.4.4, pp. 554–555, place $E_{S,0}$ in norm one. Literally it does not sweep out a band. A radial extension $\tau(\mathbb R_{>0})E_{S,0}$ is used elsewhere on p. 563, before (A.4.1.7). That gives a candidate domain for the volume rule, not a justification of the preceding trace integral, and the rule is not a volume formula on unquotiented $S$-ideles.

Assume $1/\lambda''\le\lambda$. For $0<r(w)\le\lambda\lambda'$, the new lower endpoint in (12.4.1.23) is $\max\{r(w)/\lambda',1/\lambda''\}$. The new bracket is therefore $\log\lambda-\log\max\{r(w)/\lambda',1/\lambda''\}$, not a substitution for just $\log r(w)$ in the old bracket. Subtracting the new integral from the old gives, per unit $\xi$, the error $V_S I_\xi(t)$, where

$$
t=\frac{\lambda'}{\lambda''},\qquad
I_\xi(t)=\int_{A_{F,S}}\widehat{\eta_\xi}(w)\,\log_+\!\frac{t}{r(w)}\,dw,
\qquad \log_+(s)=\max(0,\log s).
$$

Indeed, the difference is supported on $r(w)<t\le\lambda\lambda'$. The coordinate-zero sets have additive measure zero. The relative limit $t\to0$ is sufficient; merely taking both parameters to infinity does not imply it. For instance, let $F=\mathbb Q$ and $S=\{\infty\}$, and choose a nonzero, nonnegative smooth lift $\widetilde\varphi$ supported in $(2,3)$. Its periodization vanishes on norm one. For each of the two units $\xi=\pm 1$, $\eta_\xi$ from (12.4.1.20) is nonnegative and nonzero. The real part of its Fourier transform is positive near zero since its value there is the integral of $\eta_\xi$. Thus a sufficiently small **fixed** $t>0$ gives $\operatorname{Re}\sum_\xi I_\xi(t)>0$, unchanged along $\lambda'=t\lambda''\to\infty$.

**Integrability and the unit sum.** For $0<\delta<1$, $\log_+(t/r)\le (t/r)^\delta/\delta$. The moment $\int |\widehat{\eta_\xi}(w)|r(w)^{-\delta}\,dw$ is finite: $r$ is a product of local moduli, each local inverse power is integrable near zero (local balls have volume $O(s)$ at modulus radius $s$), and the Fourier transform is Schwartz-Bruhat. This gives a per-unit bound but not a summable one. In fact, put $h(g)=|g|_{F,S}^{-1/2}\widetilde\varphi(g^{-1})$ on the $S$-ideles and extend it by zero. The support is compact inside the open group, so $h$ is Schwartz-Bruhat on $A_{F,S}$. Equation (12.4.1.20) gives $\eta_\xi(z)=h(\xi^{-1}(1+z))$. The product formula gives $r(\xi)=1$, so in the plus-sign Fourier convention of (12.4.1.21),

$$\widehat{\eta_\xi}(w)=\psi_{S,\mathrm{std}}(-w)\widehat h(\xi w).$$

The absolute moments are therefore identical for all units. For infinitely many units and a nonzero lift, their sum diverges. Instead, retain the character when changing variables to $y=\xi w$ and estimate the **signed integrals**. With $G_t(y)=\widehat h(y)\log_+(t/r(y))$ and $H_\xi=\max_{v\in S}|\xi_v^{-1}|_v\ge1$, the bound is

$$|I_\xi(t)|\le C t^{1/4}H_\xi^{-1/4},\qquad \sum_\xi |I_\xi(t)|\le C' t^{1/4},$$

where the constants do not depend on the cutoffs. The argument is as follows.

1. The change of variables gives $I_\xi(t)=\int\psi_{S,\mathrm{std}}(-\xi^{-1}y)G_t(y)\,dy$. Choose $v$ attaining $H_\xi$ and a fixed $c_v$ with $\psi_v(c_v)\ne1$, where $\psi_v$ is the nontrivial local character in (A.3.1.9), p. 559. Translation in this coordinate by $b=c_v\xi_v$ multiplies the character by a fixed phase, so $|I_\xi(t)|\le |1-\psi_v(c_v)|^{-1}\|G_t(\cdot+b)-G_t\|_1$. For $H_\xi$ above $\max(1,\max_v|c_v|_v)$ we have $|b|_v\le1$. The finitely many units below that threshold can use the per-unit bound with $\delta=1/4$.
2. To bound the translation difference, let $x=y_v$, $a=t/\prod_{u\ne v}|y_u|_u$, and $f_a(x)=\log_+(a/|x|_v)$. The values of $\widehat h$ and its translates by $|b|_v\le1$ admit a bounded product envelope, rapidly decreasing at Archimedean places and compactly supported at finite places. The difference of the two $\widehat h$ values has such a bound with an extra factor $|b|_v^{1/4}$: Use the mean value estimate at Archimedean places and uniform local constancy at finite places (for shifts outside the local constancy ball, $|b|_v$ is bounded below). The term with this difference times $f_a$ is then bounded in $L^1$ by $C t^{1/4}|b|_v^{1/4}$ by the inverse-norm moment.
3. For the other term it suffices to integrate $|f_a(x+b)-f_a(x)|$ against the local envelope. In the Archimedean case put $|x|_v=|x|^d$, with $|\cdot|$ Euclidean and $d=1$ or $2$. On $|x|\le2|b|$, bound both logarithms by their inverse powers of order $1/4$. The local integral is $O(a^{1/4}|b|_v^{3/4})$ by scaling, including the translated singularity. Outside this ball, $|x+b|$ and $|x|$ are comparable. Clipping the logarithm is 1-Lipschitz, so the difference is $O(|b|/|x|)$, and if it is nonzero then $|x|^d\lesssim a$. Consequently the difference is bounded by $C a^{1/4}|b|^{1/2}|x|^{-1/2-d/4}$. This is integrable against the envelope, since $1/2+d/4<d$, and $|b|^{1/2}\le |b|_v^{1/4}$. At a finite place, the difference is zero outside $|x|_v\le |b|_v$ by the ultrametric inequality. Inside the ball the inverse-power estimate and scaling again give $O(a^{1/4}|b|_v^{3/4})$. The resulting local bound is $O(a^{1/4}|b|_v^{1/4})$ in either case. Integrating in the other coordinates uses the same inverse-norm moment and proves $\|G_t(\cdot+b)-G_t\|_1\le C t^{1/4}|b|_v^{1/4}$.
4. The $S$-unit theorem (compare (A.2.4.7)–(A.2.4.8), p. 555) gives a lattice of local log norms in the sum-zero hyperplane, with finite kernel. When $H_\xi\le R$, all the coordinates lie between $-(|S|-1)\log R$ and $\log R$. The count is $O((1+\log R)^{|S|-1})$ for $R\ge1$, so $\sum_\xi H_\xi^{-1/4}<\infty$ by dyadic shells. This sums the integrated errors, without interchanging the absolute unit sum and the $w$-integral.

This establishes vanishing of the **summed sharp lower-endpoint error** for $\lambda'/\lambda''\to0$, under the sharp volume interface above. The following comparison keeps the smooth profiles but still does not identify the traces.

As used before (A.4.1.7), p. 563, the radial extension of the norm-one fundamental domain is a fundamental domain in $G$: Write $x=\tau(r(x))e$ with $e\in G^1$, and use $U\subset G^1$ to choose the representative of $e$. With $\mu_S$ on $G$ and the norm-one quotient volume $V_S$ as above, Definition A.3.4.1(3), p. 561, gives $\int_{E^*} F(r(x))\,d\mu_S(x)=V_S\int_0^\infty F(s)\,ds/s$ for radial integrands, by the product and counting-measure quotient formulas.

Consider the per-unit integral suggested by (12.4.1.12)–(12.4.1.21), but with $x\in E^*$ and weights $p(r(x))q(r(u))$ retained. Using $w=xu$ and $d\mu_S(x)=c_S d\nu_S(x)/r(x)$ from the measure relation preceding (12.4.1.17), the integral (including $c_S$) becomes

$$
V_S\int_{A_{F,S}}\widehat{\eta_\xi}(w)\,W(r(w))\,d\nu_S(w),\qquad
W(r)=\int_0^\infty p(s)q(r/s)\,\frac{ds}{s}.
$$

This uses the norm-one quotient volume only **after** the change of variables, when the remaining $x$-integrand is radial. It does not interpret a non-invariant per-unit kernel as a function on $G/U$. For the profiles below, $0\le W(r)\le\log_+(B_+D_+/r)$. Apply Tonelli to the absolute per-unit integrand after the change of variables: The bound by $\log_+(B_+D_+/r)$ and the inverse-norm moment show it is integrable, so Fubini applies to the complex integrand. The coordinate-zero sets are null.

**Average the sharp bands.** Choose a position profile increasing from $0$ to $1$ on $[A_-,A_+]$ and decreasing from $1$ to $0$ on $[B_-,B_+]$, with $0<A_-\le A_+\le B_-\le B_+$. Choose a one-sided Fourier profile decreasing from $1$ to $0$ on $[D_-,D_+]$, with $0<D_-\le D_+$. These can be chosen smooth. They are averages of $\mathbf 1_{[A,B]}(s)$ and $\mathbf 1_{(0,D]}(s)$, respectively, for independent endpoint choices $A,B,D$ in those intervals: Use the increasing lower edge as a distribution function and the decreasing upper edges as survival functions. Endpoints have no effect on the radial integral. Put $L_K(r)=\log_+(K/r)$. For fixed endpoints the radial weight is

$$
\int_A^B \mathbf 1_{\{r/s\le D\}}\,\frac{ds}{s}=L_{BD}(r)-L_{AD}(r).
$$

Thus $W$ is exactly the average of these differences. Removing the position lower edge while keeping both upper profiles changes the normalized sum of signed integrals by at most $C(A_+D_+)^{1/4}$, by the **uniform** signed estimate above applied to $AD$. In particular, with $D_+=\lambda'$, a transition contained in $[1/\lambda'',2/\lambda'']$ gives the desired bound when $\lambda'/\lambda''\to0$. Just requiring the lower transition's absolute width to tend to zero does not give this uniformity.

**Upper edges.** An upper transition can also be compared to its sharp endpoint, without summing absolute Fourier integrands over the units. For $k>0$ define

$$
J_\xi(k)=\int_{A_{F,S}}\psi_{S,\mathrm{std}}(-\xi^{-1}y)\widehat h(y)\,\mathbf 1_{\{r(y)<k\}}\,d\nu_S(y).
$$

Uniformly in $k$, $|J_\xi(k)|\le C H_\xi^{-1/2}$. Use the same coordinate and phase shift $b=c_v\xi_v$ as in the lower-edge bound. The $L^1$ change of the translated $\widehat h$ is $O(|b|_v^{1/2})$ for $|b|_v\le1$, by the mean value estimate at real and complex places (the complex modulus is squared), and local constancy at finite places. For the indicator term, fix the other coordinates off their zeros. In an Archimedean coordinate the indicator is that of a Euclidean ball with a radius depending on those coordinates and $k$. Its change is supported in a radial shell of thickness at most $2|b|_{\rm Euc}$. Against the rapidly decreasing local envelope for $\widehat h$ (or its small translates), the shell mass is $O(|b|_{\rm Euc})$ uniformly in the radius: The radial density times the envelope is bounded. At a finite place the modulus is unchanged outside $|y_v|_v\le |b|_v$, a ball of mass $O(|b|_v)$. Integrate the remaining product envelopes. The translation bound is uniform in $k$, and the finitely many small $H_\xi$ are covered by $\|\widehat h\|_1$. The $S$-unit count above sums $H_\xi^{-1/2}$.

Since $L_{K_2}-L_{K_1}=\int_{K_1}^{K_2}\mathbf 1_{\{r<k\}}\,dk/k$ for $K_1\le K_2$, the upper-edge estimate in the normalized sum is $O(\log(B_+/B_-)+\log(D_+/D_-))$, uniformly over endpoints. Together the three estimates compare the weighted scalar sum to the one with weight $L_{B_+ D_+}$, with error

$$
O\!\left((A_+D_+)^{1/4}+\log(B_+/B_-)+\log(D_+/D_-)\right).
$$

At each fixed cutoff level the averages commute with the sum of **signed per-unit integrals**: The earlier $L_K$ bound gives a summable majorant uniform in endpoints with $K\le B_+D_+$. No absolute unit sum inside the integral over $w$ is used. The upper error tends to zero for upper transition ratios tending to $1$.

If the Fourier profile is annular too, with a lower endpoint $C$ averaged in $[C_-,C_+]\subset(0,D_-]$, the sharp weight instead is $L_{BD}-L_{AD}-L_{BC}+L_{AC}$. The same argument adds $O((B_+C_+)^{1/4})$ to the sufficient bound. Neither one-sided Fourier smoothing nor both small cross-products follow from using the unmodified annular definition. For example, with nominal reciprocal lower endpoints $A=1/B$, $C=1/D$, the two cross-products $AD$ and $BC$ multiply to one.

This compares weighted and sharp integrals on a repaired domain only. The next section identifies these scalar integrals with a diagonal of a particular quotient operator at fixed cutoffs.

The operator spaces also need checking before applying that recipe. A genuine upper-only position multiplier equal to $1$ near norm zero does not send arbitrary Schwartz functions to $\mathcal S_{00}$ as declared in Step II on p. 516: Over $\mathbb R$, take a Schwartz function nonzero at zero and use (5.3.3.10), p. 252. An annular position profile avoids this counterexample, but by itself gives no identification with the asserted Paley-Wiener operator. In particular, a product-norm upper bound does not imply the compact Fourier support imposed on the generators in (7.3.2.13)–(7.3.2.14), p. 355, when $|S|>1$. The local diagonal identification, the use of the sharp asymptotic on p. 519, and the bridge to the global $S$ contribution and its Mellin corrections remain separate from the scalar comparison.

## A fixed-cutoff quotient kernel and its Haar diagonal

**Provisional source reading.** In Proposition 12.2.2.2, pp. 507–509, (12.2.2.3)–(12.2.2.7), the input is a locally integrable kernel on the quotient, and the lift multiplies its pullback at $(x,y)$ by the sum $\sum_h u_0(h^{-1}x)u_0(h^{-1}y)$ formed from an $H$-indentation $u_0$. The proposition unfolds a sufficiently regular, absolutely integrable Haar diagonal; it does not construct a quotient operator from an additive adelic one. Equation (12.2.2.8) concerns compatibility with multiplication by a compactly supported quotient kernel factor, not operator descent. On pp. 515–517 the proof asserts a factorization and appeals to this recipe, but (12.4.1.4) only periodizes the lift of the *test* and (12.4.1.8)–(12.4.1.12) calculate with the upstairs, additive-measure kernel. Those lines do not give the matching quotient kernel or justify the interchange of its unit sum and diagonal averaging. Page 515 writes the convolution with $r(g^{-1}x)^{1/2}=r(x)^{1/2}r(g)^{-1/2}$, but (12.4.1.5)–(12.4.1.7) retain only $r(g)^{-1/2}$ without showing a change to unweighted coordinates. The periodization (2.3.1.2), pp. 99–100, is unweighted; the weight in (11.2.1.6), p. 488, yields $r(x)^{1/2}\sum_{\xi\in U} f(\xi x)$.

The earlier Theorem 5.2.4.1(1), p. 239, (5.2.4.2), does intertwine the additive Fourier transform with a quotient Fourier transform through $\theta$-periodization on the Schwartz source. Together with termwise descent of radial multiplication (when it maps the stated source to itself), this suggests a manuscript-based descent route. It does not by itself identify that composite on the specific weighted Paley-Wiener level or justify the diagonal calculation. These readings were independently checked by Yah against the digest-matched extraction.

The $r(x)^{1/2}$ factor alone cannot make the displayed lifted convolution on p. 515 quotient invariant. For $F=\mathbb Q$, $S=\{\infty\}$, choose a nonnegative lift supported in $(2,3)$ and a nonnegative smooth $f$ supported in $(1/3,1/2)$, both positive at a reciprocal pair. Such an $f$ is in $\mathcal S_{00}(\mathbb R)$ by (5.3.3.10). The displayed integral is positive at $x=1$ and zero at $x=-1$, although $-1\in U$. This is a counterexample to taking values in invariant functions *before* periodization, not to the possibility of a quotient operator after periodization.

**Project repair, not the manuscript's trace identification.** Use $G$, $U$, $E^*$, $r$, $c_S$, and $V_S$ as above. Keep the cutoffs fixed. Take $p$ continuous with compact radial support in $(0,\infty)$ and $q$ a bounded monotone upper profile as above, or an average of sharp annuli. Take the lift $\widetilde\varphi$ smooth and compactly supported in $G$. Use additive Haar $\nu_S$ self-dual for $\psi=\psi_{S,\mathrm{std}}$ and quotient Haar $m$ given by $\mu_S$ and counting measure on $U$, **before** division by $V_S$. Define $B$ by the order in (12.4.1.8), p. 517: Apply the Fourier multiplier with symbol $q(r(\cdot))$, then the twisted convolution with the lift, then multiply by $p(r(\cdot))$. The following construction starts on compactly supported Bruhat-smooth functions on $G$, extended by zero. It does not require a Schwartz kernel on the entire additive adele space, nor compact support of the Fourier multiplier in the individual coordinates.

**Compute before periodizing.** Let $b(x,y)$ be the kernel density against additive input measure, with output at $x$. Use the plus-sign Fourier transform of (12.4.1.21), and denote the transform of $h$ from the lower-edge argument by $\widehat h$. On $G\times G$ one obtains

$$
b(x,y)=\frac{c_S p(r(x))}{r(x)}
\int_{A_{F,S}} \widehat h(w)\,q\!\left(\frac{r(w)}{r(x)}\right)
\psi(-w y/x)\,d\nu_S(w).
$$

Indeed, substitute $z=g^{-1}$ in the twisted convolution. Its weight against $d\nu_S(z)$ is $c_Sh(z)$, by inversion invariance of $\mu_S$ and $d\mu_S=c_S\,d\nu_S/r$. For compactly supported Bruhat-smooth input $f$, the Fourier inversion integral after the multiplier is absolute since $q$ is bounded and $\widehat f$ is integrable. The lifted convolution therefore gives the factor $c_S\widehat h(-xu)$. After setting $w=-xu$, the integral with $\widehat h(w)$ is also absolutely integrable. Expanding the transform of $f$ and interchanging this integral with the input integral is justified by $\widehat h,f\in L^1$. To check joint continuity even for sharp $q$, use the expression before the last substitution: The factor $q(r(u))$ does not depend on $x,y$, and the dilates $\widehat h(-xu)$ for $x$ in a compact subset of $G$ have a common integrable envelope. Dominated convergence applies with $p$ continuous. The formula also shows $b(\xi x,\xi y)=b(x,y)$ for $\xi\in U$: It needs simultaneous equivariance, not invariance in the input alone.

**Sum on the quotient.** Put $k(x,y)=r(y)b(x,y)/c_S$, the density against $d\mu_S(y)$. The separately invariant kernel on $G/U$ is

$$K([x],[y])=\sum_{\xi\in U}k(x,\xi y).$$

This converges absolutely and locally uniformly on $G\times G$. To see this, apply the upper-edge proof's translation estimate for $\widehat h(w)\mathbf 1_{r(w)<k_0}$ to the character with coefficient $a=\xi y/x$ instead of $\xi^{-1}$. In a coordinate where $|a_v|_v$ is maximal, use the shift $c_v/a_v$ with the same fixed nontrivial phase. For $x,y$ in compact subsets of $G$, $\max_v|a_v|_v$ is bounded below by a positive constant times $\max_v|\xi_v|_v$. The estimate is uniform in the threshold $k_0$, so averaging thresholds (subtracting two for a band) gives decay $O((\max_v|\xi_v|_v)^{-1/2})$ with a constant on each such compact pair. Either convention for including a threshold works in the translation estimate, including when finite-place norm levels have positive measure. The $S$-unit log lattice count makes this bound summable. Consequently the kernel is continuous and the sum descends in both variables by reindexing. No sum of absolute values of the Fourier integrands is used.

For the ordinary periodization $\Pi f([x])=\sum_{\xi\in U}f(\xi x)$ (locally a finite sum on $G$ for compactly supported $f$), the integral operator with kernel $K$ acts by $K\Pi f=\Pi Bf$. In fact, in $\Pi Bf$ one sums $k(\xi x,y)$ against $f(y)\,d\mu_S(y)$; the compact support of $f$ and the bound above allow the sum inside the integral. Simultaneous equivariance rewrites this sum as $K([x],[y])$. The counting-measure quotient formula then gives the stated action against $m$. In particular the output depends only on $\Pi f$, not on its lift. This defines an operator from periodized Bruhat tests into $C_c(G/U)$, not necessarily an endomorphism of the test space for continuous $p$. For periodization weighted by a radial factor $l(r(x))$, for instance $l(s)=s^{1/2}$, conjugate on the quotient: The kernel acquires $l(r(x))/l(r(y))$. For a continuous positive $l$ this preserves local uniform summability and continuity, and the ratio is exactly $1$ on the diagonal.

**Take the Haar diagonal at these fixed cutoffs.** Average the second quotient variable near the first, over neighborhoods shrinking to the identity, with Haar mass normalized to one. The averaged integral converges to $\int K([x],[x])\,dm([x])$ (with the same limit for the weighted version). The first variable is confined to the compact norm band on the quotient, since $G^1/U$ is compact, and the second stays in a compact neighborhood of that band. Use continuity on these compact sets for the limit, or cover them by finitely many relatively compact lifts to use the uniform unit-sum bounds before averaging. The same proof works if both variables are averaged in genuinely shrinking neighborhoods: Enlarge the first-variable band by the norm ratios in a fixed relatively compact neighborhood. No uniformity as the cutoff band grows is used. On the diagonal the unit estimate is uniform over the whole band of representatives, since $y/x=1$ in the phase estimate. Also $r(\xi x)=r(x)$ there, so the input measure conversion cancels the outer measure conversion:

$$
\int_{G/U}K([x],[x])\,dm([x])
=\sum_{\xi\in U}\int_{E^*} b(x,\xi x)\,d\nu_S(x).
$$

In the additive kernel formula the diagonal phase is $\psi(-\xi w)$. Reindex $\xi$ by $\xi^{-1}$ in the **sum**, then set $w=\xi u$. Since $r(\xi)=1$, this gives the phase and transform $\widehat{\eta_\xi}(u)=\psi(-u)\widehat h(\xi u)$ used above. Thus division by $V_S$ gives exactly the previously estimated sum with radial weight $W$. This comparison does not require identifying the two per-unit formulas with the same label. The justified order is the inner Fourier integral, the locally uniformly convergent sum of kernel values, and the diagonal integration or averaging, not absolute unit summation inside the Fourier integral.

An independent mathematical challenge checked the Fourier sign, measure conversion, unit-sum estimate, and diagonal limit, and requested the continuity and range qualifications above. For the profile families and endpoint conditions in the preceding section, the smooth-versus-sharp estimate now applies to this diagonal in an iterated limit (remove the quotient-diagonal averaging at fixed cutoffs first). It does **not** identify the operator with the manuscript's Paley-Wiener endomorphism on its asserted spaces, show a nuclear trace there, or show equality with the common global diagonal or its limit. The quotient kernel itself can be constructed without any of those claims.

## The Fourier multiplier is not the invariant Paley-Wiener projection

**Provisional source reading, independently checked against the digest-matched extraction.** Definition 7.3.2.3, (7.3.2.12)–(7.3.2.17), pp. 354–355, uses generators with **compact** additive Fourier support inside the product-norm bound, imposes the two moments in the $\mathcal S_0$ case, and then closes the periodized image. Definition 11.2.1.3, (11.2.1.6)–(11.2.1.8), pp. 488–489, takes weighted Sobolev closures and defines an **orthogonal** projector for the global level. The local level (11.2.1.6) is a separate subspace. Pages 515–517 assert a nuclear trace transfer through the polar-free image, but use a smooth additive Fourier multiplier in (12.4.1.8)–(12.4.1.9). Page 515 does not exhibit the intervening nuclear arrow $A_0:E_2\to E_1$ in Lemma C.3.2.2, p. 695, (C.3.2.5). The lemma also needs approximation properties and a continuous injection $T:E_1\to E_2$ with $A=T A_0$; it compares traces of $T A_0$ and $A_0 T$, not a trace with an arbitrary kernel calculation. Proposition 12.2.2.2 unfolds a quotient-Haar diagonal but does not establish these hypotheses. Even when the additive Fourier support is compact, a literal replacement of the local level projector by the multiplier has a problem independent of the noncompact product-norm bound for larger $S$.

**Project calculation in the real local model.** Take $F=\mathbb Q$ and $S=\{\infty\}$. The units are $\{\pm1\}$, the quotient is $\mathbb R_{>0}$, and the weighted $\theta$-map on even functions is $f\mapsto 2\sqrt{x}\,f(x)$ for $x>0$. At $a=1/2$ and $s=0$ it identifies the local Hilbert space with even $L^2(\mathbb R,dx)$ up to a fixed norm scalar, by $d^\times x=dx/x$ on the quotient. Write $Q_Y^{(s)}$ for the **local** orthogonal projector onto the level corresponding to (11.2.1.6), not the global projector of (11.2.1.8). In this example the level-$Y$ generators have even Fourier transforms supported in $[-Y,Y]$, with $\widehat f(0)=0$ and $\int\widehat f=0$. These conditions follow from $\mathcal S_0$, not from the polar-free condition.

For $s=0$ the level is exactly the even functions with Fourier support in $[-Y,Y]$ and $\int\widehat f=0$. The support and the integral survive closure by Plancherel and Cauchy–Schwarz on that bounded interval. Conversely, approximate on the Fourier side by even smooth compactly supported functions in $(-Y,Y)\setminus\{0\}$, and subtract a multiple of one fixed such bump of integral one to restore the integral condition. The coefficients tend to zero. Thus the point condition $\widehat f(0)=0$ is lost in this closure, but $f(0)=0$ survives for the continuous bandlimited representative. In particular, if $P_Y=\mathcal F^{-1}\mathbf 1_{[-Y,Y]}\mathcal F$ on even $L^2$ and $\widehat e_Y=(2Y)^{-1/2}\mathbf 1_{[-Y,Y]}$, then

$$Q_Y^{(0)}=P_Y-|e_Y\rangle\langle e_Y|.$$

At every $s\ge 0$, the local norm at $a=1/2$, $I=\{0\}$ in (10.1.1.5), p. 434, dominates $L^2$ in the additive coordinates, up to a fixed normalization constant. Thus the support and $f(0)=0$ conditions still hold for the level, without asserting the same orthogonal projection formula.

For a direct fixed-level test, take $f$ with Fourier transform the difference of two even smooth bumps of equal positive integral, both supported away from zero. Put the first strictly inside $[-Y,Y]$ in a nonempty passband where $q=1$, and the second outside both $[-Y,Y]$ and the support of the upper-bounded radial symbol $q$. Then $f\in\mathcal S_0$. Both the raw sharp multiplier and the smooth multiplier retain exactly the first bump, so their output has nonzero value at zero, unlike $Q_Y^{(s)}f$.

This difference survives the subsequent convolution and position cutoff. Take a smooth nonnegative nonzero $\varphi$ supported in $(2,3)$, so it vanishes at norm one. In additive coordinates the convolution is $A=\int_0^\infty \varphi(g)D_g\,dg/g$ with $D_g f(x)=g^{-1/2}f(x/g)$, by conjugating quotient convolution by the weighted $\theta$-map. The difference of the two cutoff outputs is bandlimited and has nonzero value at zero. Applying $A$ gives an entire function by the bounded Fourier support and the compact range of $g$; its value at zero is the input value there times $\int\varphi(g)g^{-1/2}\,dg/g>0$. Multiplication by any nonzero continuous position profile $p(|x|)$ cannot kill it, since the zeros of a nonzero entire function contain no real interval. This distinguishes the two local composed operators even for the positive degrees used in Chapter 11. It does not preclude a separate trace or limit comparison between different operators.

**A trace difference that persists for the repaired cutoff limit at $s=0$.** At fixed $Y$ and fixed compactly supported annular $p$, $M_p A P_Y$ is trace class on even $L^2$. Expand $e^{\pm 2\pi i\omega x/g}$ in the kernel from Fourier input to position output: $|x|\le B$, $|\omega|\le Y$, and $g\ge a_0>0$ on the support of $\varphi$. The terms are rank one with trace norms bounded by a constant independent of $n$ times $(2\pi BY/a_0)^n/n!$. This also gives the integrated diagonal: After expanding the Fourier input transform, the position-diagonal terms are uniformly absolutely summable on the compact position band. On the whole real line, symmetrize in the input by averaging the kernel at $y$ and $-y$; this kills the odd subspace. Its integrated diagonal is exactly the sum of the two unit integrals on $x>0$ in the [quotient calculation above](#a-fixed-cutoff-quotient-kernel-and-its-haar-diagonal). Indeed, the symmetrized diagonal is even, canceling the factor $1/2$. The norm-one quotient volume here is one for $dx/x$.

The trace with $Q_Y^{(0)}$ is therefore different by the trace of a rank-one operator:

$$\operatorname{Tr}(M_p A P_Y)-\operatorname{Tr}(M_p A Q_Y^{(0)})
=\langle M_p A e_Y,e_Y\rangle.$$

Using $\widehat{D_g e_Y}(\omega)=g^{1/2}\widehat e_Y(g\omega)$, the overlap of the two frequency intervals gives $\langle D_g e_Y,e_Y\rangle=\min(\sqrt g,1/\sqrt g)$. For profiles $0\le p_Y\le1$ with plateau $[A_+,B_-]$, take $A_+Y\to0$ and $B_-Y\to\infty$ as $Y\to\infty$. Since $e_Y(x)=\sqrt{Y}e_1(Yx)$, the squared mass outside the plateau tends to zero. Replacing $M_{p_Y}$ by the identity in this matrix coefficient costs at most $\|A\|\|(1-M_{p_Y})e_Y\|_2$, so the trace difference tends to

$$\int_0^\infty \varphi(g)\min(\sqrt g,1/\sqrt g)\,\frac{dg}{g},$$

strictly positive for the example. For a fixed annular position cutoff and $Y\to\infty$ first, the difference instead tends to zero, since $e_Y$ concentrates at zero. Theorem 11.2.1.4, p. 489, holds the position cutoff fixed in its level limit; the joint-limit example does not refute that theorem. The fixed-position order cannot justify substitution in the joint cutoff regime above. This scalar is the defect of the single closed local moment in this model, not a calculation of the global pair of Mellin corrections. An independent Yah challenge checked the density, the rank-one comparison, the trace-norm bound, and the direction of the cutoff limit. The quantitative scalar result is at $s=0$ only, and neither it nor the fixed-level operator obstruction identifies or disproves the manuscript's global trace identity.
