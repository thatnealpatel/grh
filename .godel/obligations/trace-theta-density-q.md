# Theta density at positive width over the rationals

**Scope:** Project repair for $\mathbb Q$, the trivial compact isotype, $a=1/2$, $s=2r$ for integers $r\ge1$, and almost every $0<\alpha<1/2$. It identifies the weighted theta closure with the common kernel of all the interior zeta jets in the same Hilbert norm. It supplies the missing input for the [positive-width spectral trace](trace-spectral-strip-q.md), not a geometric diagonal or a Lean theorem.

## Source and claim

In the provisional layout extraction of the [pinned PDF](../sources/masullo/README.md), (9.2.2.34), p. 417, specializes at $I=[-\alpha,\alpha]$ to the norm with weight $w(x)=e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2)^r$ for $x=\log|g|_{\mathbb Q}$. Definition 10.1.1.1(1), (10.1.1.3), p. 434, closes the theta image **after** multiplying by $|\cdot|^a$. Equations (10.2.2.1)–(10.2.2.2), p. 445, quotient by that closure, including on a fixed isotype. Compact averaging is orthogonal for the radial weight and preserves the weighted theta image, so the radial closure used below is the trivial-isotype part of the manuscript closure. Equations (10.2.2.4), (10.2.2.6), p. 446, use all the interior zero orders, with exponent $b+it$ on **already twisted** vectors. The later (10.4.2.22), p. 463, instead extracts as $a+b+it$; using it on those vectors would introduce a second twist. The repair uses the p. 446 interface, not that later exponent or the [reversed nesting argument](trace-spectral-strip-q.md#source-interface-a-missing-totality-step) on p. 426.

Use the positive-sign transform $F(z)=\int u(x)e^{izx}\,dx$ of $u\in H=L^2(w\,dx)$, and write $Z(z)=\zeta(1/2+iz)$. The sum of squared boundary $H^r(\mathbb R_t)$ norms at $z=t\pm i\alpha$ is equivalent to $\|u\|_H^2$: Plancherel replaces horizontal derivatives of orders $0,\ldots,r$ by the corresponding log moments on the two boundary lines. This equivalence tests convergence in the original norm. It does not replace its orthogonal projections. The transform is continuous and uniformly bounded on the closed strip by Cauchy–Schwarz, since $\int(1+x^2)^{-r}\,dx<\infty$.

Let $G_\theta$ be the closed weighted theta image and $G_{\mathrm{jet}}$ the common kernel of the jets of orders below the multiplicity at every interior zero of $Z$. The [Mellin identity for rational theta lifts](trace-orbital-q.md#genuine-global-theta-lifts) gives $G_\theta\subseteq G_{\mathrm{jet}}$; the reduction there to even real tests preserves both adelic moments. We will prove the reverse inclusion using just compact log tests $B\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\int e^x B(x)\,dx=0$. For these, $b_\infty(y)=B(\log|y|)/2$ for $y\ne0$, and zero at $0$, has both required moments. The genuine lift $e^{x/2}\Theta_B(x)$ has transform $Z(z)\widehat{e^{x/2}B}(z)$ on the closed strip. The sign and the shift follow from inserting $s=1/2+iz$ in the Mellin identity.

## Bounds for division by zeta

Take widths satisfying

$$
\big|\alpha-|\operatorname{Re}\rho-1/2|\big|\ge c_\alpha(1+|\operatorname{Im}\rho|)^{-3}
$$

for **all** nontrivial zeros $\rho$, on either side of the strip edges. This is a common full-measure refinement of the [jet-trace width condition](trace-spectral-strip-q.md#strip-multipliers-and-high-zeros), whose displayed bound only concerns included zeros: The excluded intervals have summable length by the zero count, and after removing the exact boundary widths the constant can be reduced to handle the finitely many exceptions. Thus both boundary lines in the $z$ variable are separated from the zeros by at least $c(1+|\operatorname{Re}z|)^{-3}$. Indeed zeros with ordinate within $1$ have comparable ordinate weight, and all others are at distance at least $1$. The separation also holds with a smaller constant on disks of radius $c'(1+|\operatorname{Re}z|)^{-3}$ centered on the boundary. The disks can be chosen away from the pole at $1/2+iz=1$.

Here is a minimum-modulus estimate supplying the needed growth control. In the $s$ variable, center a disk at $s_0=2+iT$, for $|T|$ large, and put $U=2+|T|$. On the radius $4$ disk, $\zeta$ is analytic and bounded by $U^C$ up to a constant. For instance [DLMF (25.2.9)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2.E9) with $N=1$, $n=2$ gives a polynomial bound uniformly for $-2\le\operatorname{Re}s\le6$ and large $|\operatorname{Im}s|$: The integral is bounded by absolute convergence and the Bernoulli factor is bounded. The Euler product at $s_0$ gives $|\zeta(s_0)|\ge 1/\zeta(2)$. Jensen bounds the zero count within radius $7/2$ by $O(\log U)$, with multiplicity. Choose a radius $R\in(3,7/2)$ avoiding zeros. Divide $\zeta$ by the disk Blaschke factors of the zeros in the radius $R$ disk. Each factor is unimodular on its boundary, bounded by $1$ inside, and has modulus at least $c|s-\rho|$ on the concentric radius $5/2$ disk, by the formula $R(s-\rho)/(R^2-\overline{(\rho-s_0)}(s-s_0))$. The resulting analytic nonvanishing function $g$ has $\log|g|\le C_1\log U$ and $|g(s_0)|\ge1/\zeta(2)$. Harnack applied to $C_1\log U-\log|g|$ on the disk gives $\log|g|\ge-C_2\log U$ within radius $5/2$, uniformly in $R$. Multiplying the factors back gives

$$
|\zeta(s)|^{-1}\le \exp(C_3\log^2 U)
$$

there whenever the distance to every zero is at least $cU^{-3}$, with constants allowed to depend on $c$. The same conclusion with a constant depending on the width holds on the boundary disks in the $z$ variable, using $T=\operatorname{Re} z$ at the center and compactness at bounded heights. Cauchy's estimate on those disks bounds each fixed derivative of $1/Z$ on the edges by $\exp(O(\log^2(2+|t|)))$; the extra powers of $1+|t|$ fit in the bound. On both edges the derivatives of $Z$ itself have polynomial growth, by the same upper bound and Cauchy on a fixed neighborhood of the strip avoiding the pole.

For each sufficiently large signed integer $n$, choose $T_n\in(n,n+1)$ with distance at least $(1+|n|)^{-3}$ to all zero ordinates. In $[n-1,n+2]$ there are $O(\log(2+|n|))$ ordinates with multiplicity, by the same Jensen bound centered at $2+i(n+1/2)$ (the critical strip over this interval fits in radius $5/2$). Deleting the indicated neighborhoods cannot exhaust $(n,n+1)$. Thus $1/Z$ has the same sub-Gaussian bound along the entire segments $\operatorname{Re}z=T_n$, $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le\alpha$. These segments are used only to control growth inside, not to avoid division at the interior zeros permanently.

## Gaussian division and compact theta approximation

Let $u\in G_{\mathrm{jet}}$ have transform $F$. For $\epsilon>0$ divide the Gaussian-damped transform by $Z$:

$$Q_\epsilon(z)= e^{-\epsilon z^2}F(z)/Z(z).$$

Every interior apparent pole is removable by jet vanishing. The quotient is continuous on the closed strip, including the zero-free boundary. Since $F$ is bounded there, the edge and segment estimates give $|Q_\epsilon(z)|\ll_\epsilon e^{-\epsilon t^2/2}$ on their union, for $t=\operatorname{Re}z$. Apply the maximum principle to $Q_\epsilon(z)e^{\epsilon z^2/4}$ on rectangles between successive good segments (and the one bridging the bounded central interval). This gives uniform Gaussian decay of $Q_\epsilon$ throughout the closed strip, with exponent $\epsilon/4$.

For every integer $j\ge0$ and on either edge, $(-iz)^jQ_\epsilon\in H^r(\mathbb R_t)$: The factor $(-iz)^j e^{-\epsilon z^2}/Z(z)$ and its horizontal derivatives up to $r$ are bounded there by the reciprocal estimates, and the boundary values of $F$ are in $H^r$. Write $q_\epsilon$ for the inverse Fourier transform on the middle line. For each $y\in[-\alpha,\alpha]$, Cauchy contour shifting in the inverse Fourier integral, with the vertical sides tending to infinity by uniform Gaussian decay, identifies the line inverse transform as $e^{-yx}q_\epsilon(x)$. The argument at the endpoints follows by continuity and dominated convergence from the interior. The same shift applies to $(-iz)^jQ_\epsilon$, whose inverse on the middle line is $q_\epsilon^{(j)}$, including the minus sign for the transform convention. Plancherel on the two edges therefore gives $q_\epsilon^{(j)}\in H$ for every $j$. These estimates concern a fixed $\epsilon$; no uniform norm bound for the quotient as $\epsilon\downarrow0$ is needed.

Choose an integer $M$ large enough that all derivatives of $Z$ through $r$ on the edges are $O((1+|t|)^M)$. Then multiplication by $Z$ is continuous from compact log approximations measured by $\sum_{j=0}^M\|q^{(j)}\|_H$ into the pair of boundary $H^r$ norms of the product transform $Z\widehat q$. To check the loss, differentiate the product through $r$ on each edge. Each resulting term is bounded in $L^2$ by the boundary $H^r$ norms of $\widehat q$ and $(-iz)^M\widehat q$: Applying Leibniz to the latter bounds $|t|^M\partial_t^k\widehat q$ inductively for $0\le k\le r$, since derivatives of the polynomial have growth at most $(1+|t|)^M$. Use Plancherel and the boundary sign as above, or apply this estimate to differences of the compact approximants and $q_\epsilon$.

In the derivative norm approximate $q_\epsilon$ by smooth compactly supported log functions $q_j$. It is already smooth by Fourier inversion with Gaussian decay, so ordinary cutoffs equal to one on growing intervals suffice by Leibniz and dominated convergence. For each such $q_j$, correct its exponential moment $m_j=\int e^{x/2}q_j(x)\,dx$. Choose $\beta\in C_c^\infty$ with $\int e^{x/2}\beta(x)\,dx=1$ and subtract $m_j e^{-L/2}\beta(x-L)$. The correction has moment $m_j$ and its derivative norm is $O_\beta(|m_j|e^{-(1/2-\alpha)L}(1+L)^r)$ for $L\to+\infty$. Choose $L$ after fixing $q_j$, making that norm at most $1/j$. This step uses the strict inequality $\alpha<1/2$: The pole-canceling moment lies outside the strip, and convergence in the derivative norm never asserts convergence of these moments. Now $B_j(x)=e^{-x/2}$ times the corrected compact test has $\int e^x B_j(x)\,dx=0$. The transforms of the associated weighted theta lifts converge to $e^{-\epsilon z^2}F(z)$ in the two boundary $H^r$ norms.

Finally $e^{-\epsilon z^2}F(z)\to F(z)$ on both edges in $H^r$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$, by Leibniz and dominated convergence: The Gaussian multipliers and their finitely many derivatives are uniformly bounded for $0<\epsilon\le1$ and tend pointwise to those of the constant $1$. Boundary norm equivalence now gives $u\in G_\theta$. This proves $G_\theta=G_{\mathrm{jet}}$.

## Transfer and formalization boundary

The two subspaces are equal in the original norm, so their orthogonal-complement projections are equal, $P=Q$. For this isotype and these widths the [trace-class and trace calculation for $AQ$](trace-spectral-strip-q.md#trace-and-remaining-transfer) now applies to $AP$ and its block $(1-P)AP$, in the required order. This does not establish a global trace identity: It does not treat other isotypes, widths at which a boundary jet enters, or equality of the geometric and spectral Haar diagonals. In particular there is no freedom to change the width in a comparison without also establishing the hypotheses of the geometric computation at that width.

For a Lean treatment, the two separable inputs to the density interface are (i) polynomial-distance minimum modulus and Gaussian quotient estimates, and (ii) density of moment-zero compact log tests in the stronger weighted derivative norm. The latter uses no zeta facts and its essential standalone claim is the vanishing norm of the explicitly normalized translated bump. With both inputs, the target is equality of closed subspaces, not a bound for biorthogonal lifts. No Lean declaration is supplied here.
