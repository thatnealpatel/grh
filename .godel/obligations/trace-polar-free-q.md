# The polar-free range needed for the local trace transfer

**Status: Provisional source comparison and a rational local calculation, not a Lean result.** This checks an upstream premise of the [geometric trace handoff](trace-geometric.md), independently of the Chapter 13 divisor drafts. The cited passages are from a digest-matched layout extraction of the [pinned edition](../sources/masullo/README.md), not a new visual reading. The one-sided range obstruction remains. A fixed smooth annulus admits a nuclear factorization without replacing the level projection.

## What Chapter 5 supplies

On p. 515, the proof of Theorem 12.4.1.1 claims that $R_\rho A(\varphi)$ factors through the half-weighted $S$-local theta image of $\mathcal S_{00}(A_{F,S})$. It identifies the image linearly and topologically with $|\cdot|_{F,S}^{1/2} \mathcal S_E(J_{F,S})$, citing Proposition 5.3.4.4 and n. 4 ("Return here after the Proposition is updated"). In the extracted proposition on pp. 263–264 there are only three equivalent conditions: Entireness on the specified isotype, existence of a flat lift with the same zeta transform there, and all-strip decay of the isotypic theta transform. Lemma 5.3.4.3, (5.3.4.2), pp. 261–263, gives a lift for the trivial isotype; its proof constructs it via tensor decompositions, not an operator factorization of $R_\rho A(\varphi)$. Even a topological identification of the theta image would not put the cutoff operator's outputs in it.

The next citation, Lemma C.3.2.2, (C.3.2.5), pp. 695–696, needs more than nuclearity on an ambient space or an identification of test spaces. For $T:E_1\hookrightarrow E_2$ a continuous injection of spaces with the approximation property, it requires a **nuclear** $A_0:E_2\to E_1$ with $A=T A_0$. It then compares the traces of $A$ and $A_0 T$. To apply it on p. 515 one must exhibit that factorization for the same cutoff operator whose trace is computed. Neither the Chapter 5 citation nor this abstract trace lemma establishes its range inclusion. This comparison does not rule out proving the topology assertion separately.

## The literal upper position cutoff does not supply the range

**Project calculation, $F=\mathbb Q$, $S=\{\infty\}$, trivial compact isotype.** Interpret the one-sided position cutoff on pp. 515–517 as a smooth upper cutoff $r(t)$ equal to one for all sufficiently small $t>0$, as in smoothing the upper endpoint of $\mathbf 1_{(0,\lambda]}$. This is not the annular cutoff of Definition 9.3.3.1, p. 427. Consider convolution on the half-weighted real Paley–Wiener level followed by multiplication by $r$. Up to a fixed nonzero normalization, the half-weighted theta image is $f\mapsto 2\sqrt t f(t)$ on even $f$, since the units are $\{\pm 1\}$. Its polar-free subspace comes from functions flat at zero, by (5.3.3.10), p. 252, and (5.3.4.1), p. 259. In particular, a positive-half-line function whose unweighted representative has a nonzero second derivative at zero cannot have a flat lift: The even part of any flat lift is flat.

For any bandwidth $Y>0$, choose a nonzero, even, nonnegative $b\in C_c^\infty((-Y,Y)\setminus\{0\})$. Put $u(\xi)=b(\xi)-2b(2\xi)$ and $f=\mathcal F^{-1}u$, with the $2\pi$ Fourier convention. The two terms have equal integrals, and $u(0)=0$. Thus $f(0)=0$ and $\widehat f(0)=0$. But

$$f''(0)=-4\pi^2\int_{\mathbb R}\xi^2 u(\xi)\,d\xi=-3\pi^2\int_{\mathbb R}\xi^2 b(\xi)\,d\xi\ne0.$$

With no auxiliary places, $f$ is already a generator satisfying the two moments in (7.3.2.12)–(7.3.2.17), pp. 354–355. Its theta image therefore survives the projection onto the closed level; no substitution of an additive Fourier multiplier for the level is needed for this test.

Under convolution by a test $\varphi\in C_c^\infty((1,2))$, the unweighted output before position truncation is, in the convention of (12.4.1.4) and the twisted convolution on p. 515,

$$h(t)=\int_0^\infty \varphi(g) g^{-1/2} f(t/g)\,\frac{dg}{g},\qquad h''(0)=f''(0)\int_0^\infty \varphi(g)g^{-5/2}\,\frac{dg}{g}.$$

The compact support in $g$ justifies the differentiation, and the last integral is nonzero for any nonnegative nonzero $\varphi$ in this class. Also $\varphi(1)=0$. Even if the two Mellin moments at $\pm 1/2$ are imposed, such a nonzero integral is possible: The three distinct powers $g^{-5/2},g^{-1/2},g^{1/2}$ are linearly independent on $(1,2)$, hence their integrations against $C_c^\infty((1,2))$ are independent functionals. The output is $2\sqrt t\,r(t)h(t)$. It agrees with $2\sqrt t\,h(t)$ near zero, so it does not belong to the half-weighted flat image. Mere inclusion of the Paley–Wiener generator in the two-moment kernel does not help.

This refutes range inclusion for the specified one-sided local operator, not the extracted trace formula for a different cutoff. It does not compute a trace or settle the global $S$-piece.

## A fixed smooth annulus gives a nuclear lift

**Project repair in the same rational trivial isotype.** In the log coordinate $x=\log t$, put $H=L^2(w(x)\,dx)$, with $w(x)=e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2)^r$, $0<\alpha<1/2$, and $r\ge1$ integral, as in the [local level comparison](trace-diagonal-q.md#the-orders-being-compared). Let $Q_Y$ be the closed local two-moment band projection of that comparison, in these coordinates. Use $A_\phi v=\phi*v$, with $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ the test in the half-twisted coordinate, and let $p(x)=\rho(e^x)$ for a smooth position cutoff supported on a fixed annulus. The operator is $B=M_p A_\phi Q_Y$, not $M_p A_\phi$ followed by a projection on its output, and not the multiplier composition of (12.4.1.8). There is no restriction to tests with support above norm one and no Mellin-moment condition in this fixed-cutoff step.

Choose a closed interval $K$ containing $\operatorname{supp}p$ in its interior. Let $E=C_K^\infty(\mathbb R)$ be the Fréchet space of smooth log profiles supported in $K$, and $J:E\hookrightarrow H$ its continuous inclusion. In additive coordinates $v\in E$ lifts to the even function $(2|t|)^{-1/2}v(\log|t|)$, extended by zero at $t=0$. The lift is continuous into the even Schwartz topology, vanishes on a neighborhood of zero, and is a genuine flat real theta lift, with the normalization of the unitary model. Thus $E$ represents a fixed-support subspace of the half-weighted polar-free image used on p. 515. This does not identify the entire image or its topology with the space cited from Proposition 5.3.4.4.

Define $B_0v=p(\phi*Q_Yv)\in E$. For every $j\ge0$ the differentiated convolution on $K$ is bounded in sup norm by $C_{K,\phi,j}\|Q_Yv\|_H$. The inputs $y$ with $x\in K$ and $x-y\in\operatorname{supp}\phi$ lie in one compact interval, and Cauchy–Schwarz against $w(y)\,dy$ gives this bound with the derivative $\phi^{(j)}(x-y)$ in the reciprocal weight. Multiplication by $p$ preserves the estimates. In particular, the same argument works for any bounded operator in place of $Q_Y$; it does not derive or use a band-kernel replacement.

Take a period strictly containing $K$, let $e_k(x)=e^{2\pi ikx/L}$ for its length $L$, and let $\ell_k(v)$ be the $k$-th Fourier coefficient of $B_0v$ on this period. Integration by parts gives $\|\ell_k\|_{H'}=O_m((1+|k|)^{-m})$ for every $m$, with no boundary terms. Choose $\eta\in C_c^\infty(K^\circ)$ equal to one on $\operatorname{supp}p$. Then

$$B_0v=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\ell_k(v)\eta e_k.$$

For a nuclear presentation, use the summable scalars $(1+|k|)^{-2}$ and the equicontinuous functionals $(1+|k|)^2\ell_k/\|\ell_k\|^{1/2}$ and vectors $\|\ell_k\|^{1/2}\eta e_k$, omitting zero terms. The functionals are bounded in $H'$ and the vectors are bounded in every $E$ seminorm by rapid decay. This proves nuclearity into $E$, not just trace class on $H$. The factorization is $B=JB_0$.

Both spaces have the approximation property needed in Lemma C.3.2.2, (C.3.2.5), pp. 695–696. For $E$, first cut off within a shrinking neighborhood of the endpoints of $K$. The error tends to zero in each derivative seminorm uniformly on compact sets in $E$, by Taylor bounds from flatness at the endpoints and uniform bounds on higher derivatives. For each fixed inner cutoff, take finite Fourier sums of the input on the larger period, and multiply the partial sums by that cutoff. This produces finite-rank maps into $E$ that approximate the cutoff operator uniformly on compact sets in each seminorm. Hence $\operatorname{Tr}_H(B)=\operatorname{Tr}_E(B_0 J)$ for the *same* local level operator. Equivalently the series of rank-one traces on either side is $\sum_k \ell_k(J(\eta e_k))$.

**What this repairs and what it does not.** The extraction of p. 514 n. 2 explicitly suggests an independent lower edge; the fixed-annulus argument supplies the missing nuclear factorization for that change in the real model. It does not give the factorization for the literal upper cutoff (p. 515). In particular, the arrow $P_{\rho,\lambda}:\mathcal S\to\mathcal S_{00}$ in Step II on p. 516 does not hold for smooth multiplication equal to one near zero: An even Schwartz function nonzero at zero is an immediate counterexample on that stated domain. The input projection is the local (11.2.1.6) model, not the global (11.2.1.8) level. The trace equality neither computes the trace in the joint limit nor identifies the level with the multiplier on p. 517. In fact, the [real joint-cutoff comparison](trace-joint-local-q.md#below-norm-one-the-defect-is-not-a-sum-of-mellin-moments) gives different scalar limits for those two local operators on some tests with both Mellin moments zero. A geometric comparison using these cutoffs must therefore identify the actual global level contribution or exhibit a contribution accounting for that non-Mellin defect. No conclusion about the orbital shell follows from the fixed-annulus factorization.
