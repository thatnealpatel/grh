# The continued finite function on the quadratic class isotype

**Result (ordinary mathematics):** For $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$, its
nontrivial ideal-class character $\chi$, and $S=S_\infty$, the finite
product in the [corrected inverse lift](isotypic-inverse-range-q-sqrt-minus-five.md)
is $L(s,\chi_{-4})L(s,\chi_5)$ on $\Re s>1$, with both Euler factors
above the field-ramified rational primes retained. The product of
these entire Dirichlet functions is therefore the continuation of
that denominator. No horizontal reciprocal bound is proved here.

The sign computation below concerns the finite product in the repair, not
the retained product in those two fractions. Since a local unit has
zero finite ideal valuation, the ideal-class character is trivial on
all local units, including those above $2$ and $5$. Thus $S_\chi$ is
empty. The single-place uniformizer has ideal class $[\mathfrak p_v]$
(or its inverse under the opposite ideal convention, which gives the
same sign). The right-hand side below has no archimedean factor or
power of $20$; the different enters the **reflected quotient**, not
this equality of finite functions.

## The norm test and the grouped Euler polynomials

Write $a=\sqrt{-5}$. The integer ring is $\mathbb Z[a]$, the field
discriminant is $-20$, and $N_{F/\mathbb Q}(x+ya)=x^2+5y^2$.
Minkowski's imaginary quadratic bound is $(2/\pi)\sqrt{20}<3$. Hence
all ideal classes have representatives of norm $1$ or $2$. The
unique prime above $2$, $\mathfrak p_2=(2,1+a)$, has norm $2$ and is
not principal because $x^2+5y^2=2$ has no integer solution. Thus the
class group has order two, generated by $[\mathfrak p_2]$.
The unique prime $\mathfrak p_5=(5,a)=(a)$ above $5$ has norm $5$ and
is principal.

For a split odd prime $p\ne 5$, let $\mathfrak p$ be either prime over
$p$. If it is principal, a generator has norm $p$, so the two
coordinates of its generator have opposite parity, and $p\equiv1
\pmod4$. If it is nonprincipal, $\mathfrak p_2\mathfrak p$ is
principal of norm $2p$. In $x^2+5y^2=2p$, reducing modulo $4$ forces
both coordinates odd. Modulo $8$ the norm is then $6$, so $p\equiv3
\pmod4$. These are the only two classes, so the two implications
identify the sign at **each** of the two split places as
$\chi_{-4}(p)$. This uses norms of generators of actual ideals, not an
unproved representation of every prime by one of two forms.

For $p\nmid 10$, splitting is governed by $(-20/p)$. By multiplicativity
of the symbol and quadratic reciprocity,
$(-20/p)=\chi_{-4}(p)\chi_5(p)$, with $\chi_5(p)=(p/5)$.
Thus the two signs agree at a split prime, and are opposite at an
inert prime. For an inert prime, the only ideal over $p$ is the
principal $(p)$ of norm $p^2$, so its class sign is $+1$.
At $2$ the Dirichlet values are $(0,-1)$, and at $5$ they are $(1,0)$.
In both cases the nonzero value is the ideal-class sign, not a
character ramification omission on $F$.

With $X=p^{-s}$, the following are **denominators**. Each row equals
$(1-\chi_{-4}(p)X)(1-\chi_5(p)X)$:

| Rational prime | Ideal norms | Ideal-class signs | Grouped denominator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Split, $p\equiv1\pmod4$ | $p,p$ | $+1,+1$ | $(1-X)^2$ |
| Split, $p\equiv3\pmod4$ | $p,p$ | $-1,-1$ | $(1+X)^2$ |
| Inert | $p^2$ | $+1$ | $1-X^2$ |
| $2$ | $2$ | $-1$ | $1+X$ |
| $5$ | $5$ | $+1$ | $1-X$ |

The factors at $2$ and $5$ are $(1+2^{-s})^{-1}$ and
$(1-5^{-s})^{-1}$, respectively. Neither factor may be discarded. As a finite control, a Sage check of
class principality and residue degrees verified all 303 rational
primes below $2000$, with witnesses $2,3,5,11,13,29$ for all six
ordered pairs of Dirichlet signs that occur. The norm argument and
polynomial equality, not this sample, prove the result at all primes.

Absolute convergence for $\Re s>1$ justifies regrouping the finite
Hecke product and multiplying the two rational Euler products. Both
Dirichlet characters are nonprincipal, so each has an entire
continuation, and uniqueness identifies their product with any
meromorphic continuation of $L_f(s,\chi)$ from that half-plane.

## Lean scope and next check

The split-prime class sign is now proved for the actual ring of
integers in [`IdealClassSplitPrimeSign`](../../Proofs/Theorems/IdealClassSplitPrimeSign.lean).
`Masullo.qSqrtNegFiveIntegersEquiv` identifies the full ring of integers
with the order of discriminant $-20$, and
`Masullo.qSqrtNegFive_split_isPrincipal_iff` proves the norm test above.
It takes the two-class dichotomy and a norm-two prime representative as
inputs. It derives the ideal norm $p$ from residue degree one, and
obtains integral generators of norm $p$ and $2p$ in the two branches
from principality and multiplicativity of the ideal norm. The
congruences then give $1$ and $3$ modulo $4$, respectively.
`Masullo.qSqrtNegFive_split_classCharacter` evaluates any class-group
character normalized to be $-1$ on the representative as the existing
`dirichletNegFour` at each odd degree-one place, in particular at both
places of an odd split prime. At $5$ this would apply once its degree-one
place is identified; the lemma does not prove that identification or the
local factor.

The grouped local polynomial identity for the actual quadratic class
character, including its construction on idèle classes, is still open
in Lean. [`HeckeDirichletFactorization`](../../Proofs/Theorems/HeckeDirichletFactorization.lean)
proves `Masullo.finiteHeckeCharacterEulerProduct_eq_dirichlet_of_local`
by regrouping the convergent product over the finite fibers of
contraction to $\mathbb Z$ and evaluating the local polynomials at
the appropriate residue-degree powers. The endpoint
`Masullo.finiteHeckeCharacterEulerProduct_eq_qSqrtNegFiveDirichletProduct_of_local`
uses the explicit quadratic characters modulo $4$ and $5$;
`Masullo.differentiable_qSqrtNegFiveDirichletProduct` proves their
product entire without any Hecke hypothesis. The local premise
ranges over **every** rational prime; it cannot omit $2$ or $5$.
The endpoint does not instantiate that premise for the number field.

For p. 309's analytic argument, the denominator to estimate on
$-1/2\le\Re s\le2$ is precisely the entire Dirichlet product above.
The [common good-height proof](isotypic-good-heights-q-sqrt-minus-five.md)
supplies the $m=0$ polynomial reciprocal bound in Lean for this
explicit product, without assuming the provisional estimates of pp. 641–643, (B.6.1.35) and
(B.6.1.45). One moving disk counts all zeros of the product with
multiplicity; averaging the **total** logarithmic loss bounds it by
$O(\log H)$ at one common height. The prior lift still uses the provisional local
image criterion, Theorem 5.4.1.1(1), pp. 265–266, (5.4.1.3), the
provisional completed and complex local normalizations on pp. 123 and
129, and the compact-strip Schwartz convention, Definition D.3.1.3,
pp. 800–801, identified in its [holomorphy section](isotypic-inverse-range-q-sqrt-minus-five.md#holomorphy-and-overlap).
