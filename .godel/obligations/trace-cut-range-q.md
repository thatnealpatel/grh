# Finite prime cut ranges on the rational zero line

**Result.** In the trivial norm-one isotype over $\mathbb Q$, **every finite set of primes gives the same closed range after either position cutoff** in (11.1.2.6), including the empty set. The quotient of any two nested cut stages is therefore zero. There is also an exact, one-prime finite-band statement, which survives the input band projection, compact convolution, and terminal theta projection. The two arguments use opposite telescopes.

## Lower-bounded cutoff: Equality after closure

Write $x=\log|g|$, $d=\log p$, and $D_pu(x)=u(x)-u(x+d)$. Use $H_s=L^2((1+x^2)^{s/2}\,dx)$, the radial form of the printed norm on p. 399 up to a fixed positive Haar factor. The Lean coordinate in ordinary $L^2(dx)$ is $w_su$, where $w_s(x)=(1+x^2)^{s/4}$. It is the **raw** profile $u$ that is translated, not $w_su$.

Let $E$ be the profiles of global two-moment Schwartz–Bruhat tests whose class theta is norm-one invariant. For a measurable log window $S\subset[A,\infty)$ and any $u\in E$, put $c_N(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}u(x+jd)$. Real dilation and addition preserve the two-moment test space and its radial theta condition, so $c_N$ is a single genuine profile. Then

$$1_S D_pc_N=1_Su-1_Su(\cdot+Nd).$$

Rapid decay of the translated theta tail gives $\|1_Su(\cdot+Nd)\|_{H_s}\to0$. The opposite inclusion holds before closure by the same stability under dilation and subtraction. This proves $\overline{1_SD_pE}=\overline{1_SE}$ for $s\ge0$, without density in an ambient space, closed range for the uncut difference, or an assumption that a single unramified tensor spans the radial isotype. It applies both to $t\ge1/\Lambda$ and to $1/\Lambda\le t\le\Lambda$ with $t=e^x$. The forward dilations do **not** preserve a fixed Fourier band.

For a finite prime set $P$, put $D_P=\prod_{p\in P}D_p$ on raw inputs. The factors commute with each other and with real translations. To insert $q\notin P$, apply the same telescope with output map $u\mapsto 1_S D_Pu$. Its tail is the translate of the single genuine profile $D_Pu$, so the same decay estimate applies. Induction identifies every finite cut closure with the uncarved cut closure; it does not require a bound uniform in $P$.

**Dictionary for the printed statement.** The rational LF stages represent finite-adelic compact support and open translation invariance with Schwartz real slices (the $K/H$ stages of Proposition 2.1.3.2, pp. 69–70). Impose both zero moments, then impose invariance on the *class theta function*, not on its representative. This realizes the trivial isotype of the theta image, using the manuscript's isotype identification (Proposition 5.2.3.2 and Remark 5.2.3.5). A prime uniformizer has norm $1/p$, and the source uses inverse precomposition, $g\mapsto\pi_p^{-1}g$, in Proposition 11.2.0.1, pp. 484–485. This gives exactly $D_p$ above with no scalar. Definition 5.3.1.2, p. 241, distinguishes the trivial local-unit component (where the operator is a difference) from the nontrivial components (where it is the identity). Averaging a representative over local units preserves both zero moments and its radial class theta: It is a finite coset average on a smooth finite-place test, and theta intertwines the unit action. Thus only the trivial unit component contributes to this comparison. Theorem 6.3.2.1(2), p. 304, describes the two-moment coinvariant as a quotient by the kernel of projected theta. In Lean, `range_rationalAdeleClassThetaFiniteUnitAverage` identifies the normalized finite-unit averaged two-moment theta image with the represented radial image; it does not identify quotient topologies. Reading the manuscript's trivial-isotype projection as this average is the remaining source interpretation.

Thus for $F=\mathbb Q$, $\chi=1$, $S_{\rm places}=\{\infty\}$, and $s\ge2$, the strict-subsequence assertion of Theorem 11.1.2.1(2), p. 480, fails for every proper pair $S_i\subsetneq S_{i'}$ and **every** $\Lambda>0$ in the [pinned edition](../sources/masullo/README.md). The error on p. 482 after (11.1.2.16) is to infer equality of the two uncut spaces from equality after each cut and strong convergence of the cutoffs to the identity. The realizing inputs depend on the cutoff. This does not contest the strictness of the *uncut* inclusion (11.1.2.5).

## Upper-bounded cutoff: Exact equality, including after convolution

Let $E_Y$ consist of $\Theta_b(t)=\sum_{n\ne0}b(nt)$ for even Schwartz $b$, $b(0)=\widehat b(0)=0$, and $\operatorname{supp}\widehat b\subset[-Y,Y]$, $Y>0$. The band is on the **additive Fourier transform of the real input**, not on the Fourier transform in $x$. For the tensor with finite factor $1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$, imposing the actual prime-unit condition on the positive section removes the $p$-divisible indices of the absolutely convergent series. Its theta is $D_p\Theta_b$ in log coordinates. This literal multiplication of a test by a unit mask is not asserted to descend independently of the representative on all adelic tests.

For $0<t\le\Lambda$, choose $N$ with $p^N>Y\Lambda$ and set $c=-\sum_{j=1}^N b(\cdot/p^j)$. Its band is at most $Y/p$. Subtraction of its $p$-dilate gives $b-b(\cdot/p^N)$. The Poisson support edge makes $\Theta_b(t/p^N)=0$ on the whole window, including the upper endpoint. Conversely the difference of any such $c$ has band at most $Y$. Hence $M_\rho E_Y=M_\rho D_pE_{Y/p}$ **before closure** whenever $\rho$ vanishes above $\Lambda$.

Take $A_fu(x)=\int f(y)u(x-y)\,dy$ with $f$ continuous and compactly supported, $\operatorname{supp}f\subset[a,\infty)$. Let $Q_Y$ project onto the closed band image and $P$ onto the full closed even theta image in the zero-line norm. For bounded measurable $\rho$ supported on $x\le\log\Lambda$, one has

$$M_\rho P A_f Q_Y E_Y=M_\rho P A_f Q_Y D_pE_{Y/p}.$$

Both inputs belong to the $Y$ level, so $Q_Y$ fixes them. On a genuine input $A_f$ is the integral of translated theta profiles; boundedness extends invariance to the full closed image, so $P$ fixes its output. For $x\le\log\Lambda$, only $x-y\le\log\Lambda-a$ is sampled. Use the preceding backward telescope with $p^N>Y\Lambda e^{-a}$; the original bound suffices if $a\ge0$. This respects the order of the global operators in (11.2.1.9), pp. 488–489, in the stated real-band model.

**Lean endpoints.** In namespace `Masullo`, `rationalTheta_primeMasked_cutBand_image_eq` gives the exact equality with the literal unit mask, and `rationalTheta_projected_cutBand_image_eq` gives the projected convolution identity. For the entire represented radial core, `rationalThetaRadialPrimeDifferences_class_insert` identifies insertion with the inverse-uniformizer difference of raw class theta, `rationalThetaRadialPrimeDifferencesCut_haar_ae` identifies the weighted, cut Haar representative, and `rationalThetaRadialPrimeDifferencesCut_haar_closure_eq` compares any two finite sets in normalized quotient Haar $L^2$.

**What remains.** Equality of cut output ranges says nothing by itself about multiplicities, diagonals, or traces. The author's [five-page overview](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IeXAs1jGyr5xx5i4RcrNAYj_Lr0jlFi-/view) calls for computing the trace of each removed kernel piece and controlling the residual diagonal. A cutoff on the source stratum of the indented kernel in §11.3 is not the terminal multiplication considered here. To assess that strategy one must identify the actual source-stratum operator with a specified transport of the uncut orbital data, and compare its finite-stage and limiting diagonal masses. The citation of Theorem 11.1.2.1 on p. 495 does not by itself make (11.3.2.1) false. See the [shell comparison](trace-orbital-q.md#main-cutoff-test-no-haar-lift-of-the-literal-shell-kernel) for the separate identification problem.
