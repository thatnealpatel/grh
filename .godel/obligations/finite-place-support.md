# Finite-place support on the idele class group

**Status: Lean endpoints in [`Lemmas.FinitePlaceSupport`](../../Proofs/Lemmas/FinitePlaceSupport.lean), including a finite exceptional set for compactly supported tests that vanish on the norm-one subgroup.** This is a project calculation for the [finite-place support repair](trace-geometric.md#narrowing-the-bridge-project-reasoning-not-a-source-reading). The provisional text of the proof of Theorem 12.5.1.1, p. 520 in the [pinned edition](../sources/masullo/README.md), chooses $S$ so that $|\operatorname{supp}\varphi|_F\subset(T^{-1},T)$ and $T<N(\mathfrak p_v)$ outside $S$. The proof there invokes the orbital trace theorem, not the algebraic result below. The module formalizes the vanishing of the whole single-place restriction under the support and norm-one vanishing hypotheses, without evaluating an orbital trace or comparing any kernel diagonals.

**Local functional (provisional text).** Equation (12.5.1.2), p. 519, applies the linear local distribution with off-identity density $|g|_v^{1/2}/|1-g|_v$ to $g\mapsto\varphi(i_v(g))$, and sums over all finite places. The corresponding term of (12.4.1.3), p. 514, sums over $S_f$. The extracted superscript in the denominator there is unclear, but the prose immediately following names the local unit group, in agreement with (12.5.1.2) and the multiplicative measure in (A.3.2.2), p. 560. The same prefactor appears in (B.4.4.21), p. 625, where the surrounding prose identifies the local test as the pullback from the class group. The prefactor on the unit-group reading is $\log(q_v)/\mu_v(\mathcal O_{F_v}^{\times})$. For a place outside $S$, the input to the distribution is identically zero, including in a neighborhood of the identity, so the omitted term is zero regardless of the prefactor or the regularization at the identity. This does not compare the diagonal traces or justify their Mellin terms. In particular, the orbital terms invoked on p. 520 are indexed by rational residue characteristics absent from $S$, not by individual omitted places.

The support premise uses the nonzero locus of the test; it follows from the manuscript's stronger topological-support inclusion. The whole norm-one subgroup, not just the identity, is needed in the zero-exponent case.

The algebraic declarations in `namespace Masullo` are `ideleNorm`, `ideleClassNorm`, `ideleClassNorm_ofAdicCompletion`, and `finitePlaceRestriction_eq_zero`. The restriction theorem applies to every nonzero local field element (not just units of the local integer ring) and uses no topology or test regularity. The class norm uses `QuotientGroup.lift` and Mathlib's product formula, and its single-place evaluation uses the [single-place norm lemma](single-place-norm.md). For the restriction theorem, write the local norm as $q_v^n$ for $n\in\mathbb Z$. When $n<0$ the norm is at most $q_v^{-1}<T^{-1}$, and when $n>0$ it is at least $q_v>T$. For $n=0$, the whole norm-one vanishing premise applies.

The pinned text extraction distinguishes $q_v=|\pi_v|_v^{-1}$ in (12.5.1.2) from the use of $N(\mathfrak p_v)$ on p. 520. The module uses `v.asIdeal.absNorm`, the residue norm used to define Mathlib's normalized finite-place absolute value. Identifying it with the reciprocal norm of a chosen uniformizer is a separate interface for formalizing the coefficient in (12.5.1.2). The maps are real-valued; this algebraic step does not formalize the local measure, the orbital tail, or the Mellin terms. ## Choosing the finite exceptional set

**Appendix A continuity in Lean, followed by a project deduction for Chapter 12.** Definition A.1.3.1, p. 541, uses the topology on units induced by $g\mapsto(g,g^{-1})$. Equations (A.1.3.2)–(A.1.3.4) specify the norm and its kernel; Lemmas A.1.3.3 and A.1.3.4(3), pp. 541–542, assert continuity and descent. Equation (A.1.4.2), p. 542, names the descended norm on $C_F$. `continuous_ideleNorm` and `continuous_ideleClassNorm` prove continuity for the existing real-valued maps, and `ideleClassNorm_pos` proves that the range is positive as in the manuscript. The proof uses the existing Mathlib idele and quotient types and the product formula, not replacement types.

The continuity proof only needs an open neighborhood of the identity. In the finite adele ring the set of elements integral at **all** places is open (`RestrictedProduct.isOpen_forall_mem` using `Valued.isOpen_valuationSubring`). In the idele group impose this condition on both the finite component and its inverse. The resulting set is open by `Units.continuous_val` and `Units.continuous_coe_inv`, and it contains one. The two integrality conditions force the local norms at all finite places to be one. Thus near one the norm is just the finite product of continuous infinite-place norms to the powers `InfinitePlace.mult`, and it is continuous there. Apply `continuous_of_continuousAt_one` to the bundled multiplicative map and `(QuotientGroup.isQuotientMap_mk _).continuous_iff` to its lift. The product on any idele is positive, since the local coordinates are nonzero and the finite product has finite multiplicative support. Surjectivity, a norm section, and compactness of the norm-one subgroup are not needed for this argument.

For `exists_ideleClassNorm_band_of_hasCompactSupport`, if the topological support is nonempty, the norm attains a positive minimum $m$ and a finite maximum $M$ there. Choose $T>\max(1,M,m^{-1})$; with empty support, $T=2$ works. The norm of the whole topological support is in $(T^{-1},T)$. For `exists_finitePlaceRestriction_eq_zero_of_hasCompactSupport`, take the exceptional set to be the finite places $v$ with $q_v\le\lceil T\rceil$, finite by `Ring.HasFiniteQuotients.finite_absNorm_heightOneSpectrum_le`. Every nonzero value of the test lies in the band, and $q_v>T$ outside the exceptional set, so the algebraic restriction lemma applies to every element of the local multiplicative group. There is no assumption of continuity of the test itself.
