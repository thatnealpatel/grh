# Positive-width spectral trace on the rational jet quotient

**Scope:** A project repair for $F=\mathbb Q$, the trivial compact isotype, $a=1/2$, $s=2r$ with integer $r\ge1$, and almost every $0<\alpha<1/2$. For the projection onto the closed span of the interior zeta jet functionals, convolution **after** projection is trace class, including its geometric-output block for the jet model. Identifying this projection with the manuscript's spectral projection requires the separate totality of these jets on the weighted theta quotient. No common geometric diagonal or Lean declaration is established.

## Source interface: A missing totality step

**Provisional layout extraction of the [pinned PDF](../sources/masullo/README.md).** The quotient in (10.2.2.1), p. 445, is by the closed weighted geometric space. Theorem 10.2.2.1 and (10.2.2.4)–(10.2.2.6), p. 446, give coefficient functionals with exponent $b+it$ and full orders for interior zeros, and assert that the functionals form the dual side of a Markushevich basis. We use only the jets, not their biorthogonal vectors. With the positive-sign Fourier convention below the ordinate stays $t$ and $b=-\operatorname{Im} z$. The estimate in (10.4.2.23)–(10.4.2.34), pp. 463–464, still has the [canonical-lift and trace-norm problems](trace-spectral.md#why-the-canonical-lift-norm-bound-fails); the argument here replaces it, rather than bounding those rank-one terms.

The p. 446 proof cites Theorem 9.3.2.6. In the extracted (9.3.2.10), p. 426, the range of $P_T$ is the intersection of the jet kernels at ordinates $|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\le T$. These ranges **decrease**, contrary to the inclusion stated in its proof. Their projections converge strongly to the projection onto the intersection, not to the identity. In fact, once any nonzero continuous jet enters the intersection, every subsequent output is in its fixed proper closed kernel. For an explicit control on $\mathbb R_{>0}$, use the negative log derivative of $(4\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/4}$ as the test. Its Mellin transform is $\rho e^{\rho^2}$, so the only zero is the simple interior zero at $0$, and every $P_T$ for $T>0$ projects onto the same proper kernel of evaluation there. This breaks the cited argument as written; it does not disprove totality on the theta quotient. Write $G_\theta$ for the weighted theta closure and $G_{\mathrm{jet}}$ for the common jet kernel. The [rational theta Mellin identity](trace-orbital-q.md#genuine-global-theta-lifts) gives $G_\theta\subseteq G_{\mathrm{jet}}$, by continuity of the interior jets. The reverse inclusion is not assumed in the project argument.

## Strip multipliers and high zeros

By (9.2.2.34), p. 417, take the [rational trivial-isotype normalization](trace-spectral-q.md#translation-and-local-finite-rank) with physical weight $w(x)=e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2)^r$ for $x=\log|g|_{\mathbb Q}$. Under the positive-sign Fourier transform $u\mapsto F(z)=\int u(x)e^{izx}\,dx$ (an inessential Plancherel normalization on $u$), the space $H=L^2(w\,dx)$ is identified with analytic functions on $|\operatorname{Im} z|<\alpha$ of bounded horizontal $H^r$ norm, uniformly over the lines. Indeed, the line $z=t+iy$ has inverse Fourier transform $e^{-yx}u(x)$, so Plancherel controls all derivatives in $t$ of orders through $r$. Conversely take inverse Fourier transforms $u_y$ of the lines of such an analytic function. Cauchy–Riemann gives $\partial_y u_y=-x u_y$ in distributions, and multiplying by $e^{yx}$ on compact $x$ test supports gives $u_y=e^{-yx}u_0$. Fatou at the two endpoints bounds the norms with both exponential endpoint weights, whose sum is equivalent to $w$. This identifies the space and norm up to equivalence, not its orthogonal projections in an arbitrary equivalent metric. In particular a holomorphic multiplier with uniformly bounded horizontal derivatives through $r$ acts boundedly, with its operator norm controlled by these bounds and Leibniz. Interior jet evaluation is continuous, also directly by Cauchy–Schwarz in the physical norm.

Let $z_j=t_j+iy_j$ list the strip zeros of $Z(z)=\zeta(1/2+iz)$ with multiplicity. For almost every width there are no boundary zeros and every included zero satisfies

$$\alpha-|y_j|\ \ge\ c_\alpha(1+|t_j|)^{-3}.$$

For each nontrivial zeta zero exclude the interval of widths within $(1+|t_j|)^{-3}$ of its absolute displacement from the central line. The total lengths are summable by the $O(T\log(2+T))$ zero count (e.g. [Conrey, (8), p. 3](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.399.1)), summing in dyadic blocks. Almost every width is in only finitely many excluded intervals; remove the countable exact boundary widths and reduce $c_\alpha$ for the finite exceptions. This choice does not fix an arbitrary prescribed width.

Put $\kappa=\pi/(2\alpha)$, $\lambda=e^{\kappa z}$, and $\lambda_j=e^{\kappa z_j}$. The ratio $(\lambda-\lambda_j)/(\lambda+\bar\lambda_j)$ has modulus at most one on the strip. Define $b_j$ by multiplying that ratio by $-\bar\lambda_j/\lambda_j$ for $t_j>0$ and by one for $t_j<0$, so it tends to one at the horizontal end opposite its zero. For $N\ge2$ let $B_N$ be the product of the factors with $|t_j|>N$, counting repetitions. The following estimates hold on the whole closed strip:

- If $|t-t_j|\le1$, then $|\lambda+\bar\lambda_j|\ge\operatorname{Re}\lambda_j\gg_\alpha |\lambda_j|(\alpha-|y_j|)$. Differentiating the ratio up to a fixed order gives polynomial bounds in $1+|t_j|$ by the separation estimate.
- If $|t-t_j|>1$, expand in the smaller of $\lambda/|\lambda_j|$ and $|\lambda_j|/\lambda$. Derivatives of positive order through $r$ are $O(e^{-\kappa |t-t_j|})$, since the limiting term at either end is constant. The same estimate holds for $b_j-1$ on the end opposite the zero. This ensures locally uniform convergence of the product on the closed strip and analyticity in the interior.
- For fixed $t$, sum the bounds on the factor derivatives: The near count is polynomial by the total zero count, and the exponential terms are summable with polynomial growth in $1+|t|$, by grouping into unit distance shells around $t$. Leibniz and $|b_j|\le1$ bound derivatives of the finite products through $r$ by a fixed polynomial in this sum, uniformly in $N$ and in the number of factors. The bounds pass to the interior limit. On $|t|\le N/2$ all factors lie on their end opposite the zero. The sums of the derivative bounds and of $|b_j-1|$ are exponentially small in $N$ up to a polynomial, by the same zero count. For the zeroth derivative use telescoping and $|b_j|\le1$.

Consequently, for some fixed $M$ depending on $r$ and the width, the derivatives of $1-B_N$ through $r$ are uniformly $O((1+|t|)^M)$, and on $|t|\le N/2$ they are $O(e^{-cN})$ for some $c>0$. For any fixed $k$ with $2k>M$ and $R>\alpha$, set $m_k(z)=(R^2+z^2)^{-k}$. Its horizontal derivatives through $r$ are $O((1+|t|)^{-2k})$, so $B_N m_k$ is a bounded multiplier and

$$\|(1-B_N)m_k\|_{H\to H}\ll_{k,R,\alpha,r} N^{-(2k-M)}.$$

Here and below multiplier notation denotes the corresponding physical-space operator as well. Unlike an individual interpolation-vector estimate, these bounds do not use the gaps between ordinates.

## Convolution after projection

Let $Q$ project onto $G_{\mathrm{jet}}^\perp$, and $Q_N$ onto the span of the Riesz representers of jets with $|t_j|\le N$. Their ranks are $O(N\log(2+N))$. Convolution $A$ by the smooth compactly supported log test $h$ is multiplication by $m(z)=\int h(x)e^{izx}\,dx$. To control $A(Q-Q_N)$ it is not enough to approximate its *projected output*. Instead use orthogonality of the **input** to $G_{\mathrm{jet}}$ and a coercive smoothing multiplier.

First use the metric with a positive smooth weight $\widetilde w$, equal to $w$ off a compact interval and comparable to $w$ everywhere, with all fixed-order ratios $\widetilde w^{(j)}/\widetilde w$ bounded. Such a weight is obtained by smoothing $|x|$ only near zero. Write $\widetilde Q,\widetilde Q_N$ for the projections in this metric. Fix $f=(\widetilde Q-\widetilde Q_N)u$. It vanishes to the required orders at low zeros in the strip transform, and $B_N m_k f$ vanishes to those orders at all zeros. Thus $B_N m_k f\in G_{\mathrm{jet}}$ and $\langle f,B_N m_k f\rangle_{\widetilde w}=0$. Norm equivalence transfers the multiplier bound to this metric.

Let $L_R=R^2-\partial_x^2$ and $v=m_k f$, so $L_R^k v=f$. The physical derivatives of $v$ through $2k$ belong to the weighted space by the strip multiplier criterion applied to $(-iz)^j m_k$. For each fixed $k$ and sufficiently large $R$ there is a coercive estimate

$$\operatorname{Re}\langle L_R^k v,v\rangle_{\widetilde w}\ \ge\ \tfrac12\sum_{j=0}^k {k\choose j} R^{2(k-j)}\|\partial_x^j v\|_{\widetilde w}^2.$$

To see this, expand $L_R^k$ and integrate by parts $j$ times in the term of order $2j$. The undifferentiated weight gives the displayed square. For $1\le\ell\le j$ a derivative of the weight contributes at most a constant times $R^{2(k-j)}\|\partial_x^j v\|_{\widetilde w}\|\partial_x^{j-\ell}v\|_{\widetilde w}$. In the scaled derivative norms $R^{k-j}\|\partial_x^j v\|_{\widetilde w}$ this has the factor $R^{-\ell}$, so all these terms are absorbed for fixed $k$ and large $R$. Integration by parts extends from compact smooth functions by cutting off and mollifying in the weighted Sobolev norm through $2k$.

By orthogonality the left side is also $\operatorname{Re}\langle f,(1-B_N)m_k f\rangle_{\widetilde w}$. Cauchy–Schwarz and the multiplier bound give $\|v\|_{\widetilde w}\ll N^{-(2k-M)/2}\|f\|_{\widetilde w}$. Since $A f=A L_R^k v=(L_R^kh)*v$, the same bound holds for $\|Af\|_{\widetilde w}$, by boundedness of convolution with a smooth compact kernel (compact translations are uniformly bounded in the physical weight). Take $k$ with $(2k-M)/2>2$. The finite-rank differences of $A\widetilde Q_{2^j}$ have summable trace norms, by multiplying the rapidly decreasing operator norm bound by their $O(2^j(1+j))$ ranks. The operator-norm limit is $A\widetilde Q$, hence $A\widetilde Q$ is trace class.

The original, nonsmooth weight cannot simply be substituted into the integration by parts. Instead write $W=w/\widetilde w$ as a physical multiplier. Then $WQ$ has range in $G_{\mathrm{jet}}^{\perp_{\widetilde w}}$, so

$$ AQ=\big(A\widetilde Q+A(W^{-1}-1)\widetilde Q\big)WQ.$$

The first term in parentheses is trace class also in the equivalent original ambient norm by bounded isomorphism. For the second, $W^{-1}-1$ is bounded and compactly supported, so $A(W^{-1}-1)$ has output in a fixed compact interval and bounds all output derivative norms by the input norm. Factor through $H^m$ on a slightly larger periodic interval, whose inclusion into $L^2$ is trace class for $m>1$ by its Fourier singular values; on the output interval the weight is bounded. Thus $AQ$ is trace class in the **original** norm. In particular both $QA=QAQ$ (by invariance of the common jet kernel) and $(1-Q)AQ$ are trace class.

## Trace and remaining transfer

In the original metric $Q_N\to Q$ strongly, by the definition of the common kernel. Then $AQ_N=(AQ)Q_N\to AQ$ in trace norm by finite-rank approximation and self-adjointness. The trace of $AQ_N$ is the trace of its compression to $Q_NH$. The finite jet coordinates are an isomorphism there: Hermite interpolation with a polynomial times $e^{-z^2}$ gives arbitrary finite jets in the strip space, and the representers span $Q_NH$. Compression does not change these jets, so the Leibniz rule gives triangular blocks with diagonal $m(z_j)$. Therefore

$$\operatorname{Tr}(AQ)=\sum_{z:\, Z(z)=0,\ |\operatorname{Im}z|<\alpha}\operatorname{ord}_{z}(Z)\,m(z).$$

The sum is absolutely convergent by uniform rapid horizontal decay of $m$ and the zero count. The [strong cutoff argument](trace-spectral.md#what-the-cutoff-limit-actually-needs) now gives $\operatorname{Tr}(M_\rho AQ)\to\operatorname{Tr}(AQ)$ for the stated exhausting cutoffs. **The manuscript specialization with $P$ in place of $Q$ remains conditional on $G_\theta=G_{\mathrm{jet}}$.** The equality affects the operator and cannot be bypassed by equality of traces. Next, test the reverse inclusion by dividing the Gaussian-damped transform by $Z$. Start with a boundary minimum-modulus estimate at the chosen widths, using the polynomial distance from every zero, a local zero count, and a nonzero anchor on $\operatorname{Re}s=2$. Then test approximation of the quotient by Fourier transforms of compact log tests with the required zero exponential moment, in a norm that controls multiplication by $Z$. This does not address other isotypes, identify a common Haar diagonal, or permit freely changing the width in the geometric evaluation.
