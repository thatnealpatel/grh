# The spectral trace on the rational singleton strip

**Scope:** Project repair for $F=\mathbb Q$, the trivial $C_{\mathbb Q}^1$-isotype, $a=1/2$, $I=\{0\}$, and $s=2r$ for any integer $r\ge1$. This proves trace class in the order $AP$ and the trace with the singleton multiplicity cap, without a Markushevich basis estimate. It does not give the positive-width spectral trace required on p. 520. An independent Yah review checked both arguments and their manuscript interfaces. Page locators refer to provisional layout extraction of the [pinned edition](../sources/masullo/README.md), not visual readings. No Lean declaration is claimed.

## Translation and local finite rank

Put $x=\log|g|_{\mathbb Q}$. Use the normalization of (A.3.2.1), p. 560, for which the compact norm-one group has volume one. In this isotype (9.1.2.3), pp. 399–400, and (9.2.2.34), p. 417, give $H=L^2(\mathbb R,(1+x^2)^r dx)$. Let $G$ be the closed weighted theta image as in (10.1.1.3), p. 434, and let $P$ project orthogonally onto $G^\perp$ as in (10.4.1.2), p. 456. The restriction is valid because compact angular averaging commutes with the weight and preserves the theta image. On this isotype (9.3.1.2), p. 419, is convolution by the compact angular average $h$ of the smooth compactly supported test $\varphi$. Use the unitary positive-sign log Fourier transform on functions, so $A$ becomes multiplication by $m(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}h(x)e^{itx}\,dx$, a Schwartz function. The transform identifies $H$ isometrically with $H^r(\mathbb R)$ with squared norm $\sum_{k=0}^r\binom rk\|f^{(k)}\|_2^2$. In what follows $P$ and $G$ denote the transformed projection and subspace.

Write $Z(t)=\zeta(1/2+it)$. The subspace $G$ contains $Z v$ for every Schwartz function $v$ on the frequency line. To see this, use the [rational theta model and Mellin identity](trace-orbital-q.md#genuine-global-theta-lifts). For a smooth compact log test $B$ with $\int e^x B(x)\,dx=0$, the theta profile is $\Theta_B(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}B(x+\log n)$ and the transform of $e^{x/2}\Theta_B$ is $Z\mathcal F(e^{x/2}B)$. Given $v$, approximate $\mathcal F^{-1}v$ in Schwartz topology by compact smooth $g_j$ with $\int e^{x/2}g_j(x)\,dx=0$. First take smooth compact truncations. For truncation $j$, cancel its remaining exponential moment with a translate by $R_j$ to the right of a fixed compact bump with nonzero moment. For any fixed truncation the required coefficient is a constant times $e^{-R_j/2}$, whereas each Schwartz seminorm of the translate grows only polynomially in $R_j$. Choose $R_j$ so the correction tends to zero in the first $j$ seminorms of an exhausting sequence. Set $B_j=e^{-x/2}g_j$. The Mellin identity then gives the required approximants to $Zv$ in $H^r$. In fact, $Z$ and its fixed-order derivatives have polynomial growth: Differentiate the partial-summation identity $\zeta(z)=z/(z-1)-z\int_1^\infty\{u\}u^{-z-1}\,du$ on $\operatorname{Re}z=1/2$, with every fixed log moment of $u^{-3/2}$ integrable. Thus multiplication by $Z$ preserves Schwartz convergence.

For every interval component $J$ of $\mathbb R\setminus Z^{-1}(0)$, each function in $C_c^\infty(J)$ belongs to $G$ by dividing the test by $Z$ on its support. Orthogonality with respect to the $H^r$ inner product then implies

$$ (1-\partial_t^2)^r(Pf)|_J=0 $$

in distributions. Distributional solutions of this constant-coefficient ODE are smooth and belong to the span of $t^j e^{\pm t}$ for $0\le j<r$. For example, factor into powers of $\partial_t-1$ and $\partial_t+1$ and use that a distribution with derivative of order $r$ zero is a polynomial of degree less than $r$. Membership in $H^r(J)$ can only reduce the dimension, so the restriction of the projected range to each $J$ has dimension at most $2r$.

Use the isometry $E:H^r(\mathbb R)\to\bigoplus_J H^r(J)$ given by restriction, with the same derivative norms. The integral is the sum of the interval integrals because the discrete zero set has measure zero; there is no claim of surjectivity or of gluing arbitrary local Sobolev functions. Let $E_J$ be its $J$th row. The row $E_J AP$ has rank at most $2r$, and the local Leibniz bound gives

$$\|E_J AP\|_1\le 2r C_r\max_{0\le k\le r}\sup_{t\in J}|m^{(k)}(t)|.$$

Here both $P$ and $E_J$ have norm at most one; the multiplier bound is independent of the length of $J$. The right sides are summable. Apart from at most one interval crossing $0$ and the possible two exterior intervals, assign each interval its closest endpoint to $0$. Each zero is assigned at most twice, and the number of ordinates of absolute value at most $T$ is $O(T\log(2+T))$ by Riemann–von Mangoldt (see [Conrey, (8), p. 3](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.399.1)). Schwartz decay and a dyadic sum give summability. Including each row in the direct sum yields an absolutely trace-norm convergent expansion of $EAP$; applying $E^*$ on the left yields $AP\in\mathcal S_1$. Neither the row estimate nor the summation loses a factor as a zero gap closes.

## Trace and cutoff limit

For a zero $t_0$ of order $d_{t_0}$, let $n_{t_0}=\min(d_{t_0},r)$. The jet evaluations at $t_0$ of orders $0\le k<n_{t_0}$ are continuous on $H^r$. They annihilate all weighted theta profiles in the rational trivial isotype by the Mellin identity, which has a factor $Z$, and the [reduction to an even Schwartz real test with the standard finite ring factor](trace-orbital-q.md#genuine-global-theta-lifts). They therefore annihilate $G$.

Their Riesz representers span a dense subspace of $G^\perp$. Indeed, for $u\in G^\perp$ the linear functional $D(f)=\langle f,u\rangle_{H^r}$ defines a distribution in $H^{-r}$. As $Zv\in G$ for all Schwartz $v$, $ZD=0$. Thus off the zeros $D=0$, and locally at $t_0$ it is a linear combination of delta derivatives of orders less than $d_{t_0}$: Divide out the nonvanishing smooth factor of $Z$ and use the structure of distributions supported at a point. Localize by a smooth bump equal to one near $t_0$. Localization is bounded on $H^{-r}$ by duality, and the Fourier transform of a finite delta-derivative sum has modulus given by that of a polynomial. With the reciprocal Sobolev weight, its top degree must be strictly less than $r-1/2$. Consequently the local terms involve only the indicated jets. If $u$ is orthogonal to all their representers, its jets vanish. Multiplying $u$ by smooth compact cutoffs exhausting the line preserves that vanishing and converges to $u$ in $H^r$. On each cutoff, $D$ acts by the finitely many jets on a neighborhood of its support, extending the distribution identity by smooth approximation in $H^r$ and continuity of the jets. Hence $D(u)=0$, so $u=0$.

Let $P_N$ be the orthogonal projection onto the span of the representers at the zeros with $|t_0|\le N$. Then $P_N\to P$ strongly, and $AP_N=(AP)P_N\to AP$ in trace norm by finite-rank approximation and self-adjointness of the projections. On that finite-dimensional space, the compression of multiplication by $m$ acts in jet coordinates by the Leibniz rule (compression does not change these jets). The coordinates are an isomorphism there: The jet functionals on the finite set are independent by compactly supported smooth interpolation, and each is represented in the projected span. The matrix is triangular in derivative order at each zero with diagonal $m(t_0)$. Thus

$$\operatorname{Tr}(AP)=\sum_{t_0:Z(t_0)=0}\min(d_{t_0},r)\,m(t_0).$$

The series is absolutely convergent by the same zero count. This is the trivial-isotype term in (10.3.1.3)–(10.3.1.4), p. 448: The cap $\lfloor(s-1)/2\rfloor+1$ equals $r$. The [strong multiplier argument](trace-spectral.md#what-the-cutoff-limit-actually-needs) now gives $\operatorname{Tr}(M_\rho AP)\to\operatorname{Tr}(AP)$ on this isotype for the indicated exhausting cutoffs, as required by the corresponding specialization of (10.4.2.20), p. 463.

**Remaining transfer:** The interval test functions used above are not available in the same transformed space at positive strip width, where the norm requires analytic continuation across the strip. This does not establish $AP$ is trace class at any positive width, identify a common weighted or Haar kernel, or bound any orbital shell of it. Use the singleton proof as a control when testing a positive-width argument; do not substitute its singleton trace for the sum on p. 520.
