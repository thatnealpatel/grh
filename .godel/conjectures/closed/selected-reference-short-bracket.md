# Short brackets at a selected reference cost at most $q^{.995+o(1)}$

At $g=1$, fix a first incidence, its ordered two-position list, and the actual remaining partner fibers $C_d$ after the full cofactor cut. For each nonempty $C_d$ choose any actual $c_0(d)$, with no restriction on how the choice depends on the fiber. For either row $i$, write $x_i(c)=F_i h_i(c)$ and $cF_i h_i(c)-dy_i(c)=q\delta_i(c)$, where $q$ is prime and $(x_i(c),d)=1$. The smooth coordinates have prime factors at most $X\asymp(\log q)^2$, $Z/X<c,d\le Z=q^{1.055+o(1)}$, and $h_i(c)=q^{.665+o(1)}$. A column fixes the rows on its list.

**Claim:** Uniformly in the input incidence, denominator, position, and choice of reference, at most $q^{.4675+o(1)}$ distinct endpoints $c\in C_d$ satisfy $|\delta_i(c)h_i(c_0)-\delta_i(c_0)h_i(c)|\le q^{.935}$. The union over positions and denominators costs at most $q^{.995+o(1)}$. This is a short-bracket exclusion, not an estimate for the remaining far endpoints.

**Source and payoff:** [One actual reference per denominator](../../alice/one-reference-lifts.md), “Price of a short bracket,” is used in the same note's partner reduction and in the new “After Work” paragraph of [Simultaneous modular output tests](../../alice/simultaneous-output-cuts.md). Without the exclusion at this price, the selected-reference reduction cannot charge short brackets to the stated exceptional budget by this argument.

**Discriminating test:** Derive the signed interval and its unit-slope congruence modulo the actual denominator at fixed $h=h_i(c)$. Reconstruct a uniform strip count with $K=q^{.935}/h_i(c_0)$ and $V=ZK/d$, charging singleton filters, ordinary direction occupancies, and any exceptional nearly parallel direction separately. Check that filtering by $e\mid c$ multiplies the index even for $(e,d)>1$, recover $e$ with the filtered step content, count positive smooth values along an affine line, and verify the exponent after summing over $h$ and $d$. As finite checks of the arithmetic, enumerate smooth numerators for $X=5$, $d=110592$, $Z=2d$, $K=3$: Use $(a,b,e)=(1,0,512)$ and $(125^{-1}\bmod d,402-d,625)$ in the strip $u\equiv ac\pmod d$, $|u-b|\le K$, $e\mid c$. These probe horizontal filters sharing factors with the modulus and oblique filters with nontrivial line step. They test the relaxed strip, not the existence of asymptotic prime-conductor partner families.

**Proof.** Fix $d,i,c_0$ and then the endpoint cofactor $h$. Put $u=\delta_i(c)$, $h^0=h_i(c_0)$, and $b=\delta_i(c_0)h/h^0$. The short bracket requires $|u-b|\le K=q^{.935}/h^0$. Reduction of the row equation modulo $d$ gives $u\equiv ac\pmod d$ with $a=F_i h q^{-1}$ a unit: $d$ is smooth with $X<q$, and $(F_i h,d)=1$. Write $V=ZK/d=q^{.270+o(1)}$. We have $1\le K\le V$, $2K<d$, and $64XV<Z/X$ for large $q$. All counts below are uniform in $b$ and the unit $a$. We use $\#\{n\le H:n\text{ is }X\text{-smooth}\}\le H^{1/2}q^{o(1)}$ for $H\ge1$: The Rankin factor $\prod_{p\le X}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}$ has logarithm $O(\sqrt X/\log X)=o(\log q)$.

Every eligible $c$ has a smooth divisor $64V<e\le64XV$ by multiplying its prime factors up to the first crossing. Sum over these filters, allowing overcount. The graph lattice with $e\mid c$ has basis $(e,ae),(0,d)$ regardless of $(e,d)$. Two differences in the strip rectangle have determinant at most $8ZK<ed$ in absolute value, so all points for one filter are collinear. Filters with only one point cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$ in total.

For a nonsingleton filter take the direction $v$ primitive in the **unfiltered graph lattice**, up to sign. It is not vertical since $2K<d$. The filtered step is $kv$, where $k=e/(e,|v_c|)$ is smooth and $e/k\mid v_c$. Put $R=\min(2Z/|v_c|,2K/|v_u|)$, dropping the second entry for $v_u=0$. Then $k\le R\le2V$: If $v_u\ne0$ use $K\le V$, and otherwise $d\mid v_c$ by the unit condition. Group by dyadic **filtered** length $T\le R/k<2T$, $T\ge1$. Thus $k\le2V/T$. Choose $f\mid e/k$ strictly above $8V/(kT)^2$ with overshoot at most $X$, or $f=1$ if the threshold is below one. This crossing exists since $e/k>64V/k$. For fixed $k,T$, the smooth count allows $q^{o(1)}(1+\sqrt V/(kT))$ choices for $f$. In the direction box with half-widths $(2Z,2K)/(kT)$, determinants are bounded by $8ZK/(kT)^2<fd$. As $f\mid v_c$, the same graph-index argument forces a single direction up to sign. Primitivity fixes $v$, and the divisors of $0<|v_c|\le2Z$ recover $e/k$ at subpower cost. For each recovered $e$ there is at most one nonsingleton line in the original rectangle.

Along that line $c=A+Bj$ with $B\ne0$ on a parameter interval of length at most $L=2T$. Divide $A,B$ by their gcd. Reduced positive smooth values at most $L$ cost $q^{o(1)}\sqrt L$ by injectivity. Each larger reduced smooth value has a divisor in $(L/X,L]$ (take the last partial product at most $L$). This divisor is coprime to the reduced step, so selects one residue class of $j$ with $O(1+X)$ representatives. Counting those smooth divisors gives the same bound. Every original smooth $c$ has smooth reduced value.

Hence the dyadic block costs at most

$$q^{o(1)}\sqrt T\sum_{k\le 2V/T,\ k\ \mathrm{smooth}}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt V}{kT}\right)\le q^{o(1)}\left(\sqrt V+\sqrt{V/T}\right).$$

The first term uses the half-power count of $k$, and the second only the harmonic sum. Summing the logarithmically many blocks gives $q^{o(1)}\sqrt V$ for fixed $h$. The smooth choices of $h$ and $d$ cost $q^{.3325+o(1)}$ and $q^{.5275+o(1)}$, respectively. Thus one position costs $.3325+.135=.4675$ in the exponent per denominator, and the union of two positions costs $.4675+.5275=.995$ in aggregate. No reference multiplicity is charged, and the fixed column and list identify the rows.

**Finite cross-check.** Enumerating the seven possible $u$ in each stated fixture and lifting $c\equiv a^{-1}u\pmod d$ gives exactly $(110592,0),(221184,0)$ and $(50000,-110192),(50625,-110187)$, respectively, after the range and filter cuts. All four numerators are smooth. Dividing each difference by its content in the graph basis gives $v=(110592,0)$ with $k=1$, and $v=(125,1)$ with $k=5$. These are checks of the strip arithmetic only. The proof prices the deletion in test 3; it gives no saving for endpoints passing all four tests.
